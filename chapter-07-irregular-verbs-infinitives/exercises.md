# Chapter 7 Exercises

Complete these without notes. Put a question mark beside uncertain answers.

## A. Transform or translate

1. I can read.
2. We want to leave.
3. She begins to write.
4. They try to understand.
5. I leave the house.
6. We go out tonight.

:::answer
1. `Je peux lire.` — `pouvoir` is followed directly by the infinitive `lire`; no preposition appears.
2. `Nous voulons partir.` — `vouloir` also takes the infinitive directly.
3. `Elle commence à écrire.` — `commencer` requires `à` before a following infinitive.
4. `Ils essaient de comprendre.` — `essayer` requires `de` before a following infinitive.
5. `Je quitte la maison.` — `quitter` needs a direct object, here `la maison`.
6. `Nous sortons ce soir.` — Use `sortir` for going out.
:::


## B. Controlled production

1. Write a new sentence demonstrating **movement and arrival**. Use `Je pars demain.` only as a structural model; change its content.
2. Write a new sentence demonstrating **communication and action**. Use `Vous dites la vérité.` only as a structural model; change its content.
3. Write a new sentence demonstrating **ability, desire, obligation**. Use `Je peux venir.` only as a structural model; change its content.
4. Write a new sentence demonstrating **verb + verb patterns**. Use `Elle aime lire.` only as a structural model; change its content.

:::answer
Answers vary. A correct answer must demonstrate the named structure and use the correct verb frame.

Sample answers:

1. Movement and arrival: `Je pars pour Lyon demain.` — `partir` works without a direct object.
2. Communication and action: `Nous écrivons à nos amis.` — `écrire` needs `à` before the people receiving the message.
3. Ability, desire, obligation: `Ils doivent finir le travail.` — `devoir` is followed directly by the infinitive.
4. Verb + verb patterns: `Elle commence à lire le chapitre.` — `commencer` takes `à` before the infinitive.

Mark yourself wrong if `quitter` appears without an object, if `écrire` to a person lacks `à`, or if you add `à` or `de` after `pouvoir`, `vouloir`, or `devoir`.
:::


## C. Four-way variation

Choose one sentence from each lesson unit and rewrite it four ways:

1. change the subject;
2. make it negative;
3. turn it into a question;
4. change one meaningful detail.

:::answer
Worked example, starting from `Je veux lire ce livre.`

1. Change the subject: `Nous voulons lire ce livre.` — `veux` changes to `voulons`; `lire` stays infinitive.
2. Make it negative: `Je ne veux pas lire ce livre.` — The negative wraps the conjugated verb `veux`.
3. Turn it into a question: `Est-ce que tu veux lire ce livre ?` — The second verb stays infinitive.
4. Change one meaningful detail: `Je veux écrire ce message.` — The activity changed, but the no-preposition frame after `vouloir` stayed the same.

Check that only the conjugated verb changes for subject agreement. The infinitive should not be conjugated.
:::


## D. Dictation and correction

Use text-to-speech to hear six examples from `lessons.md`. Write what you hear without looking. Correct in separate passes:

1. vocabulary;
2. verb form;
3. agreement or pronoun placement;
4. spelling and accents.

Do the dictation first, using this chapter's own examples as your audio. Then open the answers to check: they list six sentences worth dictating and the trap each one sets.

:::answer
Six sentences from this chapter that make good dictation material, and the trap each one sets:

1. `Je pars demain matin.` — The final **s** in `pars` is silent; the subject tells you it is first person.
2. `Nous venons de dîner.` — This is recent past. Listen for `de` before the infinitive and the accent in `dîner`.
3. `Nous écrivons à nos amis chaque semaine.` — The trap is the **v** sound in `écrivons` and the required `à` before the people.
4. `Ils ne peuvent pas venir ce soir.` — `peuvent` has a special plural stem, and the negative wraps the conjugated verb.
5. `Elle commence à apprendre le français.` — `commencer` takes `à` before the infinitive.
6. `J'essaie de finir ce rapport avant huit heures.` — `je` elides to `j'`, and `essayer` takes `de` before the infinitive.

Correct in separate passes. A missing silent ending is a verb-form problem; a missing `à` or `de` is a frame problem.
:::

## E. Personal writing

Write eight decisions for the coming month using pouvoir, vouloir, devoir, commencer à, and essayer de.

:::answer
Answers vary. A model set:

1. `Je veux lire un livre en français.`
2. `Je peux étudier vingt minutes chaque matin.`
3. `Je dois finir ce chapitre avant vendredi.`
4. `Je vais commencer à écrire un petit journal.`
5. `Je vais essayer de parler français au café.`
6. `Nous voulons sortir moins tard le soir.`
7. `Je dois répondre à mes messages plus vite.`
8. `Je peux mettre mon téléphone loin de mon bureau.`

Self-check: include all five required patterns. Mark yourself wrong if you wrote `commencer de`, `essayer à`, or added a preposition after `pouvoir`, `vouloir`, or `devoir` before the infinitive.
:::


## F. Oral recall

Explain each of the chapter's four main rules aloud in simple English, then give one French example from memory.

:::answer
1. **Movement and arrival.** Use `partir` for departure, `sortir` for going out, and `quitter` with a direct object. Example: `Je quitte le bureau à six heures.`
2. **Communication and action.** Learn irregular forms such as `dire`, `lire`, `écrire`, `mettre`, and `recevoir` in whole phrases. Example: `Nous écrivons à nos amis.`
3. **Ability, desire, obligation.** `Pouvoir`, `vouloir`, and `devoir` are followed directly by an infinitive. Example: `Je peux venir.`
4. **Verb + verb patterns.** Some verbs take no preposition, some take `à`, and some take `de`. Example: `Elle commence à écrire et essaie de comprendre.`

If your example does not match the rule you named, redo the rule aloud with a different sentence.
:::
