# Chapter 7 Lessons: Irregular Verbs I and Verb + Infinitive Patterns
This chapter gives you a new group of high-frequency irregular verbs and shows you how French links one verb to another. By the end, you should be able to talk about leaving, coming, holding, saying, reading, writing, putting, receiving, being able to, wanting to, having to, and having just done something.
## Learning objectives
You will learn to:
1. distinguish `partir`, `sortir`, and `quitter` in natural contexts;
2. conjugate and use `venir` and `tenir`, including `venir de + infinitive` for the recent past;
3. conjugate `dire`, `lire`, `écrire`, `mettre`, and `recevoir` in the present tense;
4. use `pouvoir`, `vouloir`, and `devoir` with the correct meaning in context;
5. recognize when a second verb follows directly, follows `à`, or follows `de`;
6. build longer sentences with two verbs without translating word for word from English.
## Prerequisite quick check
Before beginning, make sure you can already:
- use `aller + infinitive` for the near future from Chapter 6;
- form negative sentences with `ne...pas`;
- use common time expressions such as `demain`, `ce soir`, and `après le travail`;
- recognize an infinitive such as `partir`, `lire`, or `mettre`.
This chapter adds new irregular forms, but the sentence frame will often feel familiar: `je vais partir`, `je veux partir`, `je viens de partir`, and `je dois partir` all depend on your earlier control of infinitives.
## How to study this chapter
Memorize the verb blocks aloud and in contrast. For example, say `je pars`, `je sors`, `je quitte` together so the meaning differences stay attached to the forms.
## Unit 1: `partir`, `sortir`, and `quitter`
### Core idea
These three verbs can all relate to leaving, but they do **not** mean the same thing.
- `partir` = to leave, to depart
- `sortir` = to go out, to take out
- `quitter` = to leave something or someone behind; it takes a direct object
### Present tense forms
| Pronoun | `partir` | `sortir` | `quitter` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `je` | `pars` | `sors` | `quitte` |
| `tu` | `pars` | `sors` | `quittes` |
| `il/elle/on` | `part` | `sort` | `quitte` |
| `nous` | `partons` | `sortons` | `quittons` |
| `vous` | `partez` | `sortez` | `quittez` |
| `ils/elles` | `partent` | `sortent` | `quittent` |
### How the meanings differ
Use `partir` when the focus is the departure itself.
- `Je pars demain matin.` — I am leaving tomorrow morning.
- `Le train part à sept heures.` — The train leaves at seven o'clock.
Use `sortir` when someone goes out or takes something out.
- `Elle sort ce soir avec ses collègues.` — She is going out tonight with her coworkers.
- `Nous sortons les chaises sur le balcon.` — We are taking the chairs out onto the balcony.
Use `quitter` with a direct object.
- `Ils quittent le bureau à six heures.` — They leave the office at six o'clock.
- `Je quitte Paris vendredi.` — I leave Paris on Friday.
### Pronunciation note
The plural forms `partent`, `sortent`, and `quittent` end in written `-ent`, but that ending is silent. By ear, `ils partent` sounds like the same stem as `il part`.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not say `*je quitte à six heures` when you mean "I leave at six." `Quitter` needs an object: `je quitte le bureau à six heures`. If there is no object, use `partir`.
### Sentence-building practice
Choose the correct verb and write a complete sentence.
1. You are leaving tomorrow for Lyon.
2. We are going out after dinner.
3. She is leaving the classroom now.
4. They are taking the dog outside.
Then explain why one sentence needs `quitter`.
## Unit 2: `venir`, `tenir`, and `venir de + infinitive`
### Core idea
`Venir` means **to come** and `tenir` means **to hold / keep**. Their forms are closely related, and `venir de + infinitive` creates the **recent past**: "to have just done something."
### Present tense forms
| Pronoun | `venir` | `tenir` |
|---|---|---|
| `je` | `viens` | `tiens` |
| `tu` | `viens` | `tiens` |
| `il/elle/on` | `vient` | `tient` |
| `nous` | `venons` | `tenons` |
| `vous` | `venez` | `tenez` |
| `ils/elles` | `viennent` | `tiennent` |
### Main uses of `venir`
- `Tu viens au café avec nous ?` — Are you coming to the café with us?
- `Elle vient de Marseille.` — She comes from Marseille.
- `Mes cousins viennent ce week-end.` — My cousins are coming this weekend.
### Main uses of `tenir`
- `Je tiens la porte pour vous.` — I am holding the door for you.
- `Nous tenons beaucoup à ce projet.` — We care a lot about this project.
- `Elle tient toujours un carnet dans son sac.` — She always keeps a notebook in her bag.
### The recent past: `venir de + infinitive`
Use `venir de + infinitive` for something that has **just happened**.
| Subject | `venir` | `de` | Infinitive | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `je` | `viens` | `de` | `finir` | I have just finished |
| `nous` | `venons` | `de` | `rentrer` | we have just come back |
| `ils` | `viennent` | `d'` | `arriver` | they have just arrived |
- `Je viens de lire ton message.` — I have just read your message.
- `Nous venons de dîner.` — We have just had dinner.
- `Il vient de partir.` — He has just left.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not confuse `venir de + infinitive` with origin.
- `Elle vient du Canada.` — She comes from Canada.
- `Elle vient de téléphoner.` — She has just called.
The grammar after `de` tells you which meaning is intended.
### Sentence-building practice
Translate and say aloud.
1. We are coming from the station.
2. I have just written the email.
3. Are you holding my keys?
4. They have just gone out.
Then change sentence 2 to the negative.
## Unit 3: `dire`, `lire`, `écrire`, `mettre`, and `recevoir`
### Core idea
These verbs appear constantly in daily life. They do not follow one single pattern, so learn them in clear families and useful phrases.
### Present tense forms
| Pronoun | `dire` | `lire` | `écrire` | `mettre` | `recevoir` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `je` | `dis` | `lis` | `écris` | `mets` | `reçois` |
| `tu` | `dis` | `lis` | `écris` | `mets` | `reçois` |
| `il/elle/on` | `dit` | `lit` | `écrit` | `met` | `reçoit` |
| `nous` | `disons` | `lisons` | `écrivons` | `mettons` | `recevons` |
| `vous` | `dites` | `lisez` | `écrivez` | `mettez` | `recevez` |
| `ils/elles` | `disent` | `lisent` | `écrivent` | `mettent` | `reçoivent` |
### Examples in context
- `Vous dites toujours la vérité.` — You always tell the truth.
- `Je lis davantage le soir.` — I read more in the evening.
- `Nous écrivons à nos amis chaque semaine.` — We write to our friends every week.
- `Ils mettent leurs manteaux près de la porte.` — They put their coats near the door.
- `Elle reçoit souvent des lettres de sa grand-mère.` — She often receives letters from her grandmother.
- `Que disent les enfants ?` — What are the children saying?
- `Tu lis quel journal ?` — Which newspaper do you read?
- `Je mets la table avant le dîner.` — I set the table before dinner.
### Pronunciation note
The written endings in `vous dites`, `vous lisez`, and `vous écrivez` help distinguish the `vous` form in writing, but in fast speech you must listen for the whole sound group. Also notice `nous écrivons`: the stem changes, and the `v` is clearly pronounced.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not forget that `écrire à quelqu'un` needs `à` before a person: `j'écris à mon frère`. But `lire` takes a direct object: `je lis le message`.
### Sentence-building practice
Complete or transform each sentence.
1. `Je ___ un message à ma mère.` Use `écrire`.
2. `Nous ___ le journal pendant le petit-déjeuner.` Use `lire`.
3. `Elle ___ ses chaussures près du lit.` Use `mettre`.
4. Turn sentence 2 into a question with `vous`.
5. Turn sentence 1 into the recent past with `venir de`.
## Unit 4: `pouvoir`, `vouloir`, and `devoir`
### Core idea
These verbs are often followed directly by an infinitive. They express ability, desire, necessity, probability, and debt depending on context.
### Present tense forms
| Pronoun | `pouvoir` | `vouloir` | `devoir` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `je` | `peux` | `veux` | `dois` |
| `tu` | `peux` | `veux` | `dois` |
| `il/elle/on` | `peut` | `veut` | `doit` |
| `nous` | `pouvons` | `voulons` | `devons` |
| `vous` | `pouvez` | `voulez` | `devez` |
| `ils/elles` | `peuvent` | `veulent` | `doivent` |
### Direct link to the infinitive
| Pattern | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| `pouvoir + infinitive` | `Je peux venir.` | I can come. |
| `vouloir + infinitive` | `Nous voulons apprendre.` | We want to learn. |
| `devoir + infinitive` | `Tu dois travailler.` | You must work. |
### The meanings of `devoir`
`Devoir` has three important uses.
1. obligation: `Je dois finir ce travail ce soir.` — I have to finish this work tonight.
2. probability: `Il doit être chez lui maintenant.` — He is probably at home now.
3. debt/owing: `Je dois dix euros à Paul.` — I owe Paul ten euros.
### Examples in context
- `Je veux lire davantage, mais je dois finir mon travail.` — I want to read more, but I must finish my work.
- `Nous pouvons sortir après le cours.` — We can go out after class.
- `Elle veut recevoir plus de nouvelles de sa famille.` — She wants to receive more news from her family.
- `Vous devez mettre vos téléphones sur la table.` — You must put your phones on the table.
- `Ils ne peuvent pas venir ce soir.` — They cannot come tonight.
- `On veut partir tôt demain.` — We want to leave early tomorrow.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not add `de` or `à` after these three verbs before a second verb.
- correct: `je peux venir`, `nous voulons apprendre`, `elle doit partir`
- incorrect: `*je peux de venir`, `*nous voulons à apprendre`
### Sentence-building practice
Translate into French.
1. I can read this book.
2. We want to leave now.
3. He probably has to be at the office. Make the meaning probability, not obligation.
4. They owe their friend twenty euros.
Then rewrite sentence 1 in the negative and sentence 2 as a question.
## Unit 5: Verb + infinitive patterns: no preposition, `à`, or `de`
### Core idea
French verbs do not all connect to a following infinitive in the same way. Some take **no preposition**, some take `à`, and others take `de`. Learn the whole frame.
### Group 1: no preposition before the infinitive
| Verb | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| `aimer` | `J'aime lire le matin.` | I like to read in the morning. |
| `préférer` | `Nous préférons rester ici.` | We prefer to stay here. |
| `aller` | `Elle va téléphoner.` | She is going to call. |
| `pouvoir` | `Tu peux sortir.` | You can go out. |
| `vouloir` | `Ils veulent apprendre.` | They want to learn. |
| `devoir` | `Je dois partir.` | I must leave. |
| `venir de` | `Il vient de finir.` | He has just finished. |
### Group 2: verbs followed by `à`
| Verb + `à` | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| `commencer à` | `Elle commence à apprendre le français.` | She is beginning to learn French. |
| `apprendre à` | `Nous apprenons à cuisiner.` | We are learning to cook. |
| `aider à` | `Tu m'aides à porter les sacs ?` | Are you helping me carry the bags? |
| `réussir à` | `Ils réussissent à finir à temps.` | They manage to finish on time. |
| `se mettre à` | `Je me mets à rire.` | I start laughing. |
### Group 3: verbs followed by `de`
| Verb + `de` | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| `essayer de` | `J'essaie de parler chaque jour.` | I try to speak every day. |
| `arrêter de` | `Elle arrête de travailler à six heures.` | She stops working at six o'clock. |
| `oublier de` | `Nous oublions de fermer la fenêtre.` | We forget to close the window. |
| `choisir de` | `Ils choisissent de rester.` | They choose to stay. |
| `refuser de` | `Il refuse de répondre.` | He refuses to answer. |
### How to study the pattern
Never memorize only `essayer` or only `commencer`. Memorize `essayer de`, `commencer à`, `pouvoir`, `vouloir`, `devoir`, and `aller + infinitive` as full frames.
### Common mistake for English speakers
English often uses "to" before the second verb, but French does not always match that structure. `Je veux partir` has no preposition, while `je commence à partir` and `j'essaie de partir` do.
### Sentence-building practice
Sort each frame into the correct group, then use it in a complete sentence: `vouloir`, `commencer`, `essayer`, `aimer`, `réussir`, `devoir`.
## Cumulative sentence-usage practice
These sentences combine the chapter's new verbs and infinitive patterns the way they appear in real speech. Read each sentence aloud twice, cover it, and write it from memory.
1. `Je veux lire davantage, mais je dois finir mon travail.` — I want to read more, but I must finish my work.
2. `Nous pouvons sortir après le cours et écrire le message plus tard.` — We can go out after class and write the message later.
3. `Elle commence à apprendre le français et essaie de parler chaque jour.` — She is beginning to learn French and tries to speak every day.
4. `Ils quittent le bureau et partent à six heures.` — They leave the office and depart at six o'clock.
5. `Je viens de recevoir ta lettre et je vais y répondre ce soir.` — I have just received your letter and I am going to answer it tonight.
6. `Vous dites que vous voulez venir, mais vous ne pouvez pas sortir avant neuf heures.` — You say that you want to come, but you cannot go out before nine o'clock.
### Notice and mark
For each sentence above:
1. circle every conjugated verb;
2. box each infinitive;
3. label the link before the infinitive as **none**, `à`, or `de`;
4. identify the sentence that uses the recent past.
### Change the sentence
Rewrite sentences 1-4 twice each:
1. change the subject, person, or number;
2. change one meaningful detail such as the place, time, or activity.
Then turn sentence 5 into the near future and sentence 6 into a yes/no question.

## Mini-dialogue: after work plans
**Julie :** `Tu pars directement après le bureau ce soir ?` — Are you leaving directly after the office tonight?
**Samir :** `Non, je viens de recevoir un message de ma sœur.` — No, I have just received a message from my sister.
**Julie :** `Qu'est-ce qu'elle dit ?` — What is she saying?
**Samir :** `Elle veut sortir, mais elle doit d'abord écrire à son professeur.` — She wants to go out, but she first has to write to her professor.
**Julie :** `Et toi, tu peux venir au restaurant avec nous ?` — And you, can you come to the restaurant with us?
**Samir :** `Je peux venir plus tard, mais je dois quitter le bureau à l'heure et passer chez moi.` — I can come later, but I have to leave the office on time and stop by my place.
**Julie :** `D'accord. Nous allons te garder une place près de la fenêtre.` — Okay. We are going to save you a seat by the window.
**Samir :** `Parfait. J'essaie de finir ce rapport avant huit heures.` — Perfect. I am trying to finish this report before eight o'clock.
### Guided analysis
1. Find one example each of `partir`, `venir de + infinitive`, `dire`, `vouloir`, `devoir`, and `essayer de`.
2. Explain why the dialogue uses `quitter le bureau` instead of `partir le bureau`.
3. Rewrite Samir's last answer with `nous` instead of `je`.
4. Change `elle doit d'abord écrire à son professeur` into the recent past.
5. Create another natural sentence using `tenir`, for example one meaning to hold something or to care about something.
## Connected reading: un nouveau rythme
`Depuis le début du semestre, ma routine change beaucoup. Je pars plus tôt le matin parce que je veux lire dans le bus avant les cours. Après la classe, je sors souvent avec deux amis, mais je quitte la bibliothèque seulement quand je finis vraiment mon travail. Nous venons de commencer un projet important, alors nous devons écrire beaucoup et nous recevons des messages du professeur presque tous les jours. Le soir, j'essaie de me détendre, mais je ne peux pas toujours arrêter de penser au travail. Heureusement, ma sœur vient parfois dîner avec moi, et nous tenons encore à notre petit repas du jeudi.`
— Since the beginning of the semester, my routine has been changing a lot. I leave earlier in the morning because I want to read on the bus before class. After class, I often go out with two friends, but I leave the library only when I truly finish my work. We have just started an important project, so we have to write a lot and we receive messages from the professor almost every day. In the evening, I try to relax, but I cannot always stop thinking about work. Fortunately, my sister sometimes comes to have dinner with me, and we still care about our little Thursday meal.

5. Write three true sentences about your own schedule using the reading as a model.
## Common mistakes to avoid
1. **Using `quitter` without an object.** Say `je pars maintenant`, but `je quitte la maison maintenant`.
2. **Confusing origin with recent past.** `venir de Paris` is not the same as `venir de partir`.
3. **Adding an article before an infinitive.** Write `je veux partir`, not `*je veux le partir`.
4. **Using the wrong connector before the infinitive.** `commencer à`, but `essayer de`.
5. **Forgetting that `écrire` often uses `à` with a person.** `j'écris à ma tante`.
6. **Missing the special `nous` and `vous` forms.** `nous venons`, `vous dites`, `nous écrivons`, `nous pouvons`, `vous voulez`.

### Core habits
- Learn each verb with a useful phrase and a contrasting verb where possible.
- Check whether the second verb needs no preposition, `à`, or `de`.
- Use `venir de + infinitive` for something that happened a moment ago.
- Keep the infinitive unchanged; only the first verb is conjugated.
