# Chapter 3 Lessons: Calendar Language, Regular -er Verbs, and Questions

This chapter moves French from isolated words to daily life. You will learn how to place actions in the week or the year, conjugate the very common regular `-er` verbs, and ask the kinds of questions that keep a conversation moving.

## Learning objectives

You will learn to:
1. say the days of the week, months, seasons, and parts of the day;
2. use core time expressions such as `le lundi`, `en septembre`, `en hiver`, and `ce soir`;
3. conjugate regular present-tense `-er` verbs in all six persons;
4. recognize the main uses of the French present tense;
5. ask yes/no questions with intonation, `est-ce que`, and inversion;
6. ask information questions with common question words;
7. combine chapter grammar in short speech, dialogue, and paragraph writing.

## Prerequisite quick check

Before you begin, make sure you can still:
- choose the correct subject pronoun from Chapter 2;
- conjugate `être` and `avoir` in the present;
- recognize article + noun groups from Chapter 1;
- read short negative sentences such as `Je ne suis pas prêt` and `Il n'y a pas de café`.

If those skills feel shaky, review them briefly first. This chapter assumes you can already see the subject of a sentence and match a verb to it.

## How to study this chapter

Say every French example aloud. Then cover the French, reproduce it from memory, and do the substitution task before moving on.

## Unit 1: Days, months, seasons, and parts of the day

### Core idea

French uses time words constantly, and most of them stay lowercase in normal writing.

### Core vocabulary tables

| Days | English | Months | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| `lundi` | Monday | `janvier` | January |
| `mardi` | Tuesday | `février` | February |
| `mercredi` | Wednesday | `mars` | March |
| `jeudi` | Thursday | `avril` | April |
| `vendredi` | Friday | `mai` | May |
| `samedi` | Saturday | `juin` | June |
| `dimanche` | Sunday | `juillet` | July |
|  |  | `août` | August |
|  |  | `septembre` | September |
|  |  | `octobre` | October |
|  |  | `novembre` | November |
|  |  | `décembre` | December |

| French | English | French | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| `le printemps` | spring | `le matin` | morning |
| `l'été` | summer | `l'après-midi` | afternoon |
| `l'automne` | autumn, fall | `le soir` | evening |
| `l'hiver` | winter | `la nuit` | night |

### Examples

- `Nous sommes mardi.` — It is Tuesday.
- `Demain, c'est mercredi.` — Tomorrow is Wednesday.
- `Mon anniversaire est en mai.` — My birthday is in May.
- `J'adore l'automne.` — I love fall.
- `Le soir, mes voisins téléphonent à leur famille.` — In the evening, my neighbors call their family.
- `Cette nuit, le vent est fort.` — Tonight / Last night, the wind is strong.

### Pronunciation note

Many final consonants are silent in these words: `mars`, `avril`, `septembre`, `soir`. Also notice that `août` is short and crisp.

### Common mistake for English speakers

Do not capitalize days and months in ordinary French writing: `lundi`, `septembre`, not *Monday*, *September* style capitals.

### Sentence-building practice

Complete aloud:
1. `Nous sommes ___ .`
2. `Mon cours est en ___ .`
3. `J'aime ___ parce que ___ .`
4. `Le soir, je ___ .`
Then write one original sentence with a day, one with a month, one with a season, and one with a part of the day.

## Unit 2: Using calendar expressions naturally

### Core idea

French needs a few small structure words to turn calendar vocabulary into usable sentences.

### High-frequency patterns

| Pattern | Use | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Nous sommes + day/month` | what day or month it is | `Nous sommes lundi.` | It's Monday. |
| `le + day` | repeated action on that day | `Le samedi, je travaille.` | On Saturdays, I work. |
| `en + month` | in a month | `Nous voyageons en juillet.` | We travel in July. |
| `en + season` | in a season | `Il pleut souvent en hiver.` | It often rains in winter. |
| `au printemps` | in spring | `Au printemps, le parc change vite.` | In spring, the park changes quickly. |
| `ce matin / cet après-midi / ce soir` | this morning / afternoon / evening | `Ce matin, j'étudie.` | This morning I'm studying. |
| `demain matin / demain soir` | tomorrow morning / evening | `Demain soir, on dîne dehors.` | Tomorrow evening we're eating out. |
| `la nuit` | at night | `La nuit, la ville est calme.` | At night the city is calm. |

### Examples

- `Le lundi, je travaille à la maison.` — On Mondays, I work at home.
- `En septembre, nous étudions le français avec un nouveau professeur.` — In September, we study French with a new teacher.
- `Au printemps, les cafés ouvrent leurs terrasses.` — In spring, cafés open their patios.
- `Ce soir, je téléphone à ma sœur.` — This evening, I am calling my sister.
- `Demain matin, vous arrivez tôt ?` — Are you arriving early tomorrow morning?

### Pronunciation note

Notice `ce` versus `cet`: `ce soir`, but `cet après-midi`. `Cet` appears before a vowel sound.

### Common mistake for English speakers

French usually uses singular `le lundi` for a habit, not a direct copy of English plural "on Mondays." Also say `en juillet`, not `à juillet`.

### Sentence-building practice

Turn each prompt into a full sentence: every Friday / we / study at the library; in December / my family / visit Paris; this evening / I / watch a movie; at night / the children / sleep badly. Then change each sentence to a different time expression.

## Unit 3: Regular -er verbs in the present tense

### Core idea

Most beginner verbs are regular `-er` verbs. Remove `-er` from the infinitive and add the present-tense endings.

### Model endings with `parler`

| Subject | Ending | Form | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| `je` | `-e` | `je parle` | I speak / I am speaking |
| `tu` | `-es` | `tu parles` | you speak |
| `il / elle / on` | `-e` | `il parle` | he speaks |
| `nous` | `-ons` | `nous parlons` | we speak |
| `vous` | `-ez` | `vous parlez` | you speak |
| `ils / elles` | `-ent` | `ils parlent` | they speak |

### Other common regular `-er` verbs

| Infinitive | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `aimer` | to like, to love |
| `travailler` | to work |
| `habiter` | to live |
| `regarder` | to watch, look at |
| `étudier` | to study |
| `arriver` | to arrive |
| `chercher` | to look for |
| `danser` | to dance |

### Examples

- `Je parle français avec mes collègues.` — I speak French with my coworkers.
- `Tu travailles demain ?` — Are you working tomorrow?
- `Elle habite près de la gare.` — She lives near the train station.
- `Nous regardons un film français le vendredi soir.` — We watch a French film on Friday evenings.
- `Vous aimez la musique classique ?` — Do you like classical music?
- `Ils arrivent en septembre.` — They are arriving in September.

### Pronunciation note

In most regular `-er` verbs, `je parle`, `tu parles`, `il parle`, and `ils parlent` sound almost the same. The written endings differ more than the spoken ones.

### Common mistake for English speakers

Do not pronounce the final `-ent` in `ils parlent`. Also remember `j'` before a vowel sound: `j'aime`, `j'étudie`.

### Sentence-building practice

Conjugate `parler`, `travailler`, and `aimer` for all six persons. Then write three short original sentences with three different subjects.

## Unit 4: What the present tense means

### Core idea

One French present-tense form can cover several meanings that English sometimes separates.

### Main uses of the present tense

| Use | French example | English meaning |
|---|---|---|
| habitual action | `Le mardi, nous étudions au café.` | On Tuesdays, we study at the café. |
| action happening now | `Je regarde le tableau.` | I am looking at the board. |
| general truth | `Les étudiants parlent avec le professeur.` | Students speak with the teacher. |
| near future / scheduled event | `Le train arrive demain matin.` | The train arrives tomorrow morning. |

### Examples

- `Je travaille à la maison le lundi.` — I work at home on Mondays.
- `Nous étudions le français en septembre.` — We study French in September.
- `On regarde la carte maintenant.` — We are looking at the map now.
- `Le magasin ferme tôt le dimanche.` — The store closes early on Sundays.
- `Ma sœur arrive jeudi soir.` — My sister is arriving Thursday evening.

### Pronunciation or listening note

Time clues often tell you which meaning is intended. In `Ma sœur arrive jeudi soir`, the present tense points to a future scheduled event because of `jeudi soir`.

### Common mistake for English speakers

Do not force an English-style separate "am doing" form into every sentence. `Je travaille` may mean either `I work` or `I am working`, depending on context.

### Sentence-building practice

Label each sentence as habit, happening now, general truth, or near future: `Nous parlons avec la directrice.` / `Le jeudi, vous dansez ?` / `Mon train arrive demain.` / `Les enfants regardent la neige.` Then rewrite two sentences with a different time clue.

## Unit 5: Yes/no questions in three common forms

### Core idea

French gives you three common ways to ask a yes/no question, and you should be able to recognize all three.

### The three patterns

| Pattern | Structure | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| rising intonation | statement + `?` | `Tu travailles demain ?` | Are you working tomorrow? |
| `est-ce que` | `Est-ce que` + statement order | `Est-ce que vous aimez Paris ?` | Do you like Paris? |
| inversion | verb-pronoun order | `Parlez-vous anglais ?` | Do you speak English? |

### Examples

- `Vous regardez souvent des films français ?` — Do you often watch French movies?
- `Est-ce que vous regardez souvent des films français ?` — Do you often watch French movies?
- `Regardez-vous souvent des films français ?` — Do you often watch French movies?
- `Est-ce qu'on travaille ce soir ?` — Are we working this evening?
- `Aime-t-elle la musique ?` — Does she like music?

### Pronunciation note

Treat `est-ce que` as one fixed sound group. In inversion, keep the hyphen: `Parlez-vous ?`, `Aime-t-elle ?` The extra `-t-` makes pronunciation smoother.

### Common mistake for English speakers

Do not mix two systems: avoid `*Est-ce que parlez-vous anglais ?` French also does not use an extra word like English `do/does` in questions.

### Sentence-building practice

Turn each statement into three yes/no questions: `Tu habites à Lyon.` / `Vous aimez ce quartier.` / `Ils arrivent demain matin.` Then decide which version feels most natural in casual speech.

## Unit 6: Information questions with question words

### Core idea

Information questions ask for a specific detail: place, time, reason, quantity, identity, or category.

### Common question words

| French | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `où` | where | `Où habitez-vous ?` |
| `quand` | when | `Quand est-ce qu'elle arrive ?` |
| `pourquoi` | why | `Pourquoi étudient-ils ?` |
| `comment` | how | `Comment travaillez-vous ?` |
| `combien de` | how many / how much | `Combien de films regardez-vous ?` |
| `qui` | who | `Qui parle ?` |
| `qu'est-ce que` | what | `Qu'est-ce que tu regardes ?` |
| `quel / quelle / quels / quelles` | which / what | `Quelle saison préférez-vous ?` |

### Useful structures and examples

- `Où habitez-vous ?` — Where do you live?
- `Quand est-ce qu'elle arrive ?` — When is she arriving?
- `Pourquoi est-ce qu'elle arrive si tôt ?` — Why is she arriving so early?
- `Comment travaillez-vous le soir ?` — How do you work in the evening?
- `Combien de cours regardez-vous en ligne ?` — How many classes do you watch online?
- `Quel jour sommes-nous ?` — What day is it?
- `Quelle musique aimes-tu ?` — What kind of music do you like?

### Pronunciation note

Learn `qu'est-ce que` and `quand est-ce qu'` as chunks. They are much easier to hear and produce that way.

### Common mistake for English speakers

Do not confuse `qui` and `qu'est-ce que`. Use `qui` for a person doing the action, and `qu'est-ce que` for a thing that is the object of the action. Also remember agreement in `quel`: `quel jour`, `quelle saison`, `quels films`, `quelles langues`.

### Sentence-building practice

Write a full question for each answer clue: `À Montréal.` / `Le vendredi soir.` / `Parce qu'elle travaille demain.` / `Deux films.` / `Le printemps.` Use a different question word each time.

## Mini-dialogue: planning a study week

**Nora :** `Salut, Malik. Tu travailles demain matin ?` — Hi, Malik. Are you working tomorrow morning?

**Malik :** `Non, je travaille l'après-midi, mais le matin, j'étudie à la bibliothèque.` — No, I work in the afternoon, but in the morning I study at the library.

**Nora :** `Est-ce que tu révises aussi le jeudi soir ?` — Do you also review on Thursday evening?

**Malik :** `Oui, et le vendredi, je regarde une vidéo en français avec ma sœur.` — Yes, and on Fridays I watch a video in French with my sister.

**Nora :** `Pourquoi est-ce que vous étudiez ensemble ?` — Why do you study together?

**Malik :** `Parce que nous parlons français pendant une heure, et ça nous aide beaucoup.` — Because we speak French for an hour, and that helps us a lot.

**Nora :** `Quelle saison préférez-vous pour étudier au parc ?` — Which season do you prefer for studying in the park?

**Malik :** `Nous préférons le printemps, parce qu'il fait doux et le parc est calme.` — We prefer spring because the weather is mild and the park is calm.

### Guided analysis

1. Find three different time expressions.
2. List the conjugated `-er` verbs and identify their subjects.
3. Find one yes/no question with intonation and one with `est-ce que`.
4. Find two information questions and name their question words.
5. Rewrite Malik's first answer with `nous` instead of `je` where possible.

## Connected reading: ma routine du mercredi

`Le mercredi, je commence la journée assez tôt. Le matin, j'arrive au café du quartier vers huit heures et j'étudie pendant une heure avant le travail. À midi, je déjeune avec une collègue qui parle aussi français, alors nous pratiquons ensemble. L'après-midi, je travaille à la maison et je téléphone souvent à mes clients. Le soir, je regarde une courte vidéo, puis je note cinq nouvelles expressions dans mon cahier. En hiver, cette routine demande plus d'énergie, mais au printemps, je travaille près de la fenêtre et tout semble plus facile.`

— On Wednesdays, I start the day fairly early. In the morning, I arrive at the neighborhood café around eight o'clock and study for an hour before work. At noon, I have lunch with a coworker who also speaks French, so we practice together. In the afternoon, I work at home and often call my clients. In the evening, I watch a short video, then I write down five new expressions in my notebook. In winter, this routine takes more energy, but in spring, I work near the window and everything seems easier.

### Reading practice

1. List every time expression in the paragraph.
2. Find four regular `-er` verbs and give their infinitives.
3. Identify one sentence showing a habit and one showing a scheduled action.
4. Rewrite the paragraph in the `nous` form where reasonable.
5. Write three true sentences about your own study routine using the reading as a model.

## Common mistakes to avoid

1. Capitalizing time words as in English instead of writing `lundi` and `septembre`.
2. Using the wrong preposition: say `en juillet`, `en hiver`, but `au printemps`.
3. Forgetting that repeated days usually take singular `le`: `le dimanche`.
4. Pronouncing every written ending in `-er` verbs, especially the silent `-ent`.
5. Adding English `do/does` to French questions.
6. Mixing question systems instead of choosing one.
7. Forgetting agreement in `quel`.
