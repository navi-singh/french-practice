# Chapter 3 Exercises

Complete these without notes. Put a question mark beside uncertain answers.

## A. Transform or translate

1. nous / parler
2. ils / étudier
3. Vous habitez à Lyon.
4. Elle arrive mardi.
5. Je travaille le matin.
6. tu / commencer

:::answer
Items 1, 2 and 6 ask you to conjugate. Items 3, 4 and 5 ask you to turn a statement into a question.

1. `nous parlons` — regular `-er`: drop `-er`, add `-ons`.
2. `ils étudient` — the `-ent` ending is completely silent, so this sounds identical to `il étudie`.
3. `Est-ce que vous habitez à Lyon ?` — a yes/no question, because the statement contains no detail to ask about. `Vous habitez à Lyon ?` and `Habitez-vous à Lyon ?` are equally correct.
4. `Quand est-ce qu'elle arrive ?` — `mardi` is a time, so the question word is `quand`. Note `qu'` before the vowel of `elle`.
5. `Quand travaillez-vous ?` — `le matin` is again a time. This one uses inversion, so keep the hyphen.
6. `tu commences` — `commencer` is regular here; the cedilla only appears in `nous commençons`, where `c` would otherwise harden before `o`.
:::

## B. Controlled production

1. Write a new sentence demonstrating **calendar language**. Use `Nous sommes lundi.` only as a structural model; change its content.
2. Write a new sentence demonstrating **regular -er verbs**. Use `Je parle français.` only as a structural model; change its content.
3. Write a new sentence demonstrating **yes/no questions**. Use `Tu travailles demain ?` only as a structural model; change its content.
4. Write a new sentence demonstrating **information questions**. Use `Où habitez-vous ?` only as a structural model; change its content.

:::answer
Answers vary by design — the point is to produce your own sentence, not to recover a hidden one. A correct answer must clearly show the named structure, conjugate its verb correctly, and keep French word order.

Sample answers:

1. Calendar language: `En octobre, nous visitons ma grand-mère.` — uses `en` + month and keeps the month lowercase.
2. Regular `-er` verbs: `Vous travaillez avec des collègues sympathiques.` — `-ez` for `vous`.
3. Yes/no question: `Est-ce que tu danses souvent ?` — no English-style *do* appears anywhere.
4. Information question: `Quand est-ce que le magasin ferme ?` — question word first, then a complete question structure.

Mark yourself wrong if you reused the model sentence's content, if the verb ending does not match the subject, or if you inserted a word translating English *do* or *am*.
:::

## C. Four-way variation

Choose one sentence from each lesson unit and rewrite it four ways:

1. change the subject;
2. make it negative;
3. turn it into a question;
4. change one meaningful detail.

:::answer
Worked example, starting from `Je parle français le lundi.`

1. Change the subject: `Nous parlons français le lundi.` — the ending must move with the subject, `-e` to `-ons`.
2. Make it negative: `Je ne parle pas français le lundi.` — `ne` and `pas` wrap the conjugated verb. Before a vowel sound `ne` becomes `n'`, as in `Je n'aime pas`.
3. Turn it into a question: `Est-ce que tu parles français le lundi ?`
4. Change one meaningful detail: `Je parle espagnol le lundi.`

Check that each version still has exactly one conjugated verb agreeing with its subject, and that changing the subject did not leave the old ending behind — that is the most common slip in this drill.
:::

## D. Dictation and correction

Use text-to-speech to hear six examples from `lessons.md`. Write what you hear without looking. Correct in separate passes:

1. vocabulary;
2. verb form;
3. agreement or pronoun placement;
4. spelling and accents.

Do the dictation first, using this chapter's own examples as your audio. Then open the answers to check: they list six sentences worth dictating and the trap each one sets.

:::answer
Six sentences from this chapter that make good dictation material, and the trap each one sets:

1. `Nous sommes le douze octobre.` — the date takes `le`, and months stay lowercase in French.
2. `Le vendredi, je travaille à la maison.` — `le` before a day means *every* Friday. Without it, you mean this coming Friday.
3. `Est-ce que tu étudies le français ?` — `étudies` and `étudie` sound identical, so only the subject `tu` tells you to write `-es`.
4. `Quand est-ce qu'elle arrive ?` — `que` elides to `qu'` before `elle`.
5. `Ils parlent avec le professeur.` — the `-ent` ending is silent, so this sounds exactly like `il parle`. The plural `Ils` is your only clue.
6. `En hiver, nous commençons à huit heures.` — `en` with a season, and the cedilla in `commençons` keeps the `c` soft before `o`.

Mark each pass separately. A silent ending you failed to write is a listening problem; an ending you heard but spelled wrong is a spelling problem, and they need different practice.
:::

## E. Personal writing

Write a seven-line weekly routine. Include four -er verbs and two questions you could ask a classmate.

:::answer
Answers vary. A model to compare against:

1. `Le lundi, je travaille à la maison.`
2. `Le mardi, j'étudie le français avec une amie.`
3. `Le mercredi, nous regardons un film.`
4. `Le jeudi, je téléphone à ma sœur.`
5. `Le vendredi soir, nous dînons au restaurant.`
6. `Est-ce que tu travailles le samedi ?`
7. `Quand est-ce que tu étudies ?`

Count before you check: four different `-er` verbs (here `travailler`, `étudier`, `regarder`, `téléphoner`, `dîner`) and two questions. Verify that every `le` + day means a genuine habit, and that each verb ending matches its subject.
:::

## F. Oral recall

Explain each of the chapter's four main rules aloud in simple English, then give one French example from memory.

:::answer
1. **Calendar language.** Days and months are lowercase. Use `en` with months and most seasons, `au` with `printemps`, and `le` + a singular day for a habit. Example: `Le samedi, je travaille.`
2. **Regular `-er` verbs.** Drop `-er` and add `-e, -es, -e, -ons, -ez, -ent`. Four of the six forms sound identical. Example: `Nous étudions le français.`
3. **Yes/no questions.** Three ways: rising intonation, `est-ce que`, or inversion. French never adds a word for English *do*. Example: `Est-ce que vous aimez Paris ?`
4. **Information questions.** Put a question word first, then a question structure. `quel` agrees with its noun. Example: `Où habitez-vous ?`

If you could state the rule but not produce the example, the rule is not learned yet — recognition and production are different skills. Redo this section tomorrow before moving on.
:::
