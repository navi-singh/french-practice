# Chapter 13 Lessons: The Passé Composé
The passé composé is the first major French past tense you can use to tell what happened, what someone did, and what changed. In this chapter, you will learn how to build it accurately, choose the correct auxiliary, place negatives and short adverbs correctly, and manage the agreement patterns that make this tense feel tricky at first but highly usable once the system becomes clear.
## Learning objectives
You will learn to:
1. explain when the passé composé is the right tense for a completed past event;
2. form the passé composé with `avoir` plus a past participle;
3. recognize and produce regular and common irregular past participles;
4. place negation, short adverbs, and common question patterns correctly around the auxiliary;
5. use `être` with movement/change-of-state verbs and make the participle agree with the subject;
6. use pronominal verbs in the passé composé and understand when agreement does and does not happen;
7. make agreement with a preceding direct object pronoun, as in `Je les ai écrites.`
## Prerequisite quick check
Before beginning, make sure you can already do the following comfortably:
- conjugate present-tense `avoir` and `être` without hesitation;
- recognize a past participle in English (`finished`, `seen`, `taken`) as a verb form, not a full tense by itself;
- identify direct object pronouns such as `le`, `la`, `l'`, and `les`;
- recognize subject agreement from earlier chapters (`fatigué`, `fatiguée`, `fatigués`, `fatiguées`).
If any of those feel uncertain, review them first. The passé composé depends on all four skills at once.
## How to study this chapter
Memorize one pattern at a time. Read every French example aloud, then cover the French and rebuild it from the English cue. When you make an agreement mistake, stop and ask yourself **why** the agreement happened before you continue.
## Unit 1: What the passé composé expresses and how to build it with `avoir`
### Core idea
The passé composé usually presents a past action as a completed whole: something happened, finished, or moved the story forward.
- `J'ai terminé mes devoirs.` — I finished my homework.
- `Elle a choisi un dessert.` — She chose a dessert.
- `Nous avons attendu le bus.` — We waited for the bus.
In English, this tense may translate as **did**, **have done**, or sometimes just the simple past, depending on context.
### Structure
For most French verbs, the pattern is:
**subject + present tense of `avoir` + past participle**
| Part of the structure | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| subject | `je` | I |
| auxiliary | `ai` | have |
| past participle | `parlé` | spoken |
| complete tense | `j'ai parlé` | I spoke / I have spoken |
### Full model: `parler` in the passé composé
| Pronoun | French | English |
|---|---|---|
| `je` | `j'ai parlé` | I spoke / I have spoken |
| `tu` | `tu as parlé` | you spoke |
| `il/elle/on` | `il a parlé` / `elle a parlé` / `on a parlé` | he/she/we spoke |
| `nous` | `nous avons parlé` | we spoke |
| `vous` | `vous avez parlé` | you spoke |
| `ils/elles` | `ils ont parlé` / `elles ont parlé` | they spoke |
### Regular past participles
| Infinitive type | Rule | Example infinitive | Past participle | English |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `-er` | replace `-er` with `-é` | `parler` | `parlé` | spoken |
| regular `-ir` | replace `-ir` with `-i` | `finir` | `fini` | finished |
| regular `-re` | replace `-re` with `-u` | `vendre` | `vendu` | sold |
### More model pairs
| Infinitive | Past participle | Example sentence |
|---|---|---|
| `chanter` | `chanté` | `Nous avons chanté dans la voiture.` — We sang in the car. |
| `réussir` | `réussi` | `Tu as réussi l'examen.` — You passed the exam. |
| `attendre` | `attendu` | `Ils ont attendu devant le café.` — They waited in front of the café. |
### Pronunciation note
In normal speech, many written agreement endings are silent. `parlé`, `parlée`, `parlés`, and `parlées` are all pronounced the same. That means you often have to think grammatically even when your ear does not hear a difference.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do **not** put the infinitive after the auxiliary.
- incorrect: `*j'ai parler`
- correct: `j'ai parlé`
The second verb must be a **past participle**, not an infinitive.
### Sentence-building practice
1. Change `j'ai parlé` to `nous`, `vous`, and `elles`.
2. Build three new sentences with these cues: `finir / le projet`, `vendre / la voiture`, `regarder / un film`.
3. Answer aloud: Which part tells you the time? Which part tells you the lexical meaning?
## Unit 2: High-frequency irregular past participles
### Core idea
Many of the most useful French verbs have irregular past participles. Learn them as vocabulary chunks, the same way you memorize an article with a noun.
### Essential irregular participles
| Infinitive | Past participle | English |
|---|---|---|
| `avoir` | `eu` | had |
| `boire` | `bu` | drank |
| `connaître` | `connu` | known / met |
| `courir` | `couru` | run |
| `croire` | `cru` | believed |
| `devoir` | `dû` | had to / owed |
| `dire` | `dit` | said / told |
| `écrire` | `écrit` | written |
| `être` | `été` | been |
| `faire` | `fait` | done / made |
| `lire` | `lu` | read |
| `mettre` | `mis` | put |
| `offrir` | `offert` | offered |
| `ouvrir` | `ouvert` | opened |
| `pouvoir` | `pu` | been able to |
| `prendre` | `pris` | taken |
| `recevoir` | `reçu` | received |
| `savoir` | `su` | known / found out |
| `voir` | `vu` | seen |
| `vouloir` | `voulu` | wanted |
### Learn them in real sentences
- `Il a fait du sport après le travail.` — He exercised after work.
- `J'ai lu le message dans le train.` — I read the message on the train.
- `Elles ont pris le dernier métro.` — They took the last subway.
- `Nous avons écrit à notre professeur.` — We wrote to our teacher.
- `Tu as ouvert la fenêtre trop tôt.` — You opened the window too early.
- `On a vu ce film l'année dernière.` — We saw that movie last year.
### Useful meaning notes
Some irregular verbs change meaning depending on context.
- `j'ai su la nouvelle` — I found out the news
- `j'ai connu ce quartier` — I knew / was familiar with this neighborhood
- `j'ai pu venir` — I was able to come
### Pronunciation note
The final consonant of many past participles is silent: `lu`, `vu`, `dit`, `fait`, `mis`. Do not try to pronounce every written letter.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not invent a regular pattern where French has an irregular one.
- incorrect: `*j'ai prendu`, `*elle a ouvré`, `*nous avons fais`
- correct: `j'ai pris`, `elle a ouvert`, `nous avons fait`
### Sentence-building practice
1. Write the past participle of `faire`, `voir`, `dire`, `boire`, `mettre`, and `recevoir`.
2. Turn these cues into full sentences: `nous / écrire / un courriel`, `je / voir / tes clés`, `ils / dire / la vérité`.
3. Say three verbs from the table without looking, then use each in a complete sentence.
## Unit 3: Negation, questions, and adverb placement in the passé composé
### Core idea
The auxiliary is the center of the tense. Negation and short adverbs usually attach to it, not to the past participle.
### Negation pattern
`ne` (`n'` before a vowel sound) goes before the auxiliary, and `pas` goes after it.
| Affirmative | Negative | English |
|---|---|---|
| `J'ai mangé.` | `Je n'ai pas mangé.` | I did not eat. |
| `Elle a fini.` | `Elle n'a pas fini.` | She did not finish. |
| `Nous avons répondu.` | `Nous n'avons pas répondu.` | We did not answer. |
### Short adverbs
Very common short adverbs often go between the auxiliary and the past participle.
| Pattern | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| auxiliary + `bien` + participle | `Il a bien travaillé.` | He worked well. |
| auxiliary + `déjà` + participle | `Tu as déjà vu ce musée.` | You have already seen this museum. |
| auxiliary + `souvent` + participle | `Nous avons souvent parlé de ce projet.` | We often talked about this project. |
Longer adverbial expressions usually come later in the sentence.
- `Elle a travaillé avec beaucoup d'énergie.` — She worked with a lot of energy.
- `J'ai parlé très lentement.` — I spoke very slowly.
### Questions in the passé composé
You can ask questions in the same three ways you already know in the present tense.
| Style | French | English |
|---|---|---|
| intonation | `Tu as fini ?` | Did you finish? |
| `est-ce que` | `Est-ce que tu as fini ?` | Did you finish? |
| inversion | `As-tu fini ?` | Did you finish? |
More examples:
- `Pourquoi est-ce que vous avez changé d'avis ?` — Why did you change your mind?
- `Où ont-elles garé la voiture ?` — Where did they park the car?
- `Quand as-tu lu ce roman ?` — When did you read that novel?
### Pronunciation note
In inversion, the auxiliary and subject pronoun form one sound group: `As-tu`, `A-t-il`, `Ont-elles`. Read them as linked units, not as separate isolated words.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not place `pas` after the past participle.
- incorrect: `*Je n'ai mangé pas.`
- correct: `Je n'ai pas mangé.`
Also avoid placing short adverbs after the participle when French normally prefers them between auxiliary and participle.
- more natural: `J'ai déjà vu ce film.`
- less natural for beginners: `J'ai vu déjà ce film.`
### Sentence-building practice
1. Make these sentences negative: `Elle a répondu.`, `Nous avons compris.`, `Tu as écrit la carte.`
2. Add `déjà`, then `bien`, to `J'ai fait mes exercices.`
3. Ask a question three ways with `vous / finir / le rapport`.
## Unit 4: The passé composé with `être`
### Core idea
A smaller group of verbs uses `être` instead of `avoir` in the passé composé. These verbs usually express movement, arrival/departure, or a change of state.
### Full model: `arriver` in the passé composé
| Pronoun | French | English |
|---|---|---|
| `je` | `je suis arrivé(e)` | I arrived |
| `tu` | `tu es arrivé(e)` | you arrived |
| `il/elle/on` | `il est arrivé` / `elle est arrivée` / `on est arrivé(s)` | he/she/we arrived |
| `nous` | `nous sommes arrivé(e)s` | we arrived |
| `vous` | `vous êtes arrivé(e)(s)` | you arrived |
| `ils/elles` | `ils sont arrivés` / `elles sont arrivées` | they arrived |
### The common `être` verb group
| Verb | Meaning | Verb | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `aller` | to go | `naître` | to be born |
| `arriver` | to arrive | `partir` | to leave |
| `descendre` | to go down | `passer` | to pass / spend |
| `devenir` | to become | `rentrer` | to return home |
| `entrer` | to enter | `rester` | to stay |
| `monter` | to go up | `retourner` | to return |
| `mourir` | to die | `sortir` | to go out |
| `venir` | to come | `tomber` | to fall |
You will often see this group taught as a memory set rather than as a deep semantic rule. That is practical advice: memorize the common verbs as a family.
### Agreement with the subject
With non-pronominal `être` verbs, the past participle agrees with the subject in gender and number.
- `Marie est arrivée à huit heures.` — Marie arrived at eight o'clock.
- `Ils sont partis très tôt.` — They left very early.
- `Mes sœurs sont restées à la maison.` — My sisters stayed at home.
- `Paul et Nora sont venus ensemble.` — Paul and Nora came together.
### Spoken French reminder
The written endings in `arrivé`, `arrivée`, `arrivés`, and `arrivées` are usually not pronounced differently. The grammar is visible mainly in writing, so check it deliberately.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Two frequent problems appear here:
1. choosing `avoir` instead of `être` for these verbs;
2. forgetting written agreement.
- incorrect: `*elle a arrivée`
- correct: `elle est arrivée`
### Sentence-building practice
1. Put `aller`, `venir`, and `sortir` into the passé composé with `nous`.
2. Change `Il est arrivé tard.` into a sentence about two women.
3. Build three true or invented sentences about where people went yesterday.
## Unit 5: Pronominal verbs in the passé composé
### Core idea
All pronominal verbs use `être` in the passé composé.
- `se lever` -> `je me suis levé(e)`
- `s'appeler` -> `elle s'est appelée...`
- `se coucher` -> `nous nous sommes couché(e)s`
### Full model: `se lever` in the passé composé
| Pronoun | French | English |
|---|---|---|
| `je` | `je me suis levé(e)` | I got up |
| `tu` | `tu t'es levé(e)` | you got up |
| `il/elle/on` | `il s'est levé` / `elle s'est levée` / `on s'est levé(s)` | he/she/we got up |
| `nous` | `nous nous sommes levé(e)s` | we got up |
| `vous` | `vous vous êtes levé(e)(s)` | you got up |
| `ils/elles` | `ils se sont levés` / `elles se sont levées` | they got up |
### Basic reflexive agreement
When the reflexive pronoun functions as the direct object, the past participle usually agrees.
- `Elle s'est levée tôt.` — She got up early.
- `Nous nous sommes habillés rapidement.` — We got dressed quickly.
- `Ils se sont vus à la gare.` — They saw each other at the station.
### When there is **no** agreement
Do **not** agree the participle when the reflexive pronoun is indirect, or when another direct object comes after the verb.
| Sentence | Why there is no agreement | English |
|---|---|---|
| `Ils se sont parlé.` | `parler à quelqu'un`: `se` = indirect object | They spoke to each other. |
| `Elle s'est lavé les mains.` | `les mains` is the direct object; `se` is indirect | She washed her hands. |
| `Nous nous sommes téléphoné hier.` | `téléphoner à quelqu'un`: `se` = indirect object | We phoned each other yesterday. |
### A useful comparison
- `Elles se sont regardées.` — They looked at one another. (`se` = direct object -> agreement)
- `Elles se sont parlé.` — They spoke to one another. (`se` = indirect object -> no agreement)
### Pronunciation note
`Je me suis`, `tu t'es`, and `il s'est` each form a tight spoken group. Practice them aloud as chunks so that the written apostrophes stop feeling intimidating.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not assume that every pronominal verb automatically agrees in every sentence. With pronominal verbs, agreement depends on the grammatical role of the reflexive pronoun.
### Sentence-building practice
1. Put `se coucher` in the passé composé with `je`, `nous`, and `elles`.
2. Decide whether agreement is needed: `se voir`, `se parler`, `se téléphoner`, `se retrouver`.
3. Rewrite `Elle s'est lavé les mains.` with `ils` and then with `nous`.
## Unit 6: Agreement with a preceding direct object pronoun
### Core idea
With `avoir`, the past participle normally does **not** agree. But if a **direct object pronoun** comes before the auxiliary, the past participle agrees with that pronoun.
### Basic contrast
- `J'ai écrit la lettre.` — I wrote the letter. (`la lettre` comes after -> no visible agreement trigger)
- `Je l'ai écrite.` — I wrote it. (`l'` = `la lettre`, feminine singular -> `écrite`)
### Pattern table
| Pronoun before the auxiliary | Hidden noun | Example | Agreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| `l'` | `le livre` | `Je l'ai lu.` | masculine singular |
| `l'` | `la lettre` | `Je l'ai écrite.` | feminine singular |
| `les` | `les billets` | `Nous les avons pris.` | masculine plural |
| `les` | `les photos` | `Je les ai prises.` | feminine plural |
### More examples
- `Les lettres ? Je les ai écrites hier soir.` — The letters? I wrote them last night.
- `Les exercices ? Tu les as faits tout seul.` — The exercises? You did them by yourself.
- `La chanson ? Nous l'avons entendue à la radio.` — The song? We heard it on the radio.
- `Les photos ? Je les ai prises pendant le voyage.` — The photos? I took them during the trip.
### A pronominal extension
This same logic can show up with pronominal verbs when a direct object pronoun comes before the auxiliary.
- `Les mains ? Elle se les est lavées.` — Her hands? She washed them.
Here, `les` is the direct object and it comes before the auxiliary, so the participle agrees with `les mains`.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not agree with a preceding **indirect** object pronoun.
- `Je lui ai parlé.` — I spoke to him/her. (`lui` is indirect -> no agreement)
- `Je les ai vus.` — I saw them. (`les` is direct -> agreement)
### Sentence-building practice
1. Replace the noun with a pronoun: `J'ai vu les étudiantes.` -> `Je ___ ai ___`.
2. Replace the noun with a pronoun: `Nous avons pris les clés.`
3. Decide whether agreement happens: `Je leur ai écrit.` / `Je les ai écrites.` / `Ils se sont parlé.` / `Ils se sont vus.`

## Mini-dialogue: after a long Saturday
**Camille :** `Alors, tu as passé une bonne journée ?` — So, did you have a good day?
**Nassim :** `Oui, mais elle a été très longue.` — Yes, but it was very long.
**Camille :** `Qu'est-ce que tu as fait ce matin ?` — What did you do this morning?
**Nassim :** `Je me suis levé tard, puis j'ai préparé le dîner pour ce soir.` — I got up late, then I prepared dinner for this evening.
**Camille :** `Et les courses ?` — And the shopping?
**Nassim :** `Je les ai faites après le déjeuner.` — I did it after lunch.
**Camille :** `Tes amis sont arrivés à l'heure ?` — Did your friends arrive on time?
**Nassim :** `Oui, ils sont arrivés vers huit heures, et on a bien parlé jusqu'à minuit.` — Yes, they arrived around eight o'clock, and we talked a lot until midnight.
### Guided analysis
1. Find one passé composé with `être`, one with `avoir`, and one pronominal form.
2. Explain why `faites` in `Je les ai faites` agrees.
3. Rewrite `ils sont arrivés` with `Marie` and then with `mes sœurs`.
4. Turn `On a bien parlé jusqu'à minuit.` into a negative sentence.
5. Ask `Tu as passé une bonne journée ?` again using inversion.
## Connected reading: un dimanche qui a bien commencé
`Dimanche matin, je me suis réveillé avant tout le monde, mais je ne me suis pas levé tout de suite. J'ai ouvert la fenêtre et j'ai vu un ciel très clair. Ma sœur est descendue quelques minutes plus tard, et nous avons préparé le petit déjeuner ensemble. Mes parents sont partis tôt pour le marché, alors je leur ai écrit un message. Vers dix heures, mes cousins sont arrivés sans prévenir, mais nous les avons accueillis avec plaisir. Finalement, on a passé toute la journée à parler, à manger et à rire.`
— On Sunday morning, I woke up before everyone else, but I did not get up right away. I opened the window and saw a very clear sky. My sister came downstairs a few minutes later, and we prepared breakfast together. My parents left early for the market, so I wrote them a message. Around ten o'clock, my cousins arrived without warning, but we welcomed them gladly. In the end, we spent the whole day talking, eating, and laughing.
### Reading practice
1. List every passé composé form in the paragraph and sort it by auxiliary.
2. Find one negative form and explain where `ne...pas` is placed.
3. Find one sentence with a preceding pronoun and explain whether it is direct or indirect.
4. Change the paragraph so the narrator is `nous` instead of `je`.
5. Write three follow-up sentences telling what happened in the evening.
## Common mistakes to avoid
1. Writing an infinitive after the auxiliary: `*j'ai parler` instead of `j'ai parlé`.
2. Using `avoir` with common `être` verbs such as `aller`, `venir`, or `arriver`.
3. Forgetting that `ne...pas` surrounds the auxiliary, not the participle.
4. Assuming that all pronominal verbs always agree automatically.
5. Forgetting agreement after a preceding direct object pronoun: `Les lettres ? Je les ai écrites.`
6. Agreeing after an indirect object pronoun such as `lui` or `leur`, where no agreement happens.

| Infinitive ending | Participle ending | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `-er` | `-é` | `parler` -> `parlé` |
| regular `-ir` | `-i` | `finir` -> `fini` |
| regular `-re` | `-u` | `vendre` -> `vendu` |

| Verb | Participle | Verb | Participle |
|---|---|---|---|
| `faire` | `fait` | `voir` | `vu` |
| `prendre` | `pris` | `lire` | `lu` |
| `écrire` | `écrit` | `dire` | `dit` |

| `ouvrir` | `ouvert` | `avoir` | `eu` |
| `être` | `été` | `recevoir` | `reçu` |
### Quick guide to agreement
| Situation | Agreement? | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `avoir` + noun after verb | no | `J'ai écrit la lettre.` |
| `avoir` + preceding direct object pronoun | yes | `Je l'ai écrite.` |
| non-pronominal `être` verb | yes, with subject | `Elle est arrivée.` |
| pronominal verb with direct-object `se` | yes | `Ils se sont vus.` |
| pronominal verb with indirect-object `se` | no | `Ils se sont parlé.` |
| pronominal verb with direct object after verb | usually no | `Elle s'est lavé les mains.` |
### Word-order reminders
- negative: `ne` + auxiliary + `pas` + participle
- short adverb: auxiliary + `déjà/bien/souvent` + participle
- question: `Tu as fini ?` / `Est-ce que tu as fini ?` / `As-tu fini ?`
