# Chapter 2 Exercises

Complete these without notes. Put a question mark beside uncertain answers.

## A. Transform or translate

1. Je suis content.
2. Nous avons une maison.
3. Il y a des fenêtres.
4. Marie et Léa sont françaises.
5. Paul a faim.
6. Tu es en retard.

:::answer
1. `Je ne suis pas content.` — `ne` and `pas` wrap the conjugated verb `suis`; no **de** rule is involved with `être`.
2. `Nous n'avons pas de maison.` — `ne` becomes `n'` before `avons`, and `une maison` becomes `de maison` after negative `avoir`.
3. `Il n'y a pas de fenêtres.` — `il y a` becomes `il n'y a pas`, and `des fenêtres` becomes `de fenêtres`.
4. `Elles sont françaises.` — Marie and Léa are two female people, so use `elles`; the adjective is feminine plural.
5. `Il a faim.` — Paul is one male person, so use `il`; hunger uses `avoir`, not `être`.
6. `Vous êtes en retard.` — changing from `tu` to `vous` requires the `être` form `êtes`.

These match the answer key exactly. The main traps are the **de** rule after negative `avoir` or `il y a`, and agreement after replacing names with a pronoun.
:::


## B. Controlled production

1. Write a new sentence demonstrating **subject pronouns**. Use `Je suis ici.` only as a structural model; change its content.
2. Write a new sentence demonstrating **être**. Use `Nous sommes calmes.` only as a structural model; change its content.
3. Write a new sentence demonstrating **avoir and il y a**. Use `J'ai vingt ans.` only as a structural model; change its content.
4. Write a new sentence demonstrating **negation**. Use `Je ne suis pas fatigué.` only as a structural model; change its content.

:::answer
Answers vary by design. A correct answer must clearly show the named structure, use the correct verb form, and keep agreement and word order.

Sample answers:

1. Subject pronouns: `Elle est ici.` — `elle` replaces one feminine person and controls the verb form `est`.
2. `Être`: `Vous êtes très patients.` — `vous` takes `êtes`; the adjective is plural because `vous` can address more than one person.
3. `Avoir` and `il y a`: `Il y a deux fenêtres dans la chambre.` — `il y a` stays fixed even with a plural idea.
4. Negation: `Nous ne sommes pas fatigués.` — `ne` and `pas` wrap the conjugated verb.

Mark yourself wrong if you reused the model sentence's content, matched the verb to the wrong subject, or changed an article to **de** after a negative `être`.
:::


## C. Four-way variation

Choose one sentence from each lesson unit and rewrite it four ways:

1. change the subject;
2. make it negative;
3. turn it into a question;
4. change one meaningful detail.

:::answer
Worked example, starting from `J'ai une petite maison.`

1. Change the subject: `Nous avons une petite maison.` — `avoir` changes from `ai` to `avons` because the subject changed.
2. Make it negative: `Je n'ai pas de petite maison.` — after negative `avoir`, `une` becomes `de`; `ne` becomes `n'` before `ai`.
3. Turn it into a question: `Est-ce que tu as une petite maison ?` — this uses the safe `est-ce que` question pattern from Chapter 3.
4. Change one meaningful detail: `J'ai un petit appartement.` — changing the noun to masculine also changes `une petite` to `un petit`.

Check that every version still has one conjugated verb and that any adjective still agrees with its noun.
:::


## D. Dictation and correction

Use text-to-speech to hear six examples from `lessons.md`. Write what you hear without looking. Correct in separate passes:

1. vocabulary;
2. verb form;
3. agreement or pronoun placement;
4. spelling and accents.

Do the dictation first, using this chapter's own examples as your audio. Then open the answers to check: they list six sentences worth dictating and the trap each one sets.

:::answer
Six sentences from this chapter that make good dictation material, and the trap each one sets:

1. `Je suis étudiant, mais ma sœur est ingénieure.` — trap: `sœur` and `ingénieure` need exact accents and agreement.
2. `Nous avons deux chambres et un petit jardin.` — trap: final **s** in `avons` is silent, but the verb form must still be plural.
3. `Il y a une pharmacie près de la maison.` — trap: `Il y a` is fixed, and `près` needs the accent.
4. `On n'a pas de café, alors on a soif.` — trap: `ne` elides to `n'`, and negative `avoir` changes the object to `de café`.
5. `Elle a vingt ans et elle est en train d'étudier le français.` — trap: age uses `avoir`, and `de` elides before `étudier`.
6. `Vous n'êtes pas en retard ; il est encore tôt.` — trap: `êtes` and `tôt` both need accents, and `n'êtes` needs the apostrophe.

Correct in separate passes. A silent ending you failed to write is a spelling problem; a wrong verb form is a grammar problem.
:::

## E. Personal writing

Write six sentences introducing yourself and your home. Use être twice, avoir twice, il y a once, and one negative.

:::answer
Answers vary. A model six-sentence version:

1. `Je suis étudiant.`
2. `Je suis canadien.`
3. `J'ai vingt ans.`
4. `J'ai soif.`
5. `Il y a une cuisine dans mon appartement.`
6. `Je ne suis pas en retard.`

Self-marking criteria:

- `Être` appears twice and agrees with the subject.
- `Avoir` appears twice; if you mention age, hunger, thirst, heat, or cold, use `avoir`, not `être`.
- `Il y a` appears once and stays fixed.
- The negative sentence has both `ne` and `pas`; use `n'` before a vowel sound.
- Any adjective agrees with the person or noun it describes.
:::


## F. Oral recall

Explain each of the chapter's four main rules aloud in simple English, then give one French example from memory.

:::answer
1. **Subject pronouns.** Choose the pronoun by person, number, gender, and formality. Example: `Vous êtes ici.`
2. **`Être`.** Present forms are `suis`, `es`, `est`, `sommes`, `êtes`, `sont`; use it for identity, description, nationality, profession, and location. Example: `Elle est française.`
3. **`Avoir` and `il y a`.** Present forms are `ai`, `as`, `a`, `avons`, `avez`, `ont`; use `avoir` for age and many body-state expressions. `Il y a` means there is or there are. Example: `J'ai faim.`
4. **Negation.** Put `ne` before the conjugated verb and `pas` after it; `ne` becomes `n'` before a vowel sound. Example: `Il n'y a pas de café.`

If you can explain the rule but cannot produce your own French example, review that unit before continuing.
:::
