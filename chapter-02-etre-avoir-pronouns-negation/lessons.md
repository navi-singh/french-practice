# Chapter 2 Lessons: Être, Avoir, Subject Pronouns, and Negation

Use short sessions. Accuracy from memory matters more than copying volume.

## Unit 1: Subject pronouns

French verbs change with je, tu, il/elle/on, nous, vous, and ils/elles. Use vous for a plural group or polite singular address.

### Examples

- `Je suis ici.` — I am here.
- `Vous êtes prêts.` — You are ready.
- `Elles ont un cours.` — They have a class.

### Active writing

1. Copy each example once while saying it.
2. Cover the examples.
3. Rewrite all three from memory.
4. Change the subject, noun, or time expression to make three new sentences.
5. Read your new sentences aloud.

## Unit 2: Être

Present forms: suis, es, est, sommes, êtes, sont. Use être for identity, description, nationality, location in many basic statements, and selected expressions.

### Examples

- `Nous sommes calmes.` — We are calm.
- `Elle est canadienne.` — She is Canadian.
- `Ils sont à Montréal.` — They are in Montreal.

### Active writing

1. Copy each example once while saying it.
2. Cover the examples.
3. Rewrite all three from memory.
4. Change the subject, noun, or time expression to make three new sentences.
5. Read your new sentences aloud.

## Unit 3: Avoir and il y a

Present forms: ai, as, a, avons, avez, ont. French uses avoir in many expressions where English uses “to be.” Il y a means there is or there are.

### Examples

- `J'ai vingt ans.` — I am twenty years old.
- `Tu as faim.` — You are hungry.
- `Il y a une table.` — There is a table.

### Active writing

3. Rewrite all three from memory.
4. Change the subject, noun, or time expression to make three new sentences.
5. Read your new sentences aloud.

## Unit 4: Negation

Place ne before the conjugated verb and pas after it. Before a vowel sound, ne becomes n'. With indefinite or partitive objects after a negative avoir, the article often becomes de.

### Examples

- `Je ne suis pas fatigué.` — I am not tired.
- `Elle n'a pas de voiture.` — She does not have a car.
- `Il n'y a pas de café.` — There is no coffee.

### Active writing

1. Copy each example once while saying it.
2. Cover the examples.
3. Rewrite all three from memory.
4. Change the subject, noun, or time expression to make three new sentences.
5. Read your new sentences aloud.

## Chapter production task

Write six sentences introducing yourself and your home. Use être twice, avoir twice, il y a once, and one negative.

After writing, circle conjugated verbs, underline agreement or pronoun choices, and read the passage aloud twice.

## Sentence usage practice

Use these complete sentences to practice the chapter structures in context. Read each sentence aloud twice, cover it, and write it from memory.

1. `Je suis étudiant, mais ma sœur est ingénieure.` — I am a student, but my sister is an engineer.
2. `Nous avons deux chambres et un petit jardin.` — We have two bedrooms and a small garden.
3. `Il y a une pharmacie près de la maison.` — There is a pharmacy near the house.
4. `Mes voisins ne sont pas bruyants.` — My neighbors are not noisy.

### Notice and mark

For each sentence, circle the target verb or grammar structure. Underline the word or phrase that controls its form.

### Change the sentence

Rewrite every sentence twice:

1. change the subject, person, or number;
2. change one meaningful detail such as the place, object, time, or description.

Make every required change to conjugation, agreement, articles, pronouns, and word order.

### Make it personal

Write four new sentences about your own life using the same four chapter structures. Read them aloud, check them, and rewrite only the parts you corrected.
