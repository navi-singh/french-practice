# Chapter 8 Exercises

Complete these without notes. Put a question mark beside uncertain answers.

## A. Transform or translate

1. I know Marie.
2. I know the answer.
3. The man speaks. He is my teacher.
4. I read the book. The book is useful.
5. This is the city. I live there.
6. Here is the friend. I speak about him.

:::answer
1. `Je connais Marie.` — Use `connaître` for a person.
2. `Je sais la réponse.` — Use `savoir` for a fact or piece of information.
3. `L'homme qui parle est mon professeur.` — The man is the subject of `parle`, so use `qui`.
4. `Le livre que je lis est utile.` — The book is the direct object of `lis`, so use `que`.
5. `C'est la ville où j'habite.` — A place takes `où` in a relative clause.
6. `Voici l'ami dont je parle.` — `parler de` requires `de`, and `dont` replaces `de lui`.
:::


## B. Controlled production

1. Write a new sentence demonstrating **savoir versus connaître**. Use `Je sais la réponse.` only as a structural model; change its content.
2. Write a new sentence demonstrating **irregular verb families**. Use `Je vois le problème.` only as a structural model; change its content.
3. Write a new sentence demonstrating **qui, que, and où**. Use `La femme qui parle...` only as a structural model; change its content.
4. Write a new sentence demonstrating **dont and lequel**. Use `L'ami dont je parle...` only as a structural model; change its content.

:::answer
Answers vary. A correct answer must show the target contrast or pronoun role, not merely copy the model.

Sample answers:

1. Savoir versus connaître: `Je connais ce quartier, mais je ne sais pas son histoire.` — Familiarity with a place uses `connaître`; information uses `savoir`.
2. Irregular verb families: `Nous voyons la montagne depuis la fenêtre.` — `voir` has the special `nous` form `voyons`.
3. `Qui`, `que`, and `où`: `Le café où nous étudions est calme.` — `où` marks the place where the studying happens.
4. `Dont` and `lequel`: `Le projet dont je parle est important.` — `parler de` needs `de`, so `dont` is the right connector.

Mark yourself wrong if the relative pronoun has no clear antecedent when it needs one, if `dont` does not replace a de-phrase, or if a form of `lequel` does not agree with the noun it replaces.
:::


## C. Four-way variation

Choose one sentence from each lesson unit and rewrite it four ways:

1. change the subject;
2. make it negative;
3. turn it into a question;
4. change one meaningful detail.

:::answer
Worked example, starting from `Je connais la femme qui dirige ce musée.`

1. Change the subject: `Nous connaissons la femme qui dirige ce musée.` — `connais` changes to `connaissons`; the relative clause still describes the woman.
2. Make it negative: `Je ne connais pas la femme qui dirige ce musée.` — The negative wraps the conjugated verb `connais`.
3. Turn it into a question: `Est-ce que tu connais la femme qui dirige ce musée ?` — The main clause becomes a question; the relative clause stays attached to `la femme`.
4. Change one meaningful detail: `Je connais l'homme qui dirige ce musée.` — Changing the antecedent from woman to man does not change `qui`, because it is chosen by role, not gender.

Check that your relative pronoun still makes sense after every rewrite. A changed noun may require agreement elsewhere, but `qui`, `que`, `où`, and `dont` themselves do not change for gender or number.
:::


## D. Dictation and correction

Use text-to-speech to hear six examples from `lessons.md`. Write what you hear without looking. Correct in separate passes:

1. vocabulary;
2. verb form;
3. agreement or pronoun placement;
4. spelling and accents.

Do the dictation first, using this chapter's own examples as your audio. Then open the answers to check: they list six sentences worth dictating and the trap each one sets.

:::answer
Six sentences from this chapter that make good dictation material, and the trap each one sets:

1. `Je sais la réponse.` — `sais` has a silent final **s**, and `réponse` needs the accent.
2. `On voit les montagnes de notre fenêtre.` — The singular verb form `voit` ends with a silent **t**, and `montagnes` keeps its final **s** in writing.
3. `Nous courons dans le parc le matin.` — `courons` is the `nous` form of `courir`; do not spell it like a regular -er verb.
4. `Nous ouvrons les fenêtres.` — The `offrir` family has regular-looking endings, but the infinitive is not an -er verb.
5. `Voici le livre que mon professeur recommande.` — `que` is not elided here because `mon` begins with a consonant sound.
6. `Voilà ce dont nous avons besoin.` — The trap is `dont`, because `avoir besoin de` hides the `de` inside the relative pronoun.

Correct first for meaning, then for the irregular verb form, then for the relative pronoun. A sentence can sound familiar and still have the wrong connector.
:::

## E. Personal writing

Describe a profession or person in six sentences. Include savoir, connaître, and four different relative pronouns.

:::answer
Answers vary. A model description:

1. `Je connais une médecin qui travaille dans mon quartier.`
2. `Elle sait parler avec les patients qui ont peur.`
3. `Le cabinet où elle travaille est près de la gare.`
4. `C'est une personne que tout le monde respecte.`
5. `Elle explique les traitements dont les patients ont besoin.`
6. `Voilà ce que j'admire chez elle.`

Self-check: this model includes `connais`, `sait`, and the relative pronouns `qui`, `où`, `que`, `dont`, and `ce que`. Mark yourself wrong if `savoir` is used for knowing a person, if `connaître` is used before an infinitive, or if one relative pronoun does not replace a clear role in its clause.
:::


## F. Oral recall

Explain each of the chapter's four main rules aloud in simple English, then give one French example from memory.

:::answer
1. **Savoir versus connaître.** Use `savoir` for facts and know-how; use `connaître` for people, places, and familiarity. Example: `Je connais Marie, mais je ne sais pas son adresse.`
2. **Irregular verb families.** Learn common forms in phrase groups, not by guessing regular endings. Example: `Nous voyons le problème.`
3. **Qui, que, and où.** Choose by role: subject, direct object, or place/time. Example: `Voici le livre que je lis.`
4. **Dont and lequel.** Use `dont` for a de-phrase and forms of `lequel` after many other prepositions. Example: `C'est le sujet dont nous parlons.`

If your example uses the right French words but you cannot explain why, split it into two shorter sentences and identify the missing role.
:::
