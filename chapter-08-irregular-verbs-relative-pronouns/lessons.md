# Chapter 8 Lessons: Irregular Verbs II and Relative Pronouns
This chapter helps you speak more precisely about knowledge, perception, belief, movement, and connection between ideas. You will learn several irregular verb families and then use relative pronouns to join two short sentences into one smoother, more natural French sentence.
## Learning objectives
You will learn to:
1. distinguish `savoir` and `connaître` accurately;
2. conjugate and use `voir`, `croire`, `courir`, `rire`, the `offrir` group, the `conduire` group, `suivre`, `vivre`, and the `craindre` group;
3. use `qui`, `que`, and `où` to connect clauses;
4. use `dont` and forms of `lequel` after prepositions;
5. use `ce qui`, `ce que`, and `ce dont` when there is no named antecedent;
6. read and create longer sentences without losing track of the subject and object.
## Prerequisite quick check
Before beginning, make sure you can already:
- recognize a subject and a direct object in a simple sentence;
- use `à` and `de` with confidence from Chapter 6;
- build two-verb frames such as `je veux partir` and `j'essaie de comprendre` from Chapter 7;
- hear the difference between a conjugated form and an infinitive.
You will need those earlier skills immediately. For example, `le livre que je lis`, `l'étudiante à laquelle j'écris`, and `ce dont nous avons besoin` all depend on knowing what the verb controls.
## How to study this chapter
When you study the relative pronouns, split each sentence into two shorter ideas and identify the role of the missing word. Ask yourself: is it the subject, the object, a place/time word, or part of a `de` phrase?
## Unit 1: `savoir` versus `connaître`
### Core idea
Both verbs translate as "to know," but they divide the job differently.
- `savoir` = to know a fact, to know information, to know how to do something
- `connaître` = to know a person, a place, or something through familiarity
### Present tense forms
| Pronoun | `savoir` | `connaître` |
|---|---|---|
| `je` | `sais` | `connais` |
| `tu` | `sais` | `connais` |
| `il/elle/on` | `sait` | `connaît` |
| `nous` | `savons` | `connaissons` |
| `vous` | `savez` | `connaissez` |
| `ils/elles` | `savent` | `connaissent` |
### How the meanings differ
Use `savoir` with information or with an infinitive.
- `Je sais la réponse.` — I know the answer.
- `Elle sait conduire.` — She knows how to drive.
- `Nous savons que le musée ferme à six heures.` — We know that the museum closes at six.
Use `connaître` with people, places, and familiarity.
- `Je connais la femme qui dirige cette entreprise.` — I know the woman who runs this company.
- `Nous connaissons Paris assez bien.` — We know Paris fairly well.
- `Tu connais ce film ?` — Do you know this film? / Are you familiar with this film?
### Pronunciation note
`Connaissons` and `connaissent` look related, but they sound very different. Say the whole forms aloud: `nous connaissons`, `ils connaissent`.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not use `connaître` before an infinitive.
- correct: `Elle sait nager.` — She knows how to swim.
- incorrect: `*Elle connaît nager.`
### Sentence-building practice
Choose `savoir` or `connaître` and write the full sentence.
1. I know your brother.
2. We know how to solve the problem.
3. Do you know this neighborhood?
4. They know that the restaurant is closed.
## Unit 2: `voir`, `croire`, `courir`, and `rire`
### Core idea
These verbs often describe perception, belief, and quick everyday actions. They are irregular in different ways, so learn them as a family of high-frequency forms.
### Present tense forms
| Pronoun | `voir` | `croire` | `courir` | `rire` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `je` | `vois` | `crois` | `cours` | `ris` |
| `tu` | `vois` | `crois` | `cours` | `ris` |
| `il/elle/on` | `voit` | `croit` | `court` | `rit` |
| `nous` | `voyons` | `croyons` | `courons` | `rions` |
| `vous` | `voyez` | `croyez` | `courez` | `riez` |
| `ils/elles` | `voient` | `croient` | `courent` | `rient` |
### Examples in context
- `Je vois le problème maintenant.` — I see the problem now.
- `Tu crois tout ce qu'il dit ?` — Do you believe everything he says?
- `Nous courons dans le parc le matin.` — We run in the park in the morning.
- `Elles rient toujours aux mêmes blagues.` — They always laugh at the same jokes.
- `On voit les montagnes de notre fenêtre.` — We can see the mountains from our window.
- `Je ne crois pas cette histoire.` — I do not believe that story.
### Pronunciation note
The `y` in `voyons` and `croyez` changes the sound of the stem. Listen especially for `nous voyons` versus `vous voyez`.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not overtranslate English "to think" as `croire` in every case. `Croire` is about belief: `je crois que...` means "I believe / I think that..." in the sense of opinion, not mental effort or planning.
### Sentence-building practice
Translate into French.
1. We see the station from here.
2. She believes that you are right.
3. They run every Saturday.
4. Why are you laughing?
Then change sentence 2 to the negative.
## Unit 3: Other irregular verb families
### Core idea
This unit groups several important patterns that often surprise learners: the `offrir` family, the `conduire` family, `suivre` and `vivre`, and the `craindre` family.
### The `offrir` family
These verbs look like `-ir` verbs but behave in many ways like common `-er` verbs in the present tense.
| Pronoun | `offrir` | `ouvrir` | `souffrir` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `je` | `offre` | `ouvre` | `souffre` |
| `tu` | `offres` | `ouvres` | `souffres` |
| `il/elle/on` | `offre` | `ouvre` | `souffre` |
| `nous` | `offrons` | `ouvrons` | `souffrons` |
| `vous` | `offrez` | `ouvrez` | `souffrez` |
| `ils/elles` | `offrent` | `ouvrent` | `souffrent` |
- `Elle offre un livre à sa mère.` — She gives her mother a book.
- `Nous ouvrons les fenêtres.` — We open the windows.
- `Il souffre du dos.` — He suffers from back pain.
### The `conduire` family and related verbs
| Pronoun | `conduire` | `suivre` | `vivre` | `craindre` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `je` | `conduis` | `suis` | `vis` | `crains` |
| `tu` | `conduis` | `suis` | `vis` | `crains` |
| `il/elle/on` | `conduit` | `suit` | `vit` | `craint` |
| `nous` | `conduisons` | `suivons` | `vivons` | `craignons` |
| `vous` | `conduisez` | `suivez` | `vivez` | `craignez` |
| `ils/elles` | `conduisent` | `suivent` | `vivent` | `craignent` |
### Examples in context
- `Tu conduis souvent la nuit ?` — Do you often drive at night?
- `Nous suivons ce cours ensemble.` — We are following this course together.
- `Mes grands-parents vivent à la campagne.` — My grandparents live in the countryside.
- `Je crains le froid en hiver.` — I fear the cold in winter.
- `Voici la route que nous suivons chaque été.` — Here is the road that we follow every summer.
- `Ils vivent dans un quartier que je connais bien.` — They live in a neighborhood that I know well.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not confuse `suis` from `suivre` with `suis` from `être` when you see it without context. The subject and the rest of the sentence tell you which verb is meant.
### Sentence-building practice
Complete the sentence with the right verb and form.
1. `Nous ___ un guide très précis.` — follow
2. `Elle ___ la porte pour laisser entrer le chat.` — open
3. `Je ___ toujours les règles de sécurité.` — follow / obey in the sense of follow
4. `Ils ___ une vie simple près de la mer.` — live
5. `Tu ___ vraiment conduire jusqu'à Marseille ?` — know how / be driving; choose the correct chapter verb if needed.
## Unit 4: `qui`, `que`, and `où`
### Core idea
Relative pronouns connect two ideas. They replace a repeated noun and begin a relative clause.
- `qui` = subject of the relative clause
- `que` / `qu'` = direct object of the relative clause
- `où` = place or time
### How to choose the form
| Relative pronoun | What it replaces | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| `qui` | the subject | `La femme qui parle...` | the woman who is speaking... |
| `que` | the direct object | `Le livre que je lis...` | the book that I am reading... |
| `où` | place or time | `La ville où j'habite...` | the city where I live... |
### Building the sentence step by step
1. `Je connais la femme. La femme dirige cette entreprise.`
2. Replace the repeated noun as the **subject** of the second clause.
3. `Je connais la femme qui dirige cette entreprise.` — I know the woman who runs this company.
More examples:
- `Voici le livre que mon professeur recommande.` — Here is the book that my teacher recommends.
- `Montréal est la ville où ils veulent vivre.` — Montreal is the city where they want to live.
- `Le jour où nous partons arrive vite.` — The day when we are leaving is coming quickly.
- `Je vois les enfants qui jouent dans la cour.` — I see the children who are playing in the yard.
### Pronunciation note
`Que` becomes `qu'` before a vowel sound: `le film qu'elle aime`. The function stays the same even when the spelling shortens.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not choose `qui` just because English uses "who." Ask what role the missing word plays inside the relative clause.
- `la femme qui parle` — she is the subject of `parle`
- `la femme que je vois` — she is the object of `vois`
### Sentence-building practice
Combine each pair into one sentence.
1. `Je regarde un film. Le film commence à huit heures.`
2. `Nous visitons une ville. Mes parents connaissent cette ville.`
3. `Voici le café. Nous allons souvent dans ce café.`
4. `C'est le jour. Elle arrive ce jour-là.`
## Unit 5: `dont`, forms of `lequel`, and indefinite relatives
### Core idea
Use `dont` when the second clause needs `de`. Use forms of `lequel` after a preposition when you need "which one" or when a more explicit prepositional relative is useful. Use `ce qui`, `ce que`, and `ce dont` when there is no named noun before the clause.
### `dont`
| Pattern | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| verb + `de` | `C'est un métier dont elle parle souvent.` | It is a profession that she often talks about. |
| `avoir besoin de` | `Voici le document dont j'ai besoin.` | Here is the document I need. |
| possession link | `L'étudiant dont la sœur habite ici vient du Chili.` | The student whose sister lives here comes from Chile. |
### Forms of `lequel`
| Form | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `lequel` | which one / which masculine singular one |
| `laquelle` | which feminine singular one |
| `lesquels` | which masculine or mixed plural ones |
| `lesquelles` | which feminine plural ones |
After `à` and `de`, these forms contract.
| Base form | With `à` | With `de` |
|---|---|---|
| `lequel` | `auquel` | `duquel` |
| `laquelle` | `à laquelle` | `de laquelle` |
| `lesquels` | `auxquels` | `desquels` |
| `lesquelles` | `auxquelles` | `desquelles` |
Examples:
- `Le projet auquel je pense change souvent.` — The project I am thinking about changes often.
- `La chaise sur laquelle il pose son sac est cassée.` — The chair on which he puts his bag is broken.
- `Les collègues avec lesquels je travaille sont patients.` — The coworkers with whom I work are patient.
- `Je ne sais pas de laquelle de ces routes tu parles.` — I do not know which of these roads you are talking about.
### `ce qui`, `ce que`, and `ce dont`
Use these forms when there is **no named antecedent** before the relative clause.
| Form | Role in the clause | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ce qui` | subject | `Je comprends ce qui se passe.` | I understand what is happening. |
| `ce que` / `ce qu'` | direct object | `Dis-moi ce que tu veux.` | Tell me what you want. |
| `ce dont` | `de` phrase | `Voilà ce dont nous avons besoin.` | That is what we need. |
### Turning a question into a statement
- `Qu'est-ce qui se passe ?` -> `Je sais ce qui se passe.`
- `Qu'est-ce que tu cherches ?` -> `Je vois ce que tu cherches.`
- `De quoi parlent-ils ?` -> `Je comprends ce dont ils parlent.`
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not use `ce que` when the missing word is the subject. Compare:
- `Je sais ce qui arrive.` — I know what is happening.
- `Je sais ce que tu lis.` — I know what you are reading.
### Sentence-building practice
Choose the correct form: `dont`, `lequel`, `ce qui`, `ce que`, or `ce dont`.
1. `C'est le problème ___ nous discutons.`
2. `Je ne comprends pas ___ te fait rire.`
3. `Voici la table sur ___ nous allons manger.`
4. `Dis-moi ___ tu crois.`
5. `Voilà ___ j'ai besoin pour le voyage.`

## Mini-dialogue: choosing a research topic
**Lina :** `Tu connais le professeur qui anime le séminaire du jeudi ?` — Do you know the professor who leads the Thursday seminar?
**Marc :** `Oui, je le connais un peu, mais je ne sais pas tout ce qu'il attend.` — Yes, I know him a little, but I do not know everything he expects.
**Lina :** `Je vois. Moi, je cherche un sujet dont je peux parler avec plaisir.` — I see. I am looking for a topic that I can talk about with pleasure.
**Marc :** `Tu peux choisir le film dont tu me parles souvent.` — You can choose the film that you often tell me about.
**Lina :** `Peut-être. Tu crois que c'est un sujet auquel il va s'intéresser ?` — Maybe. Do you think it is a topic that he is going to be interested in?
**Marc :** `Oui, parce qu'il aime tout ce qui touche à la mémoire et à la ville.` — Yes, because he likes everything that has to do with memory and the city.
**Lina :** `Dans ce cas, je vais suivre ton conseil et écrire ce que je pense ce soir.` — In that case, I am going to follow your advice and write what I think tonight.
**Marc :** `Parfait. Dis-moi demain ce dont tu as encore besoin.` — Perfect. Tell me tomorrow what you still need.

3. Rewrite Lina's first line with `vous` instead of `tu`.
4. Change `Tu peux choisir le film dont tu me parles souvent` into two short sentences.
5. Replace `tout ce qui touche à la mémoire` with a version using `ce que` if possible; if not, explain why not.
## Connected reading: un quartier qu'on apprend à aimer
`J'habite maintenant dans un quartier que je connais encore mal. Au début, je ne sais pas toujours où faire mes courses ni quels bus suivre. Peu à peu, je connais de mieux en mieux les rues qui montent vers le parc et les cafés où les étudiants rient pendant des heures. J'aime la petite librairie dont la propriétaire recommande toujours un roman surprenant. Le samedi, je vois des voisins avec lesquels je parle parfois, et je crois que c'est cela qui me fait rester ici avec plaisir. Ce que j'aime le plus, pourtant, c'est la place centrale : c'est là que la ville semble vraiment vivre.`
— I now live in a neighborhood that I still do not know very well. At first, I do not always know where to do my shopping or which buses to follow. Little by little, I know better and better the streets that climb toward the park and the cafés where students laugh for hours. I like the small bookstore whose owner always recommends a surprising novel. On Saturdays, I see neighbors with whom I sometimes speak, and I believe that that is what makes me stay here happily. What I like most, however, is the central square: that is where the city really seems to live.

3. Underline the verbs from the irregular families studied in this chapter.
4. Change the last two sentences from `je` to `nous`.
5. Write three true sentences about a place you know using the reading as a model.
## Common mistakes to avoid
1. **Using `savoir` for people and places.** Say `je connais ce professeur`, not `*je sais ce professeur`.
2. **Choosing the relative pronoun from English meaning alone.** Check the role inside the clause first.
3. **Forgetting `de` inside `dont`.** If the verb or expression needs `de`, `dont` is often the right choice.
4. **Using `ce que` as a subject.** Say `ce qui m'intéresse`, not `*ce que m'intéresse`.
5. **Ignoring agreement in forms of `lequel`.** Match masculine/feminine and singular/plural carefully.
6. **Confusing `suis` from `suivre` with `suis` from `être`.** Read the whole sentence before deciding.

### Core habits
- Ask what the verb needs before you choose the relative pronoun.
- Keep `savoir` for facts and know-how, `connaître` for familiarity.
- Learn `dont` with common expressions like `parler de` and `avoir besoin de`.
- Split long sentences into two smaller ones when checking your grammar.
