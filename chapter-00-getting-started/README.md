# Chapter 0: Getting Started — Pronunciation and Survival French

**Start here if you have never studied French.**

Chapters 1-16 teach grammar, and from the very first page they ask you to read French examples aloud. That instruction is useless if nobody has shown you how French letters sound. This chapter fills that gap.

It also gives you enough real French to survive a first conversation, so you are speaking from day one rather than after Chapter 6.

## What you will learn

1. The alphabet, the five accent marks, and how to spell your name aloud.
2. Which letters are silent, and why `petit` and `petite` sound different.
3. The vowel sounds English does not have, especially `u` versus `ou`.
4. The four nasal vowels.
5. The consonants that trap English speakers: `r`, `j`, `ch`, `gn`, `qu`, soft `c` and `g`.
6. Elision (`j'habite`), liaison (`les amis`), and French rhythm.
7. Greetings, politeness, and when to say `tu` or `vous`.
8. Numbers 0-20 and a full self-introduction.

## Best learning order

1. Read one unit at a time in [`lessons.md`](lessons.md).
2. **Say every French example aloud.** On the website, tap the French text to hear it, then imitate it immediately.
3. Cover the French and write it from memory.
4. Pick five phrases from [`survival-phrases.md`](survival-phrases.md) and use them the same day.
5. Look up any unfamiliar grammar word in [`glossary.md`](glossary.md).
6. Complete [`exercises.md`](exercises.md) with no notes visible.
7. Correct with [`answer-key.md`](answer-key.md), writing down the **rule** behind each error, not just the right answer.
8. Follow [`review-plan.md`](review-plan.md).

## Why pronunciation comes before grammar

Three practical reasons:

1. **You cannot memorize what you cannot say.** Vocabulary learned only by eye fades quickly and does not transfer to listening.
2. **French spelling hides sound.** `parlent` has seven letters and sounds like `parle`. Without this chapter, later verb tables look arbitrary.
3. **Agreement is audible.** The difference between `petit`/`petite` and `bon`/`bonne` is a pronunciation fact. Chapter 1's grammar becomes much easier once you can hear it.

## How long this takes

| Pace | Time |
|---|---|
| Light | 1 week (one unit per day) |
| Standard | 3-4 days (two units per day) |
| Intensive | 1 long session, then daily review while doing Chapter 1 |

Do not aim for a perfect accent before moving on. Aim for a confident first attempt at any written word.

## Mastery target

Move to Chapter 1 when you can:

- read an unfamiliar French sentence aloud without freezing;
- say `tu` and `tout` so a listener could tell them apart;
- greet someone, introduce yourself, and order something in a café from memory;
- explain what elision and liaison are, in your own words;
- count from 0 to 20 without hesitating.

You do **not** need a native accent. You need to be understood, and to stop guessing.
