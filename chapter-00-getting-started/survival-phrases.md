# Survival Phrases: Your First 100 French Sentences

This is a phrasebook, not a grammar lesson. Every sentence here is a **fixed chunk** you can use today without understanding how it is built. The grammar behind each one arrives in Chapters 1-16.

Tap any French phrase on the website to hear it. Say it aloud immediately after. If it goes by too fast, slow **Speaking speed** down in the sidebar, or select the phrase and press **Slower**.

## How to use this page

Do not memorize all of it. Choose **five phrases per day**, say each one ten times aloud, and use them in a real or imagined situation the same day. A phrase you have used once is worth more than ten you have only read.

Star the phrases you actually need. A learner in a French-speaking city needs the café and directions sections first; a learner in a classroom needs the classroom section first.

## 1. Greetings and goodbyes

| French | English |
|---|---|
| `Bonjour` | Hello / Good morning |
| `Bonsoir` | Good evening |
| `Salut` | Hi / Bye (informal) |
| `Au revoir` | Goodbye |
| `Bonne journée` | Have a good day |
| `Bonne soirée` | Have a good evening |
| `À bientôt` | See you soon |
| `À demain` | See you tomorrow |
| `Bonne nuit` | Good night (only when going to bed) |

Careful: `Bonne nuit` is not a greeting. Use `Bonsoir` when you arrive in the evening and `Bonne nuit` only when someone is going to sleep.

## 3. How are you?

| French | English |
|---|---|
| `Ça va ?` | How's it going? |
| `Comment ça va ?` | How are you? |
| `Comment allez-vous ?` | How are you? (formal) |
| `Ça va bien, merci.` | I'm well, thank you. |
| `Ça va, et toi ?` | I'm fine, and you? (informal) |
| `Et vous ?` | And you? (formal) |
| `Pas mal.` | Not bad. |
| `Ça ne va pas très bien.` | I'm not doing very well. |

Always return the question with `Et toi ?` or `Et vous ?`.

## 5. When you don't understand

These are the most important phrases in this file. Learn them first.

| French | English |
|---|---|
| `Je ne comprends pas.` | I don't understand. |
| `Je ne sais pas.` | I don't know. |
| `Pouvez-vous répéter, s'il vous plaît ?` | Can you repeat, please? |
| `Plus lentement, s'il vous plaît.` | More slowly, please. |
| `Comment dit-on ___ en français ?` | How do you say ___ in French? |
| `Qu'est-ce que ça veut dire ?` | What does that mean? |
| `Comment ça s'écrit ?` | How is that spelled? |
| `Parlez-vous anglais ?` | Do you speak English? |
| `Je parle un peu français.` | I speak a little French. |
| `J'apprends le français.` | I am learning French. |

A useful habit: instead of switching to English, say `Je ne comprends pas` and wait. Most people will simply rephrase more simply.

## 6. In a café or restaurant

| French | English |
|---|---|
| `Une table pour deux, s'il vous plaît.` | A table for two, please. |
| `La carte, s'il vous plaît.` | The menu, please. |
| `Je voudrais ___.` | I would like ___. |
| `Un café, s'il vous plaît.` | A coffee, please. |
| `Une carafe d'eau, s'il vous plaît.` | A jug of tap water, please. |
| `C'est tout, merci.` | That's all, thank you. |
| `Ça fait combien ?` | How much is it? |
| `L'addition, s'il vous plaît.` | The bill, please. |
| `Je peux payer par carte ?` | Can I pay by card? |
| `C'était délicieux.` | It was delicious. |

`Je voudrais` (I would like) is far more polite than `Je veux` (I want). Use `Je voudrais` for every request.

## 8. Directions and travel

| French | English |
|---|---|
| `Où est ___ ?` | Where is ___? |
| `Où sont les toilettes ?` | Where are the toilets? |
| `Je cherche la gare.` | I'm looking for the train station. |
| `C'est loin ?` | Is it far? |
| `C'est près d'ici ?` | Is it near here? |
| `À gauche` / `À droite` / `Tout droit` | Left / Right / Straight ahead |
| `Je suis perdu(e).` | I am lost. |
| `Un billet pour ___, s'il vous plaît.` | A ticket to ___, please. |
| `À quelle heure part le train ?` | What time does the train leave? |

Note the trap: `tout droit` means "straight ahead," while `à droite` means "to the right."

## 12. Two ready-made conversations

Memorize these end to end. They cover most first encounters.

### Meeting someone

**A :** `Bonjour ! Je m'appelle Ana. Et vous ?` — Hello! My name is Ana. And you?

**B :** `Bonjour Ana, je m'appelle Paul. Enchanté.` — Hello Ana, my name is Paul. Nice to meet you.

**A :** `Enchantée. Vous habitez ici ?` — Nice to meet you. Do you live here?

**B :** `Oui, j'habite à Lyon. Et vous ?` — Yes, I live in Lyon. And you?

**A :** `Je suis canadienne, mais j'habite à Paris maintenant. Je parle un peu français.` — I am Canadian, but I live in Paris now. I speak a little French.

**B :** `Vous parlez très bien !` — You speak very well!

### Ordering a drink

**A :** `Bonjour, un café et une eau, s'il vous plaît.` — Hello, a coffee and a water, please.

**B :** `Bien sûr. C'est tout ?` — Of course. Is that all?

**A :** `Oui, c'est tout. Ça fait combien ?` — Yes, that's all. How much is it?

**B :** `Quatre euros cinquante.` — Four euros fifty.

**A :** `Pardon, je ne comprends pas. Plus lentement, s'il vous plaît ?` — Sorry, I don't understand. More slowly, please?

**B :** `Quatre euros cinquante.` — Four euros fifty.

**A :** `Merci beaucoup. Au revoir !` — Thank you very much. Goodbye!
