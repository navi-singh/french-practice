# Grammar Glossary: Every Term This Course Uses

Grammar words are often the real obstacle for a beginner. A lesson may be perfectly clear except for one unexplained term such as *infinitive* or *partitive*.

This page defines every grammar term used anywhere in Chapters 0-16, in plain English, with a French example and the chapter where you will study it properly. Keep it open beside the lessons.

You do **not** need to read this page from start to finish. Look up a term when you meet it.

## How to read the tables

The **Chapter** column tells you where the term is actually taught. If a term appears early but is taught later, you can safely treat it as a fixed phrase until you reach that chapter.

## 1. Words and word types

| Term | Plain-English meaning | French example | Chapter |
|---|---|---|---:|
| **Noun** | A word for a person, place, thing, animal, or idea. | `le jardin` — the garden | 1 |
| **Article** | The small word before a noun: "the," "a," "some." | `le`, `une`, `des` | 1 |
| **Definite article** | "The." Points to something specific or a whole category. | `le café` — the coffee | 1 |
| **Indefinite article** | "A," "an," or "some." Points to an unspecified one. | `un café` — a coffee | 1 |
| **Partitive article** | "Some" of something you cannot count. | `du café` — some coffee | 9 |
| **Adjective** | A word describing a noun. | `petit` — small | 1 |
| **Adverb** | A word describing a verb, adjective, or other adverb. Often answers "how?" | `lentement` — slowly | 10 |
| **Verb** | A word for an action or a state. | `parler` — to speak | 3 |
| **Preposition** | A small linking word of place, time, or relation. | `à`, `de`, `dans`, `sur` | 6 |
| **Conjunction** | A word joining two parts of a sentence. | `et` — and; `mais` — but | 3 |
| **Pronoun** | A word replacing a noun so you don't repeat it. | `il`, `le`, `lui` | 2, 9 |

## 2. Gender, number, and agreement

| Term | Plain-English meaning | French example | Chapter |
|---|---|---|---:|
| **Gender** | Every French noun is grammatically masculine or feminine. This is not about biology; a table is feminine. | `le livre` (m.), `la table` (f.) | 1 |
| **Masculine / Feminine** | The two genders. Learn the gender **with** the noun, never alone. | `un problème` (m.) | 1 |
| **Number** | Whether a word is singular (one) or plural (more than one). | `le livre` / `les livres` | 1 |
| **Singular / Plural** | One / more than one. | `une porte` / `des portes` | 1 |
| **Agreement** | Changing an article, adjective, or participle so it matches its noun's gender and number. | `une petite maison` | 1 |
| **Invariable** | A word that never changes form. | `il y a` — there is/are | 2 |

## 3. Sentence parts

| Term | Plain-English meaning | French example | Chapter |
|---|---|---|---:|
| **Subject** | Who or what performs the verb. | `Marie parle.` — Marie speaks. | 2 |
| **Object** | Who or what receives the action. | `Je vois **le chien**.` | 9 |
| **Direct object** | The thing acted on directly, with no preposition. Answers "what?" or "whom?" | `Je lis **le livre**.` — I read the book. | 9 |
| **Indirect object** | The person the action goes *to*, usually marked by `à`. | `Je parle **à Marie**.` — I speak to Marie. | 9 |
| **Clause** | A group of words containing its own verb. | `quand j'arrive` — when I arrive | 8 |
| **Main clause** | The clause that can stand alone as a sentence. | `Je mange` in `Je mange quand j'arrive.` | 8 |
| **Subordinate clause** | A clause that depends on the main clause. | `quand j'arrive` | 8, 16 |
| **Antecedent** | The noun that a relative pronoun refers back to. | In `le livre que je lis`, the antecedent is `le livre`. | 8 |

## 4. Pronouns

| Term | Plain-English meaning | French example | Chapter |
|---|---|---|---:|
| **Subject pronoun** | Replaces the subject noun. | `je, tu, il, elle, on, nous, vous, ils, elles` | 2 |
| **Direct object pronoun** | Replaces a direct object; goes **before** the verb. | `Je **le** vois.` — I see him/it. | 9 |
| **Indirect object pronoun** | Replaces `à` + a person. | `Je **lui** parle.` — I speak to him/her. | 9 |
| **Stressed (disjunctive) pronoun** | Stands alone or follows a preposition. | `avec **moi**` — with me | 11 |
| **Reflexive pronoun** | Shows the action returns to the subject. | `Je **me** lave.` — I wash myself. | 12 |
| **Relative pronoun** | Joins two clauses: "who," "which," "that." | `qui`, `que`, `où`, `dont` | 8 |
| **Demonstrative** | "This," "that," "these," "those." | `ce livre` — this book | 10 |
| **Possessive** | Shows ownership: "my," "your," "his." | `mon livre` — my book | 10 |
| **Indefinite pronoun/adjective** | Vague quantity words: "someone," "each," "several." | `quelqu'un`, `chaque`, `plusieurs` | 15 |
| **The pronoun `y`** | Replaces a place or `à` + a thing. | `J'**y** vais.` — I'm going there. | 9 |
| **The pronoun `en`** | Replaces `de` + a thing, or a quantity. | `J'**en** veux.` — I want some. | 9 |

## 5. Verbs: the basics

| Term | Plain-English meaning | French example | Chapter |
|---|---|---|---:|
| **Infinitive** | The unchanged "dictionary" form of a verb: "to speak." French infinitives end in `-er`, `-ir`, or `-re`. | `parler`, `finir`, `vendre` | 3 |
| **Conjugate** | To change a verb's ending to match its subject. | `je parle`, `nous parlons` | 3 |
| **Stem (radical)** | What is left after removing the infinitive ending. It carries the meaning. | `parler` -> stem `parl-` | 3 |
| **Ending** | The part added to the stem to show who and when. | `parl` + `-ons` | 3 |
| **Regular verb** | A verb following a predictable pattern for its group. | `parler`, `finir`, `vendre` | 3, 4, 5 |
| **Irregular verb** | A verb with forms you must memorize individually. | `être`, `avoir`, `aller`, `faire` | 2, 6, 7, 8 |
| **Auxiliary (helping verb)** | A verb used with a participle to build a compound tense. French uses `avoir` or `être`. | `j'**ai** parlé` — I spoke | 13 |
| **Participle** | A verb form used with an auxiliary or as a describing word. | `parlé` (past), `parlant` (present) | 12, 13 |
| **Past participle** | The form used after an auxiliary to build a past tense. | `parlé`, `fini`, `vendu` | 13 |
| **Present participle** | The `-ant` form, close to English "-ing." | `en parlant` — while speaking | 12 |
| **Pronominal (reflexive) verb** | A verb used with a reflexive pronoun. | `se lever` — to get up | 12 |

## 6. Verbs: tense and mood

**Tense** tells you *when*. **Mood** tells you *how the speaker views* the action: as a fact, a command, a wish, or a hypothesis.

| Term | Plain-English meaning | French example | Chapter |
|---|---|---|---:|
| **Tense** | The time of the action: past, present, or future. | `je parle` (present) | 3 |
| **Mood** | The speaker's attitude: statement, command, doubt, hypothesis. | indicative, imperative, subjunctive | 11, 16 |
| **Present tense** | Happening now, or generally true. | `je parle` — I speak / I am speaking | 3 |
| **Near future (futur proche)** | `aller` + infinitive. Like "going to." | `je vais parler` — I'm going to speak | 6 |
| **Passé composé** | The main past tense for completed events. | `j'ai parlé` — I spoke | 13 |
| **Imparfait** | A past tense for ongoing states, habits, and background. | `je parlais` — I used to speak | 14 |
| **Pluperfect (plus-que-parfait)** | A past *before* another past. | `j'avais parlé` — I had spoken | 15 |
| **Simple future (futur simple)** | A one-word future tense. | `je parlerai` — I will speak | 15 |
| **Conditional** | "Would." For hypotheses and polite requests. | `je parlerais` — I would speak | 15 |
| **Imperative** | A command or instruction. | `Parle !` — Speak! | 11 |
| **Subjunctive** | A mood for wish, doubt, emotion, and necessity, after certain triggers. | `que je parle` | 16 |
| **Indicative** | The ordinary "fact-stating" mood: all normal tenses. | `je parle` | 3 |

Do not be intimidated by this table. In everyday French, the present, the passé composé, the imparfait, and the near future cover the overwhelming majority of what you will say.

## 7. Sentence operations

| Term | Plain-English meaning | French example | Chapter |
|---|---|---|---:|
| **Negation** | Making a sentence mean "not." French wraps the verb in two words. | `Je **ne** parle **pas**.` | 2, 11 |
| **Interrogative** | Relating to questions. | `Où ?` — Where? | 3 |
| **Question word** | Who, what, where, when, why, how. | `qui, que, où, quand, pourquoi, comment` | 3 |
| **Inversion** | Flipping the verb and subject to form a question. | `Parlez-vous ?` — Do you speak? | 3 |
| **`Est-ce que`** | A fixed phrase placed in front of a statement to turn it into a question. | `Est-ce que tu parles ?` | 3 |
| **Comparative** | Comparing two things: more, less, as. | `plus grand que` — bigger than | 10 |
| **Superlative** | The extreme of a group: the most, the least. | `le plus grand` — the biggest | 10 |
| **Register** | How formal the language is. | `tu` (informal) vs `vous` (formal) | 0, 2 |

## 8. Sounds and spelling

| Term | Plain-English meaning | French example | Chapter |
|---|---|---|---:|
| **Vowel** | The letters `a, e, i, o, u, y` and their sounds. | `a` in `la` | 0 |
| **Consonant** | Any letter that is not a vowel. | `p` in `petit` | 0 |
| **Syllable** | One beat of a word. | `bon-jour` = 2 syllables | 0 |
| **Nasal vowel** | A vowel sent through the nose, written vowel + `n`/`m`. | `bon`, `vin`, `grand` | 0 |
| **Elision** | Dropping a final vowel before a vowel sound, marked by an apostrophe. | `je` + `ai` -> `j'ai` | 0, 1 |
| **Liaison** | Pronouncing a normally silent final consonant because the next word starts with a vowel. | `les amis` sounds "lay-z-ami" | 0, 1 |
| **Silent letter** | A written letter you do not pronounce. | the `t` in `petit` | 0 |
| **Accent mark** | A symbol changing a letter's sound or distinguishing two words. | `é`, `è`, `ê`, `ç`, `ë` | 0 |
| **Cognate** | A word that looks like its English equivalent. | `la table` — the table | 0 |
| **False friend** | A word that *looks* like an English word but means something else. | `actuellement` means "currently," not "actually" | 7 |

## 9. Study terms used in this course

| Term | What it means here |
|---|---|
| **Retrieval check** | A short quiz you answer **without looking**. Struggling to recall is what builds memory. |
| **Production task** | Creating your own sentence, not copying one. |
| **Transformation** | Rewriting a sentence with one change: subject, tense, number, or polarity. |
| **Polarity** | Whether a sentence is affirmative or negative. |
| **Dictation** | Writing down French you hear, without seeing it. |
| **Spaced review** | Revisiting material after 1, 3, 7, and 30 days rather than all at once. |
| **Closed-book** | Working with no notes visible. The only score that measures real mastery. |
| **Fixed chunk** | A phrase memorized whole, before you understand its grammar. |
| **Minimal pair** | Two words differing by one sound, used to train your ear. `tu` / `tout` |

## The ten terms that matter most at the start

If this page feels overwhelming, learn only these before Chapter 1:

1. **Noun** — a person, place, or thing.
2. **Gender** — every noun is masculine or feminine.
3. **Article** — the small word before a noun.
4. **Adjective** — a describing word.
5. **Agreement** — making words match in gender and number.
6. **Verb** — an action or state word.
7. **Infinitive** — the "to do" dictionary form.
8. **Conjugate** — change the verb ending to fit the subject.
9. **Subject** — who does the action.
10. **Elision** — `je` + `ai` becomes `j'ai`.

Everything else can wait until the chapter that teaches it.
