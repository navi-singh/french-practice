# Chapter 0 Answer Key

Correct by **rule**, not only by answer. For every mistake, write down the rule you broke. That sentence is worth more than the correction itself.

## Section A: Silent letters

| # | Word | Pronounced? | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `petit` | No | Most final consonants are silent. |
| 2 | `avec` | Yes | `c` is in CaReFuL. |
| 3 | `bonjour` | Yes | `r` is in CaReFuL. |
| 4 | `vous` | No | Final `s` is silent. |
| 5 | `neuf` | Yes | `f` is in CaReFuL. |
| 6 | `parler` | No | The `-er` infinitive ending is a standard exception to CaReFuL. |
| 7 | `avril` | Yes | `l` is in CaReFuL. |
| 8 | `grand` | No | Final `d` is silent; the `an` is nasal. |

## Section B: `u` versus `ou`

The "oo" sound is in the second word each time: 1. `tout` 2. `roue` 3. `vous` 4. `sourd`

The first word in each pair uses `u`, the rounded-lip sound that does not exist in English. If you cannot yet hear a difference, return to Unit 3 and practice the pair slowly ten times before continuing.

## Section C: Nasal vowels

| # | Word | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `bon` | Nasal | Vowel + `n` with nothing after it. |
| 2 | `bonne` | Not nasal | The `n` is doubled, so it is pronounced as a consonant. |
| 3 | `américain` | Nasal | `ain` at the end of the word. |
| 4 | `américaine` | Not nasal | A silent `-e` follows, so the `n` is pronounced. |
| 5 | `téléphone` | Not nasal | A vowel follows the `n`. |
| 6 | `combien` | Nasal (twice) | Both `om` and `ien` are nasal. |
| 7 | `un` | Nasal | Vowel + `n` with nothing after it. |
| 8 | `une` | Not nasal | A vowel sound follows, so the `n` is pronounced. |

Pairs 1/2, 3/4, and 7/8 are the pronunciation side of gender agreement. Being able to hear them will make Chapter 1 noticeably easier.

## Section D: Elision

1. `j'ai`
2. `l'hôtel` (`h` is silent, so the word counts as beginning with a vowel)
3. `n'est pas`
4. `d'eau`
5. `qu'elle`
6. `l'école`

Elision is required, not optional. `je ai` and `le hôtel` are always wrong.

## Section E: Liaison

| # | Phrase | Liaison | Linking sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `les amis` | Yes | `s` becomes "z" |
| 2 | `vous avez` | Yes | `s` becomes "z" |
| 3 | `un grand arbre` | Yes | `d` becomes "t" |
| 4 | `mon ami` | Yes | `n` links to the vowel |
| 5 | `un livre et un stylo` | No | Liaison is **never** made after `et`. |
| 6 | `nous allons` | Yes | `s` becomes "z" |

In item 5, note also that `un stylo` has no liaison because `stylo` begins with a consonant.

## Section F: Consonant sounds

1. `ça` — "s" (the cedilla forces the soft sound before `a`)
2. `le garçon` — "s" (same rule, before `o`)
3. `la chaise` — "sh"
4. `qui` — "k" only; the `u` is not pronounced
5. `la montagne` — "ny," as in *canyon*
6. `la maison` — "z," because the `s` sits between two vowels
7. `le poisson` — "s," because the `s` is doubled
8. `je` — "zh," as in *measure*

Items 6 and 7 are a genuine minimal pair: `le poisson` is the fish, `le poison` is poison.

## Section G: `tu` or `vous`?

1. `tu` — a close friend
2. `vous` — a stranger in a service role
3. `vous` — **any** group, even of children
4. `tu` — a family member
5. `vous` — someone in authority you do not know well
6. `tu` — a family member

Item 3 is the one most learners miss. `vous` is both the formal singular **and** the ordinary plural. Two people always take `vous`.

## Section H: What would you say?

Answers vary. Any of these is correct:

1. `Bonjour` — required before anything else. Saying only `Un croissant, s'il vous plaît` sounds rude.
2. `Un café, s'il vous plaît.` or `Je voudrais un café, s'il vous plaît.`
3. `De rien.` or `Je vous en prie.`
4. `Je ne comprends pas.` or `Pardon ?`
5. `Plus lentement, s'il vous plaît.` or `Pouvez-vous répéter, s'il vous plaît ?`
6. `Au revoir, bonne journée !`

An answer is correct if it is understandable **and** appropriately polite for the situation.

## Section I: Numbers

1. `trois`
2. `huit`
3. `douze`
4. `quinze`
5. `dix-sept`
6. `vingt`

Check the pronunciation traps: `sept` has a silent `p`, and `vingt` has a silent `t` when it stands alone.

## Section J: Find and fix the error

| # | Correction | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `J'habite à Paris.` | Elision is required. `h` is silent, so `je` becomes `j'`. |
| 2 | `J'ai vingt ans.` | French uses `avoir` (to have) for age, never `être`. Taught fully in Chapter 2. |
| 3 | `Bonsoir !` | `Bonne nuit` is used only when someone is going to bed, not on arrival. |
| 4 | `Je voudrais un café, s'il vous plaît.` | `Je veux` sounds blunt. Use `je voudrais` for requests, and add `s'il vous plaît`. |
| 5 | `Parlez-vous anglais ?` or `Vous parlez anglais ?` | Use `vous` with a stranger. `tu` here sounds rude. |

## Sections K-L: Production, dictation, and recall

These have no fixed answers. Score them against the following standards.

### Self-introduction

A correct introduction includes all five elements and uses:

- `Je m'appelle` for the name;
- `J'ai ___ ans` for the age, with `avoir`, not `être`;
- a nationality adjective with the correct ending for your gender (`canadien` / `canadienne`);
- `J'habite à` + the city;
- a language statement such as `Je parle un peu français.`

### Bakery script

It counts as correct only if it **opens with `Bonjour`** and **includes `s'il vous plaît`** in the request. These two habits matter more than perfect grammar.

### Dictation

Score each sentence out of three points:

1. one point for the words;
2. one point for the accents;
3. one point for the silent letters you correctly wrote but did not pronounce.

Most beginners lose points on point 3 first. That is expected, and it is exactly what this chapter is training.

### Read aloud

Judge only one thing on the first pass: **even syllable rhythm**. A learner with imperfect individual sounds but even rhythm is far easier to understand than the reverse.

### Oral recall

Model answers:

- **Final consonants:** usually silent, except `c`, `r`, `f`, `l` (CaReFuL), with `-er` infinitives as a common exception.
- **Nasal vowels:** a vowel followed by `n` or `m`, with no vowel after it and no doubled consonant; the `n` itself is not pronounced.
- **Elision vs liaison:** elision *removes* a vowel and adds an apostrophe (`j'ai`); liaison *adds* a sound by waking up a silent final consonant (`les amis`).
- **`vous`:** with strangers, older people, anyone in authority, and always with a group.
