# Chapter 4 Lessons: Numbers, Dates, Time, and Regular -ir Verbs

This chapter gives you the language you need for real-world details: prices, schedules, birthdays, addresses, appointments, and everyday choices. You will also learn the regular `-ir` verb pattern, which opens the door to many useful verbs of finishing, choosing, succeeding, and reacting.

## Learning objectives

You will learn to:
1. say and recognize French numbers from `0` through `100+`;
2. form useful larger numbers for years, prices, room numbers, and quantities;
3. use common ordinal numbers such as `premier`, `deuxième`, and `troisième`;
4. say dates naturally, including the special pattern for the first day of the month;
5. tell time with `Il est...`, including `midi`, `minuit`, quarter hours, and 24-hour time;
6. conjugate regular present-tense `-ir` verbs in all six persons;
7. combine numbers, time, and `-ir` verbs in practical speech and writing.

## Prerequisite quick check

Before beginning, make sure you can still:
- use chapter 3 time expressions such as `le lundi`, `en septembre`, and `ce soir`;
- recognize the three basic question patterns from Chapter 3;
- conjugate regular present-tense `-er` verbs;
- use `être` and `avoir` from Chapter 2 for identity, age, and simple descriptions.

That background matters because this chapter adds new vocabulary and a new verb family, but it still depends on earlier sentence structure.

:::answer
Test the old skills before you add the new ones. Answer these five without notes, then check.

1. How do you say that something happens every Monday? — `Le lundi, je travaille.` The pattern **le plus a day** means a repeated habit.
2. Ask a yes/no question from `Tu travailles demain.` — `Tu travailles demain ?` or `Est-ce que tu travailles demain ?` French does not add an English-style helper verb.
3. Conjugate `étudier` with `nous`. — `nous étudions`. Regular -er verbs use **-ons** with `nous`, and `je` becomes `j'` before a vowel sound.
4. Say that you are twenty years old. — `J'ai vingt ans.` French uses `avoir` for age, not `être`.
5. Give `être` for `elles`. — `elles sont`. This form is irregular, so you must know it by memory.

If you missed two or more, review Chapters 2 and 3 first. Numbers and regular -ir verbs will feel much harder if the subject, question pattern, or basic verb form is still unstable.
:::

## Unit 1: Cardinal numbers from 0 to 69

### Core idea

French numbers follow clear patterns, but you must know the first group very well before the larger patterns feel automatic.

### Numbers 0-20

| Number | French | Number | French |
|---|---|---|---|
| `0` | `zéro` | `11` | `onze` |
| `1` | `un` | `12` | `douze` |
| `2` | `deux` | `13` | `treize` |
| `3` | `trois` | `14` | `quatorze` |
| `4` | `quatre` | `15` | `quinze` |
| `5` | `cinq` | `16` | `seize` |
| `6` | `six` | `17` | `dix-sept` |
| `7` | `sept` | `18` | `dix-huit` |
| `8` | `huit` | `19` | `dix-neuf` |
| `9` | `neuf` | `20` | `vingt` |
| `10` | `dix` |  |  |

### Tens and building patterns through 69

| Number | French | Number | French |
|---|---|---|---|
| `21` | `vingt et un` | `40` | `quarante` |
| `22` | `vingt-deux` | `50` | `cinquante` |
| `30` | `trente` | `60` | `soixante` |
| `31` | `trente et un` | `61` | `soixante et un` |
| `35` | `trente-cinq` | `68` | `soixante-huit` |

### Core patterns to notice

- `17-19` combine `dix-` with another number: `dix-sept`, `dix-huit`, `dix-neuf`.
- `21`, `31`, `41`, `51`, and `61` use `et un`: `vingt et un`, `trente et un`.
- Most other numbers in these ranges use a hyphen: `vingt-deux`, `trente-neuf`, `cinquante-sept`.

### Examples

- `J'ai deux billets.` — I have two tickets.
- `Nous cherchons vingt-deux chaises.` — We are looking for twenty-two chairs.
- `Le café coûte cinq euros.` — The coffee costs five euros.
- `Ils habitent au trente-et-un, rue Victor-Hugo.` — They live at 31 Victor Hugo Street.
- `Elle travaille avec soixante clients.` — She works with sixty clients.

### Pronunciation note

The final consonants in `cinq`, `six`, and `dix` change depending on what follows. In fast speech, listen carefully to the number as a whole chunk rather than trying to sound out each letter separately.

### Common mistake for English speakers

Do not forget the hyphen in written compound numbers like `vingt-deux` and `quarante-neuf`. Also watch `vingt et un`: the `et` appears with `un`, but not with `deux`, `trois`, and the others.

### Sentence-building practice

Say these aloud in French, then write them:
1. 14 books
2. 21 students
3. 36 euros
4. 58 minutes
Then invent two more number phrases from your own life.

:::answer
1. `quatorze livres` — fourteen is `quatorze`; the noun is plural because there is more than one.
2. `vingt et un étudiants` — numbers ending in one use `et un` through this range. The noun is plural in normal counting.
3. `trente-six euros` — most compound numbers use a hyphen, not `et`.
4. `cinquante-huit minutes` — use a hyphen between the tens and ones.

Two personal examples:

- `deux cafés` — the noun is plural with more than one item.
- `vingt minutes` — the final **t** of `vingt` is normally silent when it stands before a consonant.
:::


## Unit 2: Numbers from 70 upward, and bigger numbers

### Core idea

French keeps building numbers rather than starting brand-new words at `70` and `90`, so pattern recognition matters.

### Key numbers from 70 to 100+

| Number | French | Number | French |
|---|---|---|---|
| `70` | `soixante-dix` | `90` | `quatre-vingt-dix` |
| `71` | `soixante et onze` | `91` | `quatre-vingt-onze` |
| `72` | `soixante-douze` | `95` | `quatre-vingt-quinze` |
| `80` | `quatre-vingts` | `100` | `cent` |
| `81` | `quatre-vingt-un` | `101` | `cent un` |
| `88` | `quatre-vingt-huit` | `200` | `deux cents` |
| `99` | `quatre-vingt-dix-neuf` | `1 000` | `mille` |

### Useful large-number reminders

| Pattern | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| `cent` + number | `cent trois` | one hundred three |
| plural `cents` when nothing follows | `deux cents` | two hundred |
| no `s` when another number follows | `deux cent cinq` | two hundred five |
| `mille` never takes `s` | `trois mille` | three thousand |
| modern years often begin with `deux mille...` | `deux mille vingt-six` | 2026 |

### Examples

- `Le voyage coûte quatre-vingt-dix euros.` — The trip costs ninety euros.
- `Nous invitons soixante-quinze personnes.` — We are inviting seventy-five people.
- `Le bureau est au cent un, avenue Centrale.` — The office is at 101 Central Avenue.
- `Mon code est mille deux.` — My code is 1002.
- `Le cours compte cent étudiants.` — The course has one hundred students.

### Pronunciation note

`Quatre-vingts` has an `s` only when it stands alone as `80`. The `s` disappears in numbers like `quatre-vingt-un` and `quatre-vingt-dix`.

### Common mistake for English speakers

English speakers often want a neat single word for `70` and `90`. Standard French instead uses `soixante-dix` and `quatre-vingt-dix`, so train yourself to hear the pattern instead of fighting it.

### Sentence-building practice

Write these in French: `71`, `84`, `99`, `105`, `230`, `2,026`. Then say them aloud without looking.

:::answer
1. `soixante et onze` — seventy-one is built as sixty and eleven, and this form keeps `et`.
2. `quatre-vingt-quatre` — after eighty, the final **s** of `quatre-vingts` disappears because another number follows.
3. `quatre-vingt-dix-neuf` — ninety-nine is literally four-twenty-ten-nine. Keep the hyphens.
4. `cent cinq` — `cent` takes no **s** because another number follows.
5. `deux cent trente` — `cent` again has no **s** because `trente` follows.
6. `deux mille vingt-six` — `mille` never takes **s**.

The common trap is writing the plural **s** from `quatre-vingts` or `deux cents` even when another number follows. It disappears before a following number.
:::

## Unit 3: Ordinal numbers, dates, and years

### Core idea

Cardinal numbers count (`un`, `deux`, `trois`), while ordinal numbers place things in order (`premier`, `deuxième`, `troisième`). Dates use both systems, but mostly cardinal numbers.

### Common ordinal numbers

| French | English |
|---|---|
| `premier / première` | first |
| `deuxième` or `second / seconde` | second |
| `troisième` | third |
| `quatrième` | fourth |
| `cinquième` | fifth |
| `sixième` | sixth |
| `septième` | seventh |
| `huitième` | eighth |
| `neuvième` | ninth |
| `dixième` | tenth |

### Dates in French

| Structure | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| `le + cardinal + month` | `le quinze septembre` | September fifteenth |
| special form for the first | `le premier novembre` | November first |
| full date with year | `le 14 juillet 2027` / `le quatorze juillet deux mille vingt-sept` | July 14, 2027 |
| `en + year` for "in" a year | `en 2027` | in 2027 |

### Examples

- `Mon anniversaire est le premier novembre.` — My birthday is November first.
- `La réunion est le vingt-trois avril.` — The meeting is April twenty-third.
- `Nous partons en deux mille vingt-sept.` — We are leaving in 2027.
- `Le troisième chapitre est plus long.` — The third chapter is longer.
- `Elle habite au deuxième étage.` — She lives on the second floor.

### Pronunciation note

For ordinary dates, only the first day of the month uses `premier`. Every other date uses a cardinal number: `le deux`, `le douze`, `le vingt-huit`.

### Common mistake for English speakers

Do not automatically use ordinal numbers for every date. French says `le cinq mai`, not the exact equivalent of "the fifth of May" in basic modern usage. The big exception is `le premier`.

### Sentence-building practice

Answer in full French phrases:
1. What is your birthday?
2. What month is a major holiday for you?
3. What floor do you live or work on?
4. What year is it now?

:::answer
Answers vary for personal details. Model full phrases:

1. Birthday: `Mon anniversaire est le premier mai.` — use `premier` only for the first day of the month.
2. Major holiday month: `Une fête importante est en décembre.` — months take `en` and stay lowercase.
3. Floor: `J'habite au deuxième étage.` — ordinal numbers place things in order, so use `deuxième` for second.
4. Current year: `Nous sommes en deux mille vingt-six.` — years use `en`; `mille` does not take **s**.

For your own birthday, use `le premier` only if the date is the first. For every other day, use the cardinal number, as in `le deux avril` or `le vingt-trois juin`.
:::


## Unit 4: Telling time

### Core idea

Use `Il est...` to tell the time. French can use either a 12-hour style with context or a 24-hour style, especially in schedules and transportation.

### Basic time patterns

| Pattern | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| `Il est + number + heure(s)` | `Il est trois heures.` | It is three o'clock. |
| `Il est + hour + minute` | `Il est neuf heures quinze.` | It is 9:15. |
| `Il est midi.` | `Il est midi.` | It is noon. |
| `Il est minuit.` | `Il est minuit.` | It is midnight. |
| quarter past | `Il est midi et quart.` | It is 12:15. |
| half past | `Il est deux heures et demie.` | It is 2:30. |
| quarter to | `Il est cinq heures moins le quart.` | It is 4:45. |

### 12-hour and 24-hour examples

- `Il est huit heures du matin.` — It is 8:00 a.m.
- `Il est deux heures de l'après-midi.` — It is 2:00 p.m.
- `Il est dix-neuf heures trente.` — It is 7:30 p.m.
- `Le cours commence à neuf heures quinze.` — The class starts at 9:15.
- `Nous finissons à midi.` — We finish at noon.
- `Le train arrive à vingt et une heures.` — The train arrives at 9:00 p.m.

### Pronunciation note

Use singular `heure` only for `une heure`; use plural `heures` for everything else. In everyday speech, `midi` and `minuit` replace longer forms.

### Common mistake for English speakers

Do not say `est` with a subject other than the fixed expression `il`. Clock time uses `Il est...`, not `C'est...` for this core pattern. Also remember that `et demie` agrees with the understood feminine noun `heure`: `deux heures et demie`.

### Sentence-building practice

Say and write these times in French:
1. 7:00
2. 9:15
3. 12:00
4. 12:15
5. 4:45
6. 19:30
Then write one sentence with `à` to say when an event starts.

:::answer
1. `Il est sept heures.` — clock time uses the fixed expression `Il est`.
2. `Il est neuf heures quinze.` — put the minutes directly after `heures`.
3. `Il est midi.` — noon has the special word `midi`, not a longer twelve-hour phrase.
4. `Il est midi et quart.` — quarter past uses `et quart`.
5. `Il est cinq heures moins le quart.` — this means a quarter to five, so it is 4:45.
6. `Il est dix-neuf heures trente.` — 24-hour time is common for schedules.

Event sentence: `Le cours commence à neuf heures quinze.` — use `à` to say at what time something starts. Do not replace `Il est` with `C'est` for basic clock time.
:::


## Unit 5: Regular -ir verbs in the present tense

### Core idea

A major group of regular `-ir` verbs follows the pattern of `finir`. These verbs add `-iss-` in the plural forms.

### Model conjugation: `finir`

| Subject | Form | English |
|---|---|---|
| `je` | `finis` | I finish |
| `tu` | `finis` | you finish |
| `il / elle / on` | `finit` | he/she/one finishes |
| `nous` | `finissons` | we finish |
| `vous` | `finissez` | you finish |
| `ils / elles` | `finissent` | they finish |

### More common regular `-ir` verbs

| Infinitive | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `choisir` | to choose |
| `réussir` | to succeed |
| `réfléchir` | to think, reflect |
| `grandir` | to grow |
| `obéir` | to obey |
| `rougir` | to blush, turn red |

### Examples

- `Je finis tôt le jeudi.` — I finish early on Thursdays.
- `Nous choisissons deux desserts pour huit euros.` — We choose two desserts for eight euros.
- `Elle réussit souvent ses examens.` — She often succeeds on her exams.
- `Vous réfléchissez trop longtemps.` — You think for too long.
- `Ils grandissent vite.` — They are growing quickly.
- `On obéit aux règles du musée.` — People obey the museum rules.

### Pronunciation note

The plural forms are easy to hear because of `-issons`, `-issez`, and `-issent`. Say them aloud as a family: `finissons`, `finissez`, `finissent`.

### Common mistake for English speakers

Not every verb ending in `-ir` is regular like `finir`. Verbs such as `partir` and `dormir` follow a different pattern, so do not assume every `-ir` verb works the same way.

### Sentence-building practice

Conjugate `finir`, `choisir`, and `réussir` for all six persons. Then write three original sentences using three different verbs from the table.

:::answer
All three verbs are regular in this pattern: remove **-ir**, then add **-is**, **-is**, **-it**, **-issons**, **-issez**, **-issent**.

| Subject | `finir` | `choisir` | `réussir` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `je` | `je finis` | `je choisis` | `je réussis` |
| `tu` | `tu finis` | `tu choisis` | `tu réussis` |
| `il / elle / on` | `il finit` | `elle choisit` | `on réussit` |
| `nous` | `nous finissons` | `nous choisissons` | `nous réussissons` |
| `vous` | `vous finissez` | `vous choisissez` | `vous réussissez` |
| `ils / elles` | `ils finissent` | `elles choisissent` | `ils réussissent` |

Original sentence models:

- `Je finis le travail à dix-huit heures.` — `je` takes `finis`.
- `Nous choisissons deux desserts.` — `nous` takes the audible **-issons** form.
- `Elle réussit son examen en mai.` — `elle` takes `réussit`, ending in silent **-t**.

The main trap is forgetting the **-iss-** in the plural forms.
:::


## Unit 6: Combining numbers, time, and -ir verbs

### Core idea

This chapter becomes truly useful when you combine its pieces: a time, a quantity, and a regular `-ir` verb inside one sentence.

### Examples in practical contexts

- `Le cours commence à neuf heures quinze, et nous finissons à midi.` — The class starts at 9:15, and we finish at noon.
- `Mon anniversaire est le premier novembre, alors je choisis souvent un bon restaurant.` — My birthday is November first, so I often choose a good restaurant.
- `Les magasins finissent leur journée à dix-neuf heures.` — The stores finish their day at 7 p.m.
- `Vous réfléchissez pendant cinq minutes avant de répondre.` — You think for five minutes before answering.
- `Nous réussissons mieux quand nous étudions le matin.` — We do better when we study in the morning.

### Pronunciation or listening note

In a long sentence, the quantity and time expressions often carry the most audible stress. Practice reading them as smooth groups: `à neuf heures quinze`, `pour huit euros`, `le premier novembre`.

### Common mistake for English speakers

Do not let the number expression distract you from the verb ending. In `nous choisissons deux desserts`, the subject `nous` still controls the verb, no matter how long the object phrase becomes.

### Sentence-building practice

Build one sentence from each set:
1. `nous / choisir / trois cafés / à dix heures`
2. `je / finir / le travail / à dix-huit heures`
3. `elle / réussir / l'examen / en mai`
4. `vous / réfléchir / cinq minutes / avant la réponse`

:::answer
1. `Nous choisissons trois cafés à dix heures.` — `nous` requires `choisissons`; the object phrase does not affect the verb ending.
2. `Je finis le travail à dix-huit heures.` — `je` takes `finis`, and `à` introduces the clock time.
3. `Elle réussit l'examen en mai.` — `elle` takes `réussit`; use `en` before a month.
4. `Vous réfléchissez cinq minutes avant la réponse.` — `vous` takes `réfléchissez`. The time quantity `cinq minutes` does not change the verb.

Check subject before details. Long number and time phrases can distract you, but the subject still controls the conjugated verb.
:::

## Mini-dialogue: choosing dessert after class

**Aïcha :** `Il est quelle heure ?` — What time is it?

**Lucas :** `Il est midi vingt, et le cours finit enfin.` — It is 12:20, and the class is finally ending.

**Aïcha :** `Tu choisis quoi pour le déjeuner ?` — What are you choosing for lunch?

**Lucas :** `Je choisis une soupe et deux tartes pour onze euros.` — I am choosing a soup and two tarts for eleven euros.

**Aïcha :** `Tu réussis toujours à manger vite avant le travail ?` — Do you always manage to eat quickly before work?

**Lucas :** `Pas toujours, mais aujourd'hui je finis tôt.` — Not always, but today I finish early.

**Aïcha :** `Ton anniversaire, c'est bien le premier novembre ?` — Your birthday really is November first, right?

**Lucas :** `Oui, et cette année, je choisis un grand dîner avec huit amis.` — Yes, and this year I am choosing a big dinner with eight friends.

### Guided analysis

1. Find two regular `-ir` verbs and identify their subjects.
2. List every number expression.
3. Find one clock time and one date.
4. Rewrite Lucas's second line with `nous` instead of `je`.
5. Change `le premier novembre` to a different date of your choice.

:::answer
1. Two regular -ir verbs in the dialogue include `finit` with subject `le cours`, `choisis` with subjects `tu` and `je`, `réussis` with subject `tu`, and `finis` with subject `je`. `Je choisis` and `tu choisis` look the same because both singular forms end in **-is**.
2. Number expressions: `midi vingt`, `deux tartes`, `onze euros`, `le premier novembre`, and `huit amis`. `Cette année` is a time expression, but it is not a number expression.
3. Clock time: `Il est midi vingt`. Date: `le premier novembre`. The date uses `premier` because it is the first day of the month.
4. `Nous choisissons une soupe et deux tartes pour onze euros.` — `nous` requires the regular -ir ending **-issons**.
5. A different date: `le quinze septembre`. Use a cardinal number for ordinary dates; only the first uses `premier`.
:::


## Connected reading: un samedi bien organisé

`Le samedi, je commence lentement, mais à dix heures je finis déjà mes courses du marché. J'achète souvent trois légumes, deux fromages et un dessert pour environ vingt euros. À midi, je déjeune avec mon frère, puis nous choisissons un film pour l'après-midi. Si le temps est mauvais, nous réfléchissons à la maison; si le soleil revient, nous sortons. Mon père téléphone parfois vers dix-sept heures parce qu'il veut savoir si je réussis ma recette. Le soir, tout le monde arrive à la même heure, et le dîner commence vers vingt heures.`

— On Saturdays, I start slowly, but by ten o'clock I have already finished my market shopping. I often buy three vegetables, two cheeses, and a dessert for about twenty euros. At noon, I have lunch with my brother, then we choose a movie for the afternoon. If the weather is bad, we think things over at home; if the sun comes back, we go out. My father sometimes calls around five o'clock because he wants to know whether I succeed with my recipe. In the evening, everyone arrives at the same time, and dinner starts around eight o'clock.

### Reading practice

1. List every number or time expression in the paragraph.
2. Find four present-tense verbs and sort them into `-er` or regular `-ir`.
3. Rewrite the second sentence with different quantities and a different price.
4. Find the sentence that contains a yes/no idea indirectly and explain it.
5. Write three new schedule sentences about your own Saturday.

:::answer
1. Number and time expressions: `Le samedi`, `à dix heures`, `trois légumes`, `deux fromages`, `un dessert`, `vingt euros`, `À midi`, `l'après-midi`, `vers dix-sept heures`, `Le soir`, `à la même heure`, and `vers vingt heures`.
2. Present-tense verbs sorted by type: regular -er verbs include `commence`, `achète`, `déjeune`, `téléphone`, `arrive`, and `commence`; regular -ir verbs include `finis`, `choisissons`, `réfléchissons`, and `réussis`. The verb `sortons` is an -ir verb, but it is not regular like `finir`.
3. Rewritten second sentence: `J'achète souvent quatre pommes, trois fromages et deux desserts pour environ trente euros.` — The quantities and price changed, but `j'achète` still agrees with `je`.
4. The indirect yes/no idea is in `il veut savoir si je réussis ma recette`. The word `si` means **whether** here; it embeds the question “Do I succeed with my recipe?” without using a question mark.
5. Answers vary. Models: `Le samedi, je finis mes courses à onze heures.` `À midi, je choisis un sandwich.` `Le soir, je réussis souvent une recette simple.` Check that each schedule sentence has a time expression and that every regular -ir verb matches its subject.
:::


## Common mistakes to avoid

1. Forgetting the hyphens in compound numbers such as `quarante-deux` and `quatre-vingt-dix-neuf`.
2. Writing `quatre-vingts-un` with an `s`; the `s` disappears when another number follows.
3. Using ordinal numbers for every date instead of only `le premier`.
4. Forgetting `Il est...` for clock time.
5. Mixing up `midi` and `minuit`.
6. Assuming every `-ir` verb is regular like `finir`.
7. Forgetting the plural `-iss-` pattern in `nous`, `vous`, and `ils/elles` forms.

## Final production assignment

Write a paragraph of 8-12 sentences about a real or imagined day that includes appointments, prices, and choices. Include:
- at least five number expressions;
- one full date and one year;
- three clock times, including either `midi` or `minuit`;
- at least four regular present-tense `-ir` verbs;
- one question about time or quantity.

After writing:
1. circle every number, date, and time phrase;
2. underline each regular `-ir` verb;
3. check whether each number is spelled and hyphenated correctly;
4. make sure your date uses `le premier` only when needed;
5. read the paragraph aloud twice;
6. rewrite any sentence you corrected from memory.

:::answer
Answers vary, so compare your paragraph to the requirements. Here is one model that satisfies the brief:

`Le quinze avril deux mille vingt-six, je commence une journée très organisée. À huit heures, je choisis deux cafés pour six euros. À neuf heures trente, je finis un dossier important. À midi, nous choisissons trois sandwichs pour vingt-quatre euros. À quatorze heures, ma sœur réussit son examen. Le soir, je réfléchis pendant quinze minutes avant de choisir un dessert. Nous finissons la journée à dix-neuf heures. Combien de cafés est-ce que tu choisis le matin ?`

How it satisfies the brief: number expressions include `quinze`, `deux mille vingt-six`, `huit heures`, `deux cafés`, `six euros`, `neuf heures trente`, `trois sandwichs`, `vingt-quatre euros`, `quatorze heures`, `quinze minutes`, and `dix-neuf heures`; the full date is `Le quinze avril deux mille vingt-six`; the clock times are `huit heures`, `neuf heures trente`, `midi`, `quatorze heures`, and `dix-neuf heures`; regular -ir verbs include `choisis`, `finis`, `choisissons`, `réussit`, `réfléchis`, and `finissons`; the final sentence asks about quantity.

Mark yourself wrong if any of these appear:
- an ordinal date such as **deuxième avril** for an ordinary date;
- missing hyphens in numbers such as `vingt-quatre`;
- `C'est` instead of `Il est` for basic clock time;
- a plural regular -ir form without **-iss-**, such as **nous choisons**;
- a question without the required space before `?`.
:::
