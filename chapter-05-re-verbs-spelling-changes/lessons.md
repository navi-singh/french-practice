# Chapter 5 Lessons: Regular -re Verbs, Depuis, and Spelling-Change Verbs

This chapter adds two important ideas at the same time: a new regular verb family, the regular `-re` verbs, and a group of familiar-looking `-er` verbs whose spelling changes in some forms. You will also learn how French uses `depuis` with the present tense to talk about actions and situations that started in the past and continue now.

## Learning objectives

You will learn to:
1. conjugate regular present-tense `-re` verbs in all six persons;
2. use `depuis` with the present tense to express an ongoing action or state;
3. conjugate `-cer` and `-ger` verbs such as `commencer` and `manger`;
4. conjugate accent-changing verbs such as `acheter` and `préférer`;
5. conjugate double-consonant verbs such as `appeler` and `jeter`;
6. conjugate `y`-changing verbs such as `envoyer` and `essayer`;
7. combine these patterns in realistic everyday sentences.

## Prerequisite quick check

Before beginning, make sure you can still:
- conjugate regular `-er` verbs from Chapter 3;
- use chapter 4 numbers and time expressions such as `midi`, `à dix-neuf heures`, and `depuis trois ans`-style time phrases in English meaning;
- form yes/no and information questions;
- recognize that the subject controls the verb ending, even in longer sentences.

You will need that foundation because the new spellings in this chapter only make sense if the regular `-er` pattern is already solid.

:::answer
Test yourself before starting. Answer these five without notes.

1. Conjugate `parler` with `nous`. — `nous parlons`. Regular -er verbs take **-ons** with `nous`.
2. Ask a yes/no question from `Vous travaillez ce soir.` — `Vous travaillez ce soir ?` or `Est-ce que vous travaillez ce soir ?` French does not add a separate word for English **do**.
3. Write a clock-time phrase for 7 p.m. — `à dix-neuf heures`. Chapter 4 schedule style often uses the 24-hour clock.
4. In `Les étudiants regardent la carte`, what controls `regardent`? — `Les étudiants`. The subject controls the verb ending, not the object `la carte`.
5. What does a phrase like “for three years and still now” require in French once you learn `depuis`? — a present-tense verb, as in `J'habite ici depuis trois ans`.

If you missed two or more, review the earlier verb and time chapters first. This chapter adds spelling changes; it does not replace the basic subject-verb system.
:::

## Unit 1: Regular -re verbs in the present tense

### Core idea

Regular `-re` verbs form the present tense by removing `-re` and adding a new set of endings.

### Model conjugation: `attendre`

| Subject | Form | English |
|---|---|---|
| `je` | `attends` | I wait |
| `tu` | `attends` | you wait |
| `il / elle / on` | `attend` | he/she/one waits |
| `nous` | `attendons` | we wait |
| `vous` | `attendez` | you wait |
| `ils / elles` | `attendent` | they wait |

### The ending pattern

| Subject | Ending | Example with `vendre` |
|---|---|---|
| `je` | `-s` | `je vends` |
| `tu` | `-s` | `tu vends` |
| `il / elle / on` | no ending | `il vend` |
| `nous` | `-ons` | `nous vendons` |
| `vous` | `-ez` | `vous vendez` |
| `ils / elles` | `-ent` | `ils vendent` |

### More common regular `-re` verbs

| Infinitive | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `attendre` | to wait (for) |
| `vendre` | to sell |
| `répondre` | to answer |
| `perdre` | to lose |
| `entendre` | to hear |
| `descendre` | to go down, descend |

### Examples

- `J'attends mon ami devant le magasin.` — I am waiting for my friend in front of the store.
- `Nous vendons des livres le samedi.` — We sell books on Saturdays.
- `Elle répond vite à ma question.` — She answers my question quickly.
- `Vous perdez souvent vos clés ?` — Do you often lose your keys?
- `Ils entendent la musique dans la rue.` — They hear the music in the street.

### Pronunciation note

In many regular `-re` verbs, the singular forms sound very close to one another, just as with regular `-er` verbs. The final written consonants are not always clearly heard, while `nous` and `vous` forms are easier to distinguish.

### Common mistake for English speakers

Do not add an extra written ending in the `il/elle/on` form. It is `il attend`, not `*il attends`.

### Sentence-building practice

Conjugate `attendre`, `répondre`, and `perdre` for all six persons. Then write three original sentences using three different subjects.

:::answer
All three verbs are regular -re verbs: remove the final -re, then add -s, -s, no ending, -ons, -ez, -ent.

| Subject | `attendre` | `répondre` | `perdre` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `je` | `j'attends` | `je réponds` | `je perds` |
| `tu` | `tu attends` | `tu réponds` | `tu perds` |
| `il / elle / on` | `il attend` | `elle répond` | `on perd` |
| `nous` | `nous attendons` | `nous répondons` | `nous perdons` |
| `vous` | `vous attendez` | `vous répondez` | `vous perdez` |
| `ils / elles` | `ils attendent` | `elles répondent` | `ils perdent` |

The trap is the singular spelling: `il attend`, `elle répond`, and `on perd` have no added ending after the stem.

Sample original sentences:

- `J'attends le bus devant la gare.` — `je` takes -s, and the final written s is silent.
- `Nous répondons au professeur après le cours.` — `nous` takes -ons, so this form is easy to hear.
- `Elles perdent souvent leurs clés.` — -ent is written for plural agreement but is silent.
:::


## Unit 2: `depuis` with the present tense

### Core idea

Use `depuis` + a point in time or a duration with the **present tense** when something started in the past and is still true now.

### Main structures

| Structure | Example | Natural English |
|---|---|---|
| present + `depuis` + duration | `J'habite ici depuis deux ans.` | I have lived here for two years. |
| present + `depuis` + starting point | `Elle attend depuis midi.` | She has been waiting since noon. |
| `Depuis quand` + present? | `Depuis quand travaillez-vous ici ?` | How long have you worked here? |

### Examples

- `Nous habitons ici depuis trois ans.` — We have lived here for three years.
- `J'étudie le français depuis septembre.` — I have been studying French since September.
- `Il attend le bus depuis vingt minutes.` — He has been waiting for the bus for twenty minutes.
- `Vous travaillez ensemble depuis quand ?` — Since when have you been working together?
- `Elle préfère ce café depuis son arrivée en ville.` — She has preferred this café since her arrival in the city.

### Pronunciation note

Treat `depuis` as one common time marker. In fast speech, you will hear it very often right after the verb phrase: `j'habite ici depuis...`, `on attend depuis...`.

### Common mistake for English speakers

English usually uses the present perfect here: "I have lived," "she has been waiting." French usually keeps the ordinary present tense: `J'habite`, `elle attend`.

### Sentence-building practice

Complete the sentences with your own ideas:
1. `J'étudie le français depuis ___ .`
2. `Nous habitons ici depuis ___ .`
3. `Il attend depuis ___ .`
4. `Depuis quand est-ce que vous ___ ?`
Then ask and answer one `Depuis quand... ?` question aloud.

:::answer
Model completions — yours can use different time phrases.

1. `J'étudie le français depuis septembre.` — The studying began in September and still continues, so French keeps the present tense.
2. `Nous habitons ici depuis trois ans.` — English says *have lived*, but French says `habitons` because the situation is still true now.
3. `Il attend depuis midi.` — `depuis` can introduce a starting point such as `midi`.
4. `Depuis quand est-ce que vous travaillez ici ?` — `depuis quand` asks when the continuing action began; the verb stays present.

Question and answer example:

- `Depuis quand est-ce que tu apprends le français ?`
- `J'apprends le français depuis deux mois.`

Mark yourself wrong if you used a past tense for an action that is still continuing, or if your answer after `depuis` does not give either a duration or a starting point.
:::


## Unit 3: `-cer` and `-ger` verbs to keep the soft sound

### Core idea

Some regular `-er` verbs change spelling in the `nous` form so that the pronunciation stays natural.

### `commencer`: add a cedilla before `o`

| Subject | Form |
|---|---|
| `je` | `commence` |
| `tu` | `commences` |
| `il / elle / on` | `commence` |
| `nous` | `commençons` |
| `vous` | `commencez` |
| `ils / elles` | `commencent` |

### `manger`: add `e` before `ons`

| Subject | Form |
|---|---|
| `je` | `mange` |
| `tu` | `manges` |
| `il / elle / on` | `mange` |
| `nous` | `mangeons` |
| `vous` | `mangez` |
| `ils / elles` | `mangent` |

### Why the spelling changes

| Verb type | Change | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `-cer` | `c` -> `ç` before `o` | keep the soft `s` sound |
| `-ger` | add `e` before `o` | keep the soft `j` sound |

### Examples

- `Nous commençons le cours à huit heures.` — We start class at eight o'clock.
- `Je commence toujours par les exercices faciles.` — I always start with the easy exercises.
- `Nous mangeons au restaurant avant de commencer les achats.` — We eat at the restaurant before starting the shopping.
- `Elle mange tôt le soir.` — She eats early in the evening.
- `Ils commencent en septembre.` — They start in September.

### Pronunciation note

These are spelling changes, not new endings. The verbs are still regular `-er` verbs; the extra mark simply protects the original sound.

### Common mistake for English speakers

Learners often forget that the change usually appears only where `o` would otherwise harden the sound. The most important form to memorize first is `nous commençons` and `nous mangeons`.

### Sentence-building practice

Conjugate `commencer` and `manger` for all six persons. Then rewrite these with `nous`: `Je commence le travail.` / `Tu manges ici.` / `Elle commence tôt.`

:::answer
These are still regular -er verbs, but the `nous` spelling changes to protect the sound.

| Subject | `commencer` | `manger` |
|---|---|---|
| `je` | `je commence` | `je mange` |
| `tu` | `tu commences` | `tu manges` |
| `il / elle / on` | `il commence` | `elle mange` |
| `nous` | `nous commençons` | `nous mangeons` |
| `vous` | `vous commencez` | `vous mangez` |
| `ils / elles` | `ils commencent` | `elles mangent` |

Rewrites with `nous`:

1. `Nous commençons le travail.` — `commencer` needs ç before -ons so the sound stays soft.
2. `Nous mangeons ici.` — `manger` adds e before -ons so the g stays soft.
3. `Nous commençons tôt.` — The subject changed, so the verb must change too; `nous commence` is not possible.
:::


## Unit 4: Accent-changing verbs such as `acheter` and `préférer`

### Core idea

Some verbs change the vowel in the stem so that the stressed syllable sounds natural. The change usually appears in all forms **except** `nous` and `vous`.

### Model 1: `acheter` (`e` -> `è`)

| Subject | Form |
|---|---|
| `je` | `achète` |
| `tu` | `achètes` |
| `il / elle / on` | `achète` |
| `nous` | `achetons` |
| `vous` | `achetez` |
| `ils / elles` | `achètent` |

### Model 2: `préférer` (`é` -> `è`)

| Subject | Form |
|---|---|
| `je` | `préfère` |
| `tu` | `préfères` |
| `il / elle / on` | `préfère` |
| `nous` | `préférons` |
| `vous` | `préférez` |
| `ils / elles` | `préfèrent` |

### Examples

- `J'achète du pain le matin.` — I buy bread in the morning.
- `Elle achète une chemise et essaie une veste.` — She buys a shirt and tries on a jacket.
- `Nous achetons nos billets en ligne.` — We buy our tickets online.
- `Je préfère la chemise bleue.` — I prefer the blue shirt.
- `Nous préférons le train.` — We prefer the train.
- `Ils préfèrent étudier le soir.` — They prefer to study in the evening.

### Pronunciation note

Say the pairs aloud: `j'achète / nous achetons`, `je préfère / nous préférons`. The sound changes are easier to feel than to memorize abstractly.

### Common mistake for English speakers

Do not spread the accented form across all six persons. `Nous achetons` and `vous préférez` keep the simpler stem.

### Sentence-building practice

Conjugate `acheter` and `préférer` for all six persons. Then replace the subject in these sentences: `J'achète un livre.` / `Elle préfère le café.` / `Ils achètent des fruits.`

:::answer
The changed stem appears in `je`, `tu`, `il / elle / on`, and `ils / elles`, but not in `nous` or `vous`.

| Subject | `acheter` | `préférer` |
|---|---|---|
| `je` | `j'achète` | `je préfère` |
| `tu` | `tu achètes` | `tu préfères` |
| `il / elle / on` | `elle achète` | `il préfère` |
| `nous` | `nous achetons` | `nous préférons` |
| `vous` | `vous achetez` | `vous préférez` |
| `ils / elles` | `ils achètent` | `elles préfèrent` |

Model subject replacements:

1. `Nous achetons un livre.` — With `nous`, the stem returns to `achet-`; do not write the accent here.
2. `Vous préférez le café.` — With `vous`, keep the é in the ending pattern: `préférez`.
3. `J'achète des fruits.` — `je` becomes `j'` before a vowel sound, and the stem changes to `achèt-`.
:::


## Unit 5: Double-consonant verbs and `y`-changing verbs

### Core idea

A few high-frequency `-er` verbs keep their pronunciation by doubling a consonant or changing `y` to `i` in certain forms.

### Model 1: `appeler` (double consonant)

| Subject | Form |
|---|---|
| `je` | `appelle` |
| `tu` | `appelles` |
| `il / elle / on` | `appelle` |
| `nous` | `appelons` |
| `vous` | `appelez` |
| `ils / elles` | `appellent` |

`Jeter` follows the same idea: `je jette`, `nous jetons`.

### Model 2: `essayer` (`y` -> `i` in the common spelling shown here)

| Subject | Form |
|---|---|
| `je` | `essaie` |
| `tu` | `essaies` |
| `il / elle / on` | `essaie` |
| `nous` | `essayons` |
| `vous` | `essayez` |
| `ils / elles` | `essaient` |

### Another common `y` verb: `envoyer`

| Subject | Form |
|---|---|
| `je` | `envoie` |
| `tu` | `envoies` |
| `il / elle / on` | `envoie` |
| `nous` | `envoyons` |
| `vous` | `envoyez` |
| `ils / elles` | `envoient` |

### Examples

- `J'appelle ma sœur ce soir.` — I am calling my sister this evening.
- `Nous appelons un taxi.` — We call a taxi.
- `Elle jette le ticket.` — She throws away the ticket.
- `J'essaie une veste noire.` — I am trying on a black jacket.
- `Nous essayons plusieurs idées.` — We are trying several ideas.
- `J'envoie un message après le cours.` — I send a message after class.
- `Nous envoyons les documents demain.` — We send the documents tomorrow.

### Pronunciation note

The doubled consonant or changed vowel helps keep the stressed sound consistent. Compare `j'appelle` with `nous appelons`, and `j'essaie` with `nous essayons`.

### Common mistake for English speakers

Do not assume the spelling change appears everywhere. As with `acheter` and `préférer`, the simplest stem usually returns in `nous` and `vous`.

### Sentence-building practice

Conjugate `appeler`, `essayer`, and `envoyer` for all six persons. Then write one sentence with each verb using a different subject.

:::answer
These verbs also change mainly outside the `nous` and `vous` forms.

| Subject | `appeler` | `essayer` | `envoyer` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `je` | `j'appelle` | `j'essaie` | `j'envoie` |
| `tu` | `tu appelles` | `tu essaies` | `tu envoies` |
| `il / elle / on` | `elle appelle` | `il essaie` | `on envoie` |
| `nous` | `nous appelons` | `nous essayons` | `nous envoyons` |
| `vous` | `vous appelez` | `vous essayez` | `vous envoyez` |
| `ils / elles` | `ils appellent` | `elles essaient` | `ils envoient` |

Sample sentences:

- `J'appelle ma mère ce soir.` — `appeler` doubles the l in the `je` form.
- `Nous essayons une autre solution.` — With `nous`, keep y in `essayons`.
- `Ils envoient un message au professeur.` — The y changes to i in `ils envoient`, and -ent is silent.
:::


## Unit 6: Putting the chapter patterns together

### Core idea

This chapter becomes useful when you can move quickly from one pattern to another without losing track of the subject.

### Examples in practical situations

- `J'attends mon ami devant le magasin depuis midi.` — I have been waiting for my friend in front of the store since noon.
- `Nous habitons ici depuis trois ans.` — We have lived here for three years.
- `Elle achète une chemise et essaie une veste.` — She buys a shirt and tries on a jacket.
- `Nous mangeons au restaurant avant de commencer les achats.` — We eat at the restaurant before starting the shopping.
- `Je préfère ce quartier, mais j'appelle souvent mes parents parce qu'ils habitent loin.` — I prefer this neighborhood, but I often call my parents because they live far away.
- `Vous répondez vite, puis vous envoyez le message.` — You answer quickly, then you send the message.

### Pronunciation or listening note

When several verbs appear in the same sentence, pause mentally at each subject change. That habit keeps endings accurate even when the sentence becomes long.

### Common mistake for English speakers

Do not let the more unusual spelling changes distract you from the basic sentence frame. First identify the subject, then choose the verb family, then apply the spelling change only if needed.

### Sentence-building practice

Build one sentence from each set:
1. `je / attendre / le bus / depuis vingt minutes`
2. `nous / commencer / la réunion / à neuf heures`
3. `elle / préférer / cette robe / et / essayer / l'autre`
4. `vous / répondre / au professeur / puis / envoyer / le devoir`

:::answer
1. `J'attends le bus depuis vingt minutes.` — `attendre` is a regular -re verb, and `depuis` takes the present because the waiting is still happening.
2. `Nous commençons la réunion à neuf heures.` — The `nous` form of `commencer` needs ç before -ons.
3. `Elle préfère cette robe et essaie l'autre.` — Both verbs agree with `elle`; `préférer` changes é to è, and `essayer` changes y to i in this common spelling.
4. `Vous répondez au professeur, puis vous envoyez le devoir.` — `répondre` takes the -ez ending with `vous`; `envoyer` keeps y in the `vous` form.

If your sentence has two verbs, check both. A correct first verb does not protect the second one from agreement or spelling-change errors.
:::


## Cumulative sentence-usage practice

Read each sentence aloud twice, cover it, and write it from memory.

1. `J'attends mon ami devant le magasin.` — I am waiting for my friend in front of the store.
2. `Nous habitons ici depuis trois ans.` — We have lived here for three years.
3. `Elle achète une chemise et essaie une veste.` — She buys a shirt and tries on a jacket.
4. `Nous mangeons au restaurant avant de commencer les achats.` — We eat at the restaurant before starting the shopping.
5. `Je préfère ce livre, mais j'appelle ma sœur pour demander son avis.` — I prefer this book, but I call my sister to ask for her opinion.
6. `Vous répondez depuis votre bureau ou vous envoyez un message plus tard ?` — Are you answering from your office, or are you sending a message later?

:::answer
After writing the six sentences from memory, check for these traps.

- **Silent singular endings.** `J'attends` has a written **s**, but you do not hear it clearly.
- **Present tense with ongoing time.** `Nous habitons ici depuis trois ans` uses the present because the living situation still continues.
- **Spelling-change stems.** Sentence 3 has `achète` and `essaie`; sentence 5 has `préfère` and `appelle`.
- **Nous protection spellings.** Sentence 4 has `mangeons`, with an extra **e** to keep the soft sound before **-ons**.
- **Question spacing and accents.** Sentence 6 needs `répondez`, `envoyez`, and a space before `?`.

If you got the sound right but missed an accent or stem change, rewrite that exact form three times and then use it in a new sentence.
:::

## Mini-dialogue: shopping and waiting

**Mina :** `Tu attends depuis longtemps ?` — Have you been waiting a long time?

**Paul :** `Oui, j'attends depuis midi, mais je commence juste à me détendre.` — Yes, I have been waiting since noon, but I am just starting to relax.

**Mina :** `Pourquoi ?` — Why?

**Paul :** `Parce que ma sœur essaie trois vestes et elle préfère toujours une autre couleur.` — Because my sister is trying on three jackets, and she always prefers another color.

**Mina :** `Et toi, tu achètes quelque chose ?` — And you, are you buying anything?

**Paul :** `Oui, j'achète un cadeau, puis j'appelle mon frère pour demander son avis.` — Yes, I am buying a gift, then I am calling my brother to ask for his opinion.

**Mina :** `Vous mangez ici après les achats ?` — Are you eating here after the shopping?

**Paul :** `Oui, nous mangeons au café d'en face avant d'envoyer les photos à la famille.` — Yes, we are eating at the café across the street before sending the photos to the family.

3. Find one regular `-re` verb and identify its subject.
4. Rewrite `elle préfère toujours une autre couleur` with `nous`.
5. Change the dialogue so that the waiting starts `depuis dix minutes` instead of `depuis midi`.

:::answer
1. The dialogue uses continuing-time `depuis` in `Tu attends depuis longtemps ?` and `j'attends depuis midi`. The waiting started earlier and is still happening, so French keeps `attends` in the present tense.
2. Three spelling-change verbs: `commence` belongs to the -cer family, though this form does not need ç; `essaie` changes y to i; `préfère` changes é to è; `achètes` and `achète` change e to è; `appelle` doubles **l**; `mangeons` adds **e** before **-ons**.
3. A regular -re verb is `attends`. Its subject is `tu` in `Tu attends depuis longtemps ?` and `je` in `j'attends depuis midi`.
4. `Nous préférons toujours une autre couleur.` — With `nous`, `préférer` keeps the simpler stem and takes **-ons**.
5. Revised waiting lines: `Tu attends depuis dix minutes ?` `Oui, j'attends depuis dix minutes, mais je commence juste à me détendre.` The time phrase changes, but the present tense stays because the waiting continues.
:::


## Connected reading: une soirée bien remplie

`Le jeudi, je descends du bus vers dix-huit heures et j'entre dans un petit centre commercial près de chez moi. J'attends parfois quelques minutes devant la boulangerie parce que beaucoup de gens achètent du pain à cette heure-là. Ensuite, je regarde les vitrines, j'essaie rarement des vêtements, mais je préfère comparer les prix. Si je trouve un cadeau intéressant, j'appelle ma sœur et je lui envoie une photo. Nous habitons dans la même ville depuis peu, alors nous répondons vite l'un à l'autre et nous mangeons souvent ensemble après les courses.`

— On Thursdays, I get off the bus around six o'clock and enter a small shopping center near my home. I sometimes wait a few minutes in front of the bakery because many people buy bread at that time. Then I look at the shop windows; I rarely try on clothes, but I prefer to compare prices. If I find an interesting gift, I call my sister and send her a photo. We have lived in the same city only a short time, so we answer each other quickly and often eat together after shopping.

### Reading practice

1. Find all the regular `-re` verbs in the paragraph.
2. Find all the spelling-change `-er` verbs and sort them by pattern.
3. Explain why `habitons` appears with `depuis peu`.
4. Rewrite the last sentence in the singular, changing `nous` to `je` wherever it still makes sense.
5. Write three new sentences about your own after-work routine using the reading as a model.

:::answer
1. Regular -re verbs in the paragraph: `descends` from `descendre`, `attends` from `attendre`, and `répondons` from `répondre`. Each follows the regular -re pattern.
2. Spelling-change -er verbs: `achètent` from `acheter` changes e to è; `essaie` from `essayer` changes y to i; `préfère` from `préférer` changes é to è; `appelle` from `appeler` doubles **l**; `envoie` from `envoyer` changes y to i; `mangeons` from `manger` adds **e** before **-ons**.
3. `Nous habitons dans la même ville depuis peu` uses the present tense because the living situation started recently and still continues now.
4. `J'habite dans la même ville depuis peu, alors je réponds vite et je mange souvent avec elle après les courses.` Three things change. `répondons` becomes `réponds`, where the `d` is written but silent. `mangeons` loses its extra `e` and becomes `mange`, because the `e` was only ever there to keep the `g` soft before `-ons`. And `l'un à l'autre` has to go: it means *to each other*, which needs two people, so the singular takes `vite` alone or names the other person.
5. Answers vary. Models: `Après le travail, j'attends le bus devant le bureau.` `J'achète parfois du pain depuis peu.` `Je préfère rentrer tôt, puis j'envoie un message à ma famille.` Check that each verb ending matches its subject.
:::


## Common mistakes to avoid

1. Writing `il attends` instead of `il attend`.
2. Using an English-style past tense after `depuis` for an action that is still continuing.
3. Forgetting the cedilla in `nous commençons`.
4. Forgetting the extra `e` in `nous mangeons`.
5. Extending the changed stem to `nous` and `vous` in verbs like `acheter`, `préférer`, and `appeler`.
6. Forgetting that `essayer` and `envoyer` change `y` in the forms shown here: `j'essaie`, `j'envoie`.
7. Focusing on spelling so much that you lose the basic subject-verb agreement.

## Chapter reference sheet

### Regular `-re` endings

| Pronoun | Ending | Example with `répondre` |
|---|---|---|
| `je` | `-s` | `je réponds` |
| `tu` | `-s` | `tu réponds` |
| `il / elle / on` | no ending | `elle répond` |
| `nous` | `-ons` | `nous répondons` |
| `vous` | `-ez` | `vous répondez` |
| `ils / elles` | `-ent` | `ils répondent` |

### `depuis` reminder

| Meaning | French pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| for two years and still true now | present + `depuis deux ans` | `J'habite ici depuis deux ans.` |
| since noon and still happening | present + `depuis midi` | `Elle attend depuis midi.` |
| how long? | `Depuis quand... ?` | `Depuis quand travaillez-vous ici ?` |

### Spelling-change snapshot

| Pattern | Model | Key form |
|---|---|---|
| `-cer` | `commencer` | `nous commençons` |
| `-ger` | `manger` | `nous mangeons` |
| `e` -> `è` | `acheter` | `j'achète / nous achetons` |
| `é` -> `è` | `préférer` | `je préfère / nous préférons` |
| double consonant | `appeler` | `j'appelle / nous appelons` |
| `y` -> `i` | `essayer`, `envoyer` | `j'essaie`, `j'envoie` |

## Final production assignment

Write a paragraph of 8-12 sentences about a shopping trip, daily routine, or after-work routine. Include:
- at least three regular present-tense `-re` verbs;
- one sentence with `depuis` showing an action or situation that still continues;
- one `-cer` or `-ger` verb;
- one accent-changing verb (`acheter` or `préférer` type);
- one double-consonant or `y`-changing verb;
- one question you could realistically ask another person.

After writing:
1. circle every conjugated verb;
2. label each verb family (`-re`, regular `-er`, or spelling-change `-er`);
3. underline the `depuis` phrase;
4. check whether any spelling change should disappear in the `nous` or `vous` form;
5. read the paragraph aloud twice;
6. rewrite any sentence you corrected from memory.

:::answer
Answers vary. Here is one model paragraph that meets every requirement:

`Le jeudi, j'attends ma sœur devant le magasin. Elle descend du bus vers dix-huit heures et répond vite à mon message. Nous habitons dans le même quartier depuis deux ans. Nous commençons les courses au petit centre commercial. J'achète une veste noire, mais je préfère la bleue. Ma sœur essaie un manteau et appelle notre mère pour demander son avis. Ensuite, nous mangeons au café d'en face. Est-ce que tu attends souvent quelqu'un après le travail ?`

How it satisfies the brief: regular -re verbs are `attends`, `descend`, and `répond`; `depuis deux ans` shows a situation that still continues; `commençons` is a -cer verb; `achète` and `préfère` are accent-changing verbs; `essaie` changes y to i and `appelle` doubles **l**; the final sentence is a realistic yes/no question.

Mark yourself wrong if any of these appear:
- `il attends` or another regular -re form with the wrong singular ending;
- a past tense with `depuis` for something still true now;
- `nous commencons` without ç or `nous mangons` without the extra **e**;
- a changed stem carried into `nous` or `vous`, such as **nous achètons**;
- a question without a space before `?`.
:::
