# Chapter 24 Answer Key: Recognizing Literary Pasts

These are model answers, not the only possible translations. Preserve the prompt's time relationship, mood, pronouns, and agreement.

## Section A

1. “The king spoke.” — parla: passé simple of parler.
2. “They finished the meal.” — finirent: passé simple, i-series.
3. “He took his jacket.” — prit: passé simple of prendre.
4. “We were surprised.” — fûmes: passé simple of être, completed narrative reaction.
5. “As soon as she had read, she went out.” — eut lu: passé antérieur; sortit: passé simple.
6. “When they had arrived, they knocked.” — furent arrivés: passé antérieur with être; frappèrent: passé simple.

## Section B

1. Venait: imparfait, a recurring past visit.
2. Vint: passé simple, one completed arrival.
3. Était venue: plus-que-parfait, arrival before another past reference.
4. Fut venue: passé antérieur, completed coming before partit (passé simple).
5. Serait venue: past conditional, unreal outcome; avait pu: plus-que-parfait condition.
6. Finit alone is ambiguous between present and passé simple. Do not label it with certainty without context.

## Section C

1. “The courtyard was empty. Léa entered and saw a letter. When she had opened the envelope, she recognized her mother's handwriting.”
2. Était: imparfait background. Entra, vit, reconnut: passé simple events. Eut ouvert: passé antérieur, opening completed before recognition.
3. `La cour était vide. Léa est entrée et a vu une lettre. Après avoir ouvert l'enveloppe, elle a reconnu l'écriture de sa mère.` — ordinary completed events and a past infinitive; no literary production required.

## Section D

“As soon as the travellers had found the house, they called their friends.” The finding ended first. Eurent is passé simple of avoir; eurent trouvé is passé antérieur, while appelèrent is passé simple. Contemporary retelling: `Après avoir trouvé la maison, les voyageurs ont appelé leurs amis.` Accept another modern version only if it preserves the order.

## Lesson checkpoint answers

### Checkpoint 1

1. “She entered.” — passé simple, -er third-person singular.
2. “They took the train.” — passé simple of prendre, irregular i-series.
3. “We came.” — passé simple of venir, -in- family.
4. Either “He finishes/is finishing his work” (present) or “He finished his work” (passé simple). The isolated form is ambiguous; a narrative context resolves it.

### Checkpoint 2

“When they had eaten, they went out.” Eurent mangé is passé antérieur; sortirent is passé simple. Eating finishes first; going out follows. A contemporary retelling is `Après avoir mangé, ils sont sortis.`

### Checkpoint 3

`Il a ouvert la lettre. Il était inquiet. Après avoir lu le message, il a souri.` — passé composé advances events; imparfait retains the emotional background; the past infinitive preserves completed reading before the smile.
