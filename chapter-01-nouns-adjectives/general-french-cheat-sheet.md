# General French Cheat Sheet

A quick reference for the original course, Chapters 0–16. Look up a pattern while practising, then close the sheet and try it from memory. French examples are tappable for pronunciation on the website. The separate Grammar Cheat Sheet covers the detailed tense work in Chapters 17–25.

## Pronunciation reminders — Chapter 0

| Pattern | Reminder | Example and meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Final consonants | Often silent, but not always; learn the sound with the word | `petit` (small), but `avec` (with) |
| Verb ending -ent | Silent in third-person plural verb forms | `ils parlent` (they speak) |
| é / è / ê | é is a close vowel; è and usually ê are more open | `été` (summer), `mère` (mother), `fête` (party) |
| ou / u | ou and u are different sounds; for u, round your lips while trying an “ee” sound | `vous` (you), `tu` (you) |
| au / eau / oi | au and eau sound like o; oi sounds roughly like “wah” | `eau` (water), `moi` (me) |
| an/en, on, in/ain | Common nasal-vowel spellings; usually do not pronounce a separate n | `enfant` (child), `bon` (good), `pain` (bread) |
| ç / soft c | ç sounds like s; c is soft before e, i, y | `français` (French), `cinéma` (cinema) |
| Elision | Some short words lose their vowel before a vowel sound or silent h | `j'aime` (I like), `l'homme` (the man) |
| Liaison | A normally silent consonant can link to the next vowel sound; never automatically link every word | `les amis` (the friends), with a linking z sound |

## Nouns, articles, and agreement — Chapters 1 and 9

Learn a noun with its article, not on its own: `une maison` (a house), `un problème` (a problem). Gender is not reliably predictable from English or spelling.

| Meaning | Masculine singular | Feminine singular | Before a vowel sound | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the | `le livre` (the book) | `la table` (the table) | `l'école` (the school) | `les livres` (the books) |
| a/an; some countable items | `un livre` (a book) | `une table` (a table) | un/une stays unchanged | `des livres` (some books) |
| some, uncountable | `du pain` (some bread) | `de la soupe` (some soup) | `de l'eau` (some water) | Use des for plural items |

- Adjectives usually add -e for feminine and -s for plural: `petit, petite, petits, petites` (small). Some forms are irregular: `beau, belle` (beautiful).
- Most adjectives follow the noun: `une voiture rouge` (a red car). Common short ones often precede it: `une petite voiture` (a small car).
- General preferences use definite articles: `J'aime le café.` (I like coffee.)
- Indefinite and partitive articles usually become de/d' after negation: `Je n'ai pas de voiture.` (I do not have a car.) With être, keep the article: `Ce n'est pas un problème.` (It is not a problem.)
- Quantities normally take de/d': `beaucoup de livres` (many books), `un verre d'eau` (a glass of water).

## Subject pronouns and essential verbs — Chapters 2–7

| Verb | je | tu | il/elle | nous | vous | ils/elles |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| être: to be | `je suis` (I am) | `tu es` (you are) | `il est` (he is) | `nous sommes` (we are) | `vous êtes` (you are) | `elles sont` (they are) |
| avoir: to have | `j'ai` (I have) | `tu as` (you have) | `elle a` (she has) | `nous avons` (we have) | `vous avez` (you have) | `ils ont` (they have) |
| aller: to go | `je vais` (I go) | `tu vas` (you go) | `il va` (he goes) | `nous allons` (we go) | `vous allez` (you go) | `elles vont` (they go) |
| faire: to do/make | `je fais` (I do) | `tu fais` (you do) | `elle fait` (she does) | `nous faisons` (we do) | `vous faites` (you do) | `ils font` (they do) |

Tu is informal singular; vous is formal singular or plural. On often means “we” but always takes the il/elle verb form: `On est prêts.` (We are ready, male/mixed group.) Use ils for a male or mixed group and elles for an all-female group.

Regular present endings, in je / tu / il / nous / vous / ils order:

| Family | Endings | Example |
|---|---|---|
| -er | -e, -es, -e, -ons, -ez, -ent | `je parle, nous parlons` (I speak, we speak) |
| Regular -ir | -is, -is, -it, -issons, -issez, -issent | `je finis, nous finissons` (I finish, we finish) |
| Regular -re | -s, -s, no added ending, -ons, -ez, -ent | `je vends, nous vendons` (I sell, we sell) |

Not every -ir verb is regular: partir and venir have their own patterns. After a modal, the next verb stays infinitive: `Je peux venir.` (I can come.)

Use avoir, not être, for age and several physical feelings: `J'ai vingt ans.` (I am twenty.) `J'ai faim.` (I am hungry.) `J'ai froid.` (I am cold.)

## Building statements, questions, and negatives — Chapters 2, 3, and 11

| Job | Pattern | Example and meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Statement | subject + verb + complement | `Je parle français.` (I speak French.) |
| Yes/no question | est-ce que + statement | `Est-ce que tu viens ?` (Are you coming?) |
| Conversational question | statement with questioning intonation | `Tu viens ?` (Are you coming?) |
| Formal inversion | verb + hyphen + subject pronoun | `Parlez-vous français ?` (Do you speak French?) |
| Basic negative | ne + conjugated verb + pas | `Je ne sais pas.` (I do not know.) |
| Never / no longer | ne…jamais / ne…plus | `Je ne fume plus.` (I no longer smoke.) |
| Nothing / nobody | ne…rien / ne…personne | `Je ne vois personne.` (I do not see anyone.) |
| Only | ne…que | `Je n'ai que dix euros.` (I only have ten euros.) |

Question words: `qui` (who), `que / qu'est-ce que` (what), `où` (where), `quand` (when), `comment` (how), `pourquoi` (why), `combien` (how much/many), `quel / quelle / quels / quelles` (which/what, agreeing with the noun).

Spoken French often drops ne, but retain it for careful writing. In compound tenses, ne…pas surrounds the auxiliary: `Je n'ai pas compris.` (I did not understand.)

## Possessives, demonstratives, and comparisons — Chapter 10

| Owner | Masculine singular noun | Feminine singular noun | Plural noun |
|---|---|---|---|
| my | `mon` | `ma` | `mes` |
| your, informal | `ton` | `ta` | `tes` |
| his/her | `son` | `sa` | `ses` |
| our | `notre` | `notre` | `nos` |
| your, formal/plural | `votre` | `votre` | `vos` |
| their | `leur` | `leur` | `leurs` |

Possessives match the thing owned, not the owner's gender: `son frère` (his/her brother), `sa sœur` (his/her sister). Before a feminine noun beginning with a vowel sound, use mon/ton/son: `mon amie` (my female friend).

- This/that: `ce livre` (this/that book), `cet arbre` (this/that tree), `cette maison` (this/that house).
- These/those: `ces livres` (these/those books).
- More/less/as: `plus grand que` (taller than), `moins cher que` (less expensive than), `aussi rapide que` (as fast as).
- Irregular comparisons: `meilleur` (better, adjective), `mieux` (better, adverb).
- Many adverbs use the feminine adjective + -ment: `douce → doucement` (gentle → gently). Common exceptions include `bien` (well), `vite` (quickly).

## Object and relative pronouns — Chapters 8, 9, 11, and 14

| Role | Forms | Example and meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Direct object: whom/what? | me, te, le, la, nous, vous, les | `Je la vois.` (I see her/it.) |
| Indirect object: to whom? | me, te, lui, nous, vous, leur | `Je lui parle.` (I speak to him/her.) |
| Place / à + thing | y | `J'y vais.` (I am going there.) |
| de + thing / quantity | en | `J'en veux deux.` (I want two of them.) |
| After a preposition / emphasis | moi, toi, lui, elle, nous, vous, eux, elles | `avec eux` (with them) |
| Relative subject | qui | `Le livre qui est ici.` (The book that is here.) |
| Relative direct object | que | `Le livre que je lis.` (The book that I am reading.) |
| Relative place/time | où | `La ville où j'habite.` (The town where I live.) |
| Relative de relationship | dont | `Le livre dont je parle.` (The book that I am talking about.) |

Normal preverbal order: **me/te/se/nous/vous → le/la/les → lui/leur → y → en**.

`Je le lui donne.` (I give it to him/her.) A pronoun usually sits before the verb it belongs to: `Je vais le lire.` (I am going to read it.) Affirmative commands are different: `Donne-le-moi.` (Give it to me.)

## Place, time, and useful connectors — Chapters 3–6

| Use | French | Example and meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Contractions | à + le = au; à + les = aux; de + le = du; de + les = des | `au cinéma` (at/to the cinema), `des enfants` (of the children) |
| Cities | à / de | `à Paris` (in/to Paris), `de Paris` (from Paris) |
| Countries, feminine / vowel-initial | en / de or d' for feminine origins | `en France` (in/to France), `de France` (from France) |
| Countries, masculine consonant-initial | au / du | `au Canada` (in/to Canada), `du Canada` (from Canada) |
| Countries, plural | aux / des | `aux États-Unis` (in/to the United States), `des États-Unis` (from the United States) |
| Someone's home or premises | chez | `chez moi` (at my place), `chez le médecin` (at the doctor's) |
| Since/for, still continuing | depuis + present | `J'habite ici depuis un an.` (I have lived here for a year.) |
| Duration | pendant | `J'ai étudié pendant deux heures.` (I studied for two hours.) |
| Time from now | dans | `Je pars dans dix minutes.` (I leave in ten minutes.) |
| A clock time | à + time | `à huit heures et demie` (at half past eight) |
| A recurring day | le + singular day | `le lundi` (on Mondays) |
| Linking ideas | et, mais, ou, parce que, donc, puis | and, but, or, because, so, then |

Country prepositions have exceptions; learn destinations and origins as pairs. In dates, use le + number + month; the first is premier: `le premier mai` (May 1), `le deux mai` (May 2).

## Tenses and commands at a glance — Chapters 6 and 11–16

| Meaning | Form | Example and meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Now / habit | present | `Je travaille.` (I work / I am working.) |
| Going to | present aller + infinitive | `Je vais travailler.` (I am going to work.) |
| Completed past event | present avoir/être + past participle | `J'ai travaillé.` (I worked.) |
| Past scene / habit | present nous stem without -ons + imparfait endings | `Je travaillais.` (I was working / used to work.) |
| Before another past event | imparfait avoir/être + past participle | `J'avais travaillé.` (I had worked.) |
| Will | future stem + future endings | `Je travaillerai.` (I will work.) |
| Would / polite request | future stem + imparfait endings | `Je voudrais partir.` (I would like to leave.) |
| Necessity / desire with a dependent subject | trigger + que + subjunctive | `Il faut que tu partes.` (You have to leave.) |
| Command | tu/nous/vous form without subject | `Écoutez !` (Listen! formal/plural) |
| Reflexive action | reflexive pronoun + verb | `Je me lève.` (I get up.) |
| While/by doing | en + present participle | `J'apprends en lisant.` (I learn by reading.) |

For complete endings, auxiliary choice, agreement, si clauses, and tense contrasts, use the Grammar Cheat Sheet for Chapters 17–25.

## Quick error check

1. Did I learn the noun's gender and make its article/adjective agree?
2. Did I conjugate for the actual subject, including singular on?
3. Did I leave a second verb in the infinitive when the construction requires it?
4. Did I choose a tense by meaning rather than translate the English form word for word?
5. Did I place pronouns and negation around the correct verb or auxiliary?
6. Did I keep accents and apostrophes? `a` (has) differs from `à` (to/at); `ou` (or) differs from `où` (where).
