# Lesson 1: Gender and Definite Articles

## Core idea

Every French noun has grammatical gender. Learn the article with the noun:

- `le jardin` — the garden
- `la fenêtre` — the window
- `l'école` — the school
- `les voisins` — the neighbors

Treat `le jardin` as one vocabulary unit rather than memorizing `jardin` alone.

## Definite articles

| Meaning | Form | French example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| the + masculine singular | `le` | `le café` | the café |
| the + feminine singular | `la` | `la chaise` | the chair |
| the + singular before a vowel sound | `l'` | `l'ami`, `l'histoire` | the friend, the story |
| the + plural | `les` | `les cafés`, `les chaises` | the cafés, the chairs |

`l'` hides the noun's gender, so record the gender separately:

- `l'arbre` (`m.`) — the tree
- `l'adresse` (`f.`) — the address

## Useful patterns, not guarantees

Some endings frequently suggest gender:

- Often feminine: `-tion`, `-sion`, `-té`, `-ette`
- Often masculine: `-age`, `-ment`, `-eau`, `-isme`

There are exceptions. Use these patterns to make a first guess, then verify.

## Retrieval check

Without looking above, write the four French forms of “the.” Then add the correct article:

1. ___ musée
2. ___ question
3. ___ hôtel
4. ___ étudiants

## Production task

Look around your room. Find four objects, look up each noun, and write it with its article. Say each phrase twice.

## Sentence usage practice

Read each sentence aloud twice. Then cover it and write it from memory.

1. `Le musée est près de la gare.` — The museum is near the train station.
2. `La question est importante.` — The question is important.
3. `L'hôtel est dans le centre-ville.` — The hotel is downtown.
4. `Les étudiants regardent le tableau.` — The students are looking at the board.
5. `L'adresse est sur le document.` — The address is on the document.

### Change the sentence

Rewrite sentence 1 with `restaurant`, sentence 2 with `réponse`, and sentence 4 with `professeurs`. Adjust the articles as needed.

### Make it personal

Write three sentences about places or objects near you. Use one masculine noun, one feminine noun, and one plural noun.
