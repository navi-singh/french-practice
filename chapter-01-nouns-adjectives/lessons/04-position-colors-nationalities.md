# Lesson 4: Adjective Position, Colors, and Nationalities

## Position

Most descriptive adjectives follow the noun:

- `une rue tranquille` — a quiet street
- `un exercice difficile` — a difficult exercise
- `une chemise blanche` — a white shirt

Several frequent adjectives commonly precede the noun, especially short descriptions involving beauty, age, goodness, and size:

- `un beau quartier` — a beautiful neighborhood
- `une jeune étudiante` — a young female student
- `un bon repas` — a good meal
- `une grande ville` — a large city

Learn position as part of the phrase. Instead of memorizing only `difficile`, memorize `un exercice difficile`.

## Forms before a vowel sound

Some masculine singular adjectives have a special form before a vowel sound:

- `beau` -> `bel`: `un bel appartement` — a beautiful apartment
- `nouveau` -> `nouvel`: `un nouvel ami` — a new friend
- `vieux` -> `vieil`: `un vieil hôtel` — an old hotel

## Colors

Colors usually follow the noun and agree:

- `un sac noir` — a black bag
- `une veste noire` — a black jacket
- `des sacs noirs` — some black bags
- `des vestes noires` — some black jackets

Some color expressions derived from nouns may remain unchanged. For now, master the regular high-frequency colors first.

## Nationalities

Nationality adjectives **agree** with the person or noun they describe. This means the adjective changes its ending to match:

- gender: masculine or feminine;
- number: singular or plural.

For example, `canadien` changes like this:

| Person or group | French | English |
|---|---|---|
| masculine singular | `un étudiant canadien` | a Canadian male student |
| feminine singular | `une étudiante canadienne` | a Canadian female student |
| masculine or mixed plural | `des étudiants canadiens` | Canadian male/mixed students |
| feminine plural | `des étudiantes canadiennes` | Canadian female students |

Nationality adjectives are normally lowercase:

- `un acteur français` — a French actor
- `une actrice française` — a French actress
- `des étudiants canadiens` — some Canadian male/mixed students
- `des étudiantes canadiennes` — some Canadian female students

The name of a language is also lowercase:

- `Je parle français.` — I speak French.

When a nationality is used as a noun for a person rather than as an adjective, it is capitalized:

- `Elle est canadienne.` — She is Canadian. (`canadienne` is an adjective.)
- `C'est une Canadienne.` — She is a Canadian woman. (`Canadienne` is a noun.)

## Sentence usage practice

Read each sentence aloud. Mark adjectives before the noun with `B` and adjectives after the noun with `A`.

1. `Nous habitons dans une grande ville canadienne.` — We live in a large Canadian city.
2. `Elle porte une petite veste noire.` — She is wearing a small black jacket.
3. `C'est un bel appartement moderne.` — It is a beautiful modern apartment.
4. `Un nouvel étudiant français arrive aujourd'hui.` — A new French student arrives today.
5. `Les vieilles maisons blanches sont près de la rivière.` — The old white houses are near the river.
6. `Je parle français avec mes collègues canadiens.` — I speak French with my Canadian colleagues.

### Change the sentence

1. Make sentences 1, 2, and 3 plural.
2. Change `noire` to `rouge`, then to `bleue`.
3. Replace `français` in sentence 4 with a nationality that describes you or someone you know.

### Make it personal

Describe three objects and two people. Use at least one adjective before a noun, three adjectives after nouns, one color, and one nationality.
