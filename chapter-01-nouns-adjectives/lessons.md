# Chapter 1 Lessons: Nouns, Articles, and Descriptive Adjectives

This chapter builds the noun phrase, one of the foundations of French. By the end, you should be able to choose an article, recognize a noun's gender and number, make an adjective agree, and place common adjectives naturally.

## Learning objectives

You will learn to:

1. treat a French noun and its gender as one vocabulary unit;
2. use definite articles `le`, `la`, `l'`, and `les`;
3. use indefinite articles `un`, `une`, and `des`;
4. form common noun plurals and recognize number in speech;
5. change descriptive adjectives for gender and number;
6. place common adjectives before or after the noun;
7. use colors and nationality adjectives in complete sentences.

## Prerequisite quick check

Before beginning, make sure you can identify a noun and an adjective in English.

- In “a blue house,” **house** is the noun and **blue** is the adjective.
- In “the Canadian students,” **students** is the noun and **Canadian** is the adjective.

French adds two decisions that English often does not show: grammatical gender and visible agreement.

This chapter asks you to read every French example aloud. If you are not yet sure how French letters sound, work through [Chapter 0](../chapter-00-getting-started/) first: it covers the alphabet, silent letters, nasal vowels, elision, and liaison. If a grammar term here is unfamiliar, look it up in the [glossary](../chapter-00-getting-started/glossary.md).

## How to study this chapter

For each unit, read the explanation, say every French example aloud, cover the English and test comprehension, then cover the French and write it from memory. Complete the substitution prompts before moving to the next unit.

## Unit 1: Gender and Definite Articles

### Core idea

Every French noun has grammatical gender. Learn the article with the noun:

- `le jardin` — the garden
- `la fenêtre` — the window
- `l'école` — the school
- `les voisins` — the neighbors

Treat `le jardin` as one vocabulary unit rather than memorizing `jardin` alone.

### Definite articles

| Meaning | Form | French example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| the + masculine singular | `le` | `le café` | the café |
| the + feminine singular | `la` | `la chaise` | the chair |
| the + singular before a vowel sound | `l'` | `l'ami`, `l'histoire` | the friend, the story |
| the + plural | `les` | `les cafés`, `les chaises` | the cafés, the chairs |

`l'` hides the noun's gender, so record the gender separately:

- `l'arbre` (`m.`) — the tree
- `l'adresse` (`f.`) — the address

### Useful patterns, not guarantees

Some endings frequently suggest gender:

- Often feminine: `-tion`, `-sion`, `-té`, `-ette`
- Often masculine: `-age`, `-ment`, `-eau`, `-isme`

There are exceptions. Use these patterns to make a first guess, then verify.

### Retrieval check

Without looking above, write the four French forms of “the.” Then add the correct article:

1. ___ musée
2. ___ question
3. ___ hôtel
4. ___ étudiants

### Production task

Look around your room. Find four objects, look up each noun, and write it with its article. Say each phrase twice.

### Sentence usage practice

Read each sentence aloud twice. Then cover it and write it from memory.

1. `Le musée est près de la gare.` — The museum is near the train station.
2. `La question est importante.` — The question is important.
3. `L'hôtel est dans le centre-ville.` — The hotel is downtown.
4. `Les étudiants regardent le tableau.` — The students are looking at the board.
5. `L'adresse est sur le document.` — The address is on the document.

#### Change the sentence

Rewrite sentence 1 with `restaurant`, sentence 2 with `réponse`, and sentence 4 with `professeurs`. Adjust the articles as needed.

#### Make it personal

Write three sentences about places or objects near you. Use one masculine noun, one feminine noun, and one plural noun.

## Unit 2: Indefinite Articles and Plurals

### Indefinite articles

Use these forms for “a,” “an,” or plural “some”:

| Noun | Article | French example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| masculine singular | `un` | `un cahier` | a notebook |
| feminine singular | `une` | `une lampe` | a lamp |
| plural | `des` | `des cahiers`, `des lampes` | some notebooks, some lamps |

Compare:

- `le cahier` — the notebook
- `un cahier` — a notebook
- `les cahiers` — the notebooks
- `des cahiers` — some notebooks

### Forming plurals

The common pattern is to add `-s`:

- `une porte` -> `des portes` — a door -> some doors
- `un village` -> `des villages` — a village -> some villages

Common special patterns:

- Many nouns ending in `-eau` take `-x`: `un tableau` -> `des tableaux` — a painting/board -> some paintings/boards
- Many nouns ending in `-al` change to `-aux`: `un animal` -> `des animaux` — an animal -> some animals
- A noun already ending in `-s`, `-x`, or `-z` often does not change in writing.

Learn exceptions as complete singular/plural pairs.

### Pronunciation note

The final plural `-s` is normally silent. The article often carries the clearest number signal:

- `le livre` — the book
- `les livres` — the books

### Retrieval check

Change each phrase to plural:

1. `une photo`
2. `un bureau`
3. `le journal`
4. `la voix`

### Production task

Write three singular objects you own. Convert each to plural and read both versions aloud.

### Sentence usage practice

Read, copy once, and then reproduce each sentence from memory.

1. `J'ai un cahier bleu dans mon sac.` — I have a blue notebook in my bag.
2. `Elle achète une lampe pour la chambre.` — She buys a lamp for the bedroom.
3. `Nous avons des photos de Paris.` — We have some photos of Paris.
4. `Les bureaux sont près des fenêtres.` — The desks are near the windows.
5. `Il observe des animaux au parc.` — He watches some animals at the park.

#### Change the sentence

Change each singular object to plural. Then change each plural object to singular. Remember to change both the article and the noun.

#### Make it personal

Write four sentences beginning with `J'ai...`: two with singular objects and two with plural objects.

## Unit 3: Adjective Agreement

### Core idea

A descriptive adjective normally agrees with the noun it describes.

| Noun form | Typical adjective form |
|---|---|
| masculine singular | base form |
| feminine singular | add `-e` |
| masculine plural | add `-s` |
| feminine plural | add `-es` |

Example with `petit`:

- `un petit appartement` — a small apartment
- `une petite maison` — a small house
- `des petits appartements` — some small apartments
- `des petites maisons` — some small houses

### Common spelling patterns

| Masculine | Feminine | English |
|---|---|---|
| `actif` | `active` | active |
| `heureux` | `heureuse` | happy |
| `cher` | `chère` | expensive/dear |
| `naturel` | `naturelle` | natural |
| `bon` | `bonne` | good |
| `canadien` | `canadienne` | Canadian |

Some adjectives already end in `-e` and do not change in the feminine:

- `un garçon calme` — a calm boy
- `une fille calme` — a calm girl

### Mixed groups

A plural adjective describing a group containing at least one masculine noun traditionally uses the masculine plural:

- `Paul et Marie sont contents.` — Paul and Marie are happy.

### Sentence usage practice

Read the sentences aloud and identify the noun controlled by each adjective.

1. `Paul est content de son résultat.` — Paul is happy with his result.
2. `Marie est contente de son résultat.` — Marie is happy with her result.
3. `Paul et Marc sont contents de leurs résultats.` — Paul and Marc are happy with their results.
4. `Marie et Léa sont contentes de leurs résultats.` — Marie and Léa are happy with their results.
5. `Paul et Marie sont contents de leurs résultats.` — Paul and Marie are happy with their results.
6. `Les étudiantes canadiennes sont très actives.` — The Canadian female students are very active.
7. `Le garçon sportif parle avec une fille sportive.` — The athletic boy speaks with an athletic girl.

#### Notice the pattern

Underline `content`, `contente`, `contents`, and `contentes`. Draw an arrow from each adjective to the noun or people it describes.

#### Change the sentence

Rewrite sentences 1-5 with `fatigué` and then with `heureux`. Make every gender and number change.

#### Make it personal

Write four sentences about people you know:

1. one masculine singular;
2. one feminine singular;
3. one feminine plural;
4. one mixed group.

### Retrieval check

Write the feminine form:

1. `sportif`
2. `heureux`
3. `italien`
4. `calme`

Then write the feminine plural form of each.

## Unit 4: Adjective Position, Colors, and Nationalities

### Position

Most descriptive adjectives follow the noun:

- `une rue tranquille` — a quiet street
- `un exercice difficile` — a difficult exercise
- `une chemise blanche` — a white shirt

Several frequent adjectives commonly precede the noun, especially short descriptions involving beauty, age, goodness, and size:

- `un beau quartier` — a beautiful neighborhood
- `une jeune étudiante` — a young female student
- `un bon repas` — a good meal
- `une grande ville` — a large city

Learn position as part of the phrase. Instead of memorizing only `difficile`, memorize `un exercice difficile`.

### Forms before a vowel sound

Some masculine singular adjectives have a special form before a vowel sound:

- `beau` -> `bel`: `un bel appartement` — a beautiful apartment
- `nouveau` -> `nouvel`: `un nouvel ami` — a new friend
- `vieux` -> `vieil`: `un vieil hôtel` — an old hotel

### Colors

Colors usually follow the noun and agree:

- `un sac noir` — a black bag
- `une veste noire` — a black jacket
- `des sacs noirs` — some black bags
- `des vestes noires` — some black jackets

Some color expressions derived from nouns may remain unchanged. For now, master the regular high-frequency colors first.

### Nationalities

Nationality adjectives **agree** with the person or noun they describe. This means the adjective changes its ending to match:

- gender: masculine or feminine;
- number: singular or plural.

For example, `canadien` changes like this:

| Person or group | French | English |
|---|---|---|
| masculine singular | `un étudiant canadien` | a Canadian male student |
| feminine singular | `une étudiante canadienne` | a Canadian female student |
| masculine or mixed plural | `des étudiants canadiens` | Canadian male/mixed students |
| feminine plural | `des étudiantes canadiennes` | Canadian female students |

Nationality adjectives are normally lowercase:

- `un acteur français` — a French actor
- `une actrice française` — a French actress
- `des étudiants canadiens` — some Canadian male/mixed students
- `des étudiantes canadiennes` — some Canadian female students

The name of a language is also lowercase:

- `Je parle français.` — I speak French.

When a nationality is used as a noun for a person rather than as an adjective, it is capitalized:

- `Elle est canadienne.` — She is Canadian. (`canadienne` is an adjective.)
- `C'est une Canadienne.` — She is a Canadian woman. (`Canadienne` is a noun.)

### Retrieval check

Correct each phrase:

1. `une voiture noir`
2. `un appartement beau`
3. `une étudiant canadien`
4. `des maisons blanc`

### Sentence usage practice

Read each sentence aloud. Mark adjectives before the noun with `B` and adjectives after the noun with `A`.

1. `Nous habitons dans une grande ville canadienne.` — We live in a large Canadian city.
2. `Elle porte une petite veste noire.` — She is wearing a small black jacket.
3. `C'est un bel appartement moderne.` — It is a beautiful modern apartment.
4. `Un nouvel étudiant français arrive aujourd'hui.` — A new French student arrives today.
5. `Les vieilles maisons blanches sont près de la rivière.` — The old white houses are near the river.
6. `Je parle français avec mes collègues canadiens.` — I speak French with my Canadian colleagues.

#### Change the sentence

1. Make sentences 1, 2, and 3 plural.
2. Change `noire` to `rouge`, then to `bleue`.
3. Replace `français` in sentence 4 with a nationality that describes you or someone you know.

#### Make it personal

Describe three objects and two people. Use at least one adjective before a noun, three adjectives after nouns, one color, and one nationality.

## Common mistakes to avoid

1. **Learning a noun without its article.** Learn `la table`, not only `table`.
2. **Assuming every noun ending in `-e` is feminine.** Many are, but words such as `le problème` are masculine.
3. **Forgetting that `l'` can hide either gender.** Record `l'école (f.)` and `l'hôtel (m.)` in vocabulary notes.
4. **Changing only the noun in the plural.** The article and adjective must normally change too: `une petite maison` -> `des petites maisons`.
5. **Adding feminine endings mechanically.** Some adjectives have spelling changes: `heureux` -> `heureuse`, `canadien` -> `canadienne`.
6. **Putting every adjective after the noun.** Most go after, but frequent adjectives such as `petit`, `grand`, `beau`, `jeune`, and `nouveau` often go before.
7. **Capitalizing nationality adjectives.** Write `une étudiante française`, but `une Française` when the nationality is a noun.

## Mini-dialogue: describing a new apartment

Read the dialogue first in French. Then use the English version to check meaning.

**Léa :** `Tu habites dans un nouvel appartement ?` — Do you live in a new apartment?

**Marc :** `Oui. C'est un petit appartement moderne dans un quartier tranquille.` — Yes. It is a small modern apartment in a quiet neighborhood.

**Léa :** `Il y a combien de chambres ?` — How many bedrooms are there?

**Marc :** `Il y a une grande chambre et deux petites chambres.` — There is one large bedroom and two small bedrooms.

**Léa :** `Et la cuisine ?` — And the kitchen?

**Marc :** `La cuisine est blanche, avec une table noire et des chaises rouges.` — The kitchen is white, with a black table and red chairs.

**Léa :** `Tes voisins sont français ?` — Are your neighbors French?

**Marc :** `Une voisine est française et deux voisins sont canadiens.` — One female neighbor is French and two male neighbors are Canadian.

### Guided analysis

1. Find four indefinite articles and explain why each is masculine, feminine, or plural.
2. Find three adjectives placed before nouns.
3. Find five adjectives placed after nouns.
4. Explain the endings in `petites chambres`, `chaises rouges`, and `voisins canadiens`.
5. Replace `appartement` with `maison` and rewrite Marc's first two answers with the required changes.

## Connected reading: mon quartier

`J'habite dans une petite rue près du centre-ville. La rue a de vieilles maisons blanches et de nouveaux appartements modernes. Mon immeuble a un grand jardin partagé. Une famille canadienne habite au premier étage, et deux étudiantes françaises habitent au deuxième étage. Les voisins sont sympathiques. Le samedi, nous prenons un café dans le petit jardin.`

— I live on a small street near downtown. The street has old white houses and new modern apartments. My building has a large shared garden. A Canadian family lives on the first floor, and two French female students live on the second floor. The neighbors are friendly. On Saturdays, we have coffee in the small garden.

### Reading practice

1. List every noun with its article.
2. Match every adjective to the noun it describes.
3. Change `une petite rue` to plural.
4. Change `une famille canadienne` to a masculine singular person and then to a mixed plural group.
5. Write three true sentences about your own neighborhood using the reading as a model.

### Core habits

- Memorize article + noun together.
- Check article, noun, and adjective as one agreement group.
