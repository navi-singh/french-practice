# Chapter 1 Lessons: Nouns, Articles, and Descriptive Adjectives

This chapter builds the noun phrase, one of the foundations of French. By the end, you should be able to choose an article, recognize a noun's gender and number, make an adjective agree, and place common adjectives naturally.

## Learning objectives

You will learn to:

1. treat a French noun and its gender as one vocabulary unit;
2. use definite articles `le`, `la`, `l'`, and `les`;
3. use indefinite articles `un`, `une`, and `des`;
4. form common noun plurals and recognize number in speech;
5. change descriptive adjectives for gender and number;
6. place common adjectives before or after the noun;
7. use colors and nationality adjectives in complete sentences.

## Prerequisite quick check

Before beginning, make sure you can identify a noun and an adjective in English.

- In “a blue house,” **house** is the noun and **blue** is the adjective.
- In “the Canadian students,” **students** is the noun and **Canadian** is the adjective.

French adds two decisions that English often does not show: grammatical gender and visible agreement.

This chapter asks you to read every French example aloud. If you are not yet sure how French letters sound, work through [Chapter 0](../chapter-00-getting-started/) first: it covers the alphabet, silent letters, nasal vowels, elision, and liaison. If a grammar term here is unfamiliar, look it up in the [glossary](../chapter-00-getting-started/glossary.md).

:::answer
Test the prerequisite rather than just rereading it.

1. In **a blue house**, which word is the noun? — **house**. The noun is the thing being named.
2. In **the Canadian students**, which word is the adjective? — **Canadian**. The adjective describes the noun.
3. In `une petite maison`, which French word is the noun? — `maison`. It is the thing being described.
4. In `une petite maison`, which word is the adjective? — `petite`. It describes the house and agrees with it.
5. Why does this chapter care about gender and number? — French articles and adjectives change to match the noun, so you must know whether the noun is masculine/feminine and singular/plural.

If you missed more than one, review the glossary and Chapter 0 before continuing. Chapter 1 assumes you can reliably find the noun that controls the surrounding words.
:::

## How to study this chapter

For each unit, read the explanation, say every French example aloud, cover the English and test comprehension, then cover the French and write it from memory. Complete the substitution prompts before moving to the next unit.

## Unit 1: Gender and Definite Articles

### Core idea

Every French noun has grammatical gender. Learn the article with the noun:

- `le jardin` — the garden
- `la fenêtre` — the window
- `l'école` — the school
- `les voisins` — the neighbors

Treat `le jardin` as one vocabulary unit rather than memorizing `jardin` alone.

### Definite articles

| Meaning | Form | French example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| the + masculine singular | `le` | `le café` | the café |
| the + feminine singular | `la` | `la chaise` | the chair |
| the + singular before a vowel sound | `l'` | `l'ami`, `l'histoire` | the friend, the story |
| the + plural | `les` | `les cafés`, `les chaises` | the cafés, the chairs |

`l'` hides the noun's gender, so record the gender separately:

- `l'arbre` (`m.`) — the tree
- `l'adresse` (`f.`) — the address

### Useful patterns, not guarantees

Some endings frequently suggest gender:

- Often feminine: `-tion`, `-sion`, `-té`, `-ette`
- Often masculine: `-age`, `-ment`, `-eau`, `-isme`

There are exceptions. Use these patterns to make a first guess, then verify.

### Retrieval check

Without looking above, write the four French forms of “the.” Then add the correct article:

1. ___ musée
2. ___ question
3. ___ hôtel
4. ___ étudiants

:::answer
The four forms of **the** are `le`, `la`, `l'`, and `les`.

1. `le musée` — `musée` is masculine singular.
2. `la question` — `question` is feminine singular.
3. `l'hôtel` — `hôtel` begins with a silent **h**, so the article elides. The hidden gender is masculine.
4. `les étudiants` — `étudiants` is plural, so use `les`.

For the room task, answers vary. A correct note records the article with every noun; if you write `l'`, also record the hidden gender because the apostrophe hides it.
:::

### Production task

Look around your room. Find four objects, look up each noun, and write it with its article. Say each phrase twice.

1. `Le musée est près de la gare.` — The museum is near the train station.
2. `La question est importante.` — The question is important.
3. `L'hôtel est dans le centre-ville.` — The hotel is downtown.
4. `Les étudiants regardent le tableau.` — The students are looking at the board.
5. `L'adresse est sur le document.` — The address is on the document.

:::answer
Model memory check:

1. `Le musée est près de la gare.` — `musée` is masculine, so it takes `le`; `gare` is feminine, so it takes `la`.
2. `La question est importante.` — `question` is feminine, and `importante` shows feminine agreement.
3. `L'hôtel est dans le centre-ville.` — `hôtel` is masculine, but `le` becomes `l'` before the silent **h**.
4. `Les étudiants regardent le tableau.` — `les` marks plural; the final **s** of `étudiants` is silent.
5. `L'adresse est sur le document.` — `adresse` is feminine, but `la` becomes `l'` before a vowel sound.

When checking from memory, score the article and the noun together. A correct noun with the wrong article is not learned yet.
:::

- `La réponse est importante.` — `réponse` is feminine, so keep `la` and `importante`.

## Unit 2: Indefinite Articles and Plurals

### Indefinite articles

Use these forms for “a,” “an,” or plural “some”:

| Noun | Article | French example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| masculine singular | `un` | `un cahier` | a notebook |
| feminine singular | `une` | `une lampe` | a lamp |
| plural | `des` | `des cahiers`, `des lampes` | some notebooks, some lamps |

Compare:

- `le cahier` — the notebook
- `un cahier` — a notebook
- `les cahiers` — the notebooks
- `des cahiers` — some notebooks

### Forming plurals

The common pattern is to add `-s`:

- `une porte` -> `des portes` — a door -> some doors
- `un village` -> `des villages` — a village -> some villages

Common special patterns:

- Many nouns ending in `-eau` take `-x`: `un tableau` -> `des tableaux` — a painting/board -> some paintings/boards
- Many nouns ending in `-al` change to `-aux`: `un animal` -> `des animaux` — an animal -> some animals
- A noun already ending in `-s`, `-x`, or `-z` often does not change in writing.

Learn exceptions as complete singular/plural pairs.

### Pronunciation note

The final plural `-s` is normally silent. The article often carries the clearest number signal:

- `le livre` — the book
- `les livres` — the books

### Retrieval check

Change each phrase to plural:

1. `une photo`
2. `un bureau`
3. `le journal`
4. `la voix`

:::answer
1. `des photos` — plural indefinite article `des`, with a regular written **-s**.
2. `des bureaux` — `bureau` ends in **-eau**, so the common plural is **-x**.
3. `les journaux` — `journal` changes to `journaux` in the plural, and `le` becomes `les`.
4. `les voix` — `voix` already ends in **x**, so the noun spelling does not change; only the article shows plural.

For your own objects, answers vary. Mark yourself wrong if you changed only the noun and forgot that the article must change too.
:::

### Production task

Write three singular objects you own. Convert each to plural and read both versions aloud.

### Sentence usage practice

Read, copy once, and then reproduce each sentence from memory.

1. `J'ai un cahier bleu dans mon sac.` — I have a blue notebook in my bag.
2. `Elle achète une lampe pour la chambre.` — She buys a lamp for the bedroom.
3. `Nous avons des photos de Paris.` — We have some photos of Paris.
4. `Les bureaux sont près des fenêtres.` — The desks are near the windows.
5. `Il observe des animaux au parc.` — He watches some animals at the park.

:::answer
Model memory check:

1. `J'ai un cahier bleu dans mon sac.` — `cahier` is masculine singular, so `un` and `bleu` match it.
2. `Elle achète une lampe pour la chambre.` — `lampe` and `chambre` are feminine singular.
3. `Nous avons des photos de Paris.` — `des` marks an indefinite plural; the final plural **s** is written but silent.
4. `Les bureaux sont près des fenêtres.` — `bureau` becomes `bureaux`, and the verb is plural: `sont`.
5. `Il observe des animaux au parc.` — `animal` becomes `animaux`; this is not a regular **-s** plural.

For the change task, change the whole group: article, noun, adjective, and sometimes the verb. Mark yourself wrong if only one word became plural.
:::

#### Change the sentence

Change each singular object to plural. Then change each plural object to singular. Remember to change both the article and the noun.

#### Make it personal

Write four sentences beginning with `J'ai...`: two with singular objects and two with plural objects.

:::answer
Model memory check:

1. `J'ai un cahier bleu dans mon sac.` — `cahier` and `sac` are masculine singular, so `un`, `bleu`, and `mon` all match masculine singular.
2. `Elle achète une lampe pour la chambre.` — both `lampe` and `chambre` are feminine singular, so they take `une` and `la`.
3. `Nous avons des photos de Paris.` — `des` marks an indefinite plural. The final plural **s** in `photos` is written but normally silent.
4. `Les bureaux sont près des fenêtres.` — `bureau` becomes `bureaux` in the plural, and `des` is the contraction of `de` + `les`.
5. `Il observe des animaux au parc.` — `animal` becomes `animaux`; do not form it as a regular **-s** plural.

Change the sentence:

- `J'ai des cahiers bleus dans mon sac.` / `J'ai un cahier bleu dans mon sac.` — article, noun, and adjective all change together.
- `Elle achète des lampes pour la chambre.` / `Elle achète une lampe pour la chambre.` — only the object changes; the rest of the sentence can stay.
- `Nous avons une photo de Paris.` / `Nous avons des photos de Paris.` — singular uses `une`; plural uses `des`.
- `Le bureau est près de la fenêtre.` / `Les bureaux sont près des fenêtres.` — the verb changes too: `est` for singular, `sont` for plural.
- `Il observe un animal au parc.` / `Il observe des animaux au parc.` — `animal` has the special plural `animaux`.

For your own `J'ai...` sentences, check that every singular noun has `un` or `une`, every plural noun has `des`, and plural **-s** or **-x** is not pronounced as a separate sound.
:::


## Unit 3: Adjective Agreement

### Core idea

A descriptive adjective normally agrees with the noun it describes.

| Noun form | Typical adjective form |
|---|---|
| masculine singular | base form |
| feminine singular | add `-e` |
| masculine plural | add `-s` |
| feminine plural | add `-es` |

Example with `petit`:

- `un petit appartement` — a small apartment
- `une petite maison` — a small house
- `des petits appartements` — some small apartments
- `des petites maisons` — some small houses

### Common spelling patterns

| Masculine | Feminine | English |
|---|---|---|
| `actif` | `active` | active |
| `heureux` | `heureuse` | happy |
| `cher` | `chère` | expensive/dear |
| `naturel` | `naturelle` | natural |
| `bon` | `bonne` | good |
| `canadien` | `canadienne` | Canadian |

Some adjectives already end in `-e` and do not change in the feminine:

- `un garçon calme` — a calm boy
- `une fille calme` — a calm girl

### Mixed groups

A plural adjective describing a group containing at least one masculine noun traditionally uses the masculine plural:

- `Paul et Marie sont contents.` — Paul and Marie are happy.

### Sentence usage practice

Read the sentences aloud and identify the noun controlled by each adjective.

1. `Paul est content de son résultat.` — Paul is happy with his result.
2. `Marie est contente de son résultat.` — Marie is happy with her result.
3. `Paul et Marc sont contents de leurs résultats.` — Paul and Marc are happy with their results.
4. `Marie et Léa sont contentes de leurs résultats.` — Marie and Léa are happy with their results.
5. `Paul et Marie sont contents de leurs résultats.` — Paul and Marie are happy with their results.
6. `Les étudiantes canadiennes sont très actives.` — The Canadian female students are very active.
7. `Le garçon sportif parle avec une fille sportive.` — The athletic boy speaks with an athletic girl.

:::answer
Model memory check:

1. `Paul est content de son résultat.` — one masculine person, so base form `content`.
2. `Marie est contente de son résultat.` — one feminine person, so add the feminine ending.
3. `Paul et Marc sont contents de leurs résultats.` — masculine plural group.
4. `Marie et Léa sont contentes de leurs résultats.` — feminine plural group.
5. `Paul et Marie sont contents de leurs résultats.` — a mixed group uses masculine plural agreement in standard French.
6. `Les étudiantes canadiennes sont très actives.` — both adjectives agree with feminine plural `étudiantes`.
7. `Le garçon sportif parle avec une fille sportive.` — `sportif` matches `garçon`; `sportive` matches `fille`.

The noun or people being described control the adjective. Do not choose the ending by sound alone, because several endings are silent.
:::

| Masculine | Feminine | Feminine plural | Why |
|---|---|---|---|

| `heureux` | `heureuse` | `heureuses` | **-x** changes to **-se** in the feminine. |
| `italien` | `italienne` | `italiennes` | The **n** doubles in the feminine. |
| `calme` | `calme` | `calmes` | It already ends in **-e**, so the feminine singular looks the same. |

The rule is agreement, not pronunciation. Some forms sound alike, so the noun's gender and number tell you what to write.

:::answer

1. `Paul est content de son résultat.` — `content` describes Paul, one masculine person, so it stays in the base form.
2. `Marie est contente de son résultat.` — Marie is feminine singular, so add **-e**: `contente`.
3. `Paul et Marc sont contents de leurs résultats.` — two masculine people require masculine plural agreement: `contents`.
4. `Marie et Léa sont contentes de leurs résultats.` — two feminine people require feminine plural agreement: `contentes`.
5. `Paul et Marie sont contents de leurs résultats.` — a mixed group uses masculine plural agreement in standard French.
6. `Les étudiantes canadiennes sont très actives.` — `étudiantes` is feminine plural, so both adjectives are feminine plural.
7. `Le garçon sportif parle avec une fille sportive.` — `sportif` agrees with masculine `garçon`; `sportive` agrees with feminine `fille`.

2. `Marie est fatiguée de son résultat.`
3. `Paul et Marc sont fatigués de leurs résultats.`
4. `Marie et Léa sont fatiguées de leurs résultats.`
5. `Paul et Marie sont fatigués de leurs résultats.`

Change the sentence with `heureux`:

1. `Paul est heureux de son résultat.`
2. `Marie est heureuse de son résultat.`
3. `Paul et Marc sont heureux de leurs résultats.`
4. `Marie et Léa sont heureuses de leurs résultats.`
5. `Paul et Marie sont heureux de leurs résultats.`

In your personal sentences, the subject controls the adjective. Mark yourself wrong if a feminine or plural subject still has the masculine singular adjective by accident.

## Unit 4: Adjective Position, Colors, and Nationalities

### Position

Most descriptive adjectives follow the noun:

- `une rue tranquille` — a quiet street
- `un exercice difficile` — a difficult exercise
- `une chemise blanche` — a white shirt

Several frequent adjectives commonly precede the noun, especially short descriptions involving beauty, age, goodness, and size:

- `un beau quartier` — a beautiful neighborhood
- `une jeune étudiante` — a young female student
- `un bon repas` — a good meal
- `une grande ville` — a large city

Learn position as part of the phrase. Instead of memorizing only `difficile`, memorize `un exercice difficile`.

### Forms before a vowel sound

Some masculine singular adjectives have a special form before a vowel sound:

- `beau` -> `bel`: `un bel appartement` — a beautiful apartment
- `nouveau` -> `nouvel`: `un nouvel ami` — a new friend
- `vieux` -> `vieil`: `un vieil hôtel` — an old hotel

### Colors

Colors usually follow the noun and agree:

- `un sac noir` — a black bag
- `une veste noire` — a black jacket
- `des sacs noirs` — some black bags
- `des vestes noires` — some black jackets

Some color expressions derived from nouns may remain unchanged. For now, master the regular high-frequency colors first.

### Nationalities

Nationality adjectives **agree** with the person or noun they describe. This means the adjective changes its ending to match:

- gender: masculine or feminine;
- number: singular or plural.

For example, `canadien` changes like this:

| Person or group | French | English |
|---|---|---|
| masculine singular | `un étudiant canadien` | a Canadian male student |
| feminine singular | `une étudiante canadienne` | a Canadian female student |
| masculine or mixed plural | `des étudiants canadiens` | Canadian male/mixed students |
| feminine plural | `des étudiantes canadiennes` | Canadian female students |

Nationality adjectives are normally lowercase:

- `un acteur français` — a French actor
- `une actrice française` — a French actress
- `des étudiants canadiens` — some Canadian male/mixed students
- `des étudiantes canadiennes` — some Canadian female students

The name of a language is also lowercase:

- `Je parle français.` — I speak French.

When a nationality is used as a noun for a person rather than as an adjective, it is capitalized:

- `Elle est canadienne.` — She is Canadian. (`canadienne` is an adjective.)
- `C'est une Canadienne.` — She is a Canadian woman. (`Canadienne` is a noun.)

### Retrieval check

Correct each phrase:

1. `une voiture noir`
2. `un appartement beau`
3. `une étudiant canadien`
4. `des maisons blanc`

:::answer
1. `une voiture noire` — `voiture` is feminine singular, so the color becomes `noire`.
2. `un bel appartement` — `beau` comes before the noun and becomes `bel` before a masculine noun starting with a vowel sound.
3. `une étudiante canadienne` — `étudiante` is feminine singular, so both the article and nationality adjective must be feminine.
4. `des maisons blanches` — `maisons` is feminine plural, so the color is feminine plural too.

Correct the whole noun phrase, not just the word that looks visibly wrong. Article, noun, and adjective work as a unit.
:::

### Sentence usage practice

Read each sentence aloud. Mark adjectives before the noun with `B` and adjectives after the noun with `A`.

1. `Nous habitons dans une grande ville canadienne.` — We live in a large Canadian city.
2. `Elle porte une petite veste noire.` — She is wearing a small black jacket.
3. `C'est un bel appartement moderne.` — It is a beautiful modern apartment.
4. `Un nouvel étudiant français arrive aujourd'hui.` — A new French student arrives today.
5. `Les vieilles maisons blanches sont près de la rivière.` — The old white houses are near the river.
6. `Je parle français avec mes collègues canadiens.` — I speak French with my Canadian colleagues.

:::answer
Adjective position in the six sentences:

1. `Nous habitons dans une grande ville canadienne.` — `grande` is before the noun; `canadienne` is after.
2. `Elle porte une petite veste noire.` — `petite` is before; `noire` is after.
3. `C'est un bel appartement moderne.` — `bel` is before; `moderne` is after.
4. `Un nouvel étudiant français arrive aujourd'hui.` — `nouvel` is before; `français` is after.
5. `Les vieilles maisons blanches sont près de la rivière.` — `vieilles` is before; `blanches` is after.
6. `Je parle français avec mes collègues canadiens.` — `canadiens` is after `collègues`; `français` here names the language.

The short common adjectives such as `grand`, `petit`, `beau`, `nouveau`, and `vieux` often come before the noun. Colors and nationalities usually come after.
:::

#### Change the sentence

1. Make sentences 1, 2, and 3 plural.
2. Change `noire` to `rouge`, then to `bleue`.
3. Replace `français` in sentence 4 with a nationality that describes you or someone you know.

#### Make it personal

Describe three objects and two people. Use at least one adjective before a noun, three adjectives after nouns, one color, and one nationality.

:::answer
Model memory check:

1. `Nous habitons dans une grande ville canadienne.` — `grande` comes before the noun; `canadienne` comes after it. Both agree with feminine singular `ville`.
2. `Elle porte une petite veste noire.` — `petite` comes before `veste`, while the color `noire` comes after. Both are feminine singular.
3. `C'est un bel appartement moderne.` — `bel` is the masculine singular form of `beau` before a vowel sound. `moderne` follows the noun and already ends in **-e**.
4. `Un nouvel étudiant français arrive aujourd'hui.` — `nouvel` appears before a masculine noun beginning with a vowel sound. `français` is a nationality adjective and stays lowercase.
5. `Les vieilles maisons blanches sont près de la rivière.` — both adjectives are feminine plural because `maisons` is feminine plural.
6. `Je parle français avec mes collègues canadiens.` — `français` is the language, so it is lowercase; `canadiens` agrees with plural `collègues`.

Change the sentence:

1. `Nous habitons dans des grandes villes canadiennes.` — article, noun, and both adjectives become plural.
2. `Elles portent des petites vestes noires.` — plural subject and object require `portent`, `des`, `vestes`, and `noires`.
3. `Ce sont de beaux appartements modernes.` — before a plural adjective placed before the noun, `des` often becomes `de`.
4. `Elle porte une petite veste rouge.` / `Elle porte une petite veste bleue.` — colors follow the noun and agree with feminine singular `veste`.
5. Sample nationality change: `Un nouvel étudiant canadien arrive aujourd'hui.` — the nationality remains lowercase and agrees with masculine singular `étudiant`.

For your personal descriptions, check adjective position first, then agreement. A correct answer must include at least one before-noun adjective and several after-noun adjectives, not just a list of nouns.
:::


## Common mistakes to avoid

1. **Learning a noun without its article.** Learn `la table`, not only `table`.
2. **Assuming every noun ending in `-e` is feminine.** Many are, but words such as `le problème` are masculine.
3. **Forgetting that `l'` can hide either gender.** Record `l'école (f.)` and `l'hôtel (m.)` in vocabulary notes.
4. **Changing only the noun in the plural.** The article and adjective must normally change too: `une petite maison` -> `des petites maisons`.
5. **Adding feminine endings mechanically.** Some adjectives have spelling changes: `heureux` -> `heureuse`, `canadien` -> `canadienne`.
6. **Putting every adjective after the noun.** Most go after, but frequent adjectives such as `petit`, `grand`, `beau`, `jeune`, and `nouveau` often go before.
7. **Capitalizing nationality adjectives.** Write `une étudiante française`, but `une Française` when the nationality is a noun.

## Mini-dialogue: describing a new apartment

Read the dialogue first in French. Then use the English version to check meaning.

**Léa :** `Tu habites dans un nouvel appartement ?` — Do you live in a new apartment?

**Marc :** `Oui. C'est un petit appartement moderne dans un quartier tranquille.` — Yes. It is a small modern apartment in a quiet neighborhood.

**Léa :** `Il y a combien de chambres ?` — How many bedrooms are there?

**Marc :** `Il y a une grande chambre et deux petites chambres.` — There is one large bedroom and two small bedrooms.

**Léa :** `Et la cuisine ?` — And the kitchen?

**Marc :** `La cuisine est blanche, avec une table noire et des chaises rouges.` — The kitchen is white, with a black table and red chairs.

**Léa :** `Tes voisins sont français ?` — Are your neighbors French?

**Marc :** `Une voisine est française et deux voisins sont canadiens.` — One female neighbor is French and two male neighbors are Canadian.

### Guided analysis

1. Find four indefinite articles and explain why each is masculine, feminine, or plural.
2. Find three adjectives placed before nouns.
3. Find five adjectives placed after nouns.
4. Explain the endings in `petites chambres`, `chaises rouges`, and `voisins canadiens`.
5. Replace `appartement` with `maison` and rewrite Marc's first two answers with the required changes.

:::answer
1. Four indefinite articles: `un nouvel appartement` and `un petit appartement` are masculine singular; `un quartier` is masculine singular; `une grande chambre` is feminine singular; `des chaises rouges` is plural. Any four count if you explain the gender or number.
2. Three adjectives before nouns: `nouvel`, `petit`, `grande`, and `petites`.
3. Five adjectives after nouns: `moderne`, `tranquille`, `blanche`, `noire`, `rouges`, `française`, and `canadiens`. Any five count.
4. `petites chambres` is feminine plural because `chambres` is feminine plural. `chaises rouges` is feminine plural. `voisins canadiens` is masculine plural.
5. With `maison`: `Oui. C'est une petite maison moderne dans un quartier tranquille.` Then: `Il y a une grande chambre et deux petites chambres.` The first answer changes because `maison` is feminine; the second answer can stay the same.

The main rule is agreement: when `appartement` changes to `maison`, words attached to that noun must become feminine.
:::

### Core habits

- Memorize article + noun together.
- Check article, noun, and adjective as one agreement group.
- Learn adjective position as part of a useful phrase.
- Read complete phrases aloud because articles often reveal gender and number in speech.
