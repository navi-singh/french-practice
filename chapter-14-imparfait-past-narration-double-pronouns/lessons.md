# Chapter 14 Lessons: The Imparfait, Past Narration, and Double Object Pronouns
This chapter gives you the second major past tense and shows you how French speakers combine it with the passé composé to tell stories naturally. You will also learn how to stack two object pronouns in the correct order, first in ordinary sentences and then in affirmative commands, where the order changes.
## Learning objectives
You will learn to:
1. form the imparfait from the `nous` stem and recognize the special stem `ét-` for `être`;
2. use the imparfait for background description, habitual past action, ongoing action, and past mental or emotional states;
3. contrast the imparfait with the passé composé in narration;
4. interpret and produce `quand` and `lorsque` sentences that combine both past tenses;
5. place two object pronouns before a conjugated verb in the correct order;
6. change that pronoun order correctly in affirmative imperative commands.
## Prerequisite quick check
Before beginning, make sure you can already:
- form the passé composé with both `avoir` and `être` from Chapter 13;
- recognize direct object pronouns such as `le`, `la`, `les` and indirect object pronouns such as `lui`, `leur`;
- distinguish subject agreement from object pronoun agreement in writing;
- conjugate present-tense `nous` forms accurately, because the imparfait stem comes from them.
If Chapter 13 still feels unstable, review it first. The narration contrast in this chapter only makes sense when your passé composé is automatic.
## How to study this chapter
Do not memorize the imparfait as a translation such as “was doing.” Learn the form, then ask what the sentence is doing in context: setting the scene, describing a habit, or showing an action already in progress when something else happened.
## Unit 1: Forming the imparfait
### Core idea
Take the present-tense `nous` form, remove `-ons`, and add the endings `-ais`, `-ais`, `-ait`, `-ions`, `-iez`, `-aient`. The one major irregular verb is `être`, which uses the stem `ét-`.
### The endings
| Pronoun | Ending | Pronunciation note |
|---|---|---|
| `je` | `-ais` | sounds like `eh` |
| `tu` | `-ais` | sounds like `eh` |
| `il/elle/on` | `-ait` | sounds like `eh` |
| `nous` | `-ions` | distinct spoken ending |
| `vous` | `-iez` | distinct spoken ending |
| `ils/elles` | `-aient` | sounds like `eh` |
### Building the stem
| Verb | Present `nous` form | Stem | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| `parler` | `nous parlons` | `parl-` | `je parlais` |
| `finir` | `nous finissons` | `finiss-` | `tu finissais` |
| `vendre` | `nous vendons` | `vend-` | `elle vendait` |
| `être` | `nous sommes` | `ét-` | `nous étions` |
### Full model: `parler`
| Pronoun | French | English |
|---|---|---|
| `je` | `je parlais` | I was speaking / I used to speak |
| `tu` | `tu parlais` | you were speaking |
| `il/elle/on` | `il parlait` / `elle parlait` / `on parlait` | he/she/we was speaking |
| `nous` | `nous parlions` | we were speaking |
| `vous` | `vous parliez` | you were speaking |
| `ils/elles` | `ils parlaient` / `elles parlaient` | they were speaking |
### Full model: `être`
| Pronoun | French | English |
|---|---|---|
| `je` | `j'étais` | I was |
| `tu` | `tu étais` | you were |
| `il/elle/on` | `il était` / `elle était` / `on était` | he/she/we was |
| `nous` | `nous étions` | we were |
| `vous` | `vous étiez` | you were |
| `ils/elles` | `ils étaient` / `elles étaient` | they were |
### Pronunciation note
`je parlais`, `tu parlais`, `il parlait`, and `ils parlaient` all end with the same sound. In speech, the contrast usually appears only in the subject pronoun. `nous parlions` and `vous parliez` sound clearly different.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not build the imparfait from the infinitive.
- incorrect: `*je parlerais` for “I was speaking”
- correct: `je parlais`
Also remember that `je parlerais` is a **conditional** form you will study in Chapter 15.
### Sentence-building practice
1. Conjugate `finir` and `vendre` for `je`, `nous`, and `elles`.
2. Write the full six forms of `être` in the imparfait from memory.
3. Say aloud which three persons share the same spoken ending in most verbs.
## Unit 2: What the imparfait means
### Core idea
The imparfait usually presents a past action or state as ongoing, repeated, descriptive, or unfinished from the speaker's viewpoint.
### Four high-frequency uses
| Use | French example | English |
|---|---|---|
| background description | `Il pleuvait et les rues étaient presque vides.` | It was raining and the streets were almost empty. |
| habitual past | `Nous habitions à Lyon à cette époque.` | We lived / used to live in Lyon at that time. |
| ongoing action | `Je lisais dans ma chambre.` | I was reading in my room. |
| state of mind or body | `Elle avait peur, mais elle voulait essayer.` | She was afraid, but she wanted to try. |
### More examples
- `Quand j'étais petit, je passais tous mes étés chez mes grands-parents.` — When I was little, I spent all my summers at my grandparents' house.
- `Le professeur expliquait la règle très calmement.` — The teacher was explaining the rule very calmly.
- `Nous pensions déjà à nos vacances.` — We were already thinking about our vacation.
- `Vous aviez souvent faim après le cours.` — You were often hungry after class.
### A useful translation warning
The imparfait can mean **was doing**, **used to do**, or simply **did** in the sense of an ongoing background situation. Do not force one English label onto every example.
### Pronunciation note
Because several spoken forms sound identical, time words and context matter a lot: `chaque été`, `souvent`, `à cette époque`, `pendant que`, and `quand` often help you hear the intended meaning.
### Common mistake for English speakers
English speakers often choose the passé composé for every past sentence. French uses the imparfait when the point is the setting, the habit, or the ongoing state rather than the completed event.
### Sentence-building practice
1. Turn these cues into full imparfait sentences: `il pleuvoir`, `nous habiter à Paris`, `je lire dans ma chambre`, `elles être fatiguées`.
2. Write one sentence about a childhood habit and one about yesterday's weather.
3. Decide which meaning is best in English: “was doing,” “used to do,” or simple descriptive past.
## Unit 3: Imparfait and passé composé in narration
### Core idea
The imparfait usually sets the scene; the passé composé usually tells what happened next, what interrupted the scene, or what moved the story forward.
### Contrast table
| Function in the story | Preferred tense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| background, weather, description | imparfait | `La salle était calme.` |
| repeated or habitual situation | imparfait | `Nous arrivions toujours tôt.` |
| completed event | passé composé | `Le concert a commencé.` |
| interruption or new event | passé composé | `Le téléphone a sonné.` |
### Scene + event pattern
- `Je lisais dans ma chambre quand le téléphone a sonné.` — I was reading in my room when the telephone rang.
- `Nous habitions à Paris lorsque nous avons rencontré Léa.` — We were living in Paris when we met Léa.
- `Il faisait beau, puis un vent froid est arrivé.` — The weather was nice, then a cold wind arrived.
- `Pendant que je préparais le café, les invités sont entrés.` — While I was making coffee, the guests came in.
### Two questions to ask yourself
1. Is this the background or the main event?
2. Is the action presented as ongoing/habitual, or as completed and bounded?
### A mini-story
`Le restaurant était plein et tout le monde parlait assez fort. Je cherchais une table près de la fenêtre quand un serveur m'a appelé. Il m'a montré une place tranquille, et je me suis assis tout de suite.`
— The restaurant was full and everyone was speaking rather loudly. I was looking for a table near the window when a waiter called me. He showed me a quiet seat, and I sat down right away.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not use the imparfait just because the English sentence contains “was.” In a sentence such as “The concert was great,” the choice still depends on what the speaker wants to emphasize. Context, not English wording, decides the tense.
### Sentence-building practice
1. Complete the pair: `Je dormais quand...`; `Nous marchions lorsque...`; `Il faisait froid, puis...`.
2. Rewrite `Le téléphone a sonné` as background information and rewrite `Je lisais` as the interrupting event.
3. Write a three-sentence story using at least one imparfait and two passé composé forms.
## Unit 4: Double object pronouns before a conjugated verb
### Core idea
When a sentence has two object pronouns, French uses a fixed order. The whole pronoun block comes before the conjugated verb; in compound tenses, that means before the auxiliary.
### Standard order before the verb
| Position 1 | Position 2 | Position 3 | Position 4 | Position 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `me`, `te`, `se`, `nous`, `vous` | `le`, `la`, `les` | `lui`, `leur` | `y` | `en` |
### Reading the order as a frame
Think of it as a lane system:
- first lane: people close to the verb (`me`, `te`, `se`, `nous`, `vous`)
- second lane: direct objects (`le`, `la`, `les`)
- third lane: indirect objects (`lui`, `leur`)
- fourth and fifth lanes: `y`, then `en`
### Examples
- `Je le lui donne.` — I give it to him/her.
- `Nous les leur montrions.` — We were showing them to them.
- `Elle m'y conduit.` — She takes me there.
- `Ils t'en parlent souvent.` — They often talk to you about it.
- `Paul voulait les billets, alors je les lui ai donnés.` — Paul wanted the tickets, so I gave them to him.
### With the passé composé
Pronouns still come before the auxiliary.
- `Je les lui ai donnés.` — I gave them to him.
- `Nous l'y avons mise.` — We put it there.
### Pronunciation note
In fast speech, these clusters become one rhythm group: `je-le-lui`, `nous-les-leur`, `je-les-lui-ai`. Practice slowly first, then speed up without dropping the order.
### Common mistake for English speakers
English allows more flexible object order in paraphrase, but French pronoun order is fixed. Do not translate word for word from English.
- incorrect: `*Je lui le donne.`
- correct: `Je le lui donne.`
### Sentence-building practice
1. Replace the nouns with pronouns: `Je donne le livre à Marie.`
2. Replace the nouns with pronouns: `Nous montrions les photos aux voisins.`
3. Put the pronouns into a compound tense: `Tu as envoyé la lettre à tes parents.`
## Unit 5: Double pronouns with the affirmative imperative
### Core idea
Affirmative commands use a different order: the pronouns come **after** the verb, joined by hyphens. In addition, `me` and `te` become `moi` and `toi`.
### Order after an affirmative imperative
| Verb | direct object | indirect object / stressed form | `y` | `en` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| command form | `le`, `la`, `les` | `moi`, `toi`, `lui`, `nous`, `vous`, `leur` | `y` | `en` |
### Core examples
- `Donne-le-moi !` — Give it to me!
- `Montrez-les-lui !` — Show them to him/her!
- `Mets-y la chaise !` — Put the chair there!
- `Mets-y-en un peu !` — Put a little of it there!
### Compare ordinary order and imperative order
| Ordinary statement | Affirmative command | English |
|---|---|---|
| `Tu me le donnes.` | `Donne-le-moi !` | Give it to me! |
| `Vous le leur montrez.` | `Montrez-le-leur !` | Show it to them! |
| `Tu y en mets.` | `Mets-y-en !` | Put some there! |
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not keep the ordinary pre-verb order after an affirmative imperative.
- incorrect: `*Le-moi donne !`
- correct: `Donne-le-moi !`
Also remember the form `moi` / `toi`, not `me` / `te`.
### Sentence-building practice
1. Change these statements into commands: `Tu me le passes.`; `Vous les lui envoyez.`; `Tu y en mets.`
2. Change these commands back into ordinary statements.
3. Write three classroom or household commands using double pronouns.

## Mini-dialogue: after the museum visit
**Élise :** `Alors, comment était la visite ?` — So, how was the visit?
**Romain :** `Le musée était calme au début, et peu de gens regardaient les tableaux.` — The museum was quiet at first, and few people were looking at the paintings.
**Élise :** `Et puis ?` — And then?
**Romain :** `Je lisais une explication quand le guide m'a appelé.` — I was reading an explanation when the guide called me.
**Élise :** `Pourquoi ?` — Why?
**Romain :** `Parce qu'il avait des billets supplémentaires, et il me les a donnés.` — Because he had extra tickets, and he gave them to me.
**Élise :** `Tu les as montrés à Clara ?` — Did you show them to Clara?
**Romain :** `Oui, et je les lui ai passés tout de suite.` — Yes, and I handed them to her right away.
### Guided analysis
1. Find two imparfait forms and explain why the imparfait fits.
2. Find two passé composé forms and explain what event each one advances.
3. Replace `je les lui ai passés` with a present-tense version.
4. Turn `il me les a donnés` into an affirmative imperative addressed to `tu`.
5. Rewrite the first answer as if the museum had been crowded and noisy.
## Connected reading: une soirée qui a changé de rythme
`Chaque vendredi, nous dînions chez ma tante et nous restions longtemps à table. Un soir, tout semblait normal: les enfants jouaient dans le salon, les adultes parlaient de la semaine, et la radio passait doucement en arrière-plan. Je coupais le pain quand la lumière s'est éteinte dans tout l'appartement. Ma tante a cherché des bougies, mon oncle nous en a apporté, et mes cousins les lui ont allumées avec beaucoup de soin. Après quelques minutes, l'électricité est revenue, mais la soirée n'avait plus le même calme.`
— Every Friday, we used to have dinner at my aunt's house and stay at the table for a long time. One evening, everything seemed normal: the children were playing in the living room, the adults were talking about the week, and the radio was playing softly in the background. I was cutting the bread when the lights went out in the whole apartment. My aunt looked for candles, my uncle brought us some, and my cousins lit them for her very carefully. After a few minutes, the electricity came back, but the evening no longer had the same calm.
### Reading practice
1. List the imparfait forms and explain the role each one plays.
2. List the passé composé forms and explain which event each one introduces.
3. Find two sentences with object pronouns and identify whether the pronouns are direct or indirect.
4. Change `nous` to `je` in the first sentence and rewrite it correctly.
5. Write three new sentences continuing the story after the electricity returned.
## Common mistakes to avoid
1. Building the imparfait from the infinitive instead of the `nous` stem.
2. Forgetting the irregular stem `ét-` for `être`.
3. Using the passé composé for background weather, description, or habitual action when the imparfait is needed.
4. Using the imparfait for the sudden event that interrupts the scene.
5. Reversing the order of double object pronouns before the verb: `*je lui le donne`.
6. Keeping the normal pronoun order after an affirmative imperative instead of changing to `Donne-le-moi !`
