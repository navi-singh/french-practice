# Chapter 9 Exercises

Complete these without notes. Put a question mark beside uncertain answers.

## A. Transform or translate

1. Je mange le gâteau.
2. Nous parlons à Marie.
3. Ils vont au parc.
4. Elle achète trois pommes.
5. I drink some water.
6. I do not drink milk.

:::answer
These answers match the answer key. The explanations show what each transformation is testing.

1. `Je le mange.` — `le gâteau` is a direct object, so it becomes `le`. Put the pronoun before the conjugated verb.
2. `Nous lui parlons.` — `à Marie` is **à + person**, so it becomes `lui`. Do not use `la`; `parler à` takes an indirect object.
3. `Ils y vont.` — `au parc` is a place, so it becomes `y`. The pronoun goes before `vont`.
4. `Elle en achète trois.` — `trois pommes` is a quantity phrase. Use `en` for the noun and keep the number after the verb.
5. `Je bois de l'eau.` — water is an unspecified amount, so use the partitive `de l'` before a vowel sound.
6. `Je ne bois pas de lait.` — after a simple negative, the partitive becomes `de`. The trap is writing `pas du lait`.
:::

## B. Controlled production

1. Write a new sentence demonstrating **prendre and boire**. Use `Je prends le métro.` only as a structural model; change its content.
2. Write a new sentence demonstrating **partitive articles**. Use `du pain` only as a structural model; change its content.
3. Write a new sentence demonstrating **direct and indirect objects**. Use `Je le vois.` only as a structural model; change its content.
4. Write a new sentence demonstrating **y and en**. Use `J'y vais.` only as a structural model; change its content.

:::answer
Answers vary by design. A correct answer must clearly show the named structure, use the right article or pronoun, and put object pronouns before the conjugated verb.

Sample answers:

1. Prendre and boire: `Nous prenons le bus et nous buvons de l'eau.` — `prendre` and `boire` are both conjugated for `nous`.
2. Partitive articles: `J'achète de la soupe pour le dîner.` — `de la` marks an unspecified amount of a feminine noun.
3. Direct and indirect objects: `Je vois Paul et je lui parle.` — `Paul` is direct after `voir`, but indirect after `parler à`.
4. `y` and `en`: `Je vais au marché et j'en achète deux.` — `au marché` could become `y`; `en` replaces the noun in a quantity phrase.

Mark yourself wrong if the pronoun stays after the verb in a normal statement, if `lui` or `leur` is used for a direct object, or if a negative partitive remains `du`, `de la`, or `des` after `pas`.
:::

## C. Four-way variation

Choose one sentence from each lesson unit and rewrite it four ways:

1. change the subject;
2. make it negative;
3. turn it into a question;
4. change one meaningful detail.

:::answer
Worked example, starting from `Je prends du café au marché.`

1. Change the subject: `Nous prenons du café au marché.` — `prendre` changes to `prenons` with `nous`.
2. Make it negative: `Je ne prends pas de café au marché.` — the partitive `du` becomes `de` after `pas`.
3. Turn it into a question: `Est-ce que je prends du café au marché ?` — statement order stays after `est-ce que`.
4. Change one meaningful detail: `Je prends du thé au marché.`

Check that every pronoun goes before the conjugated verb, and that `y` replaces places while `en` replaces **de + thing** or a quantity phrase.
:::

## D. Dictation and correction

Use text-to-speech to hear six examples from `lessons.md`. Write what you hear without looking. Correct in separate passes:

1. vocabulary;
2. verb form;
3. agreement or pronoun placement;
4. spelling and accents.

Do the dictation first, using this chapter's own examples as your audio. Then open the answers to check: they list six sentences worth dictating and the trap each one sets.

:::answer
Six sentences from this chapter that make good dictation material, and the trap each one sets:

1. `Je prends le métro chaque matin.` — `prends` has a silent final **s**, and `le métro` is a specific object, not a partitive.
2. `Ils boivent de l'eau pendant le cours.` — Listen for `de l'` before the vowel sound in `eau`.
3. `Je ne bois pas de lait.` — The negative changes the partitive to `de`.
4. `Je ne le prends pas.` — The direct object pronoun stays before the verb, inside the negative frame.
5. `Je lui téléphone tous les soirs.` — `lui` is indirect because `téléphoner à` takes `à` before a person.
6. `Il en veut trois.` — `en` comes before the verb, and the number `trois` stays after it.

Correct first for the article, then for pronoun position, then for silent endings. A misplaced pronoun changes the grammar even when the meaning is guessable.
:::

## E. Personal writing

Write a grocery or café dialogue using two partitives, two direct objects, one indirect object, y, and en.

## F. Oral recall

Explain each of the chapter's four main rules aloud in simple English, then give one French example from memory.

:::answer
1. **Prendre and boire.** These are irregular present-tense verbs: `je prends`, `nous prenons`, `je bois`, `nous buvons`. Example: `Je prends le train et je bois de l'eau.`
2. **Partitive articles.** Use `du`, `de la`, `de l'`, or `des` for an unspecified amount, but use `de` after many negatives and quantity expressions. Example: `Je ne mange pas de pain.`
3. **Direct and indirect object pronouns.** Direct objects become `le`, `la`, `l'`, or `les`; many **à + person** objects become `lui` or `leur`. Example: `Je lui parle et je l'écoute.`
4. **Y and en.** `Y` often replaces a place or **à + thing**; `en` replaces **de + thing** or the noun in a quantity phrase. Example: `J'y vais et j'en achète deux.`

If you can explain the rule but cannot produce a new example, redo the exercise tomorrow. Production is the goal.
:::
