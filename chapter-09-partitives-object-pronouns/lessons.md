# Chapter 9 Lessons: Prendre, Boire, Partitive Articles, and Object Pronouns
This chapter helps you speak about food, drink, choice, and replacement. You will learn how French expresses an unspecified quantity, how objects move in front of the verb as pronouns, and how short words like `y` and `en` keep sentences natural and less repetitive.
## Learning objectives
You will learn to:
1. conjugate `prendre`, `apprendre`, `comprendre`, and `boire` in the present tense;
2. use the partitive articles `du`, `de la`, `de l'`, and `des`;
3. contrast partitive, definite, and indefinite articles;
4. replace direct objects with `me`, `te`, `le`, `la`, `nous`, `vous`, and `les`;
5. replace indirect objects with `me`, `te`, `lui`, `nous`, `vous`, and `leur`;
6. use `y` for place and `à + thing`, and `en` for `de + thing` and quantities;
7. place a single pronoun before the conjugated verb with confidence.
## Prerequisite quick check
Before beginning, make sure you can already:
- recognize direct objects and indirect objects in English and in simple French sentences;
- form negative sentences with `ne...pas`;
- use `à` and `de` from Chapter 6;
- follow verb + infinitive patterns from Chapter 7.
This chapter builds directly on those ideas. If you can identify the object in `je prends le livre`, you can turn it into `je le prends`. If you know that `parler à` uses `à`, you can understand why `je lui parle` is indirect rather than direct.
## How to study this chapter
Always start from the full sentence. Identify the noun you want to replace, ask whether it is direct or indirect, and only then choose the pronoun. For food and drink, memorize complete chunks: `du pain`, `de la soupe`, `en prendre`, `y aller`.
## Unit 1: `prendre`, its family, and `boire`
### Core idea
`Prendre` means **to take**, but it also appears in many daily expressions: taking the bus, taking a seat, taking breakfast, taking notes. Its close relatives `apprendre` and `comprendre` follow the same pattern. `Boire` adds an important food-and-drink verb.
### Present tense forms
| Pronoun | `prendre` | `apprendre` | `comprendre` | `boire` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `je` | `prends` | `apprends` | `comprends` | `bois` |
| `tu` | `prends` | `apprends` | `comprends` | `bois` |
| `il/elle/on` | `prend` | `apprend` | `comprend` | `boit` |
| `nous` | `prenons` | `apprenons` | `comprenons` | `buvons` |
| `vous` | `prenez` | `apprenez` | `comprenez` | `buvez` |
| `ils/elles` | `prennent` | `apprennent` | `comprennent` | `boivent` |
### Examples in context
- `Je prends le métro chaque matin.` — I take the subway every morning.
- `Nous prenons un café après le déjeuner.` — We have a coffee after lunch.
- `Elle apprend le français avec sa voisine.` — She is learning French with her neighbor.
- `Tu comprends cette règle ?` — Do you understand this rule?
- `Ils boivent de l'eau pendant le cours.` — They drink water during class.
- `Je prends des notes quand le professeur parle.` — I take notes when the professor speaks.
### Pronunciation note
The plural stem in `prennent`, `apprennent`, and `comprennent` sounds different from `prenons` and `prenez`. Also notice `nous buvons` and `vous buvez`, which sound very different from `je bois`.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not expect `prendre` to translate literally every time. `prendre le métro`, `prendre une décision`, and `prendre un café` are normal French chunks.
### Sentence-building practice
Translate into French.
1. We understand the question.
2. I am learning a new expression.
3. Do you drink tea in the morning?
4. They take the train at eight o'clock.
Then change sentence 4 to `je` and sentence 2 to the negative.
## Unit 2: Partitive articles
### Core idea
Use a partitive article when you mean **some**, **an unspecified quantity**, or **part of a larger whole**.
### Forms of the partitive
| Before | Form | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| masculine singular noun | `du` | `du pain` | some bread |
| feminine singular noun | `de la` | `de la soupe` | some soup |
| vowel or mute h singular noun | `de l'` | `de l'eau` | some water |
| plural noun | `des` | `des légumes` | some vegetables |
### Main uses
- `Je mange du pain.` — I eat some bread.
- `Elle prépare de la soupe.` — She is making some soup.
- `Nous buvons de l'eau.` — We are drinking some water.
- `Ils achètent des fraises.` — They are buying some strawberries.
### Partitive versus definite versus indefinite
| Article type | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| partitive | `Je prends du fromage.` | I am taking some cheese. |
| definite | `Je prends le fromage.` | I am taking the cheese / the particular cheese. |
| indefinite singular | `Je prends un sandwich.` | I am taking a sandwich. |
| indefinite plural | `Je prends des biscuits.` | I am taking some cookies / cookies. |
### After negation and quantity expressions
In many negative sentences or after quantity expressions, the article becomes `de` or `d'`.
- `Je ne bois pas de lait.` — I do not drink milk.
- `Nous n'achetons pas de tomates.` — We are not buying tomatoes.
- `Il prend beaucoup de café.` — He drinks a lot of coffee.
- `Elle veut un kilo de pommes.` — She wants a kilo of apples.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not use `le/la/les` automatically when English has no article. French often needs the partitive: `je bois de l'eau`, not `*je bois eau`.
### Sentence-building practice
Choose the correct article.
1. `Je bois ___ thé.`
2. `Nous mangeons ___ salade ce soir.`
3. `Elle n'achète pas ___ sucre.`
4. `Ils prennent deux tasses ___ café.`
Then explain why sentence 3 and sentence 4 do not use the ordinary partitive form.
## Unit 3: Direct object pronouns
### Core idea
A direct object receives the action **without** a preposition. When you replace it with a pronoun, the pronoun usually goes **before** the conjugated verb.
### Direct object pronouns
| Person or thing replaced | Pronoun | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| me | `me` / `m'` | `Il me voit.` | He sees me. |
| you singular informal | `te` / `t'` | `Je te comprends.` | I understand you. |
| him / it masculine | `le` / `l'` | `Je le prends.` | I take it/him. |
| her / it feminine | `la` / `l'` | `Je la vois.` | I see her/it. |
| us | `nous` | `Elle nous appelle.` | She calls us. |
| you plural/formal | `vous` | `Je vous invite.` | I invite you. |
| them | `les` | `On les apprend.` | We learn them. |
### Position before the verb
| Full sentence | Pronoun version |
|---|---|
| `Je prends le métro.` | `Je le prends.` |
| `Elle invite ses voisins.` | `Elle les invite.` |
| `Nous comprenons la règle.` | `Nous la comprenons.` |
| `Tu manges les pommes ?` | `Tu les manges ?` |
### With negation
The pronoun still stays before the verb, inside the negative frame.
- `Je ne le prends pas.` — I am not taking it.
- `Elle ne nous comprend pas.` — She does not understand us.
### With an infinitive after the verb
With a structure like `vouloir prendre`, the object pronoun usually goes before the infinitive it belongs to in later chapters, but for now focus on the core present-tense pattern with one conjugated verb. In this chapter, master sentences such as `je le prends`, `nous la buvons`, and `ils les comprennent`.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not leave the object pronoun after the verb as in English. French says `je le prends`, not `*je prends le`.
### Sentence-building practice
Replace the underlined noun with a direct object pronoun.
1. `Je prends _le pain_.`
2. `Nous buvons _l'eau_.`
3. `Elle comprend _les instructions_.`
4. `Tu invites _Marie_ ?`
Then turn sentence 1 and sentence 3 negative.
## Unit 4: Indirect object pronouns
### Core idea
An indirect object usually follows `à` before a person: `parler à`, `offrir à`, `écrire à`, `téléphoner à`. When you replace that person, you use an indirect object pronoun.
### Indirect object pronouns
| Person replaced | Pronoun | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| to me | `me` / `m'` | `Il me parle.` | He is speaking to me. |
| to you singular informal | `te` / `t'` | `Je t'écris.` | I am writing to you. |
| to him / to her | `lui` | `Nous lui offrons un café.` | We offer him/her a coffee. |
| to us | `nous` | `Elle nous répond.` | She answers us. |
| to you plural/formal | `vous` | `Je vous téléphone.` | I am calling you. |
| to them | `leur` | `On leur parle.` | We are speaking to them. |
### Direct versus indirect
| Verb pattern | Full sentence | Pronoun version |
|---|---|---|
| direct object | `Je vois Paul.` | `Je le vois.` |
| indirect object | `Je parle à Paul.` | `Je lui parle.` |
| direct object | `Nous prenons les billets.` | `Nous les prenons.` |
| indirect object | `Nous écrivons aux voisins.` | `Nous leur écrivons.` |
### Examples in context
- `Elle parle à ses voisins et elle leur offre du café.` — She speaks to her neighbors and offers them coffee.
- `Je téléphone à ma sœur tous les soirs.` -> `Je lui téléphone tous les soirs.` — I call my sister every evening.
- `Nous écrivons à nos amis.` -> `Nous leur écrivons.` — We write to our friends.
- `Tu me réponds demain ?` — Are you answering me tomorrow?
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not use `le` or `la` after verbs that require `à` before a person. `Je lui parle` is correct; `*je le parle` is not.
### Sentence-building practice
Replace the underlined phrase with an indirect object pronoun.
1. `Je donne le livre _à Paul_.`
2. `Nous écrivons _à nos parents_.`
3. `Elle téléphone _à moi_ chaque matin.`
4. `Vous répondez _à la cliente_ ?`
Then explain why sentence 1 still keeps `le livre`.
## Unit 5: `y`, `en`, and one-pronoun order
### Core idea
`Y` and `en` are short pronouns that replace whole prepositional phrases. They also follow the same basic rule as the other object pronouns: when only one pronoun is used, it comes before the conjugated verb.
### The pronoun `y`
Use `y` for a place or for `à + thing`.
| What `y` replaces | Full sentence | Pronoun version |
|---|---|---|
| place | `Nous allons au marché.` | `Nous y allons.` |
| place | `Elle reste à la maison.` | `Elle y reste.` |
| `à` + thing | `Je pense à mon travail.` | `J'y pense.` |
| `à` + thing | `Tu réponds à la question ?` | `Tu y réponds ?` |
`Y` does **not** usually replace `à + person`.
### The pronoun `en`
Use `en` for `de + thing`, for an unspecified quantity, or after quantity expressions.
| What `en` replaces | Full sentence | Pronoun version |
|---|---|---|
| `de` + thing | `Elle parle de ce film.` | `Elle en parle.` |
| unspecified quantity | `Je prends du pain.` | `J'en prends.` |
| plural quantity | `Il veut trois pommes.` | `Il en veut trois.` |
| source/amount idea | `Nous achetons beaucoup de légumes.` | `Nous en achetons beaucoup.` |
When a number or quantity word is expressed, it stays after the verb: `j'en prends deux`, `nous en voulons beaucoup`.
### One-pronoun order
When only one pronoun is used in the present tense, put it before the conjugated verb.
| Pronoun type | Example |
|---|---|
| direct object | `Je le prends.` |
| indirect object | `Je lui parle.` |
| `y` | `J'y vais.` |
| `en` | `J'en prends.` |
With negation, the pattern remains the same.
- `Je n'y vais pas.` — I am not going there.
- `Nous n'en buvons pas.` — We are not drinking any.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not say `*je vais y` or `*je prends en`. The pronoun moves before the verb: `j'y vais`, `j'en prends`.
### Sentence-building practice
Replace the highlighted phrase with `y` or `en`.
1. `Nous allons _au marché_ le samedi.`
2. `Je bois _du jus_ le matin.`
3. `Elle pense _à son avenir_ souvent.`
4. `Ils veulent _deux billets_.`
Then turn sentence 1 and sentence 2 negative.
## Cumulative sentence-usage practice
These sentences combine food vocabulary, articles, and pronouns the way they appear in everyday French. Read each sentence aloud twice, cover it, and write it from memory.
1. `Je prends du pain et je le mets sur la table.` — I take some bread and put it on the table.
2. `Elle parle à ses voisins et elle leur offre du café.` — She speaks to her neighbors and offers them coffee.
3. `Nous allons au marché le samedi et nous y achetons des légumes.` — We go to the market on Saturdays and buy vegetables there.
4. `Il veut trois pommes, alors il en prend trois.` — He wants three apples, so he takes three of them.
5. `Tu comprends cette recette ou je l'explique encore ?` — Do you understand this recipe, or should I explain it again?
6. `Nous ne buvons pas de lait, mais nous en achetons pour les enfants.` — We do not drink milk, but we buy some for the children.

## Mini-dialogue: at the market
**Vendeur :** `Bonjour, vous prenez quelque chose à boire ?` — Hello, are you taking something to drink?
**Claire :** `Oui, je prends du jus d'orange, et mon frère prend de l'eau.` — Yes, I am taking some orange juice, and my brother is taking some water.
**Vendeur :** `Très bien. Vous voulez aussi des pommes ?` — Very good. Do you also want apples?
**Claire :** `Oui, j'en prends quatre, s'il vous plaît.` — Yes, I am taking four of them, please.
**Vendeur :** `Et ce fromage, vous le connaissez ?` — And this cheese, do you know it?
**Claire :** `Non, mais ma mère le prend souvent et elle en parle beaucoup.` — No, but my mother buys it often and talks about it a lot.
**Vendeur :** `Dans ce cas, j'en donne un petit morceau à goûter.` — In that case, I can give you a small piece to taste.
**Claire :** `Merci ! Je vais y réfléchir, mais je le prends peut-être aussi.` — Thank you! I am going to think about it, but I may take it too.
### Guided analysis
1. Find every partitive article and explain why it is partitive rather than definite or indefinite.
2. Identify each pronoun in the dialogue and label it direct, indirect, `y`, or `en`.
3. Explain why `j'en prends quatre` keeps the number after the verb.
4. Rewrite `ma mère le prend souvent et elle en parle beaucoup` without pronouns.
5. Change Claire's first answer from `je` to `nous`.
## Connected reading: un dîner simple chez moi
`Le soir, je prépare souvent un dîner simple. Je prends des légumes dans le frigo et je les coupe pendant que l'eau chauffe. Ensuite, je fais du riz ou je prends du pain si je manque de temps. Je bois rarement du soda, alors j'en achète peu ; je préfère le thé, et j'en bois presque tous les jours. Quand des amis viennent chez moi, je leur montre la recette et je l'explique étape par étape. À la fin du repas, s'il reste du dessert, nous en mangeons encore un peu.`
— In the evening, I often prepare a simple dinner. I take vegetables from the fridge and cut them while the water heats up. Next, I make rice or take some bread if I am short on time. I rarely drink soda, so I buy little of it; I prefer tea, and I drink some almost every day. When friends come to my place, I show them the recipe and explain it step by step. At the end of the meal, if there is dessert left, we eat a little more of it.

2. Find every pronoun before the verb and label its type.
3. Rewrite the first three sentences without pronouns if possible.
4. Change the reading from `je`/`nous` to `elle`/`elles` where it makes sense.
5. Write three true sentences about your own eating or shopping habits using the reading as a model.
## Common mistakes to avoid
1. **Dropping the article before food or drink.** Say `je bois de l'eau`, not `*je bois eau`.
2. **Using `le` or `la` where the meaning is partitive.** `Je prends du pain` means some bread, not a specific bread.
3. **Leaving the pronoun after the verb.** French says `je le prends`, `je lui parle`, `j'y vais`, `j'en veux`.
4. **Confusing direct and indirect objects.** `Je vois Paul` -> `je le vois`, but `je parle à Paul` -> `je lui parle`.
5. **Forgetting `de` after negation or quantity expressions.** `Je ne bois pas de café`, `beaucoup de lait`.
6. **Using `y` for people.** Usually `y` replaces place or `à + thing`, not `à + person`.
7. **Forgetting to keep the number after `en`.** `J'en prends trois`, not `*j'en trois prends`.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| `je` | `prends` | `apprends` | `comprends` | `bois` |
| `tu` | `prends` | `apprends` | `comprends` | `bois` |

### Article contrast
| Use | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| part of a mass / some | `du`, `de la`, `de l'`, `des` | `du riz`, `de la soupe`, `de l'eau`, `des fruits` |
| definite, specific | `le`, `la`, `l'`, `les` | `le riz sur la table` |
| indefinite countable item | `un`, `une`, `des` | `un café`, `une pomme`, `des biscuits` |
| after negation/quantity in many cases | `de`, `d'` | `pas de lait`, `beaucoup d'eau` |
### One-pronoun chart
| Function | Pronouns |
|---|---|
| direct object | `me`, `te`, `le/la/l'`, `nous`, `vous`, `les` |
| indirect object | `me`, `te`, `lui`, `nous`, `vous`, `leur` |
| place / `à + thing` | `y` |
| `de + thing` / quantity | `en` |
### Core habits
- Identify the full noun phrase before replacing it with a pronoun.
- Ask whether the verb takes its object directly or through `à`.
- Keep one pronoun before the conjugated verb.
- With `en`, keep the number or quantity word after the verb.
