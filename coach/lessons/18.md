# Listen beyond isolated keywords

## Goal and prerequisites

Follow an announcement, a revised arrangement and a speaker's qualified position. Review numbers/time in Chapters 1–4, past narration in Chapters 9–12 and logical links in Chapter 16. Listening is not written French delivered one word at a time: sound groups, reduced syllables and changing information matter.

## Build a situation before chasing details

On the first hearing, identify who is speaking, where and why. Is this an announcement, a negotiation or an interview? Then listen for the decision, condition or attitude asked about. Do not select an answer just because it repeats a familiar noun.

`Le train est maintenu, mais son départ est retardé.` — The train is still running, but its departure is delayed. Maintenu contradicts a cancellation; retardé changes time rather than destination.

`La navette part à quatorze heures trente ; présentez-vous dix minutes avant.` — The shuttle leaves at 2:30 p.m.; arrive ten minutes beforehand. Arrival is 2:20, not 2:30. Distinguish an announced departure from an instruction to arrive.

## Keep the final version of a changing plan

`Nous avions proposé lundi. Finalement, l'intervention aura lieu mercredi après-midi.` — We had proposed Monday. Finally, the work will take place on Wednesday afternoon. The first date is background, not the final answer.

Useful signals include finalement (in the end), plutôt (rather/instead), en revanche (on the other hand), toujours (still, or always depending on context), and désormais (from now on). Ask which information was replaced and which stayed true.

`La réunion reste jeudi. Seule la salle change.` — The meeting remains on Thursday. Only the room changes. Do not move the date because another detail changed.

## Hear the logical relationship

`Si le projecteur ne fonctionne pas, nous distribuerons les documents.` — If the projector does not work, we will distribute the documents. The printouts are conditional, not certain. The ordinary open condition uses si + present, then future; do not put the future directly after this si.

`Il aurait fallu prévenir les participants.` — The participants should have been informed. This evaluates a past action; it does not say they were informed. The past conditional combines avoir/être in the conditional with the past participle.

`Je ne suis pas opposée au projet ; je demande un essai limité.` — I am not opposed to the project; I am asking for a limited trial. Concern is not necessarily rejection.

## Separate viewpoint, evidence and uncertainty

In an interview, the interviewer's summary may be stronger than the guest's actual claim. Track the correction: `Ce n'est pas ce que je dis.` — That is not what I am saying. `Tout dépend des conditions.` — Everything depends on the conditions.

`La fréquentation a augmenté pendant la semaine gratuite.` — Attendance rose during the free week. This does not establish durable demand after fees begin. A speaker may support a project while withholding a conclusion about long-term results.

For the higher target, practise reporting a qualified stance in one sentence: support + condition + remaining unknown. Do not infer anger or certainty from a synthetic voice's prosody; use the actual wording.

## Decode connected speech

In `vous avez` — you have, liaison joins the words with a /z/. In `ils ont été avertis` — they were warned, hear a connected verb chain rather than separate written tokens. In `je ne sais pas` — I do not know, informal speech may reduce or omit ne, but preserve the full form in careful writing.

Contrast `plus de places` — more places, with `il n'y a plus de places` — there are no places left. Context and negation decide meaning; a keyword alone does not.

Listen once without reading. Answer or summarize. Only then reveal the transcript, find the missed sound group and replay a short segment. Slower speech is a decoding tool; return to normal speed afterward. These device-generated voices do not replace exposure to authentic speech or certify readiness.

## Retrieve and transfer

What stays unchanged in “Thursday, different room”? Does “if the equipment fails” mean failure has happened? What cannot be concluded from a free trial?

:::answer
Thursday remains the date. The equipment problem is a possibility, not an established event. A free trial alone does not establish sustained paid demand.
:::

In the new stretch tasks, explain the speaker's degree of commitment, not merely the topic. Relay a changed arrangement to another person without reading the script. Ask them an imagined clarification and answer without inventing missing facts. Return later with a different announcement and note whether normal-speed audio, English support or replay was needed.
