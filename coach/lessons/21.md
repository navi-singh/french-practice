# Speaking: personal exchange and information seeking

## Goal and official task windows

Prepare for the first two TCF Canada speaking tasks without memorizing a universal speech. Task 1 is a two-minute exchange without dedicated preparation. Task 2 provides two minutes of preparation followed by three minutes thirty of dialogue: five minutes thirty in total. These are the same tasks at an NCLC 7 or 8 target.

Review questions in Chapters 3–8, past narration in Chapters 9–12, future/conditional in Chapters 13–14 and responsive follow-ups in Chapter 19. The course models are solo illustrations; a real examiner can redirect the exchange.

## Task 1: answer the question, then develop proportionately

If asked where you live, give the place and a useful detail—not your entire life history. `J'habite dans un quartier assez calme, mais bien relié au centre.` — I live in a fairly quiet neighbourhood, but one well connected to the centre.

If the next question asks what you enjoy there, respond to that change. `Ce que j'apprécie surtout, c'est de pouvoir marcher jusqu'au parc.` — What I particularly appreciate is being able to walk to the park.

Use a past event when asked about a discovery: `Le mois dernier, j'ai découvert un atelier où l'on répare des vélos.` — Last month I discovered a workshop where bicycles are repaired. Use a future or conditional for a project: `J'aimerais y participer plus régulièrement.` — I would like to participate more regularly.

## Show flexibility rather than a memorized performance

A prepared speech can become irrelevant when the question changes. Practise answering your own familiar topic from a new angle: describe a place, explain a choice, recount a change, then discuss a plan.

If you need time, use a brief repair: `Je vais prendre un exemple plus précis.` — I will take a more specific example. If a word is missing, describe it. Intelligibility and a relevant message matter more than sounding native or avoiding every hesitation.

## Task 2: use preparation to map needs

Identify your role, the other person's role and the decision to make. A candidate seeking information should not behave as though they are selling the service. For a photography workshop, useful needs include level, availability, equipment, total cost and weather arrangements.

Write keywords, not full sentences. Full scripted questions consume preparation and make adaptation harder. Do not write the other person's answers before hearing them.

`Faut-il un appareil professionnel ou un téléphone suffit-il ?` — Is a professional camera needed, or is a phone sufficient? If the reply permits a phone, ask about the activity rather than asking the same equipment question again.

## Follow the answer into a new question

Suppose Saturday is full but Sunday is available. Ask whether Sunday suits your constraints. If the price excludes printing, `Combien faut-il prévoir pour les tirages ?` — How much should I budget for prints? uses the new information.

If an outdoor route is cancelled in a storm, `Une autre date est-elle proposée dans ce cas ?` — Is another date offered in that case? is a responsive follow-up. It does not presume a refund.

Check details politely: `Si j'ai bien compris…` — If I have understood correctly… . Distinguish confirmation from an assumption and close with a decision or a remaining question.

## A stronger interaction practice

Use the original scenario first. Then attempt the new stretch scenario with a different service and conflicting constraints. Have three priorities but abandon an irrelevant question when a reply already settles it. Ask at least one clarification about a restriction and one follow-up tied to a newly supplied answer.

Solo role play cannot test whether you truly understood an unexpected human reply. Treat the fixed replies as practice cues, not proof of examiner-level interaction. If another person is available, ask them to vary one condition without telling you in advance.

## Retrieve and review

What belongs in the two-minute preparation: a paragraph to recite, or a role/needs map? What follows “printing costs extra”?

:::answer
Use a role/needs map with keywords. Ask about the print price, whether printing is optional, or what quantity is required. Do not claim the initial fee is the final price.
:::

Record yourself only if you choose. On review, separate relevant answers, developed details, responsive questions, pronunciation/intelligibility and grammatical control. Save one repair target, not a generic judgement that your French was “bad.” Reattempt after a delay with a new topic. Self-ratings do not map to 12/20, and using the full time is not itself a success criterion.
