# Read to make an accurate decision

## Goal and prerequisites

Move from understanding a topic to extracting the rule that changes someone's action. You need present, past and future forms, conditionals, negation and reference words. If these are uncertain, use Chapters 9–16 for focused review; you do not need the original course.

Read a notice as a message from someone to someone for a purpose. A notice may impose a rule; a personal email may request action; an article may report competing explanations. Ask: who is speaking, to whom, why, and by when? A title suggests the topic, but the answer still needs evidence.

## Restrictions, exceptions and deadlines

`L'accès est réservé aux abonnés.` — Access is restricted to subscribers. Do not expand “subscribers” to “everyone.” `Les autres usagers pourront venir vendredi.` — Other users will be able to come on Friday. Group and time belong together.

`Dès mardi` — from Tuesday/as early as Tuesday, gives a starting point. `Avant jeudi` — before Thursday, normally excludes Thursday. `Au plus tard jeudi` — no later than Thursday, includes it. A deadline is not necessarily the event date.

`L'entrée est gratuite sur réservation.` — Entry is free with a reservation. Free does not mean no formalities. `Sauf les jours fériés` — except public holidays, limits a rule. `À condition de présenter un justificatif` — provided that you show supporting documentation, states a requirement.

`Il ne reste que deux places.` — Only two places remain. The construction ne…que means “only,” not a complete negation. `Aucun dossier ne sera traité dimanche.` — No application will be processed on Sunday. `Le service n'est plus disponible.` — The service is no longer available.

## Track what is certain and who did it

The passive uses être in the required tense plus a past participle agreeing with its subject: `Les cartes seront envoyées demain.` — The cards will be sent tomorrow. `Les cartes ont été envoyées hier.` — The cards were sent yesterday. Neither sentence tells you who sent them unless an agent is supplied.

The conditional may report unconfirmed information: `La mairie ouvrirait une annexe.` — The town hall reportedly plans to open a branch/may open one. Do not turn this into a confirmed opening. Form it with the future stem and -ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient. Context distinguishes a report, hypothesis and polite request.

The pluperfect places an event before another past event: `Le fournisseur avait prévenu la mairie avant la fermeture.` — The supplier had warned the town hall before the closure. Form it with avoir/être in the imperfect plus the past participle.

## Follow references rather than nearby words

`La ville propose un local ; l'association y organisera ses permanences.` — The city offers premises; the association will hold its drop-in sessions there. Y refers to the premises. En often replaces de + thing or a quantity: `Elle en a reçu trois.` — She received three of them.

Ce dernier usually points back to the last relevant masculine noun, but check the logic. If two organizations could be elle, reread the whole sentence. Pronoun proximity is a clue, not proof.

## Infer, but do not invent

`Malgré une fréquentation en hausse, le centre réduit ses horaires faute de personnel.` — Despite increased attendance, the centre is reducing its hours because of a staffing shortage. The supported inference is a staffing problem, not falling popularity.

`Selon la directrice, cette mesure serait provisoire.` — According to the director, this measure is reportedly temporary. Attribute the claim and preserve its uncertainty. A journalist reporting an opinion does not necessarily endorse it.

`L'enquête ne porte que sur vingt foyers.` — The survey covers only twenty households. A conclusion about all families would exceed the evidence. For NCLC 8 preparation, explain both what the text supports and what it does not establish.

## A repeatable reading method

First identify situation and purpose. Then locate the evidence needed by the question. Check scope words such as tous, certains, uniquement and sauf; identify the time and the source of a claim. Reject an option that is true elsewhere but not supported here. You need not translate every word.

Practise paraphrasing the evidence: `Les justificatifs sont à joindre.` — Supporting documents must be attached. A simpler equivalent is `Je dois ajouter les documents.` — I must add the documents. Simplification must retain the obligation.

Pronunciation review: avis has a silent final s; dossier ends with a /je/ sound. Listen after your reading attempt, not instead of extracting evidence.

## Retrieve, transfer and review

Explain the difference between “before Thursday” and “no later than Thursday.” Then decide who can use a service that is free only for residents with reservations.

:::answer
Before Thursday normally excludes Thursday; no later than Thursday includes it. Eligibility requires both residence and a reservation. Free access alone removes neither requirement.
:::

Independent interpretation of a fresh passage matters more than memorizing this answer.
