# Ask useful questions and follow the answers

## Goal and prerequisites

Obtain enough information to make a practical decision, then adapt your next question to what you heard. Review question forms in Chapters 3–8 and polite conditional requests in Chapter 14. Interaction is not a memorized sequence of unrelated questions.

## Start from a decision, not a question count

Suppose you need a trolley for moving on Saturday. You need collection/return arrangements, cost and deposit, availability and relevant restrictions. Introduce your purpose briefly: `Je déménage samedi et je voudrais louer un chariot.` — I am moving on Saturday and would like to rent a trolley.

Ask what is not already known. `Le matériel est-il fourni ?` — Is equipment supplied? is unnecessary if the advertisement states that it is. Instead ask `Le tarif comprend-il l'assurance ?` — Does the price include insurance?

Questions can use est-ce que, inversion or ordinary spoken intonation. `Est-ce que je peux le rendre lundi ?` — Can I return it on Monday? `À quelle heure dois-je le rapporter ?` — At what time must I bring it back? Keep a clear structure rather than stacking two question patterns.

## Turn an answer into a follow-up

If the reply is “a deposit of 70 euros,” ask about payment or return: `Quand la caution est-elle remboursée ?` — When is the deposit refunded? If the reply is “collection Friday afternoon,” ask what happens if you finish work late.

`Si je ne peux venir qu'à dix-huit heures, est-ce encore possible ?` — If I can only come at six p.m., is that still possible? Ne…que limits your availability. If the answer settles a point, move on instead of repeating it.

If a term is unclear, repair politely: `Quand vous dites « forfait », qu'est-ce qui est inclus exactement ?` — When you say “package,” what exactly is included? A clarification question is useful interaction, not an admission of failure.

## Ask about conditions without turning them into promises

`À partir de quarante-cinq euros` — from 45 euros, is a starting price, not the price for every option. `Sous réserve de disponibilité` — subject to availability, is not a confirmed booking. `Deux places restantes` — two places remaining, does not mean one registration covers two people.

Use the conditional for courteous requests: `Pourriez-vous préciser les frais ?` — Could you specify the fees? Use the present for a practical rule: `Que se passe-t-il en cas de retard ?` — What happens if someone is late?

## Negotiate a feasible alternative

`Le samedi est complet. Avez-vous une autre séance le week-end ?` — Saturday is full. Do you have another session at the weekend? This accepts the answer and adapts the request.

`Le retrait vendredi me conviendrait, à condition de pouvoir rentrer le matériel lundi.` — Collection on Friday would suit me, provided I could return the equipment on Monday. State the condition instead of silently assuming it.

If a word disappears, describe its function: `L'appareil qui sert à déplacer les cartons.` — The device used to move boxes. Circumlocution keeps the interaction moving without switching the whole exchange to English.

## Close with a checked summary

`Si j'ai bien compris, je retire le matériel vendredi et je le rapporte lundi avant dix heures.` — If I understood correctly, I collect the equipment on Friday and return it on Monday before ten. A summary can reveal a misunderstanding while there is time to fix it.

`Il me reste à vérifier les conditions d'annulation.` — I still need to check the cancellation conditions. Unanswered questions remain unknown; do not supply convenient facts yourself.

## Practice toward greater independence

For a first run, use a short English checklist of needs, not a translated script. For the next, keep only French keywords. Finally use a new scenario without prepared questions. After each reply, produce one follow-up that could not have been fully chosen before hearing it.

Solo practice cannot supply an adaptive interlocutor. In the existing task, cover the scenario replies physically before revealing them one at a time. The new stretch scenario introduces an unexpected constraint; respond to that constraint rather than finishing your original list.

## Retrieve and review

An advertisement says equipment is supplied and courses start at 45 euros. Name two questions still worth asking. Then make a follow-up to “The deposit is refunded after inspection.”

:::answer
Ask the actual fee for your level/session and whether a suitable Saturday place is available. A useful deposit follow-up asks when the inspection happens, what can be deducted, or how the refund is paid. Asking again whether equipment is supplied adds little.
:::

Record one successful follow-up and one missed opportunity in the error log. Listen for question intonation without rushing endings. Return after a delay with different prices, availability and return rules. Evaluate relevance, intelligibility, control and responsiveness separately; a large number of questions is not a score.
