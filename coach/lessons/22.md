# Speaking: sustain a viewpoint without preparation

## Goal and task boundaries

TCF Canada Speaking Task 3 is a four-minute-thirty exchange without dedicated preparation. Express and develop a viewpoint; be ready for the examiner to intervene. The aim is not an uninterrupted memorized monologue. Review argument development in Chapter 20 and clear logical links in Chapter 16.

The higher target calls for more consistent relevant development, flexibility and control. It does not require a fixed number of complex constructions, a native accent or a universal introduction.

## Start with a usable position

For “Can everything be learned alone?”, distinguish access to information from learning every skill independently. `On peut apprendre beaucoup de choses seul, mais pas nécessairement tout maîtriser sans retour extérieur.` — One can learn many things alone, but not necessarily master everything without outside feedback.

This is a position with a limit. It gives you something to develop without claiming that courses are always necessary.

## Develop two different lines of reasoning

One line may concern access: flexible resources help people with irregular schedules. Explain why and give an example. Another may concern feedback: a learner may repeat an error without noticing it. Do not present two restatements of “the internet is convenient” as distinct arguments.

`Une personne qui travaille de nuit peut étudier quand elle est disponible.` — A person who works nights can study when available. `En revanche, elle risque de ne pas repérer certaines erreurs.` — On the other hand, they may not notice certain errors.

An example should show how the reason works. It is not evidence that every worker has that experience. Label a hypothetical case rather than inventing a research result.

## Address the difficult case fairly

If the objection is “What about people with no access to a course?”, do not merely repeat that teachers help. Acknowledge the access problem and offer a proportionate option: occasional feedback, peer exchange or a public resource.

`Faute de cours régulier, un échange ponctuel peut déjà aider à repérer un problème.` — When a regular course is unavailable, an occasional exchange can already help identify a problem.

Keep your original distinction: useful support need not mean dependence on a full-time course. A nuanced answer can revise the scope of a proposal without contradicting itself.

## Use language that supports the thought

`Cela dépend de la compétence visée.` — It depends on the skill being targeted. `À condition que le retour soit précis…` — Provided the feedback is precise… uses the subjunctive after à condition que.

`Si j'avais davantage de retours, je corrigerais plus vite certaines habitudes.` — If I had more feedback, I would correct some habits faster. This hypothetical pattern uses si + imperfect, then conditional.

Avoid inserting a hypothetical simply to display a tense. You can also express a useful qualification accurately with `Dans certains cas…` — In some cases… .

## Manage time through development, not padding

Move from position to reasons, examples, limitation and conclusion as the question requires. Do not allocate rigid minute-long paragraphs that prevent interaction. If the examiner interrupts, answer the question and reconnect to your reasoning.

`Cette remarque m'amène à préciser mon idée.` — That comment leads me to clarify my idea. Then actually clarify it. Stock phrases without content do not create coherence.

On a recording, identify repeated sentences that added no meaning. Replace one with a specific consequence or a useful distinction. If you finish early because you have nothing else to explain, practise deeper development rather than slower recitation.

## Retrieve and transfer

For “Should public services be online only?”, state a position, an access benefit, a counterexample and a practical condition.

:::answer
A defensible position supports online access without making it the only route. It can save travel, but someone without equipment may need help. A maintained assisted channel addresses that limitation. Other coherent positions are possible if they answer the question and develop the trade-off.
:::

The stretch prompt uses another public-policy choice so that memorized learning-alone content will not suffice. Try it with no English assistance first, then review against the rubric. Mark support use honestly. Revisit with changed facts and keep notes in the error log. Neither a model nor solo fluency proves an official NCLC level.
