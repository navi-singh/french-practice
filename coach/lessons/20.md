# Develop and qualify an argument

## Goal and prerequisites

Defend a position with reasons, concrete examples, fair objections and a feasible proposal. Review cause/consequence, concession and reference in Chapter 16, hypotheses in Chapter 14 and purpose/subjunctive in Chapter 15. You do not need to insert every tense into each answer.

## Build a mechanism, not a list of adjectives

`Cette mesure est utile.` — This measure is useful, states a judgement without explaining it. `Elle réduit les déplacements, ce qui libère du temps pour les familles.` — It reduces travel, which frees up time for families, gives a mechanism.

A useful development pattern is claim → why it happens → example → limitation. It is a thinking aid, not a mandatory speech template. On free repair workshops, one reason may concern cost and another waste reduction. Two versions of “it is economical” are not two independent reasons.

## Use examples without fabricating evidence

`Prenons le cas d'une personne qui ne peut pas remplacer son vélo.` — Take the case of someone who cannot replace their bicycle. This is an illustration, not a statistical finding.

`Dans mon expérience, une démonstration ne suffit pas toujours.` — In my experience, a demonstration is not always enough. Personal experience can clarify a mechanism, but does not prove that everyone has the same outcome.

Avoid invented research or numbers. You may say `Il faudrait mesurer les effets avant de généraliser.` — The effects should be measured before extending the measure everywhere.

## Concede without abandoning your reasoning

`Certes, le matériel a un coût ; toutefois, un essai limité permettrait de l'évaluer.` — Admittedly, equipment has a cost; however, a limited trial would allow it to be assessed.

A concession acknowledges something valid. It is not a decorative “however.” Follow it with a response to that actual issue. A funding objection needs a funding or scope response, not another claim about convenience.

`Même si le service est gratuit, certaines personnes ne pourront pas venir le soir.` — Even if the service is free, some people will not be able to come in the evening. Même si ordinarily takes the indicative in this construction. `Bien que le projet soit utile, son accès reste inégal.` — Although the project is useful, access remains unequal. Bien que takes the subjunctive.

## Make proportionate claims

Tous, toujours and jamais make strong claims that need strong support. `Cela peut faciliter l'accès.` — This can improve access, is more cautious than “it solves access for everyone.”

`Je serais favorable à un essai, à condition qu'un accueil non numérique soit maintenu.` — I would support a trial provided non-digital assistance were maintained. The conditional expresses a qualified position; à condition que takes the subjunctive.

Do not soften every sentence until your position disappears. State a clear recommendation and specify the limits that matter.

## Answer the objection actually raised

If someone asks “Who pays for materials?”, distinguish volunteers' time from purchased supplies. If they ask about limited places, address allocation rather than funding. `On pourrait réserver certaines places aux personnes orientées par les associations.` — Some places could be reserved for people referred by associations.

A good proposal connects obstacle and response. If cost is low but opening hours exclude workers, another discount does not solve the stated problem. An evening session might, provided staffing is feasible.

## Sound coherent rather than rehearsed

Use connectors for meaning: donc gives a consequence, pourtant a surprising contrast, en revanche another balancing aspect. Pause between ideas, not after each word. If you lose the thread, `Pour revenir au problème principal…` — To return to the main issue… can help, but do not use it repeatedly to evade the question.

## Retrieve and transfer

Give a reason, limitation and feasible response for replacing a physical help desk with an online form. Do not use unsupported statistics.

:::answer
An online form can reduce travel and simplify routine requests. However, people without suitable equipment or confidence may lose access. Keeping a scheduled assisted service while testing the form addresses that obstacle. The usefulness of the form does not by itself justify removing all other channels.
:::

In the stretch activities, explain a causal limit and answer two different objections. On a second run, change the audience or budget and adapt your recommendation. Review fulfilment, development, coherence, language control and intelligibility separately. A sophisticated connector or long response is not evidence of an NCLC result.
