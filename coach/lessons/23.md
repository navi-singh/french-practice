# Writing: a useful message and an experience report

## Goal and official ranges

TCF Canada writing contains three tasks in sixty minutes. Task 1 asks for 60–120 words; Task 2 for 120–150 words. The course's individual task timers are practice allocations, not separate official time limits. Review everyday messages in Chapter 4, past narration in Chapters 9–12 and cohesion in Chapter 16.

At the higher target, first fulfil the task reliably. A fluent paragraph that misses the requested date, explanation or reader is not rescued by advanced vocabulary.

## Task 1: identify the action the reader must take

Before writing, identify who receives the message, your relationship, why you write and what the reader should know or do. A changed invitation may require a reason, new day/time/place, suggested contribution and confirmation.

`La visite est reportée à dimanche après-midi en raison des travaux.` — The visit has been postponed to Sunday afternoon because of building work. Add the actual time and meeting place instead of assuming “afternoon” is precise enough.

`Pourrais-tu me confirmer ta présence avant vendredi ?` — Could you confirm your attendance before Friday? This makes the requested action and deadline explicit.

## Keep register consistent

With a friend, tu and a friendly greeting fit. With a service, use vous and a suitable closing. Do not switch between them accidentally.

`Merci de me préciser les documents nécessaires.` — Please specify the necessary documents. `Je vous remercie pour votre aide.` — Thank you for your help. Learn useful phrases as adaptable tools, not an entire reusable answer regardless of prompt.

## Task 2: report an experience for a reader

A report for an association bulletin needs context, selected events and justified comments that help members decide. It is not a generic essay on why workshops are beneficial.

`Pendant l'atelier, nous avons d'abord choisi le papier, puis assemblé les pages.` — During the workshop, we first chose the paper, then assembled the pages. These completed steps use the passé composé.

`La salle était calme et l'animatrice expliquait chaque geste.` — The room was quiet and the instructor was explaining each movement. The imperfect sets background or an ongoing situation.

`J'avais déjà essayé cette technique, mais je n'avais jamais réussi la reliure.` — I had already tried this technique, but had never succeeded at binding. The pluperfect places experience before the workshop.

## Link evaluation to an observed detail

`Le petit groupe m'a permis de demander une deuxième démonstration.` — The small group allowed me to ask for a second demonstration. This explains the judgement rather than saying “it was excellent.”

`Une séance plus longue permettrait de terminer sans se presser.` — A longer session would allow participants to finish without rushing. The improvement addresses a described constraint. Do not propose an unrelated feature just to include a conditional.

Select two meaningful moments rather than narrating every minute. Use paragraphs to distinguish context, experience and appraisal if useful, without treating that as a compulsory formula.

## Edit in the right order

First check complete instructions and audience. Then check time sequence and pronoun reference. Finally check articles, agreements, verb endings and word count.

`Les activités que nous avons choisies…` — The activities that we chose… has agreement of choisies with the preceding direct object les activités. Use the rule where it applies; do not add an s to every participle after avoir.

Whitespace counting in the site treats an apostrophe/hyphen group as one word. It is an aid; follow the current official instructions. Do not aim at the exact boundary when a small edit could push you outside the range.

## Retrieve, transfer and review

A bulletin report says only “The workshop was interesting and everyone should attend.” What is missing? In a changed invitation, what must “see you Sunday” be supplemented with?

:::answer
The report needs concrete experience and reasons useful to the intended readers, not an unsupported universal recommendation. The invitation needs the required reason, precise time/place and other requested actions such as confirmation.
:::

The stretch writing task uses a different event with both a strength and a difficulty. Write before viewing its illustrative model. On revision, change one vague judgement into a detail-plus-effect sentence. Later write on a fresh experience within the range and without English support. A corrected repeat is useful learning but not an independent new result.
