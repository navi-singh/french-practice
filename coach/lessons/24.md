# Writing: compare sources before arguing

## Goal and official task constraints

Writing Task 3 requires 120–180 words in total: a 40–60-word presentation of the supplied viewpoints, followed by an 80–120-word personal position. Both subranges and the total matter. The three writing tasks share sixty minutes. Review argument in Chapter 20, clear references in Chapter 16 and editing in Chapter 23.

The higher target is not a more ornate summary. It is a faithful, concise comparison followed by a developed, relevant position with controlled language.

## Read the sources as two positions

For each document, identify the claim, reason and qualification. Ask whether the documents genuinely disagree or support the same aim under different conditions.

`Le premier auteur soutient l'élargissement des horaires ; le second l'accepte sous certaines conditions.` — The first author supports longer hours; the second accepts them under certain conditions. This is not “one is for, the other is against.”

`Tous deux reconnaissent l'utilité du service, mais ils diffèrent sur les moyens à prévoir.` — Both recognize the service's usefulness, but differ over the resources needed.

Do not attribute your own conclusion to either source. Keep a suggested effect separate from an observed result: `Cela pourrait réduire les déplacements.` — This could reduce travel, does not mean travel has already fallen.

## Paraphrase without changing scope

`Certains usagers rencontrent des difficultés.` — Some users encounter difficulties. “All users are excluded” changes both group and intensity. `Un essai limité est proposé.` — A limited trial is proposed. “A permanent policy has been adopted” changes status and duration.

Paraphrasing involves rebuilding the idea, not swapping every word for a synonym. A useful sequence is: read → look away → explain the claim simply → reopen and check its limits. Retain essential terms when a synonym would distort them.

Source attribution helps: `Selon le premier document…` — According to the first document… . But repeated attribution phrases can consume the short summary range. Combine the positions precisely.

## Separate summary from opinion

In the first part, represent both documents without slipping in “fortunately” or “absurd.” In the second, give your own position, mechanism, example and relevant limitation.

`Pour ma part, je privilégierais un essai avant une généralisation.` — For my part, I would favour a trial before extending the measure. Explain what the trial should test and why that evidence matters.

An opinion can agree partly with both documents. It must still answer the actual subject. Avoid a memorized conclusion about technology, education or society that could fit almost any prompt.

## Develop within the word budget

Forty to sixty words is enough for a concise comparison, not a detailed retelling. Eighty to one hundred twenty words gives space for one developed recommendation with a limitation; it does not require several shallow reasons.

Plan the two parts separately and leave a small margin within the ranges. For example, 50 words plus 95 words is 145 and fits all three limits. A 70-word summary and 80-word opinion has a valid total but an invalid summary range.

In your draft, use a blank line to make the parts visible. The app's total counter does not verify the two official subranges for you: count/check them separately.

## Review the language after the reasoning

Check reference: who is “they”? Check modality: could, should, will? Check time: proposed, being tested, established? Then inspect agreements, articles, prepositions and sentence boundaries.

`Il faudrait que les résultats soient publiés.` — The results would need to be published. Falloir que takes the subjunctive. Use it only if the meaning requires a recommendation. A simpler accurate sentence remains valid.

## Retrieve and transfer

Document A supports longer library hours for workers. Document B says longer hours may help but require safe transport for staff. Are they opposites? Is a 65-word summary valid if the total is 150?

:::answer
They share an interest in access; the second adds implementation conditions. The summary is too long even though the total fits. All specified ranges must be respected.
:::

Use the new two-document stretch task without opening the model. After writing, underline which idea came from each source and circle your own recommendation. Remove a distortion before polishing vocabulary. Repeat later with different documents; familiarity with one model is not readiness evidence.
