# French coach plan: orientation

## Start here

This is an independent, free-to-use French study route for an adult beginner preparing for **TCF Canada**. It is not TCF tout public, TEF Canada, or an official course. You do not need prior French, an account, a tutor, paid software, or an outside learning service to use the required material. The activities, prompts, explanations, and models are designed to work on your own. Optional free conversation with another learner can add interaction, but is never required.

If you are starting from zero, begin with **Phase 1 / Chapter 1**. Learn the sound of French, greetings, and the most useful phrases before worrying about a test score. Take the diagnostic if you want a snapshot; it is intentionally short and offers easier and harder items in each skill. If its early questions feel unfamiliar, stay with Phase 1. If the first items are comfortable, try the later items and use the recommendations below to decide what to review. You can open any chapter at any time: skip suggestions are advice, not locks.

Strong English or a previous French course cannot establish your current French ability. Keep listening, reading, speaking, and writing records separate. A strong result in one skill does not compensate for a weak result in another.

## The exam and your target

TCF Canada has four mandatory abilities. France Éducation international's TCF Canada page lists 39 listening questions in 35 minutes, 39 reading questions in 60 minutes, three writing tasks in 60 minutes, and three speaking tasks in 12 minutes including two minutes of preparation. Those are the official section-level formats, not a promise that these original practice tasks reproduce an official administration.

For the upgraded Express Entry **NCLC 8** goal, track each ability separately:

| Ability | Minimum target |
| --- | ---: |
| Listening | 503 |
| Reading | 499 |
| Speaking | 12/20 |
| Writing | 12/20 |

NCLC 7 corresponds to listening 458–502, reading 453–498 and speaking/writing 10–11/20. NCLC 8 corresponds to listening 503–522, reading 499–523 and speaking/writing 12–13/20. Higher levels also meet a minimum-NCLC-8 target. The exam format does not change with your target.

Use **NCLC 8 route** in the coach navigation for the phased transfer plan and self-review criteria. Chapters 17–24 now offer English teaching explanations and four new stretch activities each. Their original French explanations remain optional reading. The banks retain at least 100 items per chapter.

English supports learning, not exam performance: first attempt the French task, then explicitly request English guidance in learning mode. This use is recorded as assistance, not independent evidence. French-only chapter runs, checkpoints and mocks withhold help until review. Models and explanations remain unavailable until an attempt is finished.

These figures are score thresholds, not practice percentages. The assessments here have not been psychometrically calibrated to TCF scores; **never convert a number or percentage of correct practice answers into an NCLC level**. Practice completion, objective responses, and speaking/writing self-assessment are different kinds of evidence. Only an accepted official examination establishes an official result. Check current IRCC requirements and the official FEI pages before registering, since rules and materials can change.

The target date of **January 15, 2027** is a planning goal, not a readiness prediction. Starting from zero and reaching all four thresholds in roughly twelve weeks is highly ambitious. Four study hours on six days a week is a substantial commitment, but there is no reliable hour-to-NCLC conversion and no guarantee of a result by that date. If a fresh, timed task in any ability remains difficult near the date, treat that as a reason to revise the plan or seek qualified human feedback—not as a failure of willpower.

## A beginner-friendly weekly rhythm

Use a four-hour core session on six days each week. The time blocks below are flexible planning suggestions, not a scientifically established best allocation:

| Activity | Suggested time |
| --- | ---: |
| Retrieve earlier words and phrases; practise pronunciation and a small language point | 45 min |
| Listen first, answer, then reveal and review a short transcript | 45 min |
| Read a message or passage and explain its purpose, details, and viewpoint | 40 min |
| Speak aloud from a prompt, role-play a practical exchange, then self-review | 45 min |
| Write a short message or connected response, then compare with a model | 45 min |
| Review the error log and plan the next spaced retrieval | 20 min |

Take breaks between blocks. On the other five study days, adjust the balance to the abilities that need attention. The seventh day is for rest or optional enjoyable French; it is not a mandatory catch-up day. If you have additional time, use it for light listening, rereading only after an attempt, or another retrieval of difficult material—not to silently raise the required core workload.

### Optional extra time

If you choose to add two hours on a particular study day, an entirely internal extension could be 30 minutes retrieving missed items, 45 minutes of accessible French listening and reading, and 45 minutes of fresh speaking and writing. For a third optional hour, add easy French input that you can follow without turning it into another test. These 2–3 hours are optional, not part of the four-hour core and not a daily expectation; rest or stop if additional work is not useful.

### Adjustable six-study-day chapter cycle

As a calendar illustration only, you could work through two chapters during a six-day study week: use the first three study days for one chapter and the next three for the following chapter. Spread each chapter's sentence bank across those days—items 1–30, then 31–65, then 66–100—rather than trying to finish all 100 at once. On **each** of the six days, include some retrieval of earlier language and activity across all four abilities: listening, reading, speaking, and writing. The bank is one source of practice, not a substitute for connected texts or original production prompts.

This two-chapter pace is a planning illustration, not a scientifically proven learning rate, a required pace, or evidence that you will be ready by January 15. Slow down, repeat a chapter, or revisit an earlier phase when fresh work shows that a skill needs it. After every four chapters, take that phase's checkpoint **instead of** the ordinary chapter-practice blocks for a scheduled study session; it is not an extra compulsory workload. If you need more time to complete the checkpoint, spread it across scheduled study sessions and replace the corresponding practice blocks. Keep the seventh day for rest.

### Retrieval and spacing without overclaiming

Try to recall a phrase or explain an idea before opening its answer. Revisit difficult items after a delay—for example, the next day, several days later, and about a week later—and vary the context when you can. Keep a brief error log: what you misunderstood or could not express, what clue or form helped, and one new example to retrieve later. Mix old and new language in practice.

Practice testing and distributing review over time have evidence supporting learning in a range of contexts. That evidence does **not** prove that this exact course, its chapter order, a particular study schedule, or synthetic speech guarantees faster learning or exam success. Use spacing as a sensible study method, not as a promise.

## Six phases and checkpoints

The 24 chapters are grouped into six four-chapter phases: Foundations; Everyday transactions; Past narration; Planning and reasoning; Comprehension and argumentation; and TCF production focus. All four abilities begin early and recur throughout. Grammar is taught in context to support communication; there is no separate grammar test section in the TCF Canada mocks.

After each phase, take the corresponding four-skill checkpoint. Use it to find what to retrieve and practise next, not to unlock the course. If an objective listening or reading task is missed, replay or reread only after answering and note the clue that distinguishes the correct option. If you repeatedly miss times, numbers, negation, speaker intent, or implied meaning, choose a chapter/activity that practises that exact listening skill. For reading, review scanning for dates and conditions, reference words, purpose, contrast, and the difference between a stated fact and an inference.

For speaking, record yourself only if you choose and your device supports it; otherwise use the spoken timer and rubric manually. Practise answering the exact prompt, adding reasons/examples, organizing ideas, and repairing a pause with a simpler phrase. For writing, check task fulfilment, reader and register, organization, connections between ideas, and word count before comparing with a model. Models are examples, not the only correct answers. Self-assessment is not examiner feedback: no automated grammar correction, pronunciation grade, or NCLC prediction is provided.

If one ability lags, spend the next sessions on that ability while retaining brief retrieval in the other three. If all four feel difficult, return to the relevant earlier phase rather than repeating a full mock immediately. A checkpoint or mock score does not tell you which chapter you must take, and no score bars an activity.

## Three original full-format practice sets

Each mock uses the official section totals: 39 listening questions / 35 minutes, 39 reading questions / 60 minutes, three speaking tasks / 12 minutes, and three writing tasks / 60 minutes. Each prompt, recording script, passage, answer, and model in this plan is original. These are **unofficial, uncalibrated practice sets**, not official or leaked questions, and their difficulty is not validated against live TCF forms.

FEI's *Manuel du candidat toutes déclinaisons* (version Q, September; printed page 18), checked **October 21, 2026**, specifies the TCF Canada speaking-task windows: Task 1 is a 2-minute exchange without preparation; Task 2 is a 3-minute-30-second dialogue plus 2 minutes of preparation; Task 3 is a 4-minute-30-second exchange without preparation. Thus the task timers are 120, 330 (including the 120-second preparation), and 270 seconds, totalling the official 720 seconds. The manual describes Task 2 as an information-seeking interaction in an everyday situation, with the roles stated in the prompt. Here, models are illustrative solo practice: pause after a question, imagine a brief response, then continue with a follow-up. This does not reproduce a responsive interlocutor or examiner assessment.

The FEI candidate manual (version Q, September; printed pages 19–20) and the official FEI sample writing PDF for TCF TP / TCF Canada / TCF Québec, checked **October 21, 2026**, give these task limits: Task 1, 60–120 words; Task 2, 120–150 words; Task 3, 120–180 words. Task 3 asks for a 40–60-word presentation of the supplied viewpoints and an 80–120-word position. The section lasts 60 minutes; the per-task writing timers in the mocks are suggested practice allocations, not a claim that FEI assigns those exact minutes. FEI defines a word as a group of signs separated by spaces; hyphenated forms count as one under its example. Follow the current instructions given for your session.

Listening uses **synthetic, browser-generated French speech**, not authentic recordings. Voices, accent, prosody, hesitation, speed, and availability vary by device and may not resemble the official test. Browser speech may rely on a device provider or network. For an exam-style attempt, each delivered script plays once at ordinary speed; the sequence is forward-only, with no return to a previous item. If synthesis fails before an item is delivered, it is not valid evidence and may be retried. Optional slow replay belongs to learning practice, not a readiness claim. If audio fails, do not count the attempt as evidence that you heard it.

Speaking and writing models are illustrative self-study aids, not responsive human interlocutors or examiner corrections. Optional free language exchanges may provide useful interaction if you want them, but the course never requires another person. If you record yourself, do so only with explicit permission on your device; the application is designed for local practice, not uploaded grading. You may instead speak without recording and use the checklist.
