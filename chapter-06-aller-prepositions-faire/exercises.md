# Chapter 6 Exercises

Complete these without notes. Put a question mark beside uncertain answers.

## A. Transform or translate

1. I am going to travel.
2. to Canada
3. from France
4. to the United States
5. nous / faire
6. The weather is nice.

:::answer
1. `Je vais voyager.` — Near future uses `aller + infinitive`: `vais` is conjugated, `voyager` stays infinitive.
2. `au Canada` — Canada is masculine in this pattern, so `à` + `le Canada` becomes `au Canada`.
3. `de France` — France is feminine, and origin from a feminine country usually uses `de` with no article.
4. `aux États-Unis` — The United States is plural, so to or in takes `aux`.
5. `nous faisons` — `faire` is irregular; the `nous` form is `faisons`, with s pronounced like z.
6. `Il fait beau.` — Weather uses `il fait`, not `il est`, in this common expression.
:::


## B. Controlled production

1. Write a new sentence demonstrating **aller and the near future**. Use `Je vais partir.` only as a structural model; change its content.
2. Write a new sentence demonstrating **à and de**. Use `au marché` only as a structural model; change its content.
3. Write a new sentence demonstrating **geographical names**. Use `en France` only as a structural model; change its content.
4. Write a new sentence demonstrating **faire**. Use `Il fait froid.` only as a structural model; change its content.

:::answer
Answers vary. A correct sentence must show the target structure and use the right preposition or verb form.

Sample answers:

1. Aller and near future: `Nous allons visiter le musée demain.` — `allons` is conjugated and `visiter` stays infinitive.
2. `À` and `de`: `Je vais au bureau après le déjeuner.` — `à` + `le bureau` contracts to `au bureau`.
3. Geographical names: `Elle habite au Canada.` — Canada takes `au` in this pattern.
4. Faire: `Il fait beau aujourd'hui.` — Weather uses `faire` with impersonal `il`.

Mark yourself wrong if your near-future sentence conjugates both verbs, if you write `à le` or `de le`, or if your weather sentence uses `être` instead of `faire`.
:::


## C. Four-way variation

Choose one sentence from each lesson unit and rewrite it four ways:

1. change the subject;
2. make it negative;
3. turn it into a question;
4. change one meaningful detail.

:::answer
Worked example, starting from `Nous allons visiter Québec demain.`

1. Change the subject: `Je vais visiter Québec demain.` — `allons` changes to `vais`; `visiter` stays infinitive.
2. Make it negative: `Nous n'allons pas visiter Québec demain.` — The negative wraps `allons`.
3. Turn it into a question: `Est-ce que nous allons visiter Québec demain ?` — Normal word order follows `est-ce que`.
4. Change one meaningful detail: `Nous allons visiter Montréal demain.` — The destination changed, but the grammar frame stayed the same.

Check that a subject change affects the conjugated verb only. In `aller + infinitive`, the infinitive should not receive a subject ending.
:::


## D. Dictation and correction

Use text-to-speech to hear six examples from `lessons.md`. Write what you hear without looking. Correct in separate passes:

1. vocabulary;
2. verb form;
3. agreement or pronoun placement;
4. spelling and accents.

Do the dictation first, using this chapter's own examples as your audio. Then open the answers to check: they list six sentences worth dictating and the trap each one sets.

:::answer
Six sentences from this chapter that make good dictation material, and the trap each one sets:

1. `Je vais à la bibliothèque après le déjeuner.` — `vais` is the irregular `je` form of `aller`, and `à la` does not contract.
2. `Nous n'allons pas prendre le train demain.` — the negative wraps `allons`; `prendre` stays infinitive.
3. `Je vais au bureau.` — `à` plus `le bureau` contracts to `au bureau`.
4. `Elle vient de France, mais elle habite au Canada.` — origin from a feminine country is `de France`; location in Canada is `au Canada`.
5. `Il fait froid, alors je vais mettre mon manteau.` — weather uses `faire`, and the near future keeps `mettre` as an infinitive.
6. `Nous allons faire les courses avant de rentrer chez nous.` — `faire les courses`, `avant de rentrer`, and `chez nous` are fixed chunks worth memorizing whole.

Correct prepositions as complete phrases. If you wrote the right verb but the wrong contraction, the sentence still needs another pass.
:::

## E. Personal writing

Plan a four-day trip. State destinations, origins, weather, and at least four near-future activities.

:::answer
Answers vary. A model plan:

1. `Lundi, je pars de Montréal et je vais aller à Québec.`
2. `Il va faire frais, alors je vais mettre mon manteau.`
3. `Mardi, je vais visiter un musée près du fleuve.`
4. `Mercredi, je vais faire une promenade chez des amis à Trois-Rivières.`
5. `Jeudi, je vais revenir du Canada vers les États-Unis.`
6. `S'il fait beau, je vais acheter du pain au marché.`

Self-check: include at least one origin phrase, at least one destination, a weather expression with `faire`, and four near-future forms such as `vais visiter` or `va faire`. Mark yourself wrong if you conjugate the infinitive after `aller`, or if the country and city prepositions do not match the chart.
:::


## F. Oral recall

Explain each of the chapter's four main rules aloud in simple English, then give one French example from memory.

:::answer
1. **Aller and the near future.** Conjugate `aller`, then add an unchanged infinitive. Example: `Je vais partir demain.`
2. **À and de.** These prepositions contract with `le` and `les`. Example: `Nous allons au marché.`
3. **Geographical names.** Cities, countries, and plural place names use different prepositions. Example: `Elle vient de France et habite au Canada.`
4. **Faire.** `Faire` is irregular and appears in weather and activity expressions. Example: `Il fait beau, et nous faisons une promenade.`

If you can say the English rule but not give a new French example, redo the shortest drill for that unit before moving on.
:::
