# Chapter 6 Lessons: Aller, Prepositions, Geography, and Faire
This chapter expands what you can do with motion, place, and everyday action. You will learn how French talks about going somewhere, where things are located, where people come from, what is about to happen, and what someone is doing when the verb `faire` appears in weather, activities, and common expressions.
## Learning objectives
You will learn to:
1. conjugate `aller` confidently in the present tense;
2. use `aller + infinitive` to talk about the near future;
3. choose between `à` and `de` and form the contractions `au`, `aux`, `du`, and `des`;
4. use common prepositions of location and sequence, including `chez`, `entre`, `devant`, and `avant de`;
5. ask and answer questions built after prepositions, including `à qui`, `de quoi`, and forms of `lequel`;
6. choose the correct preposition with cities, countries, and regions;
7. conjugate `faire` and use it for weather, activities, and fixed expressions.
## Prerequisite quick check
Before beginning, make sure you can already:
- conjugate `être` and `avoir` in the present tense;
- make negative sentences with `ne...pas`;
- recognize singular and plural noun phrases;
- understand that a French infinitive is the dictionary form of the verb, such as `parler`, `finir`, or `prendre`.
You will use all of those skills in this chapter. For example, `nous allons partir`, `il ne va pas venir`, and `elles font attention` all depend on earlier chapters.

:::answer
Check these five basics before you begin.

1. Give `avoir` with `nous`. — `nous avons`. You will need this kind of irregular form beside `aller` and `faire`.
2. Make `Je vais partir` negative. — `Je ne vais pas partir`. The negative wraps the conjugated verb `vais`, not the infinitive `partir`.
3. Is `les livres` singular or plural? — plural. Plural noun phrases trigger contractions such as `aux` and `des` when they follow `à` or `de`.
4. Find the infinitive in `elles vont faire attention`. — `faire`. The infinitive is the unchanged dictionary form after the conjugated `vont`.
5. Give `être` with `il`. — `il est`. You still need `être`, but weather expressions in this chapter usually use `faire`, as in `Il fait froid`.

If you missed two or more, review the earlier verb and negation chapters first. This chapter has many small words, and they depend on recognizing the verb and noun phrase accurately.
:::

## How to study this chapter
Say the conjugation tables aloud before you try to memorize them silently. With prepositions, always learn a short phrase rather than a single word: `au Canada`, `chez mes parents`, `avant de sortir`, `faire du vélo`.
## Unit 1: The verb aller in the present tense
### Core idea
`Aller` means **to go** and is one of the most useful irregular verbs in French. Learn all six forms as a complete block.
### Present tense of aller
| Pronoun | Form | English idea |
|---|---|---|
| `je` | `vais` | I go / I am going |
| `tu` | `vas` | you go / you are going |
| `il/elle/on` | `va` | he/she/one goes |
| `nous` | `allons` | we go |
| `vous` | `allez` | you go |
| `ils/elles` | `vont` | they go |
### Main uses of aller
Use `aller` to talk about movement to a destination or about how things are going in a general sense.
- `Je vais à la bibliothèque après le déjeuner.` — I go to the library after lunch.
- `Tu vas souvent chez ta sœur le dimanche ?` — Do you often go to your sister's place on Sundays?
- `Nous allons au marché très tôt.` — We go to the market very early.
- `Ils vont en ville à pied.` — They go into town on foot.
- `Comment va ton grand-père ?` — How is your grandfather doing?
- `Ça va bien aujourd'hui.` — It is going well today.
### Pronunciation note
The forms `vais`, `vas`, and `va` all sound different from their spelling suggests:
- `vais` sounds roughly like **vè**;
- `vas` sounds like **va**;
- `va` sounds like **va**;
- `vont` ends with a silent `-nt`.
The written endings are irregular, so you cannot predict them from a regular pattern. Memorize them by sound and by sight.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not try to build `aller` from the endings of a regular `-er` verb. Incorrect forms such as `*je alle` or `*nous allonss` happen when learners guess instead of recalling the full irregular set.
### Sentence-building practice
Complete each idea aloud, then write it.
1. `Je vais ...` with a place in your town.
2. `Nous allons ...` with a plural destination.
3. `Elle va ...` with a person's home using `chez`.
4. `Ils vont ...` with a city.
Then turn one sentence negative with `ne...pas`.

:::answer
Model answers — your places can differ.

1. `Je vais à la bibliothèque.` — `je` takes `vais`, and a specific destination usually needs a preposition.
2. `Nous allons aux magasins.` — `nous` takes `allons`; `à` + `les` contracts to `aux`.
3. `Elle va chez sa tante.` — Use `chez` for someone's home or business, not `à`.
4. `Ils vont à Lyon.` — Cities usually take `à` for to or in.

Negative example:

- `Je ne vais pas à la bibliothèque.` — `ne` and `pas` wrap the conjugated verb `vais`; the place phrase stays after it.

Mark yourself wrong if you built `aller` like a regular -er verb or forgot the contraction in a phrase such as `aux magasins`.
:::

## Unit 2: The near future with aller + infinitive
### Core idea
French uses `aller + infinitive` to say that something is **going to happen soon**. This is called the **near future**.
### Structure
| Subject | Conjugated `aller` | Infinitive | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `je` | `vais` | `étudier` | I am going to study |
| `tu` | `vas` | `partir` | you are going to leave |
| `elle` | `va` | `préparer` | she is going to prepare |
| `nous` | `allons` | `visiter` | we are going to visit |
| `vous` | `allez` | `attendre` | you are going to wait |
| `ils` | `vont` | `dormir` | they are going to sleep |
### How the tense works
The infinitive does **not** change. Only `aller` changes.
- `Je vais téléphoner ce soir.` — I am going to call tonight.
- `Tu vas finir ce chapitre demain.` — You are going to finish this chapter tomorrow.
- `On va dîner chez des amis.` — We are going to have dinner at friends' house.
- `Nous allons visiter un village près de Québec demain.` — Tomorrow, we are going to visit a village near Quebec City.
- `Vous allez partir pour l'aéroport à six heures.` — You are going to leave for the airport at six o'clock.
- `Ils vont faire leurs devoirs après le repas.` — They are going to do their homework after the meal.
### Negative form
Put `ne...pas` around the conjugated form of `aller`, not around the infinitive.
- `Je ne vais pas sortir ce soir.` — I am not going to go out tonight.
- `Nous n'allons pas prendre le train demain.` — We are not going to take the train tomorrow.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not insert a preposition between `aller` and the infinitive.
- correct: `Je vais étudier.` — I am going to study.
- incorrect: `*Je vais à étudier.`
### Sentence-building practice
Rewrite each prompt in French.
1. We are going to buy bread.
2. She is not going to stay at home.
3. Are you going to call your parents?
4. They are going to do the dishes after dinner.
Then change sentence 1 to the `vous` form and sentence 3 to the `nous` form.

:::answer
1. `Nous allons acheter du pain.` — Near future = conjugated `aller` + infinitive. Only `allons` changes; `acheter` stays infinitive.
2. `Elle ne va pas rester à la maison.` — The negative wraps `va`, not `rester`.
3. `Est-ce que tu vas appeler tes parents ?` — In a yes/no question with `est-ce que`, keep normal subject-verb order.
4. `Ils vont faire la vaisselle après le dîner.` — `faire` stays infinitive after `vont`.

Changed forms:

- `Vous allez acheter du pain.` — `vous` requires `allez`.
- `Est-ce que nous allons appeler nos parents ?` — `nous` requires `allons`, and `tes parents` changes naturally to `nos parents`.
:::

## Unit 3: `à`, `de`, and their contractions
### Core idea
The prepositions `à` and `de` appear everywhere in French. They often express destination, location, belonging, source, or topic. Before `le` and `les`, they contract.
### Contractions with definite articles
| Full form | Contracted form | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| `à + le` | `au` | `au cinéma` | to the movie theater / at the movie theater |
| `à + les` | `aux` | `aux enfants` | to the children |
| `de + le` | `du` | `du professeur` | of the teacher / from the teacher |
| `de + les` | `des` | `des voisins` | of the neighbors / from the neighbors |
| `à + la` | no contraction | `à la gare` | to the station |
| `à + l'` | no contraction | `à l'hôtel` | to the hotel |
| `de + la` | no contraction | `de la bibliothèque` | from the library |
| `de + l'` | no contraction | `de l'aéroport` | from the airport |
### Common uses of `à`
- destination: `Je vais au bureau.` — I go to the office.
- location: `Elle est à la maison.` — She is at home.
- indirect object with a person in some structures: `Je parle à mon frère.` — I speak to my brother.
- time in certain expressions: `au printemps` — in spring
### Common uses of `de`
- origin: `Il revient du Canada.` — He is coming back from Canada.
- possession or relation: `le livre de Marie` — Marie's book
- topic: `Nous parlons du film.` — We are talking about the movie.
- content or quantity in many expressions: `un verre d'eau` — a glass of water
### Examples in context
- `Les étudiants vont au musée avec leur professeur.` — The students go to the museum with their teacher.
- `Je sors de la classe à midi.` — I leave the classroom at noon.
- `C'est la voiture du voisin.` — It is the neighbor's car.
- `Elle parle des examens avec ses amis.` — She talks about the exams with her friends.
- `Tu vas à la banque ou au supermarché ?` — Are you going to the bank or to the supermarket?
- `Nous revenons des montagnes dimanche.` — We are coming back from the mountains on Sunday.
### Pronunciation note
In `au`, `aux`, and `du`, the contraction creates one smooth sound group. Read `au musée`, `aux États-Unis`, and `du Canada` as complete chunks rather than word by word.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Never write `*à le`, `*à les`, `*de le`, or `*de les` before a regular definite article. French requires the contracted forms.
### Sentence-building practice
Complete the pairs.
1. `Je vais ...` with `le parc`.
2. `Nous parlons ...` with `les voisins`.
3. `Elle revient ...` with `la pharmacie`.
4. `C'est le sac ...` with `le professeur`.
Then explain why each phrase uses `à` or `de`.

:::answer
1. `Je vais au parc.` — Motion toward a place uses `à`; `à` + `le parc` becomes `au parc`.
2. `Nous parlons des voisins.` — Talking about a topic uses `de`; `de` + `les voisins` becomes `des voisins`.
3. `Elle revient de la pharmacie.` — Returning from a place uses `de`; there is no contraction with `la`.
4. `C'est le sac du professeur.` — Possession or relation uses `de`; `de` + `le professeur` becomes `du professeur`.

The main trap is writing two separate words where French requires one contracted form: `au`, `aux`, `du`, or `des`.
:::

## Unit 4: Prepositions of place and sequence
### Core idea
French uses different prepositions to describe where something is located and when one action happens in relation to another. Translate the **relationship**, not the English word alone.
### Common prepositions of place
| Preposition | Core meaning | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dans` | in, inside | `dans le sac` | in the bag |
| `sur` | on, on top of | `sur la table` | on the table |
| `sous` | under | `sous la chaise` | under the chair |
| `devant` | in front of | `devant l'école` | in front of the school |
| `derrière` | behind | `derrière la porte` | behind the door |
| `entre` | between | `entre les deux maisons` | between the two houses |
| `chez` | at the home/business of | `chez le médecin` | at the doctor's office |
| `près de` | near | `près de la rivière` | near the river |
| `loin de` | far from | `loin du centre` | far from downtown |
| `en face de` | opposite, across from | `en face du café` | across from the café |
### Common prepositions of time and sequence
| Expression | Use | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| `avant de + infinitive` | before doing something | `avant de partir` | before leaving |
| `après` | after | `après le cours` | after class |
| `pendant` | during, for | `pendant le voyage` | during the trip |
| `jusqu'à` | until, up to | `jusqu'à demain` | until tomorrow |
| `vers` | around, toward (time/direction) | `vers huit heures` | around eight o'clock |
### Examples in context
- `Les clés sont sur la table, derrière le livre.` — The keys are on the table, behind the book.
- `Le chat dort sous la chaise.` — The cat is sleeping under the chair.
- `Nous attendons devant le musée.` — We are waiting in front of the museum.
- `Elle habite chez ses grands-parents pendant l'été.` — She stays at her grandparents' house during the summer.
- `Je vais téléphoner avant de sortir.` — I am going to call before going out.
- `Ils vont travailler jusqu'à neuf heures.` — They are going to work until nine o'clock.
- `On arrive vers midi.` — We arrive around noon.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not use `à` for every English "in" or "at." Compare:
- `à Paris` — in Paris
- `dans la voiture` — in the car
- `chez Paul` — at Paul's place
The correct preposition depends on the relationship, not on one English word.
### Sentence-building practice
Answer each question in a complete sentence.
1. Où sont tes chaussures ? Use `sous`, `dans`, or `devant`.
2. Où vas-tu après le cours ? Use `chez`.
3. Que vas-tu faire avant de dormir ? Use `avant de + infinitive`.
4. Pendant quelle saison fais-tu le plus de promenades ? Use `pendant`.

:::answer
Sample answers — yours can be true for you.

1. `Mes chaussures sont sous la table.` — `sous` gives the location under something.
2. `Après le cours, je vais chez mon ami.` — Use `chez` for a person's home.
3. `Avant de dormir, je vais lire un chapitre.` — `avant de` must be followed by an infinitive, here `dormir`.
4. `Je fais le plus de promenades pendant l'été.` — `pendant` marks the season during which the activity happens.

Mark yourself wrong if you translate every English in or at with `à`. The correct preposition depends on the relationship: under, at someone's home, before doing, or during.
:::

## Unit 5: Geography and questions after prepositions
### Core idea
When you talk about cities and countries, French chooses the preposition according to the type of place. When you ask a question after a preposition, French often keeps the preposition directly before the question word.
### Cities, countries, and regions
| Type of place | To / in | From | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| city | `à` | `de` | `à Montréal`, `de Montréal` |
| feminine country | `en` | `de` / `d'` | `en France`, `de France` |
| masculine country | `au` | `du` | `au Japon`, `du Japon` |
| country starting with vowel sound | `en` | `d'` | `en Iran`, `d'Iran` |
| plural country | `aux` | `des` | `aux Pays-Bas`, `des Pays-Bas` |
| many regions/islands | varies; learn phrase | varies | `en Provence`, `au Québec` |
### Examples in context
- `Elle vient de France, mais elle habite au Canada.` — She comes from France, but she lives in Canada.
- `Nous allons en Italie au mois de mai.` — We are going to Italy in May.
- `Ils reviennent du Mexique samedi.` — They are coming back from Mexico on Saturday.
- `Vous travaillez aux États-Unis maintenant ?` — Are you working in the United States now?
- `Mes amis viennent des Pays-Bas.` — My friends come from the Netherlands.
- `Je suis à Lyon, mais ma sœur est en Belgique.` — I am in Lyon, but my sister is in Belgium.
### Questions after prepositions
| Preposition + question word | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `à qui` | to whom | `À qui écris-tu ?` — To whom are you writing? |
| `de qui` | from/about whom | `De qui parlez-vous ?` — About whom are you speaking? |
| `à quoi` | to what | `À quoi penses-tu ?` — What are you thinking about? |
| `de quoi` | of/about what | `De quoi avez-vous besoin ?` — What do you need? |
| `avec qui` | with whom | `Avec qui allez-vous dîner ?` — With whom are you going to have dinner? |
| `pour quoi` | for what | `Pour quoi est-ce que tu paies ?` — What are you paying for? |
### Forms of `lequel` after a preposition
Use forms of `lequel` after a preposition when you mean **which one** or **which ones**.
| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `lequel` | which one (masculine singular) | `Lequel de ces trains vas-tu prendre ?` |
| `laquelle` | which one (feminine singular) | `Laquelle de ces rues mène au centre ?` |
| `lesquels` | which ones (masculine/mixed plural) | `Lesquels de ces livres parles-tu ?` |
| `lesquelles` | which ones (feminine plural) | `Lesquelles de ces photos viens-tu de choisir ?` |
After `à` and `de`, forms contract just as articles do.
| Base form | With `à` | With `de` |
|---|---|---|
| `lequel` | `auquel` | `duquel` |
| `laquelle` | `à laquelle` | `de laquelle` |
| `lesquels` | `auxquels` | `desquels` |
| `lesquelles` | `auxquelles` | `desquelles` |
- `À laquelle de ces dates pensez-vous ?` — Which of these dates are you thinking about?
- `Le pont sous lequel nous passons est très ancien.` — The bridge under which we are passing is very old.
- `Voici les dossiers auxquels le directeur répond ce soir.` — Here are the files to which the director is replying tonight.
- `Je ne sais pas de laquelle de ces routes tu parles.` — I do not know which of these roads you are talking about.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not say `*à France` or `*de le Canada`. Geography phrases are fixed chunks: `en France`, `du Canada`, `aux Pays-Bas`, `de Montréal`.
### Sentence-building practice
Translate and say aloud.
1. We are going to Belgium in July.
2. He comes from Japan.
3. What are you thinking about?
4. Which chair are you sitting on?
For sentence 4, make two versions: one with `sur quelle chaise` and one with `sur laquelle`.

:::answer
1. `Nous allons en Belgique en juillet.` — Belgium is treated as feminine here, so to or in Belgium is `en Belgique`.
2. `Il vient du Japon.` — Japan is masculine, so from Japan is `du Japon`.
3. `À quoi penses-tu ?` — `penser à` keeps `à` before the question word.
4. `Sur quelle chaise est-ce que tu es assis ?` — This asks with the full noun phrase `quelle chaise`.
5. `Sur laquelle est-ce que tu es assis ?` — `laquelle` replaces the feminine singular noun `chaise` after the preposition `sur`.

The trap is losing the preposition in the question. French keeps it: `à quoi`, `sur quelle chaise`, `sur laquelle`.
:::

## Unit 6: The verb faire, weather, and common expressions
### Core idea
`Faire` means **to do** or **to make**, but in French it also appears in weather expressions and many daily activities.
### Present tense of faire
| Pronoun | Form | English idea |
|---|---|---|
| `je` | `fais` | I do / I make |
| `tu` | `fais` | you do / you make |
| `il/elle/on` | `fait` | he/she/one does / makes |
| `nous` | `faisons` | we do / we make |
| `vous` | `faites` | you do / you make |
| `ils/elles` | `font` | they do / they make |
### Weather expressions with faire
Use `il fait...` for many statements about weather.
| French | English |
|---|---|
| `Il fait beau.` | The weather is nice. |
| `Il fait mauvais.` | The weather is bad. |
| `Il fait chaud.` | It is hot. |
| `Il fait froid.` | It is cold. |
| `Il fait frais.` | It is cool. |
| `Il fait du vent.` | It is windy. |
| `Il fait quel temps ?` | What is the weather like? |
### Common expressions with faire
| Expression | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `faire attention` | to pay attention / be careful |
| `faire la cuisine` | to cook |
| `faire les courses` | to do the shopping / grocery shopping |
| `faire ses devoirs` | to do one's homework |
| `faire du vélo` | to ride a bicycle |
| `faire une promenade` | to take a walk |
| `faire du sport` | to play sports / exercise |
| `faire la vaisselle` | to do the dishes |
### Examples in context
- `Il fait froid, alors je vais mettre mon manteau.` — It is cold, so I am going to put on my coat.
- `Nous faisons les courses le samedi matin.` — We do the shopping on Saturday morning.
- `Tu fais du vélo près du lac ?` — Do you ride your bike near the lake?
- `Faites attention devant cette porte.` — Be careful in front of that door.
- `Elles font leurs devoirs avant de sortir.` — They do their homework before going out.
- `On fait une promenade après le dîner.` — We take a walk after dinner.
### Pronunciation note
The `s` in `faisons` is pronounced like `z`: **feh-zon**. This makes `nous faisons` easy to recognize by ear.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not translate weather with `être` in these expressions.
- correct: `Il fait chaud.` — It is hot.
- incorrect: `*Il est chaud.` in a weather context
Also learn activity phrases as complete chunks: `faire du vélo`, not `*faire vélo`.
### Sentence-building practice
Answer with a full sentence.
1. Quel temps fait-il aujourd'hui ?
2. Que fais-tu avant le dîner ?
3. Est-ce que vous faites du sport le week-end ?
4. Que vont faire tes amis demain ? Use `faire les courses`, `faire une promenade`, or another `faire` expression.

:::answer
Sample answers:

1. `Il fait beau aujourd'hui.` — Weather expressions commonly use `il fait`, not `il est`.
2. `Avant le dîner, je fais la vaisselle.` — `faire la vaisselle` is a fixed activity phrase.
3. `Oui, nous faisons du sport le week-end.` — `nous` takes `faisons`; the activity phrase is `faire du sport`.
4. `Mes amis vont faire les courses demain.` — In the near future, `vont` is conjugated and `faire` stays infinitive.

Mark yourself wrong if you wrote a weather sentence with `être`, or if you dropped the article in fixed expressions such as `faire du vélo` or `faire les courses`.
:::

6. `Nous allons faire les courses avant de rentrer chez nous.` — We are going to do the shopping before going back home.

:::answer
Use this checklist after writing the sentences from memory.

- **Aller plus infinitive.** Sentence 1 has `allons visiter`, sentence 3 has `vais mettre`, sentence 4 has `allez partir`, and sentence 6 has `allons faire`. Only `aller` changes; the infinitive stays unchanged.
- **Preposition chunks.** Copy small phrases exactly: `près de Québec`, `de France`, `au Canada`, `pour l'aéroport`, `dans le jardin`, `derrière la maison`, `avant de rentrer`, `chez nous`.
- **Contractions.** `au Canada` is not two words, and `chez nous` does not use `à`.
- **Weather.** Sentence 3 uses `Il fait froid`, not `Il est froid` for weather.
- **Question formatting.** Sentence 4 uses inversion in `allez-vous` and needs a space before `?`.

If the preposition is wrong, the sentence may still be understandable, but the chapter skill is not mastered. Rewrite the whole phrase, not just the isolated preposition.

## Mini-dialogue: planning a weekend trip
**Camille :** `Alors, où est-ce qu'on va ce week-end ?` — So, where are we going this weekend?
**Nora :** `On va à Québec samedi matin, puis on va passer la nuit chez ma cousine.` — We are going to Quebec City on Saturday morning, then we are going to spend the night at my cousin's place.
**Camille :** `Tu viens toujours de Montréal en voiture ?` — Are you still coming from Montreal by car?
**Nora :** `Oui, mais je vais d'abord m'arrêter au marché.` — Yes, but I am going to stop at the market first.
**Camille :** `Quel temps va-t-il faire là-bas ?` — What is the weather going to be like there?
**Nora :** `On dit qu'il va faire beau, mais il va faire frais le soir.` — They say the weather is going to be nice, but it is going to be cool in the evening.
**Camille :** `Parfait. Avant de partir, je vais acheter du café et du pain.` — Perfect. Before leaving, I am going to buy coffee and bread.
**Nora :** `Très bien. À quelle station de bus vas-tu m'attendre ?` — Very good. At which bus station are you going to wait for me?
### Guided analysis
1. Find every form of `aller` and decide whether it means simple movement or the near future.
2. Explain why the dialogue uses `à Québec`, `chez ma cousine`, `de Montréal`, and `au marché`.
3. Find the weather expressions and explain why they use `faire`.
4. Rewrite Nora's second line with `nous` instead of `on`, making every necessary change.
5. Replace `à quelle station` in the last line with a version using `laquelle`.

:::answer
1. Forms of `aller`: `on va` in `où est-ce qu'on va` means movement; `On va à Québec` means movement; `on va passer` is near future; `je vais m'arrêter` is near future; `va-t-il faire` is near future with weather; `il va faire beau` and `il va faire frais` are near future; `je vais acheter` is near future; `vas-tu m'attendre` is near future.
2. `à Québec` uses `à` because Québec is a city. `chez ma cousine` means at my cousin's home. `de Montréal` uses `de` for origin from a city. `au marché` is `à` plus `le marché`, so it contracts to `au`.
3. Weather expressions: `Quel temps va-t-il faire là-bas ?`, `il va faire beau`, and `il va faire frais`. French uses `faire` for these weather chunks, and here `aller` puts them in the near future.
4. `Nous allons à Québec samedi matin, puis nous allons passer la nuit chez ma cousine.` — changing `on` to `nous` changes `va` to `allons`; the infinitive `passer` stays unchanged.
5. `À laquelle des stations de bus vas-tu m'attendre ?` — `station` is feminine singular, so the form is `laquelle`; the preposition `à` stays in front.
:::

## Common mistakes to avoid
1. **Mixing up motion and destination prepositions.** Say `aller à Paris`, but `venir de Paris`.
2. **Forgetting contractions.** Write `au bureau` and `du Canada`, not `*à le bureau` or `*de le Canada`.
3. **Using `aller` with a finite verb.** Say `je vais partir`, not `*je vais je pars`.
4. **Using the wrong preposition with countries.** Say `en France`, `au Japon`, `aux Pays-Bas`.
5. **Using `être` for weather.** Say `il fait froid`, not `*il est froid` for the weather.
6. **Overusing `dans` for every kind of location.** Compare `dans la voiture`, `à la gare`, `chez mes amis`, `sur la table`.
7. **Ignoring the preposition in a question.** Say `À qui ?`, `De quoi ?`, `Avec qui ?`, not only `Qui ?` when the verb requires a preposition.
## Chapter reference sheet
### `aller` and `faire`, side by side
| Pronoun | `aller` | `faire` |
|---|---|---|
| `je` | `vais` | `fais` |
| `tu` | `vas` | `fais` |
| `il/elle/on` | `va` | `fait` |
| `nous` | `allons` | `faisons` |
| `vous` | `allez` | `faites` |
| `ils/elles` | `vont` | `font` |
### Contractions with `à` and `de`
| Meaning | Form |
|---|---|
| `à + le` | `au` |
| `à + les` | `aux` |
| `de + le` | `du` |
| `de + les` | `des` |
| `à + la`, `à + l'` | no contraction |
| `de + la`, `de + l'` | no contraction |
### Geography quick reference
| Place type | To / in | From |
|---|---|---|
| city | `à` | `de` |
| feminine country | `en` | `de` / `d'` |
| masculine country | `au` | `du` |
| plural country | `aux` | `des` |
| vowel-opening country | `en` | `d'` |
### Preposition-question patterns
| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| `à qui` | `À qui téléphones-tu ?` |
| `de quoi` | `De quoi parle-t-il ?` |
| `avec qui` | `Avec qui sors-tu ?` |
| `auquel / à laquelle / auxquels / auxquelles` | `Auquel penses-tu ?` |
| `duquel / de laquelle / desquels / desquelles` | `Duquel parlez-vous ?` |
### Core habits
- Learn prepositions inside useful phrases, not as isolated translation equivalents.
- Check whether a place is a city, a feminine country, a masculine country, or a plural country.
- In the near future, change only `aller`; keep the infinitive unchanged.
- Treat weather expressions with `faire` as fixed chunks.
