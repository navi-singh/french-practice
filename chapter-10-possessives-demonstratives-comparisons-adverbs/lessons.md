# Chapter 10 Lessons: Possessives, Demonstratives, Comparisons, and Adverbs

Use short sessions. Accuracy from memory matters more than copying volume.

## Unit 1: Possessives

Possessive adjectives agree with the possessed noun, not the owner. Use mon before a feminine noun beginning with a vowel sound.

### Examples

- `mon frère` — my brother
- `ma sœur` — my sister
- `mes parents` — my parents
- `mon amie` — my female friend

### Active writing

1. Copy each example once while saying it.
2. Cover the examples.
3. Rewrite all three from memory.
4. Change the subject, noun, or time expression to make three new sentences.
5. Read your new sentences aloud.

## Unit 2: Demonstratives

Use ce, cet, cette, and ces before nouns. Demonstrative pronouns replace nouns: celui, celle, ceux, celles.

### Examples

- `ce livre` — this/that book
- `cet arbre` — this/that tree
- `cette maison` — this/that house
- `ceux de Paul` — Paul's ones

### Active writing

1. Copy each example once while saying it.
2. Cover the examples.
3. Rewrite all three from memory.
4. Change the subject, noun, or time expression to make three new sentences.
5. Read your new sentences aloud.

## Unit 3: Comparisons

Use plus...que, moins...que, and aussi...que. Compare nouns with plus de/moins de/autant de, and verbs with plus/moins/autant.

### Examples

- `Elle est plus rapide que moi.` — She is faster than I am.
- `Il a moins de temps.` — He has less time.
- `Nous travaillons autant.` — We work just as much.

### Active writing

1. Copy each example once while saying it.
2. Cover the examples.
3. Rewrite all three from memory.
4. Change the subject, noun, or time expression to make three new sentences.
5. Read your new sentences aloud.

## Unit 4: Adverbs

Many adverbs derive from the feminine adjective plus -ment. Their placement depends on the tense and length.

### Examples

- `lentement` — slowly
- `heureusement` — fortunately/happily
- `Elle parle clairement.` — She speaks clearly.
- `Il travaille très bien.` — He works very well.

### Active writing

1. Copy each example once while saying it.
2. Cover the examples.
3. Rewrite all three from memory.
4. Change the subject, noun, or time expression to make three new sentences.
5. Read your new sentences aloud.

## Chapter production task

Compare two family members or two cities in eight sentences using possessives, demonstratives, and three comparison patterns.

After writing, circle conjugated verbs, underline agreement or pronoun choices, and read the passage aloud twice.

## Sentence usage practice

Use these complete sentences to practice the chapter structures in context. Read each sentence aloud twice, cover it, and write it from memory.

1. `Mon appartement est plus petit que celui de ma sœur.` — My apartment is smaller than my sister's.
2. `Cette voiture roule moins rapidement que la nôtre.` — This car travels less quickly than ours.
3. `Ces enfants parlent aussi clairement que leurs parents.` — These children speak as clearly as their parents.
4. `Notre famille passe davantage de temps ensemble.` — Our family spends more time together.

### Notice and mark

For each sentence, circle the target verb or grammar structure. Underline the word or phrase that controls its form.

### Change the sentence

Rewrite every sentence twice:

1. change the subject, person, or number;
2. change one meaningful detail such as the place, object, time, or description.

Make every required change to conjugation, agreement, articles, pronouns, and word order.

### Make it personal

Write four new sentences about your own life using the same four chapter structures. Read them aloud, check them, and rewrite only the parts you corrected.
