# Chapter 10 Lessons: Possessives, Demonstratives, Comparisons, and Adverbs
This chapter helps you talk about ownership, point things out precisely, and compare people, objects, and actions with more nuance. By the end, you should be able to say not only **my book** and **that one**, but also **mine is better**, **ours is less expensive**, and **she explains more clearly than I do**.

## Learning objectives
You will learn to:
1. choose the correct possessive adjective for every person;
2. distinguish possessive adjectives from possessive pronouns;
3. use demonstrative adjectives and demonstrative pronouns naturally;
4. compare adjectives, nouns, verbs, and adverbs with `plus`, `moins`, `aussi`, and `autant`;
5. form superlatives such as `le plus pratique` and `la moins chère`;
6. avoid the common mix-up between `meilleur` and `mieux`, and between `pire` and `plus mal`;
7. form many adverbs with `-ment` and place them naturally;
8. combine these structures in original speech and writing.

## Prerequisite quick check
Before beginning, make sure you can already:
- use `le`, `la`, `l'`, and `les` correctly;
- make adjectives agree: `petit`, `petite`, `petits`, `petites`;
- conjugate common present-tense verbs such as `être`, `avoir`, `aller`, `faire`, and `parler`;
- recognize that French often uses stressed pronouns after `que`: `que moi`, `que lui`, `que nous`.
If those skills still feel slow, review them briefly first.

## How to study this chapter
Say every French example aloud. When you practice comparison patterns, memorize the whole frame: `plus...que`, `moins de...que`, `le plus...`, not just the key word.

## Unit 1: Possessive adjectives
### Core idea
A possessive adjective tells whose thing you are talking about. In French, it agrees with the **thing possessed**, not with the owner.
- `ma sœur` — my sister
- `mon frère` — my brother
- `mes cousins` — my cousins
A man can say `ma sœur`, and a woman can say `mon frère`. The noun controls the form.

### Full possessive adjective chart
| Owner | Masculine singular | Feminine singular | Plural | English |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `je` | `mon` | `ma` | `mes` | my |
| `tu` | `ton` | `ta` | `tes` | your |
| `il / elle / on` | `son` | `sa` | `ses` | his, her, its |
| `nous` | `notre` | `notre` | `nos` | our |
| `vous` | `votre` | `votre` | `vos` | your |
| `ils / elles` | `leur` | `leur` | `leurs` | their |

### Feminine noun before a vowel sound
With a feminine singular noun beginning with a vowel sound or mute `h`, French uses `mon`, `ton`, or `son` instead of `ma`, `ta`, or `sa`.
| Standard feminine form | Before vowel sound | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ma` | `mon` | `mon amie` | my female friend |
| `ta` | `ton` | `ton adresse` | your address |
| `sa` | `son` | `son histoire` | his/her story |
The noun is still feminine.
- `mon amie est française.` — my female friend is French.
- `son habitude est étrange.` — his/her habit is strange.

### Examples in context
- `Mon bureau est près de la fenêtre.` — My desk is near the window.
- `Ma montre est dans mon sac.` — My watch is in my bag.
- `Notre professeur connaît vos parents.` — Our teacher knows your parents.
- `Leurs enfants font leurs devoirs après le dîner.` — Their children do their homework after dinner.

### Pronunciation note
Listen for liaison in phrases such as `mes amis` and `vos enfants`. In speech, that linked sound often helps you hear the plural.

### Common mistake for English speakers
Do **not** choose the possessive by the gender of the owner.
- correct: `Paul aime sa voiture.` — Paul likes his car.
- correct: `Marie aime son frère.` — Marie likes her brother.
- incorrect: `*Marie aime sa frère.`

### Sentence-building practice
1. Replace the owner in each phrase: `mon livre`, `ta chaise`, `ses clés`, `notre rue`.
2. Say one phrase with a feminine noun before a vowel sound.
3. Write four short sentences about objects in your home using four different possessive adjectives.

## Unit 2: Possessive pronouns
### Core idea
A possessive pronoun replaces **noun + possessive adjective**. Instead of repeating the noun, French uses forms like `le mien`, `la tienne`, `les leurs`.
- `Mon appartement est petit, mais le sien est lumineux.` — My apartment is small, but his/hers is bright.
- `Voici nos billets. Où sont les vôtres ?` — Here are our tickets. Where are yours?

### Important rule
Possessive pronouns normally come with a definite article.

### Full possessive pronoun chart
| Owner | Masculine singular | Feminine singular | Masculine plural | Feminine plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `je` | `le mien` | `la mienne` | `les miens` | `les miennes` |
| `tu` | `le tien` | `la tienne` | `les tiens` | `les tiennes` |
| `il / elle / on` | `le sien` | `la sienne` | `les siens` | `les siennes` |
| `nous` | `le nôtre` | `la nôtre` | `les nôtres` | `les nôtres` |
| `vous` | `le vôtre` | `la vôtre` | `les vôtres` | `les vôtres` |
| `ils / elles` | `le leur` | `la leur` | `les leurs` | `les leurs` |

### How to choose the form
The possessive pronoun agrees with the **thing possessed**.
- `ma veste` -> `la mienne` — my jacket -> mine
- `mes livres` -> `les miens` — my books -> mine
- `nos valises` -> `les nôtres` — our suitcases -> ours

### Common patterns
| Full phrase | Shorter version | English |
|---|---|---|
| `mon téléphone` | `le mien` | mine |
| `ta table` | `la tienne` | yours |
| `ses dossiers` | `les siens` | his/hers |
| `notre voiture` | `la nôtre` | ours |
| `vos billets` | `les vôtres` | yours |
| `leurs idées` | `les leurs` | theirs |

### Examples in context
- `Mon appartement est plus petit que celui de ma sœur, mais le mien est plus calme.` — My apartment is smaller than my sister's, but mine is quieter.
- `Ta réponse est claire, mais la sienne est encore plus précise.` — Your answer is clear, but his/hers is even more precise.
- `Je préfère votre plan au nôtre.` — I prefer your plan to ours.
- `Ces lunettes sont les miennes, pas les tiennes.` — These glasses are mine, not yours.

### Pronunciation note
Singular and plural pairs such as `le vôtre` and `les vôtres` are distinguished mainly by the article. Train your ear to notice it.

### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not omit the article.
- correct: `le mien`
- incorrect: `*mien`
Also do not choose `le mien` just because the owner is masculine. The missing noun controls the form.

### Sentence-building practice
1. Replace the repeated noun: `Voici ma clé. Où est ta clé ?` -> `Voici la mienne. Où est la tienne ?`
2. Transform these phrases into possessive pronouns: `notre train`, `vos places`, `leurs idées`, `mon journal`.
3. Write three comparison sentences using two possessive pronouns.

## Unit 3: Demonstrative adjectives and demonstrative pronouns
### Core idea
Demonstratives point something out: **this**, **that**, **these**, **those**. French has one set before nouns and another set that replaces nouns.

### Demonstrative adjectives
| Form | Use | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ce` | masculine singular before consonant | `ce quartier` | this/that neighborhood |
| `cet` | masculine singular before vowel sound or mute `h` | `cet hôtel` | this/that hotel |
| `cette` | feminine singular | `cette rue` | this/that street |
| `ces` | all plurals | `ces immeubles` | these/those buildings |
French `ce / cet / cette / ces` can mean either **this/these** or **that/those**. To be more precise, French often adds `-ci` for “this/these here” and `-là` for “that/those there.”
- `ce livre-ci` — this book here
- `ce livre-là` — that book there
- `ces chaussures-ci` — these shoes here
- `ces chaussures-là` — those shoes there

### Demonstrative pronouns
| Gender and number | Basic form | With `-ci` | With `-là` |
|---|---|---|---|
| masculine singular | `celui` | `celui-ci` | `celui-là` |
| feminine singular | `celle` | `celle-ci` | `celle-là` |
| masculine plural | `ceux` | `ceux-ci` | `ceux-là` |
| feminine plural | `celles` | `celles-ci` | `celles-là` |
Demonstrative pronouns are often followed by `-ci`, `-là`, `de + noun`, or a relative clause.
| Pattern | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| `celui-ci / celui-là` | `Je prends celui-ci.` | I'll take this one. |
| `celui de ...` | `celui de Paul` | Paul's one |
| `celle qui ...` | `celle qui est sur la table` | the one that is on the table |
| `ceux de ...` | `ceux de mes parents` | my parents' ones |
| `celles qui ...` | `celles qui brillent` | the ones that shine |

### Examples in context
- `Cette voiture roule moins rapidement que la nôtre.` — This car goes less quickly than ours.
- `Je connais cet homme, mais pas celui qui parle avec lui.` — I know this man, but not the one speaking with him.
- `Ces enfants parlent aussi clairement que leurs parents.` — These children speak as clearly as their parents.
- `Tu préfères cette table-ci ou celle-là ?` — Do you prefer this table here or that one there?

### Pronunciation note
`Cet` is pronounced with a clear final `t` before a vowel sound: `cet ami`, `cet arbre`, `cet hôtel`.

### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not use `ce` before a masculine noun beginning with a vowel sound.
- correct: `cet appartement`
- incorrect: `*ce appartement`
Also remember: `le mien` = mine, but `celui-ci` = this one.

### Sentence-building practice
1. Point to four objects around you and label them with `ce`, `cet`, `cette`, or `ces`.
2. Replace the noun in each phrase: `ce sac`, `cette photo`, `ces journaux`, `cet exercice`.
3. Write two short contrast sentences with `-ci` and `-là`.

## Unit 4: Comparisons and superlatives
### Core idea
French uses different comparison patterns depending on whether you are comparing an adjective, a noun, a verb, or an adverb. A superlative picks out the highest or lowest point inside a group.

### Comparing adjectives and adverbs
| Meaning | Pattern | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| more ... than | `plus + adjective/adverb + que` | `plus rapide que` | faster than |
| less ... than | `moins + adjective/adverb + que` | `moins lentement que` | less slowly than |
| as ... as | `aussi + adjective/adverb + que` | `aussi clairement que` | as clearly as |
- `Ce quartier est plus calme que le centre-ville.` — This neighborhood is quieter than downtown.
- `Ma sœur travaille moins rapidement que moi le matin.` — My sister works less quickly than I do in the morning.
- `Il parle aussi poliment que son collègue.` — He speaks as politely as his coworker.

### Comparing nouns
| Meaning | Pattern | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| more ... than | `plus de + noun + que` | `plus de temps que` | more time than |
| less ... than | `moins de + noun + que` | `moins d'énergie que` | less energy than |
| as much/many ... as | `autant de + noun + que` | `autant de livres que` | as many books as |
- `Nous avons plus de place que vous.` — We have more space than you do.
- `Elle boit moins de café que son frère.` — She drinks less coffee than her brother.
- `J'achète autant de fruits que mes parents.` — I buy as much fruit as my parents do.

### Comparing verbs
| Meaning | Pattern | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| more than | `verb + plus que` | `travaille plus que` | works more than |
| less than | `verb + moins que` | `sort moins que` | goes out less than |
| as much/as often as | `verb + autant que` | `lit autant que` | reads as much as |
- `Je cuisine plus que mon colocataire.` — I cook more than my roommate.
- `Tu dors autant que moi pendant le week-end.` — You sleep as much as I do during the weekend.

### Superlatives
| Meaning | Pattern | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| the most ... | `le / la / les plus + adjective` | `la plus pratique` | the most practical |
| the least ... | `le / la / les moins + adjective` | `les moins chers` | the least expensive |
| the most ... with a noun | `avoir / prendre / faire + le plus de + noun` | `avoir le plus de temps` | to have the most time |
| the least ... with a noun | `avoir / prendre / faire + le moins de + noun` | `prendre le moins de place` | to take up the least space |
- `C'est la rue la plus tranquille du quartier.` — It's the quietest street in the neighborhood.
- `C'est Clara qui a le plus d'expérience.` — Clara is the one who has the most experience.
- `C'est cette valise qui prend le moins de place.` — This suitcase is the one that takes up the least space.

### Irregular comparison forms
| Base word | Better/worse form | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `bon / bonne` | `meilleur`, `meilleure`, `meilleurs`, `meilleures` | adjective |
| `bien` | `mieux` | adverb |
| `mauvais / mauvaise` | `pire` or `plus mauvais` | adjective |
| `mal` | `plus mal` | adverb |
Use `meilleur` with a noun.
- `Ce restaurant a un meilleur menu.` — This restaurant has a better menu.
- `C'est la meilleure solution.` — It's the best solution.
Use `mieux` with a verb or adverb.
- `Elle cuisine mieux que moi.` — She cooks better than I do.
- `Aujourd'hui, je me sens mieux.` — Today, I feel better.
Use `pire` with a noun or adjective function.
- `C'est la pire excuse de la journée.` — That's the worst excuse of the day.
Use `plus mal` with a verb or adverb function.
- `Il dort plus mal en été.` — He sleeps worse in summer.
- `Je chante plus mal quand je suis fatigué.` — I sing worse when I'm tired.

### Pronunciation note
Try to hear `plus...que`, `moins de...que`, and `le plus...` as complete sound patterns. That rhythm matters more than worrying about every final consonant.

### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not forget `de` in noun comparisons: `plus de patience`, not `*plus patience`. Also do not say `*plus bon` when French normally wants `meilleur`.

### Sentence-building practice
1. Complete these frames with your own ideas: `plus ... que`, `moins de ... que`, `verb + autant que`.
2. Compare two people you know with one adjective, one noun, and one verb.
3. Write one sentence with `meilleur`, one with `mieux`, one with `pire`, and one with `plus mal`.

## Unit 5: Forming and placing adverbs
### Core idea
Many adverbs are built from adjectives. They answer questions like **how?** or **in what way?**

### The basic `-ment` pattern
A common rule is: start from the **feminine adjective form**, then add `-ment`.
| Adjective | Feminine form | Adverb | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| `rapide` | `rapide` | `rapidement` | quickly |
| `heureux` | `heureuse` | `heureusement` | fortunately |
| `clair` | `claire` | `clairement` | clearly |
| `poli` | `polie` | `poliment` | politely |
| `doux` | `douce` | `doucement` | gently |
| `prudent` | `prudente` | `prudemment` | carefully |

### Very common adverbs not built with `-ment`
| Adverb | English |
|---|---|
| `bien` | well |
| `mal` | badly |
| `vite` | quickly |
| `souvent` | often |
| `toujours` | always |
| `déjà` | already |
| `tard` | late |
| `tôt` | early |

### Placement in simple sentences
Many adverbs of manner come after the verb or after the object.
- `Elle parle clairement.` — She speaks clearly.
- `Nous écoutons attentivement le guide.` — We listen carefully to the guide.
- `Ils répondent poliment aux questions.` — They answer the questions politely.
Short frequent adverbs often stay close to the conjugated verb.
- `Je travaille souvent ici.` — I often work here.
- `Nous comprenons bien cette règle.` — We understand this rule well.
- `Tu arrives toujours en avance.` — You always arrive early.

### Comparison review with adverbs
- `Elle parle plus doucement que moi.` — She speaks more softly than I do.
- `Vous conduisez moins vite qu'avant.` — You drive less fast than before.
- `Ils révisent aussi sérieusement que leurs amis.` — They review as seriously as their friends.
- `Dans la classe, Aïcha répond le mieux.` — In the class, Aïcha answers best.

### Pronunciation note
Many `-ment` adverbs are long. Say them in syllables: `clai-re-ment`, `heu-reu-se-ment`, `pru-dem-ment`.

### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not automatically build the adverb from the masculine adjective form.
- correct: `heureuse` -> `heureusement`
- incorrect: `*heureuxment`
French also does not always place the adverb exactly where English does, so learn common whole phrases such as `parler clairement` and `répondre poliment`.

### Sentence-building practice
1. Turn these adjectives into adverbs: `lent`, `heureux`, `sérieux`, `calme`.
2. Add a suitable adverb to each verb: `parler`, `écouter`, `attendre`, `répondre`.
3. Write three sentences about your study habits using at least two `-ment` adverbs and one short frequent adverb.

## Mini-dialogue: choosing a study space
**Lina :** `Tu préfères cette bibliothèque-ci ou celle près du parc ?` — Do you prefer this library here or the one near the park?
**Samir :** `Je préfère celle près du parc. Son espace de travail est plus lumineux que celui-ci.` — I prefer the one near the park. Its work area is brighter than this one.
**Lina :** `Oui, mais la nôtre est plus calme le matin.` — Yes, but ours is quieter in the morning.
**Samir :** `C'est vrai. Pourtant, leurs chaises sont meilleures que les nôtres.` — That's true. Still, their chairs are better than ours.
**Lina :** `Et on y travaille mieux ?` — And do we work better there?
**Samir :** `Moi, j'y travaille plus efficacement, mais toi, tu te concentres mieux ici.` — I work more efficiently there, but you concentrate better here.
**Lina :** `Alors, prenons cette salle-là aujourd'hui et celle-ci demain.` — Then let's take that room there today and this one here tomorrow.
**Samir :** `Bonne idée. De toutes les options, ce sont celles qui ferment le plus tard.` — Good idea. Of all the options, those are the ones that close the latest.

### Guided analysis
1. Find two demonstrative pronouns and explain what noun each one replaces.
2. Find two possessive pronouns and state the missing noun.
3. Identify one regular comparative and one irregular comparative.
4. Why does Samir say `mieux` in line 5 but `meilleures` in line 4?
5. Rewrite line 7 as a singular informal command with `prendre`.

## Connected reading: two ways to study
`Mon frère et moi avons des habitudes très différentes. Le sien commence toujours tôt, tandis que le mien devient plus productif en soirée. Le matin, il travaille plus rapidement que moi et il organise ses notes plus soigneusement. Moi, je relis plus lentement, mais je retiens mieux les détails. Quand nous avons autant de devoirs l'un que l'autre, celui qui planifie le plus calmement finit souvent le premier. En général, sa méthode est la plus régulière, mais la mienne est parfois la plus créative.`
— My brother and I have very different habits. His starts early, while mine becomes more productive in the evening. In the morning, he works more quickly than I do and organizes his notes more carefully. I reread more slowly, but I retain details better. When we have as much homework as each other, the one who plans most calmly often finishes first. In general, his method is the most consistent, but mine is sometimes the most creative.

### Reading practice
1. List every possessive form in the paragraph and classify each one as an adjective or pronoun.
2. Find one noun comparison, one verb comparison, and two adverb comparisons.
3. Explain why the text says `le sien` and `le mien`, not `la sienne` and `la mienne`.
4. Rewrite the paragraph so that the two people are `ma sœur et moi` instead of `mon frère et moi`.
5. Write three true sentences about your own study habits using the reading as a model.

## Common mistakes to avoid
1. Choosing a possessive according to the owner's gender instead of the noun's gender.
2. Forgetting that feminine nouns before a vowel sound use `mon`, `ton`, or `son`: `mon amie`, not `*ma amie`.
3. Omitting the article before a possessive pronoun: `le mien`, not `*mien`.
4. Mixing up possessive pronouns and demonstrative pronouns: `le nôtre` is not the same as `celui-ci`.
5. Forgetting `de` in noun comparisons: `plus de patience`, `moins d'argent`, `autant de temps`.
6. Using `meilleur` where French needs `mieux`, or `pire` where French needs `plus mal`.
7. Building `-ment` adverbs from the wrong adjective form.
