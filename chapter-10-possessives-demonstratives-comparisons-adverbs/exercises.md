# Chapter 10 Exercises

Complete these without notes. Put a question mark beside uncertain answers.

## A. Transform or translate

1. my female friend
2. this tree
3. those houses
4. Marie is taller than Luc.
5. We have as much time.
6. heureuse -> adverb

:::answer
These answers match the answer key.

1. `mon amie` — `amie` is feminine, but French uses `mon` before a feminine noun beginning with a vowel sound.
2. `cet arbre` — `arbre` is masculine singular and begins with a vowel sound, so use `cet`, not `ce`.
3. `ces maisons` — plural demonstrative adjective for both masculine and feminine nouns is `ces`.
4. `Marie est plus grande que Luc.` — adjective comparison uses **plus + adjective + que**. `grande` agrees with Marie.
5. `Nous avons autant de temps.` — with a noun, the structure is `autant de`, not just `autant`.
6. `heureusement` — form the adverb from the feminine adjective `heureuse`, then add the ending.
:::

## B. Controlled production

1. Write a new sentence demonstrating **possessives**. Use `mon frère` only as a structural model; change its content.
2. Write a new sentence demonstrating **demonstratives**. Use `ce livre` only as a structural model; change its content.
3. Write a new sentence demonstrating **comparisons**. Use `Elle est plus rapide que moi.` only as a structural model; change its content.
4. Write a new sentence demonstrating **adverbs**. Use `lentement` only as a structural model; change its content.

:::answer
Answers vary. A correct answer must clearly show the named structure and keep agreement with the noun being described or replaced.

Sample answers:

1. Possessives: `Ma sœur cherche son téléphone.` — `ma` agrees with `sœur`; `son` agrees with `téléphone`.
2. Demonstratives: `Cette chaise est plus confortable que ce banc.` — `cette` is feminine singular, `ce` masculine singular.
3. Comparisons: `Mon frère est moins patient que moi.` — adjective comparison with **moins ... que**.
4. Adverbs: `Elle répond calmement.` — the adverb describes how she answers.

Mark yourself wrong if a possessive agrees with the owner instead of the noun, if you use `ce` before a vowel sound where `cet` is needed, or if a noun comparison is missing `de`.
:::

## C. Four-way variation

Choose one sentence from each lesson unit and rewrite it four ways:

1. change the subject;
2. make it negative;
3. turn it into a question;
4. change one meaningful detail.

:::answer
Worked example, starting from `Cette voiture est plus rapide que la mienne.`

1. Change the subject: `Ces voitures sont plus rapides que la mienne.` — demonstrative, verb, and adjective all change for plural.
2. Make it negative: `Cette voiture n'est pas plus rapide que la mienne.` — **ne ... pas** wraps the conjugated verb.
3. Turn it into a question: `Est-ce que cette voiture est plus rapide que la mienne ?`
4. Change one meaningful detail: `Cette voiture est moins chère que la mienne.`

Check agreement every time. Demonstratives and possessive pronouns must match the noun they point to or replace.
:::

## D. Dictation and correction

Use text-to-speech to hear six examples from `lessons.md`. Write what you hear without looking. Correct in separate passes:

1. vocabulary;
2. verb form;
3. agreement or pronoun placement;
4. spelling and accents.

Do the dictation first, using this chapter's own examples as your audio. Then open the answers to check: they list six sentences worth dictating and the trap each one sets.

:::answer
Six sentences from this chapter that make good dictation material, and the trap each one sets:

1. `Mon bureau est près de la fenêtre.` — `bureau` is masculine, so the possessive is `mon`; `près` needs its accent.
2. `Ma montre est dans mon sac.` — two possessed nouns, two genders: `ma montre` but `mon sac`.
3. `Tu préfères cette table-ci ou celle-là ?` — listen for the accent in `préfères`, the contrast `cette` / `celle`, and the space before `?`.
4. `Ce restaurant a un meilleur menu.` — `meilleur` describes the noun `menu`; do not write `mieux` here.
5. `Elle parle clairement.` — `clairement` is an adverb from the feminine adjective form, not an adjective agreeing with `elle`.
6. `Je préfère vos idées aux leurs, surtout celle qui résout le problème plus simplement.` — `aux leurs` includes the article inside `aux`, and `celle` replaces a feminine singular noun.

Correct in separate passes. First recover the words, then check agreement, then check accents and the comparison or pronoun form.
:::

## E. Personal writing

Compare two family members or two cities in eight sentences using possessives, demonstratives, and three comparison patterns.

:::answer
Answers vary. A model comparison paragraph:

1. `Ma sœur habite à Lyon, et mon frère habite à Nantes.`
2. `Son appartement est plus grand que le sien.`
3. `Cette ville est moins chère que celle-là.`
4. `Lyon a plus de musées que Nantes.`
5. `Nantes a autant de cafés que Lyon.`
6. `Ma sœur travaille plus souvent chez elle.`
7. `Mon frère parle plus rapidement qu'elle.`
8. `Je préfère son quartier au leur.`

Self-check: include possessives, demonstratives, and three comparison patterns. Mark it wrong if an adjective does not agree, if a noun comparison lacks `de`, or if a pronoun such as `le sien` does not match the noun it replaces.
:::

## F. Oral recall

Explain each of the chapter's four main rules aloud in simple English, then give one French example from memory.

:::answer
1. **Possessives.** Possessive adjectives agree with the possessed noun, not the owner. Example: `Marie cherche son livre et sa clé.`
2. **Demonstratives.** Use `ce`, `cet`, `cette`, and `ces` before nouns; use `celui`, `celle`, `ceux`, and `celles` when the noun is replaced. Example: `Cette chaise est confortable, mais celle-ci est meilleure.`
3. **Comparisons.** Use **plus/moins/aussi + adjective or adverb + que**, **plus/moins/autant de + noun**, and **verb + plus/moins/autant**. Example: `Je lis autant que toi.`
4. **Adverbs.** Many adverbs come from the feminine adjective plus the ending. Example: `Elle parle clairement.`

If your example uses the rule but has agreement wrong, the answer is not correct yet. Agreement is part of the structure.
:::
