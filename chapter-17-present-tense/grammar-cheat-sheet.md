# Grammar Cheat Sheet — Chapters 17–25

Quick formulas, endings, and tense-choice reminders for the original course's extended grammar chapters. Choose the meaning first, then build the form. Every French example includes an English meaning; tap French examples on the website to hear them.

## Choose the form by meaning

| Meaning or job | Form | Chapter | Example and meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current action, state, habit | Présent | 17 | `Je lis.` (I read / I am reading.) |
| Completed event viewed as a whole | Passé composé | 18 | `J'ai lu le livre.` (I read the book.) |
| Past background, habit, action in progress | Imparfait | 19 | `Je lisais quand il est arrivé.` (I was reading when he arrived.) |
| Completed before a past reference point | Plus-que-parfait | 19 | `J'avais déjà lu le livre.` (I had already read the book.) |
| Present intention, impending event | Futur proche | 20 | `Je vais lire.` (I am going to read.) |
| Future fact, promise, prediction | Futur simple | 20 | `Je lirai demain.` (I will read tomorrow.) |
| Completed before a future point | Futur antérieur | 20 | `À midi, j'aurai fini.` (By noon, I will have finished.) |
| Hypothetical result, politeness, advice | Conditionnel présent | 21 | `Je voudrais lire.` (I would like to read.) |
| Hypothetical past result, regret | Conditionnel passé | 21 | `J'aurais dû lire.` (I should have read.) |
| Will, necessity, emotion, doubt under a trigger | Subjonctif présent | 22 | `Il faut que tu lises.` (You have to read.) |
| Completed action under a subjunctive trigger | Passé du subjonctif | 22 | `Je suis content que tu aies lu le livre.` (I am happy you have read the book.) |
| Direct command | Impératif | 23 | `Lis !` (Read!) |
| Literary narrative event; recognize only | Passé simple | 24 | `Il lut la lettre.` (He read the letter.) |
| Earlier completion in literary narration; recognize only | Passé antérieur | 24 | `Quand il eut lu, il partit.` (When he had read, he left.) |

Chapter 25 combines these choices. Signal words help, but do not decide automatically: a long action can be a completed whole, and a short action can provide background.

## Present tense — Chapter 17

Drop the infinitive ending and add the appropriate regular endings.

| Family | je | tu | il/elle | nous | vous | ils/elles |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| -er | -e | -es | -e | -ons | -ez | -ent |
| Regular -ir | -is | -is | -it | -issons | -issez | -issent |
| Regular -re | -s | -s | no added ending | -ons | -ez | -ent |

`Je parle.` (I speak / I am speaking.) `Nous finissons.` (We finish.) `Elle vend.` (She sells.) The verb ending -ent is silent; on uses the il/elle form.

| Verb | je | tu | il/elle | nous | vous | ils/elles |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| être | `je suis` (I am) | `tu es` (you are) | `elle est` (she is) | `nous sommes` (we are) | `vous êtes` (you are) | `ils sont` (they are) |
| avoir | `j'ai` (I have) | `tu as` (you have) | `il a` (he has) | `nous avons` (we have) | `vous avez` (you have) | `elles ont` (they have) |
| aller | `je vais` (I go) | `tu vas` (you go) | `elle va` (she goes) | `nous allons` (we go) | `vous allez` (you go) | `ils vont` (they go) |
| faire | `je fais` (I do) | `tu fais` (you do) | `il fait` (he does) | `nous faisons` (we do) | `vous faites` (you do) | `elles font` (they do) |

Spelling reminders: `nous mangeons` (we eat), `nous commençons` (we begin), `j'achète / nous achetons` (I buy / we buy), `j'appelle / nous appelons` (I call / we call).

Use the present for an ongoing situation with depuis: `J'habite ici depuis deux ans.` (I have lived here for two years.) Do not imitate English “am working” by adding être before a conjugated verb.

## Compound tenses: change the auxiliary

**Auxiliary** means the helping verb avoir or être. **Past participle** means the form such as parlé (spoken), fini (finished), or pris (taken).

| Target form | Form of avoir/être | Avoir example | Être example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Passé composé | Present indicative | `j'ai fini` (I finished) | `elle est partie` (she left) |
| Plus-que-parfait | Imparfait | `j'avais fini` (I had finished) | `elle était partie` (she had left) |
| Futur antérieur | Futur simple | `j'aurai fini` (I will have finished) | `elle sera partie` (she will have left) |
| Conditionnel passé | Present conditional | `j'aurais fini` (I would have finished) | `elle serait partie` (she would have left) |
| Passé du subjonctif | Present subjunctive | `que j'aie fini` (that I have finished) | `qu'elle soit partie` (that she has left) |
| Passé antérieur, recognition only | Passé simple | `j'eus fini` (I had finished) | `elle fut partie` (she had left) |

The participle and auxiliary choice stay the same across these forms; the auxiliary's tense/mood changes. Negation usually surrounds the auxiliary: `Je n'aurais pas accepté.` (I would not have accepted.)

## Past participles, auxiliary choice, and agreement — Chapters 18–19

| Pattern / infinitive | Past participle and meaning |
|---|---|
| Regular -er → -é | `parlé` (spoken) |
| Regular -ir → -i | `fini` (finished) |
| Many regular -re → -u | `vendu` (sold) |
| avoir / être | `eu` (had) / `été` (been) |
| faire / prendre / mettre | `fait` (done/made) / `pris` (taken) / `mis` (put) |
| dire / écrire / lire | `dit` (said) / `écrit` (written) / `lu` (read) |
| voir / boire / recevoir | `vu` (seen) / `bu` (drunk) / `reçu` (received) |
| pouvoir / vouloir / devoir / savoir | `pu` (been able to) / `voulu` (wanted) / `dû` (had to) / `su` (known) |
| venir / naître / mourir | `venu` (come) / `né` (born) / `mort` (died) |

- **Avoir** is the default, including movement verbs such as marcher: `J'ai marché.` (I walked.)
- **Être** is used by a limited group in their intransitive uses, including aller, venir, arriver, partir, entrer, sortir, tomber, rester, naître, mourir, and related verbs.
- Construction matters: `Elle est sortie.` (She went out.) but `Elle a sorti la chaise.` (She took the chair out.)
- **Pronominal verbs** use être: `Elle s'est levée.` (She got up.)

| Agreement situation | Rule | Example and meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Non-pronominal verb with être | Agree with the subject | `Elles sont parties.` (They left, all female.) |
| Avoir + following direct object | No agreement with subject or following object | `Elle a écrit les lettres.` (She wrote the letters.) |
| Avoir + preceding direct object | Normally agree with that object | `Les lettres qu'elle a écrites.` (The letters she wrote.) |
| Reflexive pronoun is a preceding direct object | Agree with that direct object | `Elle s'est lavée.` (She washed herself.) |
| Reflexive pronoun is indirect; direct object follows | No agreement with se | `Elle s'est lavé les mains.` (She washed her hands.) |
| Reciprocal pronoun is indirect | No agreement with se | `Elles se sont parlé.` (They spoke to each other.) |
| Essentially pronominal verb | Normally agree with subject | `Elles se sont souvenues de lui.` (They remembered him.) |

For pronominal verbs, do not apply “être always means subject agreement” without checking the construction.

## Imparfait versus passé composé versus plus-que-parfait — Chapter 19

**Imparfait:** present nous form minus -ons + endings. Exception: être uses ét-.

| Ending | je | tu | il/elle | nous | vous | ils/elles |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Imparfait | -ais | -ais | -ait | -ions | -iez | -aient |

`Nous finissons → je finissais` (we finish → I was finishing). `J'étais fatigué.` (I was tired, male speaker.) Keep the stem i in `nous étudiions` (we were studying).

| Story function | Tense | Example and meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Scene / ongoing frame | Imparfait | `Il pleuvait.` (It was raining.) |
| Event that advances the story | Passé composé | `Le bus est arrivé.` (The bus arrived.) |
| Backward step from a past reference | Plus-que-parfait | `Le train était déjà parti.` (The train had already left.) |

`Je lisais quand tu as appelé.` (I was reading when you called.) Reading is the frame; the call is an event.

Duration is not the deciding rule: `J'ai travaillé pendant huit heures.` (I worked for eight hours.) presents the whole period as completed.

## Future and conditional stems and endings — Chapters 20–21

**Future stem:** infinitive for regular -er/-ir; drop the final e for regular -re. The future and conditional share this stem but use different endings.

| Form | je | tu | il/elle | nous | vous | ils/elles |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Futur simple | -ai | -as | -a | -ons | -ez | -ont |
| Conditionnel présent | -ais | -ais | -ait | -ions | -iez | -aient |

`Je parlerai.` (I will speak.) `Je parlerais.` (I would speak.) `Nous vendrons.` (We will sell.) `Nous vendrions.` (We would sell.)

| Infinitive | Shared future/conditional stem | Example and meaning |
|---|---|---|
| être / avoir / aller | ser- / aur- / ir- | `je serai` (I will be), `j'aurais` (I would have), `j'irai` (I will go) |
| faire / venir / voir | fer- / viendr- / verr- | `je ferai` (I will do), `je viendrais` (I would come), `je verrai` (I will see) |
| pouvoir / vouloir | pourr- / voudr- | `je pourrais` (I could), `je voudrais` (I would like) |
| devoir / savoir | devr- / saur- | `tu devrais` (you should), `je saurai` (I will know) |
| recevoir / envoyer | recevr- / enverr- | `je recevrai` (I will receive), `j'enverrai` (I will send) |
| falloir / pleuvoir | faudr- / pleuvr- | `il faudra` (it will be necessary), `il pleuvra` (it will rain) |

**Futur proche:** present aller + infinitive: `Je vais partir.` (I am going to leave.) It links an intention or prediction to the present; it is not restricted to the next few minutes.

**Futur antérieur:** future auxiliary + participle: `Quand tu auras fini, nous partirons.` (When you have finished, we will leave.)

**Conditionnel passé:** conditional auxiliary + participle: `J'aurais dû appeler.` (I should have called.)

After future time conjunctions, French often uses a future where English uses a present: `Quand tu arriveras, je sortirai.` (When you arrive, I will go out.) Conditional si clauses follow a different pattern below.

## Three si patterns — Chapter 21

| Meaning | If clause | Result clause | Example and meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open possibility | si + present | Future, present, or imperative | `Si tu viens, je cuisinerai.` (If you come, I will cook.) |
| Imagined present/future | si + imparfait | Present conditional | `Si tu venais, je cuisinerais.` (If you came, I would cook.) |
| Unreal past | si + plus-que-parfait | Past conditional | `Si tu étais venu, j'aurais cuisiné.` (If you had come, I would have cooked; male visitor.) |

Do not put a future or conditional immediately after si meaning “if” in these patterns. This restriction does not apply to si meaning “whether” in an indirect question. Use s'il/s'ils, but keep si elle.

An English habitual “would” can need imparfait, not conditional: `Quand j'étais petit, je jouais dehors.` (When I was little, I would play outside; male speaker.)

## Subjunctive versus indicative — Chapter 22

For most verbs, singular and ils/elles forms use the present ils stem minus -ent; nous/vous use the present nous stem minus -ons. Add the endings below. Learn exceptional verbs separately.

| Ending | je | tu | il/elle | nous | vous | ils/elles |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Present subjunctive | -e | -es | -e | -ions | -iez | -ent |

`Il faut que je vienne.` (I have to come.) `Il faut que nous venions.` (We have to come.) A change of stem does not mean a change of tense.

| Verb | je | tu | il/elle | nous | vous | ils/elles |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| être, after que | `je sois` (that I am) | `tu sois` (that you are) | `elle soit` (that she is) | `nous soyons` (that we are) | `vous soyez` (that you are) | `ils soient` (that they are) |
| avoir, after que | `j'aie` (that I have) | `tu aies` (that you have) | `il ait` (that he has) | `nous ayons` (that we have) | `vous ayez` (that you have) | `elles aient` (that they have) |

Other anchors: `que je fasse` (that I do), `que je puisse` (that I can), `que je sache` (that I know), `que j'aille` (that I go), `que je veuille` (that I want).

| Construction | Usual mood | Example and meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Necessity, desire, emotion, doubt | Subjunctive | `Je veux qu'elle parte.` (I want her to leave.) |
| Certainty, knowledge, positive belief | Indicative | `Je sais qu'elle est ici.` (I know she is here.) |
| Positive espérer | Indicative | `J'espère qu'elle viendra.` (I hope she will come.) |
| Negative belief withholding belief | Usually subjunctive | `Je ne pense pas qu'elle vienne.` (I do not think she is coming.) |
| avant que | Subjunctive | `Je pars avant qu'il arrive.` (I leave before he arrives.) |
| après que, careful standard French | Indicative | `Je pars après qu'il est arrivé.` (I leave after he has arrived.) |

Que alone does not trigger subjunctive, and subjunctive does not simply mean “unreal.” For the same subject with vouloir, use an infinitive: `Je veux partir.` (I want to leave.)

For completion under a trigger, use subjunctive avoir/être + participle: `Je suis contente qu'elle soit venue.` (I am happy she came; female speaker.) Modern French normally keeps present/past subjunctive even after a past main verb.

## Commands and pronoun placement — Chapter 23

Use tu/nous/vous forms with no subject. Most match the present; -er verbs and verbs such as ouvrir lose the tu -s; aller gives va.

| Verb | tu command | nous command | vous command |
|---|---|---|---|
| parler | `parle` (speak) | `parlons` (let's speak) | `parlez` (speak, formal/plural) |
| finir | `finis` (finish) | `finissons` (let's finish) | `finissez` (finish, formal/plural) |
| être | `sois` (be) | `soyons` (let's be) | `soyez` (be, formal/plural) |
| avoir | `aie` (have) | `ayons` (let's have) | `ayez` (have, formal/plural) |
| savoir | `sache` (know) | `sachons` (let's find out) | `sachez` (know, formal/plural) |

- Affirmative: **verb → le/la/les → moi/toi/lui/nous/vous/leur → y → en**, joined by hyphens: `Donne-le-moi.` (Give it to me.)
- Negative: **ne → me/te/se/nous/vous → le/la/les → lui/leur → y → en → verb → pas**: `Ne me le donne pas.` (Do not give it to me.)
- Restore -s before directly attached y/en: `Vas-y.` (Go there.) `Parles-en.` (Talk about it.)
- Before y/en, use m'/t' where needed: `Donne-m'en.` (Give me some.)
- Pronouns belonging to an infinitive stay with it: `Va le chercher.` (Go and get it.)

## Infinitives, participles, and the gerund — Chapter 23

| Construction | Formula | Example and meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Modal + action | pouvoir/devoir/vouloir + infinitive | `Je peux venir.` (I can come.) |
| Verb requiring à | apprendre/commencer/réussir à + infinitive | `J'apprends à conduire.` (I am learning to drive.) |
| Verb requiring de | essayer/décider/oublier de + infinitive | `J'essaie de comprendre.` (I am trying to understand.) |
| Before doing, same subject | avant de + infinitive | `Je vérifie avant de signer.` (I check before signing.) |
| After doing | après avoir/être + participle | `Après être arrivée, elle a appelé.` (After arriving, she called.) |
| Negated infinitive | ne pas + infinitive | `J'essaie de ne pas rire.` (I am trying not to laugh.) |
| Present participle | present nous stem minus -ons + -ant | `parlant` (speaking), `finissant` (finishing) |
| Gerund | en + present participle | `J'apprends en écrivant.` (I learn by writing.) |

Exceptions: `étant` (being), `ayant` (having), `sachant` (knowing). The gerund normally shares its subject with the main verb. Verbal present participles do not agree; adjectives can. An English -ing noun often needs an infinitive: `Lire est utile.` (Reading is useful.)

## Literary pasts: recognition only — Chapter 24

| Family | je | tu | il/elle | nous | vous | ils/elles |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Passé simple, -er | -ai | -as | -a | -âmes | -âtes | -èrent |
| Passé simple, i-series | -is | -is | -it | -îmes | -îtes | -irent |
| Passé simple, u-series | -us | -us | -ut | -ûmes | -ûtes | -urent |
| venir/tenir family | -ins | -ins | -int | -înmes | -întes | -inrent |

Recognition anchors: `il parla` (he spoke), `il fut` (he was), `il eut` (he had), `il prit` (he took), `il vint` (he came).

Passé antérieur uses passé simple avoir/être + participle: `Dès qu'elle eut fini, elle partit.` (As soon as she had finished, she left.) For everyday retelling: `Après avoir fini, elle est partie.` (After finishing, she left.)

Literary past does not mean “older event.” Register and narrative viewpoint matter. Isolated forms can be ambiguous: `il finit` can mean “he finishes” or “he finished.”

## Final selection checklist — Chapter 25

1. Locate the reference point: now, a past event, or a future point.
2. Decide whether this is a habit, background, completed whole, or an earlier completed action.
3. Check clause triggers: command, si condition, future time clause, subjunctive trigger, or infinitive construction.
4. Choose the stem and person ending, or the correct auxiliary form plus participle.
5. Check object relationships before adding participle agreement.
6. Place negation and pronouns correctly, then read the result aloud.
