# Chapter 15 Lessons: Future, Conditional, Pluperfect, and Indefinites

Use short sessions. Accuracy from memory matters more than copying volume.

## Unit 1: Future tense

Add -ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont to the infinitive or irregular future stem.

### Examples

- `Je parlerai.` — I will speak.
- `Nous finirons.` — We will finish.
- `Elle viendra.` — She will come.
- `Ils auront.` — They will have.

### Active writing

1. Copy each example once while saying it.
2. Cover the examples.
3. Rewrite all three from memory.
4. Change the subject, noun, or time expression to make three new sentences.
5. Read your new sentences aloud.

## Unit 2: Conditional

Use the future stem plus imparfait endings. It expresses politeness, wishes, advice, and hypothetical results.

### Examples

- `Je voudrais un café.` — I would like a coffee.
- `Nous voyagerions.` — We would travel.
- `Tu devrais dormir.` — You should sleep.

### Active writing

1. Copy each example once while saying it.
2. Cover the examples.
3. Rewrite all three from memory.
4. Change the subject, noun, or time expression to make three new sentences.
5. Read your new sentences aloud.

## Unit 3: Earlier past and unreal past

The pluperfect uses the imparfait of avoir/être plus a participle. The past conditional uses the conditional auxiliary plus a participle.

### Examples

- `J'avais fini.` — I had finished.
- `Elle était partie.` — She had left.
- `Nous aurions accepté.` — We would have accepted.

### Active writing

1. Copy each example once while saying it.
2. Cover the examples.
3. Rewrite all three from memory.
4. Change the subject, noun, or time expression to make three new sentences.
5. Read your new sentences aloud.

## Unit 4: Tout and indefinites

Tout changes form as an adjective or pronoun: tout, toute, tous, toutes. Other useful indefinite forms include chaque, plusieurs, certains, aucun, and chacun.

### Examples

- `toute la journée` — all day
- `tous les clients` — all the customers
- `plusieurs idées` — several ideas
- `chacun choisira` — each person will choose

### Active writing

1. Copy each example once while saying it.
2. Cover the examples.
3. Rewrite all three from memory.
4. Change the subject, noun, or time expression to make three new sentences.
5. Read your new sentences aloud.

## Chapter production task

Write future plans and a hypothetical alternative: five future sentences, four conditional sentences, and one past-conditional sentence.

After writing, circle conjugated verbs, underline agreement or pronoun choices, and read the passage aloud twice.

## Sentence usage practice

Use these complete sentences to practice the chapter structures in context. Read each sentence aloud twice, cover it, and write it from memory.

1. `Demain, nous irons à la banque et nous parlerons au directeur.` — Tomorrow, we will go to the bank and speak to the manager.
2. `Je voudrais ouvrir un compte, s'il vous plaît.` — I would like to open an account, please.
3. `Si j'avais plus de temps, je voyagerais dans tous ces pays.` — If I had more time, I would travel in all these countries.
4. `Elle avait déjà terminé quand nous sommes arrivés.` — She had already finished when we arrived.

### Notice and mark

For each sentence, circle the target verb or grammar structure. Underline the word or phrase that controls its form.

### Change the sentence

Rewrite every sentence twice:

1. change the subject, person, or number;
2. change one meaningful detail such as the place, object, time, or description.

Make every required change to conjugation, agreement, articles, pronouns, and word order.

### Make it personal

Write four new sentences about your own life using the same four chapter structures. Read them aloud, check them, and rewrite only the parts you corrected.
