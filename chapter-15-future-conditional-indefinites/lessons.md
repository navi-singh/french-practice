# Chapter 15 Lessons: The Future, the Conditional, Earlier Past, and Indefinites
This chapter moves you forward and sideways in time. You will learn how French talks about future events, imagined results, unreal past alternatives, and broad ideas such as “all,” “some,” “several,” and “each.” The chapter also gives you the classic `si` patterns that connect real possibilities, present hypotheticals, and regrets about the past.
## Learning objectives
You will learn to:
1. form the future tense with regular and irregular stems;
2. use the future after time expressions such as `quand`, `lorsque`, and `dès que`;
3. form and use the conditional for politeness, wishes, advice, and hypothetical results;
4. build the pluperfect and the past conditional;
5. choose the correct tense pattern in the three standard `si`-clause sequences;
6. use `tout` and several common indefinite adjectives and pronouns accurately.
## Prerequisite quick check
Before beginning, make sure you can already:
- form the imparfait from Chapter 14, because the conditional uses the same endings;
- form the passé composé and recognize past participles from Chapter 13, because the pluperfect and past conditional depend on them;
- identify the difference between a main clause and a subordinate clause;
- recognize agreement in gender and number, since `tout` changes form.
If you cannot produce `nous parlions`, `j'ai fini`, and `elle est arrivée` quickly, review those first.
## How to study this chapter
Learn the future and the conditional together, because they share the same stems. Then study the `si` patterns as fixed sentence frames. When you practice, say the whole two-clause sentence aloud rather than memorizing only isolated verb forms.
## Unit 1: Forming the future tense
### Core idea
For most verbs, the future uses the infinitive as the stem plus the endings `-ai`, `-as`, `-a`, `-ons`, `-ez`, `-ont`. For `-re` verbs, remove the final `-e` first.
### The endings
| Pronoun | Ending |
|---|---|
| `je` | `-ai` |
| `tu` | `-as` |
| `il/elle/on` | `-a` |
| `nous` | `-ons` |
| `vous` | `-ez` |
| `ils/elles` | `-ont` |
### Full model: `parler`
| Pronoun | French | English |
|---|---|---|
| `je` | `je parlerai` | I will speak |
| `tu` | `tu parleras` | you will speak |
| `il/elle/on` | `il parlera` / `elle parlera` / `on parlera` | he/she/we will speak |
| `nous` | `nous parlerons` | we will speak |
| `vous` | `vous parlerez` | you will speak |
| `ils/elles` | `ils parleront` / `elles parleront` | they will speak |
### Three model stems
| Verb type | Future stem | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `-er` | infinitive | `parler` -> `je parlerai` |
| `-ir` | infinitive | `finir` -> `nous finirons` |
| `-re` | infinitive minus final `-e` | `vendre` -> `elle vendra` |
### More examples
- `Demain, nous irons à la banque et nous parlerons au directeur.` — Tomorrow, we will go to the bank and speak to the manager.
- `Tu finiras ce chapitre ce soir.` — You will finish this chapter tonight.
- `On prendra le train de six heures.` — We will take the six o'clock train.
### Pronunciation note
The future endings are usually clear in speech, especially in `nous` and `vous` forms. Listen for the linking consonants in `ils auront`, `elles iront`, and `nous parlerons`.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not use the present tense automatically for future meaning just because English sometimes does. French certainly can use the present for near-future context in conversation, but the regular future is the safe default when you are learning planned or predicted future events.
### Sentence-building practice
1. Conjugate `finir` and `vendre` in the future for all six persons.
2. Build three sentences with these cues: `demain / nous / visiter le musée`, `ce soir / elle / écrire à sa sœur`, `la semaine prochaine / ils / vendre leur voiture`.
3. Say the six future endings from memory.
## Unit 2: Irregular future stems and future-time clauses
### Core idea
Many common verbs have irregular future stems, but the endings stay regular. French also uses the future in time clauses introduced by `quand`, `lorsque`, and `dès que` when the meaning is future.
### Essential irregular future stems
| Infinitive | Future stem | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `aller` | `ir-` | `j'irai` |
| `avoir` | `aur-` | `nous aurons` |
| `devoir` | `devr-` | `tu devras` |
| `envoyer` | `enverr-` | `elle enverra` |
| `être` | `ser-` | `vous serez` |
| `faire` | `fer-` | `on fera` |
| `pouvoir` | `pourr-` | `ils pourront` |
| `savoir` | `saur-` | `je saurai` |
| `venir` | `viendr-` | `elles viendront` |
| `voir` | `verr-` | `tu verras` |
| `vouloir` | `voudr-` | `je voudrai` |
### Learn them in sentences
- `Elle sera prête à huit heures.` — She will be ready at eight o'clock.
- `Nous aurons plus de temps samedi.` — We will have more time on Saturday.
- `Ils viendront après le travail.` — They will come after work.
- `Tu verras la différence demain.` — You will see the difference tomorrow.
### Future after `quand`, `lorsque`, `dès que`
English says “When I arrive, I will call you,” with a present-tense verb in the time clause. French uses the future in **both** clauses when the meaning is future.
| English idea | French structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| when + future time | future + future | `Quand j'arriverai, je t'appellerai.` |
| as soon as + future time | future + future | `Dès que nous finirons, nous sortirons.` |
| when + future time | future + future | `Lorsque vous aurez le temps, vous viendrez.` |
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not write `*Quand j'arrive, je t'appellerai` when you mean a future arrival. Use `Quand j'arriverai, je t'appellerai.`
### Sentence-building practice
1. Conjugate `être`, `avoir`, `aller`, and `venir` in the future for `je`, `nous`, and `ils`.
2. Complete these frames: `Quand je...`, `Lorsque nous...`, `Dès qu'elle...`.
3. Rewrite three English prompts in French: “When we arrive, we will eat.” “As soon as he knows, he will tell me.” “When you see Paul, you will give him the file.”
## Unit 3: Forming and using the conditional
### Core idea
The conditional uses the **future stem** plus the **imparfait endings**. It often expresses politeness, desire, advice, or a hypothetical result.
### Endings of the conditional
| Pronoun | Ending |
|---|---|
| `je` | `-ais` |
| `tu` | `-ais` |
| `il/elle/on` | `-ait` |
| `nous` | `-ions` |
| `vous` | `-iez` |
| `ils/elles` | `-aient` |
### Full model: `parler`
| Pronoun | French | English |
|---|---|---|
| `je` | `je parlerais` | I would speak |
| `tu` | `tu parlerais` | you would speak |
| `il/elle/on` | `il parlerait` / `elle parlerait` / `on parlerait` | he/she/we would speak |
| `nous` | `nous parlerions` | we would speak |
| `vous` | `vous parleriez` | you would speak |
| `ils/elles` | `ils parleraient` / `elles parleraient` | they would speak |
### Main uses
| Use | French example | English |
|---|---|---|
| politeness | `Je voudrais ouvrir un compte, s'il vous plaît.` | I would like to open an account, please. |
| wish | `J'aimerais vivre près de la mer.` | I would like to live near the sea. |
| advice | `Tu devrais dormir plus tôt.` | You should sleep earlier. |
| hypothetical result | `Si j'avais plus de temps, je voyagerais dans tous ces pays.` | If I had more time, I would travel in all these countries. |
### Stem-sharing reminder
Because the conditional uses the same stems as the future, the following pairs go together:
- `je serai` / `je serais`
- `nous aurons` / `nous aurions`
- `ils viendront` / `ils viendraient`
### Pronunciation note
In modern French, future and conditional forms can sound very close, especially outside the `nous` and `vous` forms. Context usually tells you whether the meaning is future or hypothetical.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not mix future endings and conditional endings.
- incorrect: `*je voudrai un café` when making a polite request
- correct: `je voudrais un café`
### Sentence-building practice
1. Write the conditional forms of `être`, `avoir`, `faire`, and `venir` for all six persons.
2. Turn these prompts into polite requests: `pouvoir / fermer la fenêtre`; `vouloir / parler au directeur`; `aimer / essayer ce dessert`.
3. Give three pieces of advice beginning with `Tu devrais...` or `Vous devriez...`.
## Unit 4: The pluperfect and the past conditional
### Core idea
The pluperfect (`plus-que-parfait`) places one past action before another past moment. The past conditional expresses what would have happened under different past circumstances.
### Pluperfect structure
**imparfait of `avoir` or `être` + past participle**
| Auxiliary in the imparfait | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| `j'avais` + participle | `J'avais fini.` | I had finished. |
| `elle était` + participle | `Elle était partie.` | She had left. |
| `nous nous étions` + participle | `Nous nous étions levés.` | We had gotten up. |
### Past conditional structure
**conditional of `avoir` or `être` + past participle**
| Auxiliary in the conditional | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| `j'aurais` + participle | `J'aurais accepté.` | I would have accepted. |
| `elle serait` + participle | `Elle serait venue.` | She would have come. |
| `nous nous serions` + participle | `Nous nous serions couchés plus tôt.` | We would have gone to bed earlier. |
### In context
- `Elle avait déjà terminé quand nous sommes arrivés.` — She had already finished when we arrived.
- `Ils étaient partis avant la pluie.` — They had left before the rain.
- `Avec plus d'argent, nous aurions loué un appartement plus grand.` — With more money, we would have rented a bigger apartment.
- `Sans ton message, je serais resté à la maison.` — Without your message, I would have stayed home.
### Agreement reminder
The same passé composé agreement rules still apply when the auxiliary is `être` or when a preceding direct object pronoun triggers agreement.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not confuse `j'avais fini` (had finished) with `j'ai fini` (finished / have finished). The pluperfect needs the **imparfait** auxiliary, not the present-tense auxiliary.
### Sentence-building practice
1. Build one pluperfect sentence with `avoir`, one with `être`, and one pronominal pluperfect sentence.
2. Turn these cues into past conditional sentences: `nous / venir`, `elle / accepter`, `ils / se lever plus tôt`.
3. Pair one pluperfect sentence with one passé composé sentence to show sequence in the past.
## Unit 5: The three standard `si` patterns
### Core idea
French `si` clauses follow stable tense patterns. Learn them as complete sentence frames.
### Essential `si`-clause table
| `si` clause | Main clause | Main meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| `si` + present | present / future / imperative | real possibility or general truth | `Si tu viens, nous dînerons ensemble.` |
| `si` + imparfait | conditional | present or future hypothetical | `Si j'avais plus de temps, je voyagerais.` |
| `si` + pluperfect | past conditional | unreal past result / regret | `Si nous avions réservé plus tôt, nous aurions eu de meilleures places.` |
### Three model sentences
- `Si tu as faim, prends une pomme.` — If you are hungry, have an apple.
- `Si elle trouve le dossier, elle nous appellera.` — If she finds the file, she will call us.
- `Si je gagnais à la loterie, j'achèterais une petite maison.` — If I won the lottery, I would buy a small house.
- `Si vous étiez venus plus tôt, vous auriez vu le début du film.` — If you had come earlier, you would have seen the beginning of the movie.
### Critical warning
Never put the future or the conditional directly after `si` in these standard patterns.
- incorrect: `*Si j'aurai le temps...`
- correct: `Si j'ai le temps...`
- incorrect: `*Si j'aurais le temps...`
- correct: `Si j'avais le temps...`
### Sentence-building practice
1. Finish three real-possibility sentences starting with `Si j'ai...`, `Si nous finissons...`, `Si vous voyez Paul...`.
2. Finish three hypothetical sentences starting with `Si j'étais...`, `Si tu pouvais...`, `Si elles habitaient ici...`.
3. Finish two regret sentences starting with `Si nous avions su...`, `Si je m'étais réveillé plus tôt...`.
## Unit 6: `Tout` and other common indefinite words
### Core idea
Indefinites help you speak about quantity or identity without naming exact numbers or exact people: all, every, some, several, none, each.
### `Tout` as adjective or pronoun
| Form | Typical use | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tout` | masculine singular | `tout le quartier` | the whole neighborhood |
| `toute` | feminine singular | `toute la journée` | the whole day |
| `tous` | masculine plural | `tous les clients` | all the customers |
| `toutes` | feminine plural | `toutes les réponses` | all the answers |
### `Tout` in sentences
- `Tout le monde viendra demain.` — Everyone will come tomorrow.
- `Toute la famille sera là.` — The whole family will be there.
- `Tous partiraient plus tôt si le train arrivait à l'heure.` — Everyone would leave earlier if the train arrived on time.
- `Elles ont lu toutes les consignes.` — They read all the instructions.
### Other useful indefinites
| Word | Function | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| `chaque` | adjective, singular noun after it | `chaque étudiant choisira un sujet` | each student will choose a topic |
| `plusieurs` | adjective or pronoun, plural meaning | `plusieurs sont déjà partis` | several have already left |
| `certains / certaines` | adjective or pronoun | `certaines préféreraient attendre` | some would prefer to wait |
| `quelques` | adjective, plural | `quelques amis viendront` | a few friends will come |
| `aucun / aucune` | adjective, usually with negation | `aucune solution ne marcherait` | no solution would work |
| `chacun / chacune` | pronoun | `chacun aura son billet` | each person will have his or her ticket |
### Common mistake for English speakers
Remember that `chaque` is singular in form, even though it refers to many people one by one: `chaque étudiant aura`, not `*chaque étudiants auront`.
### Sentence-building practice
1. Complete with the right form of `tout`: `___ la ville`, `___ les jours`, `___ mes amies`, `___ le repas`.
2. Write one future sentence with `chaque`, one conditional sentence with `certains`, and one negative sentence with `aucun`.
3. Replace a specific quantity with an indefinite word: `trois amis viendront` -> `quelques amis viendront`.

## Mini-dialogue: planning a move
**Nora :** `Alors, quand est-ce que tu déménageras ?` — So, when will you move?
**Yanis :** `Je partirai probablement en octobre, dès que je signerai le nouveau bail.` — I will probably leave in October, as soon as I sign the new lease.
**Nora :** `Tu vivras seul ?` — Will you live alone?
**Yanis :** `Oui, mais j'aimerais inviter quelques amis le premier soir.` — Yes, but I would like to invite a few friends on the first evening.
**Nora :** `Et si tu trouvais un appartement plus près du centre ?` — And what if you found an apartment closer to downtown?
**Yanis :** `Ce serait pratique, mais ce serait aussi plus cher.` — That would be practical, but it would also be more expensive.
**Nora :** `Tu avais déjà visité ce quartier avant ?` — Had you already visited that neighborhood before?
**Yanis :** `Oui. Si je ne l'avais pas aimé, je ne l'aurais jamais choisi.` — Yes. If I had not liked it, I would never have chosen it.
### Guided analysis

2. Explain why both verbs in `dès que je signerai le nouveau bail, je partirai probablement en octobre` are in the future.
3. Replace `quelques amis` with `toute la famille` and make every necessary agreement change.
4. Rewrite `Ce serait pratique` as direct present-tense advice to `tu`.
5. Create one more `si` sentence that Nora could say.
## Connected reading: un projet pour l'année prochaine
`L'année prochaine, je prendrai une semaine de vacances en avril et je visiterai la Bretagne avec ma sœur. Nous irons en train, et dès que nous arriverons, nous louerons des vélos pour explorer les villages voisins. J'aimerais voir la mer tous les matins, mais je voudrais aussi passer du temps dans les petites librairies du centre. Si le temps était mauvais, nous changerions peut-être notre programme, mais nous trouverions quand même quelque chose d'intéressant à faire. J'avais déjà regardé plusieurs hôtels l'hiver dernier, mais aucun ne convenait vraiment à notre budget. Si j'avais réservé plus tôt, j'aurais sans doute trouvé une meilleure offre.`
— Next year, I will take a week of vacation in April and I will visit Brittany with my sister. We will go by train, and as soon as we arrive, we will rent bicycles to explore the neighboring villages. I would like to see the sea every morning, but I would also like to spend time in the small bookstores downtown. If the weather were bad, we might change our plan, but we would still find something interesting to do. I had already looked at several hotels last winter, but none of them really suited our budget. If I had booked earlier, I probably would have found a better deal.

## Common mistakes to avoid
1. Forgetting to drop the final `-e` in `-re` verbs: `elle vendra`, not `*elle vendera`.
2. Using the present after `quand`, `lorsque`, or `dès que` when the meaning is future.
3. Using future endings instead of conditional endings in polite requests: `je voudrais`, not `*je voudrai`, for “I would like.”
4. Putting future or conditional directly after `si` in the standard patterns.
5. Confusing `j'avais fini` with `j'ai fini`.
6. Forgetting agreement and number with `tout`: `toute la journée`, `tous les livres`, `toutes les tables`.
