# Chapter 15 Exercises

Complete these without notes. Put a question mark beside uncertain answers.

## A. Transform or translate

1. I will speak.
2. She will come.
3. I would like...
4. You should leave.
5. We had finished.
6. all the houses

:::answer
1. `Je parlerai.` — Regular future uses the infinitive `parler` plus -ai for `je`.
2. `Elle viendra.` — `venir` has the irregular future stem viendr-, plus -a for `elle`.
3. `Je voudrais...` — `voudrais` is conditional and is the polite form for “I would like.”
4. `Tu devrais partir.` — `devrais` is conditional of `devoir`, used for advice.
5. `Nous avions fini.` — The pluperfect uses the imparfait auxiliary `avions` plus participle `fini`.
6. `toutes les maisons` — `maisons` is feminine plural, so `tout` becomes `toutes`.
:::

## B. Controlled production

1. Write a new sentence demonstrating **future tense**. Use `Je parlerai.` only as a structural model; change its content.
2. Write a new sentence demonstrating **conditional**. Use `Je voudrais un café.` only as a structural model; change its content.
3. Write a new sentence demonstrating **earlier past and unreal past**. Use `J'avais fini.` only as a structural model; change its content.
4. Write a new sentence demonstrating **tout and indefinites**. Use `toute la journée` only as a structural model; change its content.

:::answer
Answers vary. A correct answer must show the named structure and change the model sentence's content.

1. **Future tense:** `Nous finirons le chapitre demain.` — future stem plus -ons for `nous`.
2. **Conditional:** `Je voudrais une table près de la fenêtre.` — polite conditional request.
3. **Earlier past and unreal past:** `Si j'avais réservé plus tôt, j'aurais trouvé une place.` — pluperfect condition plus past conditional result.
4. **Tout and indefinites:** `Tous les étudiants recevront un message.` — `tous` agrees with masculine plural `étudiants`.

Mark yourself wrong if you put a future or conditional directly after `si`, or if `tout` does not agree with the noun.
:::

## C. Four-way variation

Choose one sentence from each lesson unit and rewrite it four ways:

1. change the subject;
2. make it negative;
3. turn it into a question;
4. change one meaningful detail.

:::answer
Answers vary. Worked example from `Demain, nous irons à la banque.`

1. Change the subject: `Demain, elle ira à la banque.` — irregular future stem ir-, ending -a for `elle`.
2. Make it negative: `Demain, nous n'irons pas à la banque.` — the negative wraps the future verb.
3. Turn it into a question: `Est-ce que nous irons à la banque demain ?` — the future form stays `irons`.
4. Change one meaningful detail: `Demain, nous irons au musée.` — the destination changes, but the future tense does not.

For your own sentences, check the stem first, then the ending, then any `si` pattern or indefinite agreement.
:::

## D. Dictation and correction

Use text-to-speech to hear six examples from `lessons.md`. Write what you hear without looking. Correct in separate passes:

1. vocabulary;
2. verb form;
3. agreement or pronoun placement;
4. spelling and accents.

Do the dictation first, using this chapter's own examples as your audio. Then open the answers to check: they list six sentences worth dictating and the trap each one sets.

:::answer
Six sentences from this chapter that make good dictation material, and the trap each one sets:

1. `Demain, nous irons à la banque et nous parlerons au directeur.` — The trap is the irregular future stem `ir-` beside regular `parlerons`.
2. `Quand j'arriverai, je t'appellerai.` — French uses the future in both halves when `quand` points to future time.
3. `Je voudrais ouvrir un compte, s'il vous plaît.` — `voudrais` is conditional for politeness; future `voudrai` would change the meaning.
4. `Elle avait déjà terminé quand nous sommes arrivés.` — `avait terminé` is pluperfect, earlier than the passé composé arrival.
5. `Si j'avais plus de temps, je voyagerais dans tous ces pays.` — The `si` clause uses the imparfait, not the conditional.
6. `Toutes les réponses sont importantes.` — `toutes` must be feminine plural to agree with `réponses`.

Correct in passes: first identify the tense frame, then check the stem, then the ending, then agreement in the indefinite word.
:::

## F. Oral recall

Explain each of the chapter's four main rules aloud in simple English, then give one French example from memory.

:::answer
1. **Future tense.** Use the infinitive or irregular future stem plus future endings. Example: `Elle viendra demain.`
2. **Conditional.** Use the future stem plus imparfait endings for politeness, wishes, advice, or hypothetical results. Example: `Je voudrais un café.`
3. **Earlier past and unreal past.** The pluperfect uses an imparfait auxiliary; the past conditional uses a conditional auxiliary. Example: `Si j'avais su, je serais venu.`
4. **Tout and indefinites.** `tout` agrees as `tout`, `toute`, `tous`, or `toutes`; words such as `chaque`, `plusieurs`, and `aucun` also have number rules. Example: `Toutes les maisons sont grandes.`
:::
