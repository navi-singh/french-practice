# Chapter 12 Exercises

Complete these without notes. Put a question mark beside uncertain answers.

## A. Transform or translate

1. I wake up.
2. We do not get dressed.
3. Get up! (tu)
4. Wash your hands! (vous)
5. nous parlons -> participle
6. while studying

:::answer
These answers match the answer key.

1. `Je me réveille.` — pronominal verbs need both the subject `je` and the matching reflexive pronoun `me`.
2. `Nous ne nous habillons pas.` — **ne ... pas** wraps the reflexive pronoun plus verb: `ne nous habillons pas`.
3. `Lève-toi !` — in an affirmative `tu` command, `te` becomes `toi` after the verb.
4. `Lavez-vous les mains !` — affirmative `vous` command; French uses `les mains`, not a possessive adjective.
5. `parlant` — form the participle from `nous parlons`: remove the ending and add the participle ending.
6. `en étudiant` — the structure with `en` plus a participle means while studying or by studying.
:::

## B. Controlled production

1. Write a new sentence demonstrating **reflexive verbs**. Use `Je me lève.` only as a structural model; change its content.
2. Write a new sentence demonstrating **questions, negatives, commands**. Use `Elle ne se couche pas.` only as a structural model; change its content.
3. Write a new sentence demonstrating **body parts and reciprocal meaning**. Use `Je me lave les mains.` only as a structural model; change its content.
4. Write a new sentence demonstrating **present participle**. Use `parlant` only as a structural model; change its content.

:::answer
Answers vary. A correct answer must show the named structure and keep the reflexive pronoun with the correct subject.

Sample answers:

1. Reflexive verbs: `Je me couche tôt.` — `me` matches `je`.
2. Questions, negatives, commands: `Ne te lève pas !` — negative command with the reflexive pronoun before the verb.
3. Body parts and reciprocal meaning: `Nous nous lavons les mains.` — definite article for the body part.
4. Present participle: `Je révise en écoutant la leçon.` — `en` plus participle shows a simultaneous action or method.

Mark yourself wrong if the reflexive pronoun is missing, if it stays after an infinitive, if an affirmative command uses `te` instead of `toi`, or if a body part uses a possessive adjective where the reflexive pronoun already shows the owner.
:::

## C. Four-way variation

Choose one sentence from each lesson unit and rewrite it four ways:

1. change the subject;
2. make it negative;
3. turn it into a question;
4. change one meaningful detail.

:::answer
Worked example, starting from `Je me lève tôt.`

1. Change the subject: `Nous nous levons tôt.` — both the subject and the reflexive pronoun change.
2. Make it negative: `Je ne me lève pas tôt.` — **ne ... pas** wraps the reflexive pronoun plus verb.
3. Turn it into a question: `Est-ce que tu te lèves tôt ?` — the reflexive pronoun stays before the verb.
4. Change one meaningful detail: `Je me couche tard.`

Check the reflexive pronoun first. A pronominal verb is incomplete if the subject changes but the reflexive pronoun does not.
:::

## D. Dictation and correction

Use text-to-speech to hear six examples from `lessons.md`. Write what you hear without looking. Correct in separate passes:

1. vocabulary;
2. verb form;
3. agreement or pronoun placement;
4. spelling and accents.

Do the dictation first, using this chapter's own examples as your audio. Then open the answers to check: they list six sentences worth dictating and the trap each one sets.

:::answer
Six sentences from this chapter that make good dictation material, and the trap each one sets:

1. `Je me lève tôt et je me prépare rapidement.` — two reflexive pronouns with one subject; do not drop the second `me`.
2. `Je vais me coucher plus tôt, parce que je me rends compte de ma fatigue.` — the reflexive pronoun stands before the infinitive `coucher`, and the idiom keeps `de`.
3. `Les enfants se lavent les mains avant le dîner.` — body part with `les`, not a possessive adjective.
4. `Dépêche-toi !` — affirmative reflexive command: `te` becomes `toi` after the verb.
5. `Nous nous parlons en préparant le dîner.` — `nous nous` is normal, and `en préparant` has no agreement.
6. `En étudiant chaque jour, elle fait des progrès.` — the participle comes from the verb stem and keeps the accent in `étudiant`.

Correct separately for vocabulary, reflexive pronoun placement, command form, and accents. A missing reflexive pronoun changes the grammar, not just the spelling.
:::

## F. Oral recall

Explain each of the chapter's four main rules aloud in simple English, then give one French example from memory.

:::answer
1. **Reflexive verbs.** Use `me`, `te`, `se`, `nous`, `vous`, or `se` with the matching subject. Example: `Je me prépare.`
2. **Questions, negatives, commands.** The reflexive pronoun normally stays before the verb; in affirmative commands it moves after the verb, and `te` becomes `toi`. Example: `Ne te lève pas !` / `Lève-toi !`
3. **Body parts and reciprocal meaning.** Body parts usually take the definite article, and plural pronominal verbs can mean each other. Example: `Nous nous parlons et nous nous lavons les mains.`
4. **Present participle.** Form it from the `nous` stem plus the participle ending; with `en`, it often shows simultaneous action or method. Example: `J'apprends en écrivant.`

If your example has the right verb but the wrong reflexive pronoun, count it wrong. The pronoun is not optional.
:::
