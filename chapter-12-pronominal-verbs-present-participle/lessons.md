# Chapter 12 Lessons: Pronominal Verbs and the Present Participle
This chapter gives you two very useful tools. First, you will learn pronominal verbs such as `se lever`, `se souvenir de`, and `s'appeler`, which are everywhere in everyday French. Then you will learn the present participle, which helps you connect actions smoothly: `En étudiant chaque jour, elle fait des progrès.`

## Learning objectives
You will learn to:
1. recognize what a pronominal verb is and conjugate common ones in the present;
2. place reflexive pronouns correctly with conjugated verbs and with infinitives;
3. form negative, interrogative, and imperative patterns with pronominal verbs;
4. talk naturally about body parts with reflexive verbs;
5. recognize reciprocal meaning such as `nous nous parlons`;
6. use important idiomatic pronominal verbs such as `se souvenir de` and `s'en aller`;
7. distinguish non-pronominal and pronominal verb pairs with different meanings;
8. form and use the present participle, including `étant`, `ayant`, and `sachant`.

## Prerequisite quick check
Before beginning, make sure you can already:
- conjugate regular present-tense verbs with all six persons;
- form basic negation with `ne...pas`;
- understand command word order from Chapter 11, because affirmative and negative reflexive commands behave differently;
- use definite articles confidently, because body-part expressions depend on them.
If those skills still feel uncertain, review them briefly first.

## How to study this chapter
Say the pronoun and the verb together every time: `je me lève`, `nous nous dépêchons`, `elle va se reposer`. For the present participle, memorize the sound pattern `-ant` and connect it to full phrases such as `en travaillant` and `en lisant`.

## Unit 1: What a pronominal verb is
### Core idea
A pronominal verb appears with a reflexive-form pronoun: `me`, `te`, `se`, `nous`, `vous`, `se`. Sometimes the action comes back to the subject (`je me lave`), and sometimes the pronominal form has a special meaning (`je me souviens`).

### Reflexive pronouns
| Subject | Reflexive pronoun | Before vowel sound | English idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| `je` | `me` | `m'` | myself |
| `tu` | `te` | `t'` | yourself |
| `il / elle / on` | `se` | `s'` | himself / herself / itself |
| `nous` | `nous` | — | ourselves |
| `vous` | `vous` | — | yourself / yourselves |
| `ils / elles` | `se` | `s'` | themselves |

### Model conjugation: `se lever`
| Subject | Form | English |
|---|---|---|
| `je` | `me lève` | get up |
| `tu` | `te lèves` | get up |
| `il / elle / on` | `se lève` | gets up |
| `nous` | `nous levons` | get up |
| `vous` | `vous levez` | get up |
| `ils / elles` | `se lèvent` | get up |

### Examples in context
- `Je me lève tôt et je me prépare rapidement.` — I get up early and get ready quickly.
- `Tu te couches tard le vendredi.` — You go to bed late on Fridays.
- `Nous nous reposons après le déjeuner.` — We rest after lunch.
- `Elles se maquillent avant de sortir.` — They put on makeup before going out.

### Pronunciation note
Before a vowel sound, the reflexive pronoun contracts: `je m'habille`, `tu t'arrêtes`, `elle s'appelle`. Train yourself to hear the pronoun as part of the verb chunk.

### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not forget either half of the structure.
- correct: `Je me lève.`
- incorrect: `*Je lève.`
- incorrect: `*Me lève.`
You need both the subject pronoun and the reflexive pronoun.

### Sentence-building practice
1. Conjugate `se laver` orally with all six persons.
2. Write three sentences about your daily routine with `se lever`, `se coucher`, or `se reposer`.
3. Say one example with `m'`, one with `t'`, and one with `s'`.

## Unit 2: Pronominal verbs in negatives, questions, infinitives, and commands
### Core idea
The reflexive pronoun changes position depending on the structure. With a conjugated verb, it usually stands right before the verb. With an infinitive, it goes before the infinitive. In affirmative commands, it moves after the verb and `te` becomes `toi`.

### Structure overview with `se lever`
| Structure | French | English |
|---|---|---|
| affirmative statement | `Je me lève tôt.` | I get up early. |
| negative statement | `Je ne me lève pas tôt.` | I don't get up early. |
| yes/no question | `Tu te lèves tôt ?` | Do you get up early? |
| `est-ce que` question | `Est-ce que vous vous levez tôt ?` | Do you get up early? |
| with an infinitive | `Je vais me lever.` | I am going to get up. |
| affirmative command | `Lève-toi !` | Get up! |
| negative command | `Ne te lève pas !` | Don't get up! |

### Pronominal verbs with infinitives
The reflexive pronoun stays directly before the infinitive.
- `Je vais me coucher tôt ce soir.` — I am going to go to bed early tonight.
- `Nous voulons nous asseoir ici.` — We want to sit down here.
- `Elle doit se dépêcher.` — She has to hurry.
- `Tu peux te reposer maintenant.` — You can rest now.

### Questions with pronominal verbs
At this level, the easiest question patterns are intonation or `est-ce que`.
- `Tu te sens mieux aujourd'hui ?` — Do you feel better today?
- `Est-ce qu'ils se connaissent ?` — Do they know each other?
- `Pourquoi est-ce que vous vous inquiétez ?` — Why are you worrying?

### Imperative forms of a pronominal verb
| Person | Affirmative command | Negative command |
|---|---|---|
| `tu` | `Lève-toi !` | `Ne te lève pas !` |
| `nous` | `Levons-nous !` | `Ne nous levons pas !` |
| `vous` | `Levez-vous !` | `Ne vous levez pas !` |
In affirmative commands, `te` becomes `toi` after the verb.

### Examples in context
- `Elle ne se couche pas avant minuit.` — She doesn't go to bed before midnight.
- `Vous vous levez tôt demain ?` — Are you getting up early tomorrow?
- `Nous allons nous promener après le dîner.` — We are going to take a walk after dinner.
- `Dépêche-toi !` — Hurry up!
- `Ne vous inquiétez pas.` — Don't worry.

### Pronunciation note
The hyphen in `lève-toi` and `dépêchons-nous` matters in writing. In speech, practice these as single rhythmic groups.

### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not put the reflexive pronoun after the infinitive.
- correct: `Je vais me lever.`
- incorrect: `*Je vais lever me.`
And do not keep `te` in an affirmative command.
- correct: `Lève-toi !`
- incorrect: `*Lève-te !`

### Sentence-building practice
1. Turn `Je me repose` into a negative sentence, a question, and a `je vais...` sentence.
2. Give three commands with `se dépêcher`, one for `tu`, one for `nous`, and one for `vous`.
3. Rewrite `Tu te couches tard.` as a negative command and then as a future-near sentence.

## Unit 3: Body parts and reciprocal meaning
### Core idea
With reflexive verbs and body parts, French usually uses the definite article, not the possessive adjective, because the owner is already obvious from the reflexive pronoun. With plural subjects, some pronominal verbs express actions people do to each other.

### Body parts: use the definite article
| French pattern | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| `se + verb + le / la / les + body part` | `Je me lave les mains.` | I wash my hands. |
| `se + verb + le / la / les + body part` | `Elle se brosse les dents.` | She brushes her teeth. |
| `se + verb + le / la / les + body part` | `Nous nous cassons la voix.` | We strain our voices. |
Examples:
- `Les enfants se lavent les mains avant le dîner.` — The children wash their hands before dinner.
- `Je me brosse les cheveux rapidement.` — I brush my hair quickly.
- `Il se lave le visage à l'eau froide.` — He washes his face with cold water.

### Reciprocal meaning
With `nous`, `vous`, `ils`, or `elles`, a pronominal verb can mean “each other” or “one another.”
| Verb | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| `se parler` | `Nous nous parlons souvent.` | We talk to each other often. |
| `s'écrire` | `Ils s'écrivent chaque semaine.` | They write to each other every week. |
| `se téléphoner` | `Vous vous téléphonez le soir.` | You call each other in the evening. |
| `se voir` | `Elles se voient demain.` | They are seeing each other tomorrow. |
Examples:
- `Nous nous parlons en marchant vers le travail.` — We talk to each other while walking to work.
- `Ils se comprennent bien.` — They understand each other well.
- `Vous vous retrouvez après le cours ?` — Are you meeting up after class?

### Pronunciation note
Listen carefully to repeated pronoun shapes such as `nous nous` and `vous vous`. They look heavy on the page but become natural with repetition.

### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not say `*Je me lave mes mains` in ordinary French. French normally says `Je me lave les mains.` Also remember that `nous nous parlons` means “we speak to each other,” not “we speak ourselves.”

### Sentence-building practice
1. Replace the body part: `les mains`, `les dents`, `le visage`, `les yeux`.
2. Write three reciprocal sentences with `nous`, `vous`, or `ils`.
3. Turn one reciprocal sentence into a question and one into a negative sentence.

## Unit 4: Idiomatic pronominal verbs and meaning contrasts
### Core idea
Some pronominal verbs are not simple “self” actions. They have an idiomatic meaning you should learn as a whole expression. In other cases, adding the pronominal form changes the meaning of the base verb.

### High-frequency idiomatic pronominal verbs
| Verb | Meaning | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| `se souvenir de` | to remember | `Je me souviens de son nom.` | I remember his name. |
| `s'entendre avec` | to get along with | `Elle s'entend bien avec ses collègues.` | She gets along well with her coworkers. |
| `se dépêcher` | to hurry | `Nous nous dépêchons.` | We are hurrying. |
| `se rendre compte de` | to realize | `Tu te rends compte du problème ?` | Do you realize the problem? |
| `s'en aller` | to go away / leave | `Ils s'en vont tôt.` | They leave early. |

### Examples in context
- `Je me souviens de cette adresse.` — I remember this address.
- `On s'entend bien avec nos voisins.` — We get along well with our neighbors.
- `Dépêchez-vous, le train arrive.` — Hurry up, the train is arriving.
- `Elle se rend compte de son erreur.` — She realizes her mistake.
- `Après le dîner, ils s'en vont sans bruit.` — After dinner, they leave quietly.

### Non-pronominal vs. pronominal pairs
| Non-pronominal verb | Meaning | Pronominal verb | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `appeler quelqu'un` | to call someone | `s'appeler` | to be called / named |
| `réveiller quelqu'un` | to wake someone | `se réveiller` | to wake up |
| `amuser quelqu'un` | to amuse someone | `s'amuser` | to have fun |
| `coucher quelqu'un` | to put someone to bed | `se coucher` | to go to bed |
Examples:
- `J'appelle mon frère, mais je m'appelle Karim.` — I call my brother, but my name is Karim.
- `Le bruit réveille les enfants, puis ils se réveillent en colère.` — The noise wakes the children, then they wake up angry.
- `Cette histoire amuse Léa, et Léa s'amuse beaucoup.` — This story amuses Léa, and Léa has a lot of fun.

### Pronunciation note
In `s'en aller`, the `s'` and `en` create a compact sound group. Practice `je m'en vais`, `nous nous en allons`, `allez-vous-en` separately.

### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not translate every pronominal verb word for word. `Je me souviens de` means “I remember,” not “I remember myself.” Also do not forget the prepositions built into certain expressions: `se souvenir de`, `s'entendre avec`, `se rendre compte de`.

### Sentence-building practice
1. Write one sentence with each of these: `se souvenir de`, `s'entendre avec`, `se dépêcher`.
2. Contrast the meaning of `appeler` and `s'appeler` in two short sentences.
3. Write one question with `se rendre compte de` and one answer with `s'en aller`.

## Unit 5: Forming and using the present participle
### Core idea
The present participle usually comes from the `nous` present-tense stem minus `-ons`, plus `-ant`. It often expresses a simultaneous action, especially with `en`.

### Formation rule
| `nous` form | Remove `-ons` | Add `-ant` | Present participle |
|---|---|---|---|
| `nous parlons` | `parl-` | `-ant` | `parlant` |
| `nous finissons` | `finiss-` | `-ant` | `finissant` |
| `nous prenons` | `pren-` | `-ant` | `prenant` |
| `nous écrivons` | `écriv-` | `-ant` | `écrivant` |
| `nous faisons` | `fais-` | `-ant` | `faisant` |

### The three important irregular forms
| Infinitive | Present participle | English |
|---|---|---|
| `être` | `étant` | being |
| `avoir` | `ayant` | having |
| `savoir` | `sachant` | knowing |

### Use 1: with `en` for simultaneous action or means
- `En marchant, elle téléphone.` — While walking, she is talking on the phone.
- `En étudiant chaque jour, elle fait des progrès.` — By studying every day, she makes progress.
- `Nous révisons en écoutant de la musique douce.` — We review while listening to soft music.
A helpful beginner rule: the subject of the `en + participle` phrase is normally the same as the subject of the main verb.

### Use 2: standing alone in a shorter descriptive clause
- `Connaissant déjà la réponse, il n'a rien dit.` — Knowing the answer already, he said nothing.
- `Arrivant trop tard, nous avons raté le début.` — Arriving too late, we missed the beginning.
This style is common in careful written French.

### Use 3: as an adjective
When an `-ant` form behaves like an adjective, it agrees like an adjective.
- `une histoire amusante` — an amusing story
- `des enfants souriants` — smiling children
- `une remarque étonnante` — a surprising remark
With `en`, however, the participle itself does **not** agree.

### Examples in context
- `Nous nous parlons en préparant le dîner.` — We talk to each other while preparing dinner.
- `Ayant très faim, il se sert rapidement.` — Being very hungry / As he is very hungry, he serves himself quickly.
- `C'est une solution rassurante.` — It's a reassuring solution.
- `Sachant qu'elle se lève tôt, nous partons avant dix heures.` — Knowing that she gets up early, we leave before ten o'clock.

### Pronunciation note
The ending `-ant` has a nasal sound. Practice `parlant`, `finissant`, `étant`, `ayant` slowly and clearly.

### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not form the present participle directly from the infinitive. Start from the `nous` form: `nous faisons` -> `faisant`, not `*faireant`. Also do not add agreement after `en`: `en étudiant`, not `*en étudiante`.

### Sentence-building practice
1. Form the present participle of `choisir`, `boire`, `lire`, `faire`, and `être`.
2. Write two sentences with `en + present participle` about your study habits.
3. Turn one `en + participle` sentence into a sentence with a full clause starting with `pendant que` or `quand`.

## Mini-dialogue: getting ready for class
**Inès :** `Tu te prépares déjà ?` — Are you getting ready already?
**Mathis :** `Oui, je vais m'habiller maintenant, mais je ne me sens pas très bien.` — Yes, I'm going to get dressed now, but I don't feel very well.
**Inès :** `Dépêche-toi un peu, sinon nous allons nous inquiéter.` — Hurry up a little, otherwise we are going to worry.
**Mathis :** `Ne t'inquiète pas. En buvant un café, je vais me réveiller.` — Don't worry. By drinking a coffee, I'm going to wake up.
**Inès :** `Tu te souviens de la salle du cours ?` — Do you remember the classroom?
**Mathis :** `Oui, et je m'entends bien avec les étudiants de ce groupe.` — Yes, and I get along well with the students in that group.
**Inès :** `Alors, levons-nous et allons-y.` — Then let's get up and go.
**Mathis :** `D'accord, mais attends-moi en fermant la porte.` — Okay, but wait for me while closing the door.

1. Find one pronominal verb in the near future (`aller + infinitive`) and explain the pronoun position.
2. Find one affirmative reflexive command and one negative reflexive command.
3. Which line contains an idiomatic pronominal verb with a preposition?
4. Which line contains `en + present participle`, and what relationship between the two actions does it show?
5. Rewrite line 7 so that Inès is speaking to one person formally.

## Connected reading: my evening routine at the residence hall
`Le soir, je me détends un peu après le dîner, puis je vais me laver le visage et me brosser les dents. Mes colocataires et moi nous nous racontons notre journée en rangeant la cuisine. Quand l'un de nous se sent stressé, les autres essaient de le calmer en parlant doucement. Je me souviens toujours de préparer mes affaires avant de me coucher, parce que je me rends vite compte de mes oublis le matin. Si nous nous couchons trop tard, nous nous réveillons tous plus mal le lendemain. En travaillant régulièrement, pourtant, nous nous sentons beaucoup plus tranquilles.`
— In the evening, I relax a little after dinner, then I go wash my face and brush my teeth. My roommates and I tell each other about our day while putting away the kitchen. When one of us feels stressed, the others try to calm him or her by speaking softly. I always remember to prepare my things before going to bed, because I quickly realize my oversights in the morning. If we go to bed too late, we all wake up feeling worse the next day. By working regularly, however, we feel much calmer.

4. Rewrite the paragraph with `elle` instead of `je` in the first three sentences.
5. Write three true sentences about your own evening routine using the model.

## Common mistakes to avoid
1. Forgetting the reflexive pronoun: `je me lève`, not `*je lève`.
2. Putting the reflexive pronoun after the infinitive: `je vais me coucher`, not `*je vais coucher me`.
3. Using `te` instead of `toi` in an affirmative reflexive command.
4. Using a possessive adjective with body parts when French normally prefers the definite article.
5. Translating idiomatic pronominal verbs word for word.
6. Mixing up a base verb and its pronominal partner: `j'appelle` is not the same as `je m'appelle`.
7. Forming the present participle from the infinitive instead of the `nous` stem.
