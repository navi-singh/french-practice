# Chapter 16 Lessons: The Present Subjunctive
The subjunctive is not another simple timeline tense like the future or the imparfait. It is a mood: a verb form used in certain dependent clauses after triggers involving necessity, judgment, desire, emotion, uncertainty, or doubt. In this chapter, you will learn how to form it, when it appears, and how to avoid the most frequent English-speaker mistakes.
## Learning objectives
You will learn to:
1. explain what the subjunctive is and where it usually appears;
2. form regular subjunctive verbs from the `ils` present-tense stem;
3. handle stem-changing and irregular subjunctive forms, including `être`, `avoir`, `aller`, `faire`, `pouvoir`, `savoir`, and `vouloir`;
4. use the subjunctive after impersonal expressions such as `il faut que` and `il est important que`;
5. use the subjunctive after expressions of volition, emotion, and doubt when the subject changes;
6. contrast subjunctive-triggering uncertainty with indicative-triggering certainty.
## Prerequisite quick check
Before beginning, make sure you can already:
- conjugate the present tense of common verbs, especially `ils` forms, because regular subjunctive stems come from them;
- recognize dependent clauses introduced by `que`;
- identify whether two verbs in a sentence have the same subject or different subjects;
- distinguish the imparfait stem from the present stem, because some `nous` and `vous` subjunctive forms look like imperfect-family forms.
If you still hesitate with present-tense forms such as `ils viennent`, `ils boivent`, or `ils prennent`, review them now. They matter directly in this chapter.

:::answer
Test the prerequisites before you add a new mood.

1. Give the present `ils` form of `venir`. — `ils viennent`. The regular subjunctive-style stem for singular forms is often found by removing -ent.
2. Find the dependent clause in `Je veux que tu partes`. — `que tu partes`. A dependent clause begins with `que` and has its own subject and verb.
3. Do the subjects change in `Je veux partir` ? — No; both ideas belong to `je`. French normally uses the infinitive, not `que` + subjunctive.
4. Do the subjects change in `Je veux que tu partes` ? — Yes: `je` wants, `tu` leaves. That change makes the subjunctive clause natural.
5. Which looks like an imparfait-family ending: `que nous parlions` or `que je parle` ? — `que nous parlions`. The `nous` and `vous` subjunctive endings resemble imparfait endings, so do not confuse the stem rules.

If you missed two or more, review present stems and `que` clauses first. Subjunctive errors usually begin before the subjunctive verb itself.
:::

## How to study this chapter
Always learn the trigger and the verb together: `il faut que + subjunctive`, `je doute que + subjunctive`, `je pense que + indicative`. When you practice, ask two questions first: “What is the trigger?” and “Did the subject change?”
## Unit 1: What the subjunctive is and how regular forms are built
### Core idea
The subjunctive most often appears in a dependent clause introduced by `que`, after a trigger in the main clause. It is especially common when the main clause expresses necessity, will, emotion, or uncertainty, and when the subject changes.
- `Il faut que vous arriviez avant neuf heures.` — You must arrive before nine o'clock.
- `Je veux que tu réussisses.` — I want you to succeed.
- `Nous sommes contents qu'elle soit ici.` — We are happy that she is here.
### Regular formation rule
Start from the third-person plural present form, remove `-ent`, and add `-e`, `-es`, `-e`, `-ions`, `-iez`, `-ent`.
### The endings
| Pronoun | Ending |
|---|---|
| `je` | `-e` |
| `tu` | `-es` |
| `il/elle/on` | `-e` |
| `nous` | `-ions` |
| `vous` | `-iez` |
| `ils/elles` | `-ent` |
### Full model: `parler`
| Pronoun | French | English |
|---|---|---|
| `que je` | `parle` | that I speak |
| `que tu` | `parles` | that you speak |
| `qu'il/elle/on` | `parle` | that he/she/we speak |
| `que nous` | `parlions` | that we speak |
| `que vous` | `parliez` | that you speak |
| `qu'ils/elles` | `parlent` | that they speak |
### Another regular model: `finir`
| Pronoun | French | English |
|---|---|---|
| `que je` | `finisse` | that I finish |
| `que tu` | `finisses` | that you finish |
| `qu'il/elle/on` | `finisse` | that he/she/we finish |
| `que nous` | `finissions` | that we finish |
| `que vous` | `finissiez` | that you finish |
| `qu'ils/elles` | `finissent` | that they finish |
### Same subject vs different subject
French often prefers an infinitive instead of a subjunctive clause when the subject stays the same.
- `Je veux partir.` — I want to leave.
- `Je veux que tu partes.` — I want you to leave.
### Pronunciation note
Many subjunctive singular forms sound like present-tense forms. The difference often matters more in writing and in sentence structure than in sound alone.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not use the subjunctive just because there is a `que` clause. The trigger matters.
- `Je sais qu'il vient.` — I know he is coming. (indicative, certainty)
- `Je veux qu'il vienne.` — I want him to come. (subjunctive, volition)
### Sentence-building practice
1. Build the subjunctive of `attendre` and `vendre` for all six persons.
2. Change `Je veux partir` to a sentence with a different subject.
3. In each sentence, identify the trigger and the subject of both clauses.

:::answer
1. `que j'attende`, `que tu attendes`, `qu'il attende`, `que nous attendions`, `que vous attendiez`, `qu'ils attendent` — start from `ils attendent`, remove -ent, then add subjunctive endings.
2. `que je vende`, `que tu vendes`, `qu'elle vende`, `que nous vendions`, `que vous vendiez`, `qu'elles vendent` — the stem is vend-, from `ils vendent`.
3. `Je veux que tu partes.` — changing from same subject to different subject makes `que` + subjunctive natural. `Je veux partir.` stays infinitive because the subject is still `je`.
4. In `Je veux que tu partes`, the trigger is `je veux que`; the main-clause subject is `je`, and the dependent-clause subject is `tu`.
:::

## Unit 2: Stem-changing and irregular subjunctive forms
### Core idea
Many verbs still follow the regular endings, but the stem may change. Some very common verbs are completely irregular and must be memorized as full sets.
### Stem-changing pattern: two stems in one paradigm
For many verbs, the singular forms and `ils/elles` come from the third-person plural present stem, while `nous` and `vous` line up with the stem you already know from the present/imparfait family.
### Full model: `venir`
| Pronoun | French | English |
|---|---|---|
| `que je` | `vienne` | that I come |
| `que tu` | `viennes` | that you come |
| `qu'il/elle/on` | `vienne` | that he/she/we come |
| `que nous` | `venions` | that we come |
| `que vous` | `veniez` | that you come |
| `qu'ils/elles` | `viennent` | that they come |
### Another useful pattern: `boire`
- `que je boive` — that I drink
- `que tu boives` — that you drink
- `qu'il boive` — that he drinks
- `que nous buvions` — that we drink
- `que vous buviez` — that you drink
- `qu'ils boivent` — that they drink
### Core irregular verbs
| Verb | `je` | `tu` | `il/elle/on` | `nous` | `vous` | `ils/elles` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `être` | `sois` | `sois` | `soit` | `soyons` | `soyez` | `soient` |
| `avoir` | `aie` | `aies` | `ait` | `ayons` | `ayez` | `aient` |
| `aller` | `aille` | `ailles` | `aille` | `allions` | `alliez` | `aillent` |
| `faire` | `fasse` | `fasses` | `fasse` | `fassions` | `fassiez` | `fassent` |
| `pouvoir` | `puisse` | `puisses` | `puisse` | `puissions` | `puissiez` | `puissent` |
| `savoir` | `sache` | `saches` | `sache` | `sachions` | `sachiez` | `sachent` |
| `vouloir` | `veuille` | `veuilles` | `veuille` | `voulions` | `vouliez` | `veuillent` |
### Fixed one-form verbs
| Verb | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `falloir` | `qu'il faille` | `Je regrette qu'il faille partir si tôt.` |
| `pleuvoir` | `qu'il pleuve` | `Je doute qu'il pleuve demain.` |
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not assume that the `nous` and `vous` subjunctive forms always use the same stem as the singular forms. `Que nous venions` does not match `qu'ils viennent` letter for letter.
### Sentence-building practice
1. Write the six subjunctive forms of `être` and `avoir` from memory.
2. Conjugate `venir`, `boire`, and `faire` in the subjunctive with `nous` and `ils`.
3. Build three clauses beginning with `Il faut que...`, each using a different irregular subjunctive form.

:::answer
1. **Être:** `que je sois`, `que tu sois`, `qu'il soit`, `que nous soyons`, `que vous soyez`, `qu'elles soient`.
2. **Avoir:** `que j'aie`, `que tu aies`, `qu'elle ait`, `que nous ayons`, `que vous ayez`, `qu'ils aient`.
3. `venir`: `que nous venions`, `qu'ils viennent` — the `nous` stem differs from the singular and plural third-person stem.
4. `boire`: `que nous buvions`, `qu'ils boivent` — the stem changes between buv- and boiv-.
5. `faire`: `que nous fassions`, `qu'ils fassent` — irregular stem fass-.
6. `Il faut que je sois prêt.` / `Il faut qu'elle aille au bureau.` / `Il faut que nous puissions répondre.` Each sentence has an impersonal trigger and an irregular subjunctive form.
:::

## Unit 3: Impersonal expressions of necessity and judgment
### Core idea
Many impersonal expressions trigger the subjunctive because they present an action as necessary, desirable, important, possible, or regrettable rather than as a plain fact.
### High-frequency triggers
| Trigger | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `il faut que` | it is necessary that / must | `Il faut que vous arriviez avant neuf heures.` |
| `il est important que` | it is important that | `Il est important que tu comprennes cette règle.` |
| `il vaut mieux que` | it is better that | `Il vaut mieux que nous partions maintenant.` |
| `il est possible que` | it is possible that | `Il est possible qu'elle sache déjà la réponse.` |
| `il est dommage que` | it is a shame that | `Il est dommage qu'ils soient absents.` |
### More examples
- `Il faut que mes amis puissent venir à la fête.` — My friends have to be able to come to the party.
- `Il vaut mieux que vous preniez le train plus tôt.` — It is better that you take the earlier train.
- `Il est possible qu'on ait oublié un détail.` — It is possible that we forgot a detail.
### Meaning note
These expressions do not state a fact directly; they frame the second clause as evaluated, necessary, or uncertain. That is why the subjunctive appears.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not replace the subjunctive with an infinitive after these impersonal triggers when the clause has its own subject.
- incorrect: `*Il faut vous arrivez avant neuf heures.`
- correct: `Il faut que vous arriviez avant neuf heures.`
### Sentence-building practice
1. Finish three sentences beginning with `Il faut que...`.
2. Write one sentence with `il est important que`, one with `il vaut mieux que`, and one with `il est possible que`.
3. Change `Il est possible qu'elle soit en retard.` into a sentence of certainty and decide whether the subjunctive remains.

:::answer
1. `Il faut que tu arrives à l'heure.` — `il faut que` expresses necessity, so use the subjunctive.
2. `Il faut que nous finissions ce travail.` — `finissions` is the subjunctive form after the trigger.
3. `Il faut qu'ils soient prudents.` — `être` is irregular: `qu'ils soient`.
4. `Il est important que vous compreniez la règle.` — judgment of importance triggers the subjunctive.
5. `Il vaut mieux que nous partions maintenant.` — `il vaut mieux que` also triggers the subjunctive.
6. `Il est possible qu'elle sache la réponse.` — possibility is not presented as certain fact, so use the subjunctive.
7. Certainty version: `Il est certain qu'elle est en retard.` The subjunctive does not remain, because certainty presents the clause as a fact and uses the indicative.
:::

## Unit 4: Volition, emotion, and doubt
### Core idea
Personal expressions of desire, emotion, fear, and doubt usually trigger the subjunctive when the subject of the second clause is different.
### Common trigger groups
| Group | Trigger | Example |
|---|---|---|
| volition | `vouloir que` | `Je veux que tu viennes.` |
| preference | `préférer que` | `Nous préférons que vous restiez ici.` |
| emotion | `être content(e) que` | `Nous sommes heureux qu'elle soit ici avec nous.` |
| fear | `avoir peur que` | `J'ai peur qu'il oublie l'adresse.` |
| doubt | `douter que` | `Je doute qu'ils puissent finir aujourd'hui.` |
| negative opinion | `ne pas penser que`, `ne pas croire que` | `Je ne pense pas qu'il connaisse la réponse.` |
### More examples
- `Je veux que mes amis puissent venir à la fête.` — I want my friends to be able to come to the party.
- `Elle est contente que nous soyons là.` — She is happy that we are here.
- `Ils doutent que le projet avance assez vite.` — They doubt that the project is moving fast enough.
- `J'ai peur que vous soyez déjà partis.` — I am afraid that you have already left.
### Same-subject reminder
- `Je veux réussir.` — I want to succeed. (same subject -> infinitive)
- `Je veux que mon frère réussisse.` — I want my brother to succeed. (different subject -> subjunctive)
### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not use the indicative after `je ne pense pas que` or `je doute que` in standard careful French.
- standard: `Je ne pense pas qu'il vienne.`
- standard: `Je doute qu'il soit prêt.`
### Sentence-building practice
1. Write one sentence with `vouloir que`, one with `être content que`, one with `avoir peur que`, and one with `douter que`.
2. Change each sentence so the second clause has a different subject.
3. Rewrite `Je veux que je réussisse` in the more natural infinitive pattern.

:::answer
1. `Je veux que tu viennes demain.` — desire plus a different subject triggers the subjunctive.
2. `Nous sommes contents qu'elle soit ici.` — emotion about someone else's situation triggers the subjunctive.
3. `J'ai peur qu'il oublie l'adresse.` — fear triggers the subjunctive.
4. `Je doute qu'ils puissent finir aujourd'hui.` — doubt triggers the subjunctive.
5. Same-subject contrast: `Je veux réussir.` is more natural than `Je veux que je réussisse`, because French normally uses the infinitive when the subject does not change.
6. If you change the dependent subject, the subjunctive returns: `Je veux que mon frère réussisse.`
:::

## Unit 5: Certainty vs uncertainty — indicative or subjunctive?
### Core idea
Some verbs change mood according to meaning. A statement of certainty usually takes the indicative; a statement of doubt, denial, or uncertainty often triggers the subjunctive.
### Key contrasts
| Certainty / assertion | Mood | Doubt / negation | Mood |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Je pense qu'il vient.` | indicative | `Je ne pense pas qu'il vienne.` | subjunctive |
| `Je crois qu'elle a raison.` | indicative | `Je ne crois pas qu'elle ait raison.` | subjunctive |
| `Il est certain qu'ils sont là.` | indicative | `Il n'est pas certain qu'ils soient là.` | subjunctive |
| `Je sais que tu peux le faire.` | indicative | `Je doute que tu puisses le faire.` | subjunctive |
### Practical rule
Ask yourself whether the main clause presents the second clause as a fact or as uncertain / desired / judged. If it is presented as a fact, the indicative is usually right. If not, the subjunctive often appears.
### Common mistake for English speakers
Because English rarely changes the verb form in these clauses, learners often miss the French contrast entirely. Memorize the pairs as opposites: `je pense que` vs `je ne pense pas que`, `je crois que` vs `je ne crois pas que`.
### Sentence-building practice
1. Turn these certainties into doubts: `Je pense qu'il comprend.` `Nous croyons qu'elle viendra.` `Il est certain qu'ils sont prêts.`
2. Turn these doubts back into certainties.
3. Write one pair of your own: affirmative certainty + negative doubt.

:::answer
1. `Je ne pense pas qu'il comprenne.` — negative opinion triggers the subjunctive.
2. `Nous ne croyons pas qu'elle vienne.` — the future idea is still expressed with the present subjunctive after doubt or negation.
3. `Il n'est pas certain qu'ils soient prêts.` — uncertainty triggers the subjunctive; `être` becomes `soient`.
4. Back to certainty: `Je pense qu'il comprend.` — affirmative certainty uses the indicative.
5. `Nous croyons qu'elle viendra.` — belief/assertion uses the indicative, and the future form is possible here.
6. `Il est certain qu'ils sont prêts.` — certainty uses indicative `sont`.
7. Your own pair could be: `Je sais qu'elle a raison.` / `Je doute qu'elle ait raison.` The first presents a fact; the second presents doubt, so the mood changes.
:::

## Mini-dialogue: before the exam
**Maya :** `Il faut que nous arrivions un peu en avance demain.` — We need to arrive a little early tomorrow.
**Jonas :** `Oui, et il est important que nous ayons tous nos papiers.` — Yes, and it is important that we have all our documents.
**Maya :** `Tu crois que le professeur sera déjà là ?` — Do you think the teacher will already be there?
**Jonas :** `Je pense qu'il sera là, mais je ne crois pas qu'il commence exactement à huit heures.` — I think he will be there, but I do not think he will start exactly at eight o'clock.
**Maya :** `Je veux que tu me rappelles ce soir de préparer ma carte d'identité.` — I want you to remind me tonight to prepare my ID card.
**Jonas :** `D'accord, mais j'ai peur que tu oublies quand même.` — Okay, but I'm afraid you will forget anyway.
**Maya :** `Ça m'étonnerait, parce que je suis contente que tu sois aussi organisé.` — That would surprise me, because I am glad that you are so organized.
### Guided analysis
1. Find three subjunctive forms and one indicative form after a certainty trigger.
2. Explain why `qu'il sera là` is indicative but `qu'il commence` is subjunctive.
3. Rewrite `Je veux que tu me rappelles` so that the subject stays the same and the infinitive becomes possible.
4. Change `j'ai peur que tu oublies` into a sentence with `je pense que` and explain the mood change.
5. Add one more line using `il vaut mieux que`.

:::answer
1. Subjunctive forms include `arrivions`, `ayons`, `commence`, `rappelles`, `oublies`, and `sois`. An indicative form after a certainty trigger is `il sera` in `Je pense qu'il sera là`.
2. `qu'il sera là` is indicative because `Je pense que` presents the idea as a belief or assertion. `qu'il commence` is subjunctive because `je ne crois pas que` is negative opinion and does not present the start time as certain.
3. Same subject: `Je veux me rappeler ce soir de préparer ma carte d'identité.` With the same subject, French normally uses an infinitive construction instead of `que` + subjunctive.
4. With certainty: `Je pense que tu oublieras quand même.` The mood changes to indicative because `je pense que` asserts a likely fact; the future is natural because the forgetting is later.
5. Added line: `Il vaut mieux que nous préparions nos sacs ce soir.` `Il vaut mieux que` is a judgment trigger, so `préparions` is subjunctive.
:::

## Connected reading: les conseils d'une sœur aînée
`Ma sœur veut que je prenne mon année universitaire plus au sérieux. Elle insiste pour que je fasse un vrai planning et pour que je choisisse mes priorités plus clairement. Il est important, selon elle, que je sois moins dispersé et que j'aie un espace de travail plus calme. Je sais qu'elle a de bonnes intentions, et je crois qu'elle comprend assez bien mes difficultés. Pourtant, il est possible qu'elle sous-estime le temps que prennent mes trajets quotidiens. Je suis quand même reconnaissant qu'elle m'encourage, même quand elle doute que je change mes habitudes très vite.`
— My sister wants me to take my university year more seriously. She insists that I make a real schedule and choose my priorities more clearly. According to her, it is important that I be less scattered and have a quieter workspace. I know that she has good intentions, and I think that she understands my difficulties fairly well. Yet it is possible that she underestimates how much time my daily commutes take. I am still grateful that she encourages me, even when she doubts that I will change my habits very quickly.
### Reading practice
1. List the subjunctive triggers in the paragraph.
2. Separate the subjunctive clauses from the indicative clauses.
3. Find one sentence of certainty and one of uncertainty.
4. Rewrite the first two sentences so that the narrator talks about `nous` instead of `je`.
5. Write three follow-up sentences: one with `il faut que`, one with `je pense que`, and one with `je doute que`.

:::answer
1. Triggers: `veut que`, `insiste pour que`, `Il est important que`, `Il est possible que`, `suis reconnaissant que`, `doute que`.
2. Subjunctive clauses: `que je prenne`, `que je fasse`, `que je choisisse`, `que je sois`, `que j'aie`, `qu'elle sous-estime`, `qu'elle m'encourage`, `que je change`. Indicative clauses: `qu'elle a`, `qu'elle comprend`, and `que prennent mes trajets quotidiens` inside the factual noun phrase about commute time.
3. Certainty: `Je sais qu'elle a de bonnes intentions` and `je crois qu'elle comprend assez bien mes difficultés`. Uncertainty: `il est possible qu'elle sous-estime...` and `elle doute que je change...`.
4. With `nous`: `Ma sœur veut que nous prenions notre année universitaire plus au sérieux. Elle insiste pour que nous fassions un vrai planning et pour que nous choisissions nos priorités plus clairement.` The subject in the dependent clause changes to `nous`, so the subjunctive forms change too.
5. Follow-up: `Il faut que je fasse un planning réaliste. Je pense que ma sœur a raison. Je doute que je change tout en une semaine.` Necessity and doubt take the subjunctive; affirmative belief takes the indicative.

## Common mistakes to avoid
1. Forming the subjunctive from the infinitive instead of the `ils` present stem.
2. Forgetting the special `nous` and `vous` stems in verbs such as `venir` and `boire`.
3. Using the subjunctive after every `que` clause, even after certainty expressions such as `je sais que`.
4. Using the indicative after `il faut que`, `je doute que`, or `je ne pense pas que`.
5. Keeping a `que` clause when the subject stays the same and an infinitive is more natural.
6. Forgetting that some forms are highly irregular: `sois`, `aie`, `aille`, `fasse`, `puisse`, `sache`, `veuille`.
## Chapter reference sheet
### Regular subjunctive endings
| Pronoun | Ending |
|---|---|
| `je` | `-e` |
| `tu` | `-es` |
| `il/elle/on` | `-e` |
| `nous` | `-ions` |
| `vous` | `-iez` |
| `ils/elles` | `-ent` |
### Formation shortcut
| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | take the `ils/elles` present form |
| 2 | remove `-ent` |
| 3 | add subjunctive endings |
| note | `nous` / `vous` may show a different stem in some verbs |
### High-frequency subjunctive triggers
| Type | Triggers |
|---|---|
| necessity / judgment | `il faut que`, `il est important que`, `il vaut mieux que`, `il est possible que` |
| volition | `vouloir que`, `préférer que` |
| emotion | `être content(e) que`, `être heureux(-se) que`, `avoir peur que` |
| doubt / negation | `douter que`, `ne pas penser que`, `ne pas croire que` |
| certainty (indicative instead) | `je pense que`, `je crois que`, `je sais que`, `il est certain que` |
### Core irregular forms
| Verb | Key forms |
|---|---|
| `être` | `sois, sois, soit, soyons, soyez, soient` |
| `avoir` | `aie, aies, ait, ayons, ayez, aient` |
| `aller` | `aille, ailles, aille, allions, alliez, aillent` |
| `faire` | `fasse, fasses, fasse, fassions, fassiez, fassent` |
| `pouvoir` | `puisse, puisses, puisse, puissions, puissiez, puissent` |
| `savoir` | `sache, saches, sache, sachions, sachiez, sachent` |
| `vouloir` | `veuille, veuilles, veuille, voulions, vouliez, veuillent` |
| special one-form verbs | `qu'il faille`, `qu'il pleuve` |
## Final production assignment
Write a paragraph of 8-12 sentences giving advice, hopes, reactions, and doubts about a real situation in your life. Include:
- at least three different subjunctive triggers;
- one impersonal expression such as `il faut que` or `il vaut mieux que`;
- one sentence of desire or preference;
- one sentence of emotion;
- one sentence of doubt or negative opinion;
- one contrasting sentence of certainty that uses the indicative instead;
- one example where the same-subject version uses an infinitive.
After writing:
1. circle every trigger expression;
2. underline every verb in a `que` clause;
3. label each dependent clause `SUBJ` or `IND`;
4. check whether the subjects change where a subjunctive clause appears;
5. read the paragraph aloud twice;
6. rewrite any clause whose mood choice was wrong.

:::answer
Answers vary. Here is a model paragraph that satisfies the brief:

`Cette semaine, il faut que je prépare un entretien important. Il vaut mieux que je dorme assez et que je relise mes notes. Je veux que mon ami me pose des questions difficiles. Je suis content qu'il soit disponible ce soir. Je doute que je connaisse toutes les réponses, mais je pense que je peux expliquer mon parcours clairement. Je ne crois pas que l'entretien soit facile. Je veux réussir, donc je vais pratiquer encore une heure. Si mon ami est libre, je préfère qu'il me rappelle demain matin.`

How it satisfies the brief: the triggers include `il faut que`, `il vaut mieux que`, `Je veux que`, `Je suis content que`, `Je doute que`, `Je ne crois pas que`, and `je préfère que`; the certainty sentence is `je pense que je peux...`, with indicative `peux`; and the same-subject infinitive is `Je veux réussir`, because the subject stays `je`.

Mark yourself wrong if:
- a certainty trigger such as `je sais que` or `je pense que` takes the subjunctive without a negative or doubt meaning;
- `il faut que` is followed by an indicative form;
- the same subject uses an awkward `que je...` clause where an infinitive is natural;
- an irregular form such as `soit`, `aie`, `fasse`, or `puisse` is replaced by a regular-looking form;
- you choose the mood from English wording instead of from the French trigger.
:::
