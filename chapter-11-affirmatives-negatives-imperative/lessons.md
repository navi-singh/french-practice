# Chapter 11 Lessons: Affirmatives, Negatives, Stressed Pronouns, and the Imperative
This chapter makes your French more interactive. You will learn how to agree, disagree, contradict a negative question, add emphasis with stressed pronouns, and give clear commands such as `Attends`, `Ne pars pas`, and `Donne-le-moi`.

## Learning objectives
You will learn to:
1. answer positively and negatively with more variety than simple `oui` and `non`;
2. use `si` to contradict a negative question or statement;
3. use stressed pronouns after prepositions, for emphasis, in comparisons, and as short answers;
4. form the imperative with `tu`, `nous`, and `vous`;
5. recognize the special imperative forms of `être`, `avoir`, `savoir`, and `vouloir`;
6. apply the `-er` rule in the `tu` command, including the exception before `y` and `en`;
7. form negative commands correctly;
8. place object pronouns correctly in affirmative and negative commands.

## Prerequisite quick check
Before beginning, make sure you can already:
- conjugate common present-tense verbs with `tu`, `nous`, and `vous`;
- form ordinary negation with `ne...pas`;
- recognize direct and indirect object pronouns from Chapter 9, because command word order builds directly on that chapter;
- understand comparison phrases such as `plus que moi`, because stressed pronouns appear there too.
If any of those still feel weak, review them before going on.

## How to study this chapter
Read every example aloud with energy. Commands are easier to remember when you hear them as complete speech chunks: `Attends !`, `Ne bougez pas !`, `Donne-le-moi !`.

## Unit 1: Saying yes, no, and stronger negatives
### Core idea
French has several ways to agree, disagree, or answer a negative question. Learn them as conversation tools, not as isolated vocabulary words.

### Core answer words
| Situation | French answer | Use |
|---|---|---|
| simple yes | `oui` | agreeing with a positive statement or question |
| simple no | `non` | rejecting a statement or question |
| yes to contradict a negative | `si` | correcting a negative question or statement |
| me too | `moi aussi` | agreeing with a positive statement |
| me neither | `moi non plus` | agreeing with a negative statement |
| not at all | `pas du tout` | stronger no |

### `si`: the important contradiction word
Use `si` when the other person has used a negative idea and you want to contradict it.
- `Tu n'aimes pas ça ? — Si, j'aime ça.` — You don't like that? — Yes, I do like it.
- `Vous n'êtes pas prêts ? — Si, nous sommes prêts.` — You aren't ready? — Yes, we are ready.
- `Il ne vient jamais ? — Si, il vient le samedi.` — He never comes? — Yes, he does come on Saturdays.
If you answer with `oui` here, it can sound confusing.

### Echo agreement and disagreement
- `J'aime ce café. — Moi aussi.` — I like this café. — Me too.
- `Nous ne sortons pas ce soir. — Moi non plus.` — We are not going out tonight. — Me neither.
- `Elle n'a plus faim. — Eux non plus.` — She isn't hungry anymore. — Neither are they.

### Other useful negative patterns
| Meaning | Pattern | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| never | `ne...jamais` | `Je ne mange jamais ici.` | I never eat here. |
| no longer / not anymore | `ne...plus` | `Il ne travaille plus ici.` | He no longer works here. |
| nothing | `ne...rien` | `Nous n'avons rien.` | We have nothing. |
| nobody | `ne...personne` | `Je ne vois personne.` | I see nobody. |
| not yet | `ne...pas encore` | `Elle n'est pas encore là.` | She isn't here yet. |

### Examples in context
- `Moi, je ne vois personne dans le jardin.` — As for me, I don't see anyone in the garden.
- `Eux, ils ne travaillent jamais le dimanche.` — As for them, they never work on Sundays.
- `Tu n'as pas fini ? — Si, j'ai fini.` — Haven't you finished? — Yes, I have.
- `Je prends un dessert. — Moi aussi.` — I'm having dessert. — Me too.
- `Nous n'avons plus de pain. — Nous non plus.` — We don't have bread anymore. — Neither do we.

### Pronunciation note
In fast speech, `moi aussi` often flows as one chunk, and `si` is short but important. Train yourself to hear the logic of the answer, not just the single word.

### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not use `oui` to contradict a negative question. French wants `si`.
- correct: `Tu ne viens pas ? — Si, je viens.`
- confusing: `Tu ne viens pas ? — Oui...`
Also remember `moi non plus` matches a negative statement, not a positive one.

### Sentence-building practice
1. Answer these with one or two words: `Tu aimes le chocolat ?`, `Tu n'aimes pas le chocolat ?`, `Nous partons demain.`, `Nous ne partons pas demain.`
2. Rewrite one positive sentence with `moi aussi` and one negative sentence with `moi non plus`.
3. Write four short answers using `si`, `pas du tout`, `jamais`, and `plus`.

## Unit 2: Stressed pronouns
### Core idea
Stressed pronouns are the stronger forms: `moi`, `toi`, `lui`, `elle`, `nous`, `vous`, `eux`, `elles`. They do not replace subject pronouns in ordinary sentences. Instead, they appear in special positions.

### Full chart
| Subject pronoun | Stressed pronoun | English |
|---|---|---|
| `je` | `moi` | me / I |
| `tu` | `toi` | you |
| `il` | `lui` | him / he |
| `elle` | `elle` | her / she |
| `nous` | `nous` | us / we |
| `vous` | `vous` | you |
| `ils` | `eux` | them / they |
| `elles` | `elles` | them / they |

### Main uses
| Use | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| after a preposition | `avec moi` | with me |
| for emphasis | `Lui, il comprend vite.` | He, he understands quickly. |
| in comparisons | `plus que toi` | more than you |
| alone as a short answer | `Qui veut venir ? — Moi.` | Who wants to come? — Me. |
| in a compound subject | `Toi et moi, nous commençons.` | You and I are starting. |
| after `c'est` | `C'est elle.` | It's her. |

### Examples in context
- `Le professeur parle avec nous après le cours.` — The teacher speaks with us after class.
- `Moi, je préfère travailler tôt.` — As for me, I prefer to work early.
- `Elle court plus vite que lui.` — She runs faster than he does.
- `Qui apporte les boissons ? — Eux.` — Who is bringing the drinks? — They are.
- `Lui et moi, nous partageons le même bureau.` — He and I share the same desk.

### Pronunciation note
`Eux` is short and rounded. Do not pronounce it like English “yooks.” Also notice that `lui` and `elle` keep their stressed forms after prepositions: `avec lui`, `pour elle`.

### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not use ordinary subject pronouns after prepositions.
- correct: `avec moi`, `pour eux`, `chez elle`
- incorrect: `*avec je`, `*pour ils`, `*chez elle` is correct only because `elle` is both forms
Also do not replace every subject pronoun with a stressed pronoun. `Moi travaille` is incorrect; say `Moi, je travaille` if you want emphasis.

### Sentence-building practice
1. Replace the noun phrase with a stressed pronoun: `avec Marie`, `pour Paul et moi`, `chez les voisins`, `sans toi`.
2. Build one example for each use in the table above.
3. Write three short comparisons using `que moi`, `que lui`, or `que nous`.

## Unit 3: Forming the imperative
### Core idea
The imperative gives commands, advice, invitations, and instructions. French uses only the `tu`, `nous`, and `vous` forms, and the subject pronoun is normally omitted.

### Regular imperative forms
| Infinitive | `tu` command | `nous` command | `vous` command |
|---|---|---|---|
| `parler` | `parle` | `parlons` | `parlez` |
| `finir` | `finis` | `finissons` | `finissez` |
| `attendre` | `attends` | `attendons` | `attendez` |
| `ouvrir` | `ouvre` | `ouvrons` | `ouvrez` |

### The special `-er` rule
In the `tu` form of regular `-er` verbs, the final `-s` disappears.
- `Parle plus lentement.` — Speak more slowly.
- `Regarde le tableau.` — Look at the board.
- `Ferme la porte.` — Close the door.
But before `y` or `en`, that `-s` reappears for sound flow.
- `Penses-y.` — Think about it.
- `Parles-en à ton frère.` — Talk to your brother about it.
- `Vas-y.` — Go ahead / Go there.

### Irregular imperative forms
| Infinitive | `tu` command | `nous` command | `vous` command |
|---|---|---|---|
| `être` | `sois` | `soyons` | `soyez` |
| `avoir` | `aie` | `ayons` | `ayez` |
| `savoir` | `sache` | `sachons` | `sachez` |
| `vouloir` | `veuille` | `veuillons` | `veuillez` |
| `aller` | `va` | `allons` | `allez` |

### Uses of the imperative
- `Attends ici.` — Wait here.
- `Finissons ce travail maintenant.` — Let's finish this work now.
- `Soyez prudents.` — Be careful.
- `Veuillez entrer.` — Please come in.
- `Sachons écouter.` — Let us know how to listen / Let's learn to listen.

### Pronunciation note
`Allons-y` and `vas-y` are common sound chunks. Practice them aloud until they come out smoothly.

### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not include the subject pronoun in an ordinary command.
- correct: `Fermez la fenêtre.`
- incorrect: `*Vous fermez la fenêtre.` when you mean “Close the window.”
Also remember that `parle` is correct for the `tu` form of a regular `-er` verb, not `*parles`, unless `y` or `en` follows.

### Sentence-building practice
1. Turn these infinitives into three commands each: `écouter`, `choisir`, `attendre`.
2. Write one command with `être`, one with `avoir`, and one polite instruction with `vouloir`.
3. Create two `tu` commands: one regular `-er` form without `-s`, and one with `y` or `en`.

## Unit 4: Negative commands
### Core idea
A negative command surrounds the imperative verb with the negative frame, just as in an ordinary sentence.

### Basic pattern
| Meaning | Pattern | Example | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| do not ... | `ne + imperative + pas` | `Ne parle pas.` | Don't speak. |
| never ... | `ne + imperative + jamais` | `N'arrivez jamais en retard.` | Never arrive late. |
| no longer ... | `ne + imperative + plus` | `N'en demandez plus.` | Don't ask for any more. |
| say nothing | `ne + imperative + rien` | `Ne dites rien.` | Don't say anything. |
| wait for nobody | `ne + imperative + personne` | `N'attends personne ici.` | Wait for nobody here. |

### Examples in context
- `Ne laissez rien sur la table !` — Don't leave anything on the table!
- `Ne bouge pas.` — Don't move.
- `N'oublions pas les clés.` — Let's not forget the keys.
- `Ne parlez plus pendant l'exercice.` — Don't talk anymore during the exercise.
- `N'écrivez jamais votre mot de passe ici.` — Never write your password here.

### Pronunciation note
Before a vowel sound, `ne` becomes `n'`: `N'attendez pas`, `N'oublie rien`, `N'écoutez plus`.

### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not switch to the infinitive after `ne`. French still uses the imperative form.
- correct: `Ne prends pas ça.`
- incorrect: `*Ne prendre pas ça.`
Also place the negative words around the verb, not after the whole sentence.

### Sentence-building practice
1. Turn these into negative commands: `Parle plus vite.`, `Attendez ici.`, `Finissons ce chapitre.`
2. Write one negative command with `jamais`, one with `rien`, and one with `plus`.
3. Say each one aloud with strong intonation.

## Unit 5: Commands with object pronouns
### Core idea
Pronouns move in commands. In an affirmative command, they come **after** the verb and are linked with hyphens. In a negative command, they return **before** the verb in the normal order.

### Affirmative commands: pronouns after the verb
`me` and `te` become `moi` and `toi` after the verb.
| Order after an affirmative command | Example |
|---|---|
| verb + `-le / -la / -les` | `Regarde-le !` |
| verb + `-moi / -toi / -lui / -nous / -vous / -leur` | `Donnez-moi ce livre !` |
| verb + direct object + indirect object | `Donne-le-moi !` |
| verb + `-y` / `-en` | `Vas-y !`, `Parles-en !` |
Examples:
- `Regarde-le !` — Look at it / him!
- `Donne-moi le marteau et apporte-le-lui ensuite.` — Give me the hammer and then bring it to him afterward.
- `Envoyez-nous les photos !` — Send us the photos!
- `Prenez-en un.` — Take one of them / Take one.

### Negative commands: pronouns before the verb
In a negative command, pronouns go back before the verb, in the familiar sentence order.
| Order before a negative command | Example |
|---|---|
| `ne + me / te / nous / vous + verb + pas` | `Ne me parle pas.` |
| `ne + le / la / les + verb + pas` | `Ne les ouvre pas.` |
| `ne + me / te / nous / vous + le / la / les + verb + pas` | `Ne me le donne pas.` |
| `ne + lui / leur / y / en + verb + pas` | `Ne leur écrivez pas.`, `N'y va pas.`, `N'en prends pas.` |
Examples:
- `Ne le regardez pas !` — Don't look at it / him!
- `Ne me le donne pas !` — Don't give it to me!
- `Ne leur téléphonez pas maintenant.` — Don't call them now.
- `N'y allez pas seuls.` — Don't go there alone.

### Quick contrast table
| Affirmative command | Negative command | English |
|---|---|---|
| `Donne-moi le livre.` | `Ne me donne pas le livre.` | Give me the book. / Don't give me the book. |
| `Regardez-le.` | `Ne le regardez pas.` | Look at it. / Don't look at it. |
| `Parles-en à ta sœur.` | `N'en parle pas à ta sœur.` | Talk to your sister about it. / Don't talk to your sister about it. |

### Pronunciation note
Hyphens matter in writing because they show the command structure clearly: `donne-moi`, `regardez-le`, `apporte-le-lui`.

### Common mistake for English speakers
Do not keep pronouns before the verb in affirmative commands.
- correct: `Donne-le-moi !`
- incorrect: `*Me le donne !`
And in negative commands, switch back to normal order.
- correct: `Ne me le donne pas !`
- incorrect: `*Ne donne-le-moi pas !`

### Sentence-building practice
1. Change these to affirmative commands with pronouns: `Tu donnes le stylo à moi.`, `Vous regardez le film.`, `Tu parles de ce problème.`
2. Turn each of those into a negative command.
3. Write two new pairs of your own: one affirmative command and its negative version.

## Mini-dialogue: before leaving the apartment
**Nadia :** `Tu n'as pas mes clés ?` — You don't have my keys?
**Julien :** `Si, elles sont avec moi.` — Yes, I do; they're with me.
**Nadia :** `Alors, donne-les-moi, s'il te plaît.` — Then give them to me, please.
**Julien :** `Tiens. Et prends aussi ton chargeur.` — Here. And take your charger too.
**Nadia :** `Moi, je suis prête, mais eux, ils ne sont pas encore en bas.` — I'm ready, but they aren't downstairs yet.
**Julien :** `Ne les attends pas trop longtemps.` — Don't wait for them too long.
**Nadia :** `D'accord. Toi et moi, on part maintenant ?` — Okay. You and I, are we leaving now?
**Julien :** `Oui, allons-y, mais n'oublie rien cette fois.` — Yes, let's go, but don't forget anything this time.

2. Identify three stressed pronouns and explain their function.
3. Find one affirmative command with a pronoun and one negative command.
4. Why does Nadia say `donne-les-moi` and not `*me les donne`?
5. Rewrite line 8 with `vous` instead of `tu`.

## Connected reading: classroom instructions on the first day
`Le premier jour du cours, notre professeure parle très clairement. D'abord, elle dit : « Avec moi, soyez honnêtes et posez des questions. » Puis elle ajoute : « Si vous ne comprenez pas, dites-le tout de suite ; n'attendez pas la fin. » Moi aussi, j'aime cette règle, parce que les étudiants timides ne parlent pas encore beaucoup. La professeure leur dit souvent : « Vous et moi, nous sommes ici pour apprendre ; ne vous comparez pas aux autres. » Personne ne rit, et personne ne se sent exclu.`
— On the first day of class, our teacher speaks very clearly. First, she says, “With me, be honest and ask questions.” Then she adds, “If you don't understand, say it right away; don't wait until the end.” I like that rule too, because shy students do not talk much yet. The teacher often tells them, “You and I are here to learn; don't compare yourselves to others.” Nobody laughs, and nobody feels excluded.

## Common mistakes to avoid
1. Using `oui` instead of `si` to contradict a negative question.
2. Using `moi aussi` after a negative sentence; French wants `moi non plus`.
3. Using subject pronouns after prepositions: `avec moi`, not `*avec je`.
4. Leaving the subject pronoun inside a command: `Fermez la porte`, not `*Vous fermez la porte`.
5. Forgetting that regular `-er` `tu` commands drop final `-s`: `parle`, not `*parles`, unless `y` or `en` follows.
6. Leaving object pronouns before the verb in affirmative commands.
7. Keeping affirmative command order inside a negative command.
